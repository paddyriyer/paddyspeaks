चमकम् · Chamakam · Anuvāka 7

सप्तमोऽनुवाकःThe Soma cups (grahas)

Taittirīya Saṃhitā 4.7.7 · 5 sense-groups for study · 84 words

✲

Twenty-eight items, each followed by ca me: the cups (graha) in which Soma is drawn and offered — the aṃśu, raśmi and adābhya, the overlord, the upāṃśu and antaryāma, the cups for Indra and Vāyu, Mitra and Varuṇa and the Aśvins, the pratiprasthāna, śukra and manthin, the āgrayaṇa, two vaiśvadeva cups, the dhruva and vaiśvānara, the cups for the seasons and the atigrāhyas, the cups for Indra and Agni, for Indra with the Maruts and for the great Indra, the āditya and sāvitra, and the sārasvata, pauṣṇa, pātnīvata and hāriyojana. Sāyaṇa calls them particular Soma cups known from the Soma section; he identifies the raśmi with the adābhya cup while it is drawn, the overlord with the cup of curds and the vaiśvānara with the dhruva cup at its pouring down, assigns the two vaiśvadeva cups to the morning and the third pressing, and places the sārasvata and pauṣṇa in modified rites. As throughout the Chamakam, the verb kalpatām is supplied from anuvāka 10.

Vyākaraṇa

  • मे me

    Enclitic me (for mama / mahyam). As in every Chamakam item, the verb is not in this anuvāka: it is supplied from anuvāka 10 (kalpatām), ‘may [it] be ready for me’. The last me of the unit is written म because the next item, aindravāyavaḥ, begins with a vowel (e before a vowel → a).

  • अंशुः aṃśuḥ

    u-stem, m. nom. sg. The Taittirīya text writes the nasal before ś as ँ (अँशुः); the padaccheda uses the ordinary anusvāra. Monier-Williams records aṃśu, ‘filament of the Soma plant’, also as the name of a Soma libation.

  • रश्मिः raśmiḥ

    i-stem, m. nom. sg. In the dictionaries raśmi is a ray, a rein or cord; Sāyaṇa says no separate raśmi cup is found and takes the word as a name of the adābhya cup in the course of its drawing.

  • अदाभ्यः adābhyaḥ

    a- (negation) + dābhya, gerundive of dabh ‘to harm, to deceive’: ‘not to be deceived’. As a noun, the adābhya cup (Monier-Williams: a graha of the Jyotiṣṭoma).

  • उपांशुः upāṃśuḥ

    As an adverb upāṃśu is ‘in a low voice, in a whisper’; as a noun, the cup of that name (Monier-Williams: the upāṃśu-graha). Written उपाँशुः in the text.

  • अन्तर्यामः antaryāmaḥ

    antar ‘within’ + yāma (from yam ‘to hold, restrain’). Monier-Williams: a Soma libation performed with suppression of the breath and voice.

Translation

LiteralThe aṃśu [cup] and for me, the raśmi and for me, the adābhya and for me, the overlord and for me, the upāṃśu and for me, the antaryāma and for me [may they be ready].

In readable EnglishMay the aṃśu cup be mine, and the raśmi, the adābhya, the overlord, the upāṃśu and the antaryāma.

Interpretation: Sāyaṇa does not take raśmi as a cup of its own but as the adābhya cup while it is being drawn; Keith translates it as a separate cup (‘the Raśmi’). The word table gives the literal sense ‘ray’ and Sāyaṇa’s identification.

Reading: In the bhāṣya witness, not in the mantra: Sāyaṇa’s list opens with अश्वादयः, evidently for अंश्वादयः (‘aṃśu and the rest’), since the list begins with aṃśu; and the quoted formula ends रस्मिभिः for रश्मिभिः. Neither form is quoted in the note.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa says that the items of this anuvāka are particular Soma cups, well known from the section on the Soma rite: «सोमग्रहविशेषाः सोमप्रकरणे प्रसिद्धाः». Since no separate raśmi cup is found, the adābhya cup itself, in the state of being drawn, is singled out and named by the word raśmi («रश्मिशब्देन निर्दिश्यते»), because the rays are the means of drawing it; he cites its drawing formula, «शुक्रं ते शुक्रेण गृह्णाम्यह्नो रूपेण सूर्यस्य» …, ‘I draw you, the bright, with the bright, with the form of the day, with the rays of the sun’. By adhipati he understands the cup of curds: «अधिपतिशब्देन दधिग्रहो विवक्षितः»; its lordship is its pre-eminence, as śruti says: «ज्येष्ठो वा एष ग्रहाणाम्», ‘this is the eldest of the cups’. The upāṃśu and antaryāma he leaves as known.

Vyākaraṇa

  • ऐन्द्रवायवः aindravāyavaḥ

    A vṛddhi (taddhita) derivative of the dvandva indra-vāyū, in the sense ‘whose deity is …’; masculine, agreeing with graha understood. With maitrāvaruṇa and āśvina it forms the group Sāyaṇa calls the cups for two deities (द्विदेवत्यग्रह).

  • मैत्रावरुणः maitrāvaruṇaḥ

    From the deity-dvandva mitrā-varuṇau, whose first member keeps the long ā of the Vedic dual; the vṛddhi falls on the first member only.

  • प्रतिप्रस्थानः pratiprasthānaḥ

    Monier-Williams gives pratiprasthāna as the name of a Soma-graha (VS) and, in the neuter, the office of the Pratiprasthātṛ (the Adhvaryu’s assistant) and his milk-vessel. Here masculine, as a cup.

  • मन्थी manthī

    Nom. sg. of the in-stem manthin (from manth ‘to stir’): the Soma into which meal has been stirred (Monier-Williams).

  • मे me

    The last me of the unit is written म because the next item, āgrayaṇaḥ, begins with a vowel.

Translation

LiteralThe aindravāyava [cup] and for me, the maitrāvaruṇa and for me, the āśvina and for me, the pratiprasthāna and for me, the śukra and for me, the manthin and for me [may they be ready].

In readable EnglishMay the cup for Indra and Vāyu be mine, and the cup for Mitra and Varuṇa, the cup for the Aśvins, the pratiprasthāna, the śukra and the manthin.

Uncertain: Sāyaṇa’s definition of pratiprasthāna reads, in the witness, प्रतिनि(धि)र्ग्राह्यो, the syllable धि supplied by its editor; so the precise sense (‘a counterpart or substitute to be drawn’?) is not secure. The note quotes only the secure words. Monier-Williams records the pratiprasthāna as a Soma-graha (VS) and as the Pratiprasthātṛ’s milk-vessel (ĀpŚr.).

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa comments only on pratiprasthāna. By that word, he says, is meant a cup to be drawn that goes together with the cups for two deities — «प्रतिप्रस्थानशब्देन द्विदेवत्यग्रहसहभावी» … «ग्राह्यो विवक्षितः» — that is, with the aindravāyava, maitrāvaruṇa and āśvina named just before it. The other cups he includes among those «सोमप्रकरणे प्रसिद्धाः», known from the Soma section.

Vyākaraṇa

  • आग्रयणः āgrayaṇaḥ

    A derivative of agra ‘front, first’. Monier-Williams: a Soma libation (graha) of the Agniṣṭoma.

  • वैश्वदेवः vaiśvadevaḥ

    A vṛddhi derivative of viśve devāḥ, ‘the All-gods’, in the sense ‘whose deity is …’. The same name recurs in unit 4; Sāyaṇa distinguishes the two by the pressing at which each is drawn.

  • वैश्वानरः vaiśvānaraḥ

    A vṛddhi derivative of viśvānara; as an epithet of Agni, Vaiśvānara. Sāyaṇa does not count it as a separate cup (see the traditional note).

  • ऋतुग्रहाः ṛtugrahāḥ

    Plural: the cups drawn for the seasons. The pañcāśat mark [13] falls between its ca and me; no sandhi is made across it.

  • अतिग्राह्याः atigrāhyāḥ

    ati ‘beyond, in addition’ + grāhya, gerundive of grah ‘to draw (a cup)’: ‘to be drawn over and above’. Monier-Williams: three successive libations at the Jyotiṣṭoma. Plural.

  • मे me

    The last me of the unit is written म because the next item, aindrāgnaḥ, begins with a vowel.

LiteralThe āgrayaṇa [cup] and for me, the vaiśvadeva and for me, the dhruva and for me, the vaiśvānara and for me, the cups for the seasons and for me, the atigrāhya [cups] and for me [may they be ready].

In readable EnglishMay the āgrayaṇa cup be mine, and the cup for the All-gods, the dhruva, the vaiśvānara, the cups for the seasons and the atigrāhya cups.

Interpretation: Keith translates vaiśvānara as a cup of its own (‘the (cup) for Vaiśvānara’); Sāyaṇa takes it as the dhruva cup at the moment of its pouring down. The word table gives the literal sense and Sāyaṇa’s identification.

Reading: In the bhāṣya witness, not in the mantra: Sāyaṇa’s sentence reads घ्रुवाख्यस्य, evidently for ध्रुवाख्यस्य, and तदर्वस्थापन्नो, evidently for तदवस्थापन्नः (‘having come into that state’). Only the undisturbed words are quoted in the note.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa identifies the vaiśvānara with the dhruva cup: because the hymn to Vaiśvānara is recited when the dhruva is poured down, the dhruva in that state is called vaiśvānara — «ग्रहस्यावनयनदशायां वैश्वानरसूक्तपाठात्» … «वैश्वानरशब्देनोच्यते». Of the two vaiśvadeva cups in the anuvāka, the first, named here, belongs to the morning pressing: «आद्यो वैश्वदेवः प्रातःसवनगतः». The āgrayaṇa, the cups for the seasons and the atigrāhya cups he leaves as known from the Soma section.

    A vṛddhi derivative of patnīvat ‘having wives, accompanied by the wives’. Monier-Williams: ‘belonging to Agni patnīvat’.

  • हारियोजनः hāriyojanaḥ

    A vṛddhi derivative of hari-yojana, ‘yoker of the two bay horses’, an epithet of Indra; as a noun, the Soma-graha of that name (Monier-Williams).

    The anuvāka ends here (॥ [14]); in the recitation the list runs on into the next anuvāka without a break.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa places the first two of these outside the basic rite. The sārasvata is to be seen in the modified rite (vikṛti) called Abhiṣecanīya — «सारस्वतग्रहोऽभिषेचनीयाख्याविकृतौ» — for there the text prescribes «सारस्वतं ग्रहं गृह्णाति», ‘he draws the sārasvata cup’; and in the same way «पौष्णोऽपि विकृतिगामी द्रष्टव्यः», the pauṣṇa too is to be understood as belonging to a modified rite. On the pātnīvata and the hāriyojana he says nothing.

सारस्वतग्रहोऽभिषेचनीयाख्याविकृतौ दृष्टव्यः ।

“सारस्वतं ग्रहं गृह्णाति” इति तत्राऽऽम्नानात् ।

एवं पौष्णोऽपि विकृतिगामी द्रष्टव्यः ॥

इति श्रीमत्सायणाचार्यविरचिते माधवीये वेदार्थप्रकाशे कृष्णयजुर्वेदीयतैत्तिरीयसंहिताभाष्ये चतुर्थकाण्डे सप्तमप्रपाठके सप्तमोऽनुवाकः ॥

७ ॥
