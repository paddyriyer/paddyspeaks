How the text was established

  1. Base text. W1, anuvāka by anuvāka: Namakam = TS 4.5.1–11, Chamakam = TS 4.7.1–11 (4.7.1 opens with the verse agnāviṣṇū sajoṣasā, which Sāyaṇa treats as the caturgṛhīta offering before the vasordhārā itself).
  2. Collation. W1 and W2 reduced to one phonemic skeleton (visarga ≡ doubled sibilant, any nasal before a consonant ≡ one nasal, jihvāmūlīya/upadhmānīya ≡ visarga …) and compared letter by letter. Every surviving difference is classified below; a new one fails the build.
  3. Arbitration. Where W1 and W2 differ in a letter, Sāyaṇa’s gloss and Abhinava Śaṅkara’s printed text decide; in every case they support W1, and W2’s reading is a keying error or a convention of its line divisions.
  4. Corrections. Three places where W1 itself is mis-keyed were corrected, each supported by W2 and by W1’s own spelling elsewhere. No other letter of W1 has been changed.
  5. Units. Namakam 1, 10 and 11 by Sāyaṇa’s ṛks (in 11 the refrain of ṛk 1 is supplied to ṛks 2–9 by anuṣaṅga, as he says; the Saṃhitā gives it twice, and so does this edition); Namakam 2–9 one yajus to each namaskāra, matching the yajus count Sāyaṇa states for each anuvāka (13, 17, 17, 15, 15, 16, 17, 19); the Chamakam, which Sāyaṇa reads as a single mantra of eleven anuvākas, in short sense-groups for study only — the Saṃhitā of each anuvāka is also given whole and unbroken.
  6. Svara. Shown as W1 marks it. W2’s marks were compared syllable by syllable: 38 syllables in 23 units differ and are flagged on their units. No accent was supplied or changed on the strength of one witness alone, except where W1 had dropped a mark W2 has and the pattern requires (Chamakam 10.3).
  7. Padaccheda. A learner’s word division, not the traditional pada-pāṭha (which none of the witnesses gives). For every anuvāka the separated words are rejoined by the rules of external sandhi and must spell the Saṃhitā exactly (scripts/rudram/check.py); junctions outside the general rules are declared and listed on their units.
  8. IAST. Generated from the Devanagari, never typed, and proven by round trip.
  9. Interpretation. The traditional note on every unit reports Sāyaṇa; every Sanskrit phrase it quotes is verified against his bhāṣya on that anuvāka. Reflections are optional, separate and labelled modern. The check rejects the vocabulary of modern esoterica in any translation or word meaning.
