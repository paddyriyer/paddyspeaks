चमकम् · Chamakam · Anuvāka 10

दशमोऽनुवाकःCattle of every age; life and the breaths made fit by the sacrifice

Taittirīya Saṃhitā 4.7.10 · 4 sense-groups for study · 90 words

✲

The tenth anuvāka has two halves. The first carries the refrain ca me through the herd: the unborn young and the calves, then bulls and cows by age, which Sāyaṇa makes a year and a half, two, two and a half, three, three and a half and four years old, then the breeding bull and the barren cow, the older bull and the cow that miscarries, the draught ox and the newly calved cow. The second states the verb that Sāyaṇa supplies to every item of the Chamakam: ‘may life be made fit by the sacrifice’, said of the breaths, the eye and ear, mind and speech, the self (Sāyaṇa: the body) and the sacrifice itself. Sāyaṇa reads kalpatām as ‘may it become able to do its work’.

Vyākaraṇa

  • गर्भाः garbhāḥ

    Nominative plural, as is vatsāḥ: the whole class is asked for. Every item of the Chamakam is a nominative subject of the supplied kalpatām.

  • त्र्यविः tryaviḥ

    i-stem tryavi, feminine tryavī in -ī. The second member is avi, ‘sheep’; MW renders the compound ‘three sheep-times old’, a calf of eighteen months. Sāyaṇa's ages (tryavi a year and a half, pañcāvi two and a half) imply that one avi is half a year.

  • दित्यवाट् dityavāṭ

    Nominative singular of the stem dityavāh (MW: strong stem -vāh, nom. -vāṭ, instr. dityauhā). Final h becomes ḍh at the end of a word (P. 8.2.31), ṭ in pause.

  • दित्यौही dityauhī

    Feminine of dityavāh in -ī. Before the vowel of the ending vāh contracts to ūh (P. 6.4.132, cited by MW), and a + ū gives au: ditya + ūhī → dityauhī.

Translation

LiteralEmbryos and for me, calves and for me, the [male] of three avis and for me, the [female] of three avis and for me, the two-year-old [male] and for me, the two-year-old [female] and for me [may they be ready].

In readable EnglishMay the unborn young be mine, and the calves; the bull and the heifer of a year and a half, and the bull and the heifer of two years.

Uncertain: The unit of age in tryavi (and pañcāvi in unit 2) is not explained by Sāyaṇa; MW gives ‘three sheep-times old’, a calf of eighteen months. That one avi is half a year is an inference from the ages Sāyaṇa states, not something he says.

Uncertain: The first member of dityavāh is taken by MW, with ‘prob.’, as ditya = dvitīya, ‘second’. Sāyaṇa states only the age (two years).

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa calls the unborn young and the calves well known («गर्भा वत्साश्च प्रसिद्धाः») and then gives each of the following terms an age. The tryavi is a young bull of a year and a half, still calf-like (वत्सरूपः); the printed bhāṣya is corrupt at this point (सार्धसंमत्सरो … पुंगवख्यविः), but the age and the bull are clear. A cow of that kind is tryavī: «तथाविधा गौस्त्र्यवी». The dityavāh is a bull of two years, «द्विसंवत्सर ऋषभो दित्यवाट्», and the dityauhī the cow of that age («तथाविधा गौर्दित्यौही»).

Contemplative reflection · modern, not a translation

The list of cattle begins before birth, with the unborn young, and moves through the herd half a year at a time: what is asked for is a herd that continues.

Vyākaraṇa

  • पञ्चाविः pañcāviḥ

    pañca + avi, the two a's fused (pañcāvi); feminine pañcāvī in -ī. Formed like tryavi in unit 1.

  • तुर्यवाट् turyavāṭ

    Nominative singular of turyavāh, formed like dityavāh: -vāṭ in the nominative, feminine turyauhī (vāh → ūh, a + ū → au).

Translation

LiteralThe [male] of five avis and for me, the [female] of five avis and for me, the three-year-old [male] and for me, the three-year-old [female] and for me, the turyavāh [male] and for me, the turyauhī [female] and for me [may they be ready].

In readable EnglishMay the bull and the heifer of two and a half years be mine, the bull and the heifer of three years, and the bull and the heifer of three and a half.

Interpretation: MW makes turyavāh / turyauhī an ox or cow four years old (turya, ‘fourth’); Sāyaṇa makes them three and a half, which keeps his series in half-years (1½, 2, 2½, 3, 3½, 4) and leaves four years for paṣṭhavāh in unit 3. The readable translation follows Sāyaṇa; the word table gives both.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa continues the ages half a year at a time. The pañcāvi is a bull of two and a half years (सार्धद्विसंवत्सर), and the cow of that age is pañcāvī («गौः पञ्चावी»). The trivatsa is a bull of three years, «संवत्सरत्रयोपेत ऋषभस्त्रिवत्सः», and trivatsā the cow of that age («तथाविधा गौस्त्रिवत्सा»). The turyavāh is a bull of three and a half years, «सार्धत्रिवत्सर ऋषभस्तुर्यवाट्», and turyauhī the cow of that age.

Sandhi & sandhi-viccheda

  • पष्ठवाच्च paṣṭhavācca → पष्ठवात् + च

    consonant sandhi (palatalisation)

    final t before c becomes c (stoḥ ścunā ścuḥ, P. 8.4.40). The Taittirīya nominative of paṣṭhavāh ends in t (MW: nom. -vāṭ in the VS, -vāt in the TS), so the text has paṣṭhavāc ca, where dityavāṭ and turyavāṭ keep ṭ before ca

  • म उक्षा ma ukṣā → मे + उक्षा

    vowel sandhi (ayādi, y dropped)

    final e before a vowel becomes ay (P. 6.1.78), and the y is dropped (P. 8.3.19): me ukṣā → ma ukṣā

  • म ऋषभश्च ma ṛṣabhaśca → मे + ऋषभः + च

    vowel sandhi (ayādi, y dropped); visarga sandhi

    me before ṛ becomes ma, as before u; ṛṣabhaḥ ca → ṛṣabhaś ca

  • वेहच्च vehacca → वेहत् + च

    consonant sandhi (palatalisation)

    final t before c becomes c (P. 8.4.40)

  • मेऽनड्वाञ्च me'naḍvāñca → मे + अनड्वान् + च

    vowel sandhi (pūrvarūpa); nasal sandhi

    after word-final e an initial a is absorbed (eṅaḥ padāntād ati, P. 6.1.109), marked by the avagraha; final n before c is written as the palatal nasal ñ (P. 8.4.40). The Taittirīya text has no inserted sibilant here, where classical usage writes anaḍvāṃś ca (P. 8.3.7)

  • धेनुश्च म dhenuśca ma → धेनुः + च + मे

    visarga sandhi; vowel sandhi (ayādi, y dropped)

    dhenuḥ ca → dhenuś ca; the last me stands before āyuḥ, the first word of unit 4, and becomes ma

Vyākaraṇa

  • पष्ठवात् paṣṭhavāt

    Nominative singular of paṣṭhavāh. MW records the nominative as -vāṭ in the Vājasaneyi and -vāt in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā; the text's paṣṭhavāc ca (t + c → cc) shows the t. Sāyaṇa's printed bhāṣya also writes the word with t (षष्ठवात्, with ṣ for p). The feminine is paṣṭhauhī, formed like dityauhī.

  • उक्षा ukṣā

    n-stem ukṣan, nominative singular ukṣā (the n is lost in the nominative).

  • वेहत् vehat

    A feminine consonant stem in t; the nominative is the bare stem, vehat, so vehac ca.

  • अनड्वान् anaḍvān

    Nominative singular of anaḍuh, from the strong stem anaḍvāh- with n inserted in the nominative singular (P. 7.1.98, 7.1.82).

Translation

LiteralThe four-year-old [male] and for me, the four-year-old [female] and for me, the bull (ukṣan) and for me, the barren cow and for me, the bull (ṛṣabha) and for me, the cow that miscarries and for me, the ox and for me, the milch cow and for me [may they be ready].

Reading: corrections.json C10.3-vehat: the base witness runs च and मे together after वेहत् and omits the svarita on च; the edition spaces them and restores the mark, as the second witness has it and as every other ‘X ca me’ of the Chamakam stands. Interpretation: Sāyaṇa distinguishes ukṣan (the bull able to breed) from ṛṣabha (a still older bull); the dictionaries give both simply as ‘bull’. For vehat MW gives ‘a barren cow or a cow that miscarries’; Sāyaṇa: गर्भघातिनी. The translation follows Sāyaṇa.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa: the paṣṭhavāh is a bull of four years (चतुःसंवत्सर ऋषभः), and paṣṭhauhī a cow of that age («तथाविधा गौः पष्ठौही»). The ukṣan is the bull able to impregnate, «सेचनसमर्थ ऋषभ उक्षा»; the vaśā is a barren cow, «वन्ध्या गौर्वशा»; the ṛṣabha is a bull older even than the ukṣan, «उक्ष्णोऽप्याधिकवयस्क ऋषभः»; the vehat is a cow that loses her embryo (गर्भघातिनी); the anaḍvān is the bearer of a cart, «शकटस्य वाहकोऽनड्वान्»; and the dhenu is a cow that has newly calved, «नवप्रसूता गौर्धेनुः». In his comment on the eleventh anuvāka Sāyaṇa quotes the Brāhmaṇa on this first half of the tenth: it is the form of cattle, and by the form the sacrificer secures cattle.

Translation

LiteralMay life be fit through the sacrifice; may the breath be fit through the sacrifice; may the down-breath be fit through the sacrifice; may the diffused breath be fit through the sacrifice; may the eye be fit through the sacrifice; may the ear be fit through the sacrifice; may the mind be fit through the sacrifice; may speech be fit through the sacrifice; may the self be fit through the sacrifice; may the sacrifice be fit through the sacrifice.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa treats life and the rest as well known («आयुरादयः प्रसिद्धाः») and glosses only ātman, as the body: «आत्मा शरीरम्». The prayer is that these become able to do their work by my sacrifice: «उक्ता आयुरादयो मदीयेन यज्ञेन कार्यक्षमा भवन्तु». The last yajñaḥ is a sacrifice still to be performed (करिष्यमाणः), such as the Aśvamedha (the printed text misprints the name), and it too is to become fit for its own work through the sacrifice now being performed: «सोऽप्यनेनानुष्ठीयमानेन यज्ञेन स्वकार्यक्षमो भवतु». In his comment on the eleventh anuvāka Sāyaṇa quotes the Brāhmaṇa, which calls these ten formulas kalpas and says they are offered so that what is not yet fit may become fit; in his comment on the first anuvāka he says that kalpatām, stated here, is to be supplied to every item of the Chamakam.

Contemplative reflection · modern, not a translation

The last of the ten asks that the sacrifice be made fit by the sacrifice: the act is asked to set itself right as it is done.

Bhāṣya

तथाविधा गौस्त्रिवत्सा ।

Bhāṣya

सार्धत्रिवत्सर ऋषभस्तुर्यवाट् ।

Bhāṣya

तथाविधा नौस्तुर्यौहि ।

Bhāṣya

चतुःसंवत्सर ऋषभः षष्ठवात् ।

Bhāṣya

तथाविधा गौः पष्ठौही ।

Bhāṣya

सेचनसमर्थ ऋषभ उक्षा ।

Bhāṣya

वन्ध्या गौर्वशा ।

Bhāṣya

उक्ष्णोऽप्याधिकवयस्क ऋषभः।

Bhāṣya

गर्भघातिनी गोर्वेहत् ।

Bhāṣya

शकटस्य वाहकोऽनड्वान् ।

Bhāṣya

नवप्रसूता गौर्धेनुः

Bhāṣya

आयुरादयः प्रसिद्धाः ।

Bhāṣya

आत्मा शरीरम् ।

उक्ता आयुरादयो मदीयेन यज्ञेन कार्यक्षमा भवन्तु ।

Bhāṣya

यज्ञः करिष्यमाणोऽश्वगेधादिः ।

सोऽप्यनेनानुष्ठीयमानेन यज्ञेन स्वकार्यक्षमो भवतु ॥

चतुर्थकाण्डे सप्तमप्रपाठके दशमोऽनुवाकः
