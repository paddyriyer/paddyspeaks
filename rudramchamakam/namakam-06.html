नमकम् · Namakam · Anuvāka 6

षष्ठोऽनुवाकःHomage to the eldest and the youngest, in field and forest, and to the armed warrior

Taittirīya Saṃhitā 4.5.6 · 15 yajus · 75 words

✲

Fifteen yajus of the same shape as the fifth anuvāka: one namaḥ, two names, each followed by ca. Rudra is saluted as the eldest and the youngest, the first-born and the last-born, then as present in places — the world of men, Yama’s realm, the field and the threshing floor, the Vedic verses and their end, forest and thicket — and in sound and its echo, and finally as the armed warrior with a swift army and chariot, armour, helmet and a renowned host. Sāyaṇa reads most names as Rudra present in a thing, in the form of a thing, or worthy of it.

Samāsa & samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this unit; every word is a simple word.

Vyākaraṇa

  • ज्येष्ठाय jyeṣṭhāya

    Superlative in -iṣṭha, with jya- as its stem (P. 5.3.61); ‘eldest’ and ‘most excellent’. The dative goes with namaḥ: ‘homage to X’ takes the dative (P. 2.3.16). So does every name in this anuvāka.

  • कनिष्ठाय kaniṣṭhāya

    Superlative in -iṣṭha on the stem kan- (‘young, small’), the opposite of jyeṣṭha: ‘youngest, least’.

  • च ca

    Each yajus of this anuvāka has the shape namaḥ X ca Y ca: one salutation, two names, each followed by ca.

Translation

LiteralHomage [be] to the eldest and to the youngest.

In readable EnglishHomage to the eldest and to the youngest.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa opens the anuvāka by recalling that the fifth gave yajus with namaḥ on one side only («पञ्चमेऽनुवाके कानिचिदन्यतरतोनमस्काराणि यजूंष्युक्तानि») and that the sixth gives more of the same kind, fifteen of them («विद्यमानानि पञ्चदश यजूंष्याह»). He reads the pair as rank rather than age: «विद्यैश्वर्यादिभिरधिको ज्येष्ठः» — the eldest is the one greater in learning, lordship and the like — and «तै रहितोऽल्पः कनिष्ठः», the youngest is the small one, without them.

Contemplative reflection · modern, not a translation

One salutation covers the greatest and the least; neither is left outside it.

Interpretation

Vyākaraṇa

  • जघन्याय jaghanyāya

    From jaghana ‘hind part’ with -ya (the dig-ādi group takes -ya in the sense ‘being there’). ‘Hindmost, last’ in ordinary use; Sāyaṇa keeps the sense of place.

  • बुध्नियाय budhniyāya

    From budhna ‘bottom, base, root’. The Taittirīya text spells the derivative budhniya- (the dictionaries’ budhnya-, ‘being at the base, of the depths’). A taddhita in -ya, ‘being in, belonging to’ (tatra bhavaḥ, P. 4.3.53); not a compound.

Translation

LiteralHomage [be] to the hindmost and to the one at the base.

In readable EnglishHomage to the one at the hind part and to the one at the root.

Uncertain: Sāyaṇa’s witness prints both budhna and budhniya with घ for ध (बुघ्ने, बुघ्नियः); the quotation above uses only the undamaged part of the gloss.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa derives both names from a place: jaghanya from jaghana, the hind part — «जघने गवादीनां पश्चाद्भागे वत्सादिरूपेण भवो जघन्यः», present at the hind part of cows and the like in the form of the calf — and budhniya from budhna, the root: «वृक्षादीनां मूले शाखादिरूपेणोत्पन्नो», born at the root of trees and the like in the form of branches.

  • नमस्सोभ्याय namassobhyāya → नमः + सोभ्याय

    visarga sandhi

    visarga before a sibilant becomes that sibilant (doubled sibilant, as the Taittirīya text writes it; P. 8.3.36 allows visarga or the sibilant)

Samāsa & samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this unit; every word is a simple word.

Vyākaraṇa

  • सोभ्याय sobhyāya

    A -ya derivative of sobha. Sāyaṇa takes sobha as sa- ‘with’ + ubha ‘both’ (merit and sin). A taddhita in -ya, ‘being in, belonging to’ (tatra bhavaḥ, P. 4.3.53); not a compound.

  • प्रतिसर्याय pratisaryāya

    From pratisara (prati + √sṛ) ‘cord or band worn as an amulet’; -ya in the sense ‘worthy of it’ (Sāyaṇa: tam arhati).

Translation

LiteralHomage [be] to the one in Sobha and to the one of the amulet-cord.

In readable EnglishHomage to him who is in the world of men and to him of the protective cord.

Interpretation: The sense of sobhya is not settled. Monier-Williams gives ‘being in or belonging to Sobha’ and ‘a particular personification’; Sāyaṇa’s ‘present in the world of men’ rests on his analysis sa + ubha. The witness prints उभाम्यां and सोमो for उभाभ्यां and the sobha the derivation requires; the quotations above avoid those words.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa analyses sobha as the world that exists together with both merit and sin, that is, the world of men («मनुष्यलोकः»), and supports it with a saying of the Ātharvaṇikas: «पुण्येन पुण्यं लोकं जयति पापेन पापमुभाभ्यामेव मनुष्य लोकम्» — by merit one wins a world of merit, by sin a world of sin, by both the world of men. The one present there is «तत्र भवः सोभ्यः». The pratisara is the protective band held on the hand at a wedding and like occasions: «प्रतिसरो विवाहादौ हस्ते धार्यमाणो रक्षा बन्धस्तमर्हतीति प्रतिसर्यः».

In readable EnglishHomage to him who wears a helmet and to him who wears a cuirass.

Uncertain: Sāyaṇa’s gloss on bilma begins «बिल्मं विलेपेतं …» in the witness; विलेपेतं is not clear and is not used here. The rare word bilma is otherwise ‘chip, splinter’ (Ṛgveda), with ‘helmet’ given by the commentators (Monier-Williams: ‘having a helmet’, after Mahīdhara).

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa: bilma is the head-protector in battle and the one who has it is bilmin — «युद्धे शिरोरक्षकं तदस्यास्तीति बिल्मी»; «कवचः शरीररक्षकः सोऽस्यास्तीति कवची» — the kavaca protects the body, and the one who has it is kavacin.

Translation

LiteralHomage [be] to the famous one and to the one whose army is famous.

In readable EnglishHomage to him who is renowned and to him whose army is renowned.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa: «श्रुतो वेदेषु प्रसिद्धः» — śruta is the one famous in the Vedas; «श्रुता वेदेषु प्रसिद्धा सेना» — his army is renowned in the Vedas, and so he is śrutasena. With this the sixth anuvāka closes.
