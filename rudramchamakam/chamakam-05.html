चमकम् · Chamakam · Anuvāka 5

पञ्चमोऽनुवाकःEarth, metals and plants; cattle, wealth and the rite

Taittirīya Saṃhitā 4.7.5 · 6 sense-groups for study · 98 words

✲

The list turns to the earth and what comes from it: stone, clay, hills, mountains, sand and trees; gold and the other metals; fire, water and plants, cultivated and wild; and the animals of village and forest, for which the text writes its own verb: may they be fit through the sacrifice. It closes with wealth held and wealth to come, prosperity, goods and dwelling, the rite and the power to perform it, the aim and the reaching of it. Sāyaṇa glosses the metals (śyāma is black iron; loha bronze, copper and the like) and reads the last items as possessions and the means of reaching what is desired.

Vyākaraṇa

  • अश्मा aśmā

    Nom. sg. of the n-stem aśman (m.): the n is lost and the vowel lengthened (aśmā).

  • वनस्पतयः vanaspatayaḥ

    The first member vanas- is probably an old genitive of vana, kept inside the compound (Monier-Williams; compare bṛhas-pati). Nom. pl. of the i-stem pati with guṇa (-ay-aḥ).

  • मे me

    Enclitic me (for mama / mahyam). Sāyaṇa supplies the verb kalpatām from anuvāka 10 to these items; this anuvāka writes its own verb only once, in unit 4 (yajñena kalpantām).

Translation

LiteralStone and for me, clay and for me, hills and for me, mountains and for me, sand and for me, forest trees and for me [may they be ready].

In readable EnglishMay stone be mine, and clay, hills and mountains, sand, and the trees of the forest.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa treats this yajus as clear: «अश्मादयः प्रसिद्धाः», stone and the rest are well known.

Vyākaraṇa

  • अयः ayaḥ

    Nom. sg. of the neuter s-stem ayas; hence ayaś ca.

  • त्रपुः trapuḥ

    Nom. sg. of the neuter s-stem trapus, which Monier-Williams cites for this passage (beside trapu); hence trapuś ca, not trapu ca.

  • श्यामम् śyāmam

    A neuter adjective, ‘the dark’, standing for a metal; Sāyaṇa supplies the noun: black iron.

Translation

LiteralGold and for me, metal and for me, lead and for me, tin and for me, the dark [metal] and for me, copper and for me, fire and for me [may they be ready].

In readable EnglishMay gold be mine, and metal, lead and tin, black iron, copper and bronze, and fire.

Uncertain: ayas is ‘metal, iron’ in the dictionaries. Since Sāyaṇa makes śyāma black iron, ayas here is perhaps metal in general or a particular metal he does not name; he does not gloss it.

Interpretation: loha is ‘red metal, copper’ in the dictionaries; Sāyaṇa widens it to bronze, copper and the like. The readable translation gives his reading.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa glosses two of the metals: śyāma is black iron («श्यामं कृष्णायसम्»), and loha is bronze, copper and the like («लोहं कांस्यताम्रादि»). Gold, ayas, lead and tin are covered by his note on the first unit, «अश्मादयः प्रसिद्धाः»; fire and what follows it he calls well known in the same way («अग्न्यादयः प्रसिद्धाः»).

LiteralWaters and for me, plants and for me, herbs and for me, what ripens in ploughed land and for me, what ripens in unploughed land and for me [may they be ready].

  • ग्राम्याश्च grāmyāśca → ग्राम्याः + च

    visarga sandhi

    visarga before c becomes ś (P. 8.3.34, 8.4.40); so āraṇyāś ca

  • पशव आरण्याश्च paśava āraṇyāśca → पशवः + आरण्याः

    visarga sandhi (y dropped)

    -aḥ / -āḥ before a vowel: the visarga becomes y (P. 8.3.17), which is dropped (P. 8.3.19), leaving the hiatus

  • कल्पन्ताव्ँ kalpantāv̐ → कल्पन्ताम्

    anusvāra sandhi

    final m before v (vittam, the first word of the next unit) becomes the nasal of the next sound, a nasalised v, which the Taittirīya text writes व्ँ (P. 8.4.58–59)

Samāsa & samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this unit; every word is a simple word.

Vyākaraṇa

  • पशवः paśavaḥ

    Stands once, between the two adjectives, and serves both: grāmyāḥ … paśavaḥ and āraṇyāḥ [paśavaḥ]. Nom. pl. of the u-stem paśu with guṇa (-av-aḥ).

  • च ca

    This second ca has no me after it: the text runs on from āraṇyāḥ ca to yajñena kalpantām. It is analysed as it stands.

  • कल्पन्ताम् kalpantām

    Imperative (loṭ) 3rd pl. ātmanepada of √kṛp ‘to be fit, to be in order’ (the r becomes l, P. 8.2.18). The plural agrees with the animals. It is the only verb written in anuvākas 4–6; for every other item Sāyaṇa supplies the singular kalpatām from anuvāka 10.

  • यज्ञेन yajñena

    Instrumental of means or cause: Sāyaṇa reads it as the cause (निमित्तीभूतेन).

Translation

LiteralThe domestic and for me animals, the wild [animals] and: through the sacrifice may they be fit.

In readable EnglishMay the animals of the village and of the forest be mine, made fit through the sacrifice.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa: «ग्राम्यारण्यपशवो यज्ञेन निमित्तीभूतेन कल्पन्तां समर्था भवन्तु» — may the animals of the village and of the forest, with the sacrifice as the cause, be capable.

Contemplative reflection · modern, not a translation

Here, for once, the text names the means as well as the thing wanted: the animals are to be made fit through the sacrifice.

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Vyākaraṇa

  • कर्म karma

    Nom. sg. of the neuter n-stem karman (the n is lost: karma); hence karma ca.

  • एमः emaḥ

    From √i ‘go’. The text has emaḥ (emaś ca), and Sāyaṇa reads it so; the dictionaries list ema / eman ‘course, way’ as neuter.

    A feminine action noun from √i ‘go’, ‘going’ — not the particle iti; hence the visarga (itiś ca).

Translation

LiteralAct and for me, power and for me, aim and for me, course and for me, going and for me, attainment and for me [may they be ready].

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

His gloss on gati is damaged in the witness («गति रेष्ठप्राप्तिः»); it appears to mean the attainment of what is desired.
