नमकम् · Namakam · Anuvāka 9

नवमोऽनुवाकःRudra in every place, and the hearts of the gods

Taittirīya Saṃhitā 4.5.9 · 19 yajus · 83 words

✲

Nineteen yajus, the last of the run (anuvākas 5–9) of salutations with namaḥ on one side. Most names are formed with -ya, ‘being in’, and Sāyaṇa reads them as Rudra present in every kind of place: barren land and highway, stony and habitable ground, cow-pen and house, bed and mansion, thicket and cave, pool and mist, dust, dry and green, bare ground and grass, earth and river, leaves and fallen leaves. Then come the one who threatens and strikes, afflicts a little and greatly, and in the last five yajus the kirikas, ‘the hearts of the gods’. Sāyaṇa closes by saying that each of these yajus names one form, unmoving or moving, to show that the Supreme Lord is the self of all.

Vyākaraṇa

  • वः vaḥ

    Enclitic vaḥ for yuṣmabhyam (dat. pl.; P. 8.1.21), in apposition to kirikebhyaḥ and hṛdayebhyaḥ: ‘homage to you, the kirikas, the hearts of the gods’. As an enclitic it does not open its clause; it follows namaḥ.

  • किरिकेभ्यः kirikebhyaḥ

    Sāyaṇa derives kirika from kṝ ‘to scatter’ (kiranti); Monier-Williams gives kirika ‘sparkling, beaming’. This and the four yajus that follow have one namaḥ and no ca.

  • हृदयेभ्यः hṛdayebhyaḥ

    Dative plural of the neuter hṛdaya ‘heart’, in apposition to kirikebhyaḥ, with devānām ‘of the gods’ depending on it.

Translation

LiteralHomage to you, the scatterers [of wealth], the hearts of the gods.

In readable EnglishHomage to you who scatter gifts, the hearts of the gods.

Interpretation: kirika is ‘sparkling, beaming’ in Monier-Williams (Keith: ‘sparkling hearts of the gods’); Sāyaṇa derives it from kṝ ‘to scatter’ — those who scatter wealth. The translation follows Sāyaṇa; the word table gives both.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa: «किरन्ति भक्तेभ्यो धनानिति किरिका उदारा रुद्रावताराः» — the kirikas are those who scatter wealth to the devotees, generous forms of Rudra; they are the hearts of the gods («हृदयभूताः») because they are dear to all the gods; «तादृशेभ्यो वो युष्मभ्यं नमः» — homage to you who are such.
