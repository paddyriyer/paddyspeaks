चमकम् · Chamakam · Anuvāka 6

षष्ठोऽनुवाकःThe gods, each with Indra

Taittirīya Saṃhitā 4.7.6 · 5 sense-groups for study · 121 words

✲

Twenty deities and regions are named, and after each comes Indra: Agni and Indra, Soma and Indra, through the Maruts, the All-gods, earth, atmosphere and sky, the quarters and the ‘head’, to Prajāpati. Sāyaṇa explains the pairing: Indra has an equal share with all of them, so he is recited with each deity in turn. The quarters are the four directions beginning with the east, and the ‘head’ is the upward direction, named apart as the chief.

Sandhi & sandhi-viccheda

  • अग्निश्च agniśca → अग्निः + च

    visarga sandhi

    visarga before c becomes ś (P. 8.3.34, 8.4.40); so somaś ca, indraś ca throughout

  • म इन्द्रश्च ma indraśca → मे + इन्द्रः + च

    vowel sandhi (ayādi, y dropped)

    final e before a vowel becomes ay (P. 6.1.78), and the y is dropped (P. 8.3.19), leaving the hiatus (ma indraḥ); then visarga before c becomes ś

Samāsa & samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this unit; every word is a simple word.

Vyākaraṇa

  • इन्द्रः indraḥ

    Indra’s name, with its own ca me, follows every deity in the anuvāka: twenty pairs in all.

  • सविता savitā

    Nom. sg. of the agent noun savitṛ (√sū ‘impel’): the strong stem savitār- loses its r (savitā).

  • मे me

    Before indraḥ every me becomes ma (ma indraḥ). The verb is supplied from anuvāka 10, as throughout.

Translation

LiteralAgni and for me, Indra and for me, Soma and for me, Indra and for me, Savitṛ and for me, Indra and for me, Sarasvatī and for me, Indra and for me [may they be ready].

In readable EnglishMay there be for me Agni and Indra, Soma and Indra, Savitṛ and Indra, Sarasvatī and Indra.

Uncertain: Sāyaṇa’s sentence on the pairing reads in the witness «समानभागेपेतत्वादिन्द्र एकैकया देवतया सह पढ्येते»; samāna-bhāga-upeta- and paṭhyete are the evident readings. Only the undamaged words are quoted.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa: «अग्न्यादयः प्रसिद्धा देवताः» — Agni and the rest are well-known deities. Indra is recited «एकैकया देवतया सह», with each deity in turn, because he has an equal share («समानभाग») with all of them.

Vyākaraṇa

  • त्वष्टा tvaṣṭā

    Nom. du. of aśvin, ‘possessed of horses’, the twin gods; the dual is the only grammatical number they take here. No hiatus question arises: ca follows the dual directly.

LiteralTvaṣṭṛ and for me, Indra and for me, Dhātṛ and for me, Indra and for me, Viṣṇu and for me, Indra and for me, the two Aśvins and for me, Indra and for me [may they be ready].

Sandhi & sandhi-viccheda

  • मरुतश्च marutaśca → मरुतः + च

    visarga sandhi

    visarga before c becomes ś (P. 8.3.34, 8.4.40)

  • विश्वे च मे देवा इन्द्रश्च viśve ca me devā indraśca → विश्वे + च + मे + देवाः + इन्द्रः + च

    visarga sandhi (y dropped)

    devāḥ before indraḥ: -aḥ / -āḥ before a vowel: the visarga becomes y (P. 8.3.17), which is dropped (P. 8.3.19), leaving the hiatus

  • मेऽन्तरिक्षञ्च me'ntarikṣañca → मे + अन्तरिक्षम् + च

    vowel sandhi (pūrvarūpa)

    final e absorbs a following a, which the text marks with the avagraha (eṅaḥ padāntād ati, P. 6.1.109); then m before c becomes ñ

Samāsa & samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this unit; every word is a simple word.

Vyākaraṇa

  • विश्वे viśve

    viśve devāḥ, ‘the All-gods’, is the name of a class of deities; the refrain ca me falls inside the name, between viśve and devāḥ, and devāḥ is followed directly by indraḥ ca me. Nom. pl. m. of the pronominal adjective viśva (-e, like sarve).

  • देवाः devāḥ

    Has no ca me of its own: the refrain of the pair stands after viśve.

  • मरुतः marutaḥ

    Nom. pl. of marut (m.), the storm gods, always a troop.

Translation

LiteralThe Maruts and for me, Indra and for me, the All and for me gods, Indra and for me, the earth and for me, Indra and for me, the atmosphere and for me, Indra and for me [may they be ready].

In readable EnglishMay there be for me the Maruts and Indra, the All-gods and Indra, the earth and Indra, the atmosphere and Indra.

Reading: corrections.json, C6.4: as at C6.3, the second witness spells the elided a of अन्तरिक्षम् because of its line division; orthographic, not a reading.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa adds nothing here beyond his note at the head of the anuvāka: the deities are well known («प्रसिद्धा देवताः»), and Indra is named with each.

Vyākaraṇa

  • द्यौः dyauḥ

    Nom. sg. of div / dyo ‘sky’ (dyauḥ), feminine here as often, also masculine.

  • मूर्धा mūrdhā

    Nom. sg. of the n-stem mūrdhan (m.), ‘head’; Sāyaṇa takes it as the zenith, the upward direction.

  • दिशः diśaḥ

    Nom. pl. of diś (f.).

Translation

LiteralThe sky and for me, Indra and for me, the quarters and for me, Indra and for me, the head and for me, Indra and for me, Prajāpati and for me, Indra and for me [may they be ready].

In readable EnglishMay there be for me the sky and Indra, the quarters and Indra, the zenith and Indra, Prajāpati and Indra.

Interpretation: mūrdhan is ‘head, summit’ in the dictionaries; Sāyaṇa takes it as the upward direction (ऊर्ध्वा दिक्), named after the four quarters. The literal translation keeps ‘head’, the readable one follows Sāyaṇa.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa: by the word diś the four quarters beginning with the east are meant («दिक्शब्देन प्राच्याद्याश्चतस्र उच्यन्ते»), and by mūrdhan the upward quarter («मूर्धशब्देन चोर्ध्वा दिक्»). It is named apart from the others because it is the chief: «सा च मुख्यत्वाभिप्रायेण पृथङ् निर्दिष्टा».
