चमकम् · Chamakam · Anuvāka 9

नवमोऽनुवाकःThe fires and rites, the deities and quarters, the Vedas, consecration and the seasons

Taittirīya Saṃhitā 4.7.9 · 3 sense-groups for study · 68 words

✲

The fire being piled, the gharma, the arka, the sun, the breath and the horse sacrifice; earth, Aditi, Diti and the sky; the Śakvarīs, the fingers and the quarters, with a verb of their own, ‘may they be fit through the sacrifice’ (yajñena kalpantām); the ṛc, sāman, stoma and yajus; consecration, austerity, the season and the vow; the rain of day and night; and the two chants Bṛhat and Rathantara, again with their own verb, in the dual (yajñena kalpetām). Sāyaṇa reads the first group as rites (the Pravargya, a cake to Indra, a caru to Sūrya, the oblation ‘to breath’), earth and the rest as deities, and takes the me before yajñena as ‘my’: ‘by my sacrifice’.

Vyākaraṇa

  • मे me

    Enclitic me (for mama / mahyam). For the items of this unit the verb is supplied, as throughout the Chamakam, from anuvāka 10 (kalpatām); this anuvāka also has verbs of its own (units 2 and 3).

  • घर्मः gharmaḥ

    a-stem, m. nom. sg. Monier-Williams gives ‘heat’ and ‘a cauldron, especially the vessel in which the milk-offering to the Aśvins is boiled’; Sāyaṇa names the rite, the Pravargya.

  • अर्कः arkaḥ

    From arc ‘to shine, to praise’: ray, sun, fire, hymn. Sāyaṇa takes it as the name of a rite.

  • अश्वमेधः aśvamedhaḥ

    aśva ‘horse’ + medha ‘sacrifice, sacrificial victim’.

Translation

LiteralThe fire and for me, the gharma and for me, the arka and for me, the sun and for me, the breath and for me, the horse sacrifice and for me [may they be ready].

In readable EnglishMay the fire be mine, and the gharma, the arka, the sun, the breath and the horse sacrifice.

Interpretation: Sāyaṇa takes arka, sūrya and prāṇa as the names of rites (a cake to Indra, a caru to Sūrya, the oblation ‘prāṇāya svāhā’) and gharma as the Pravargya; Keith translates the words as things (‘the cauldron, the beam, the sun, breath’). The word table gives the literal senses and Sāyaṇa’s readings.

Reading: In the bhāṣya witness, not in the mantra: Sāyaṇa’s gloss on gharma reads धर्मः, evidently for घर्मः; his note on sūrya ends सूयः, evidently for सूर्यः; and the injunction he cites for arka reads इन्द्रायार्क वंते पुरोडाशम्, which is defective (apparently a cake to Indra with an epithet in arka-; the exact word cannot be restored from this witness). None of these forms is quoted in the note.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa reads this group as rites. Agni is «चीयमानो वह्निः», the fire that is being piled — the fire built up in the brick altar. The gharma is «प्रवर्ग्यः», the Pravargya. The arka is the rite enjoined with a cake to Indra («विहितो यागोऽर्कः»); the sun, the rite enjoined by the words «सौर्यं चरुमिति», ‘a pot-offering for Sūrya’; the breath, the oblation enjoined by «प्राणाय स्वाहेति», ‘to breath, svāhā’. The horse sacrifice he calls well known: «अश्वमेधः प्रसिद्धः».

Vyākaraṇa

  • शक्वरीः śakvarīḥ

    Vedic nom. pl. of the ī-stem śakvarī, in -īḥ, where the classical language has śakvaryaḥ — the form Sāyaṇa gives as his gloss (शक्वर्यः). Monier-Williams: f. pl. the name of particular verses (especially the Mahānāmnī verses of the Śākvara sāman); a metre of seven times eight syllables; also, in the plural, water.

  • दिशः diśaḥ

    The three plurals śakvarīḥ, aṅgulayaḥ, diśaḥ share one ca me, and are followed by a verb of their own; Sāyaṇa takes ca as bringing in the intermediate quarters (चशब्दाद्विदिशोऽपि).

  • मे me

    The me after diśaḥ ca Sāyaṇa reads with yajñena as ‘my’ (मदीयेन): ‘by my sacrifice’. The other me of the unit carry the supplied kalpatām, as elsewhere in the Chamakam.

  • कल्पन्ताम् kalpantām

    Imperative 3rd pl. ātmanepada of kḷp (kalpate) ‘to be fit, to be in order, to succeed’ — the plural of the kalpatām that is supplied to the other items from anuvāka 10. Here it is in the text.

  • द्यौः dyauḥ

    Nom. sg. of the stem dyu / div ‘sky’, masculine or feminine in the Veda. Sāyaṇa counts it among the deities with earth, Aditi and Diti.

  • ऋक् ṛk

    c-stem ṛc, nom. sg. ṛk; before ca the k is unchanged.

  • साम sāma

    n-stem sāman, n. nom. sg. sāma (the final n is lost in the nominative).

  • यजुः yajuḥ

    s-stem yajus, n. nom. sg. yajuḥ.

Translation

LiteralEarth and for me, Aditi and for me, Diti and for me, the sky and for me [may they be ready]; the Śakvarīs, the fingers, the quarters too — by my sacrifice may they be fit; the ṛc and for me, the sāman and for me, the stoma and for me, the yajus and for me [may they be ready].

In readable EnglishMay earth be mine, and Aditi, Diti and the sky; may the Śakvarīs, the fingers and the quarters be made fit by my sacrifice; may the ṛc be mine, and the sāman, the stoma and the yajus.

Interpretation: The reach of kalpantām. Grammatically it is plural, and the three plurals before it (śakvarīḥ, aṅgulayaḥ, diśaḥ) share one ca me, so it governs at least these; the translation gives the earlier items the supplied kalpatām, as elsewhere. Sāyaṇa’s «ताः सर्वाः» follows directly on his note that ca adds the intermediate quarters, so it may mean all the quarters; but it is feminine, and earth, Aditi and Diti are feminine too (and dyauḥ can be), so it could reach back to them. Keith makes the verb govern everything from the fire to the quarters.

Uncertain: śakvarīḥ: Sāyaṇa restates the word in its classical plural and does not say what the Śakvarīs are. The dictionaries give the Śakvarī verses (the Mahānāmnīs), the śakvarī metre, and, in the plural, water; Keith has ‘the Śakvari verses’. The translation keeps the name.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa: «पृथिव्यादयो देवताविशेषाः» — earth and the others are particular deities. He glosses śakvarīḥ only by its ordinary plural, «शक्वरीः शक्वर्यः», and takes the fingers figuratively: «अङ्गुलयोऽङ्गुलिवद्विराट्पुरुषस्यावयवविशेषाः» — particular limbs of the Virāṭ-puruṣa, like fingers. The quarters are «प्राच्याद्याः», the east and the rest, and the word ca brings in the intermediate quarters; then «ताः सर्वा मे मदीयेन यज्ञेन कल्पन्तां स्वस्वव्यापारसमर्था भवन्तु» — may they all, by my sacrifice, be fit, each able for its own work. The ṛc and the rest are kinds of mantra («ऋगादयो मन्त्रविशेषाः»), and the stoma is «सामावृत्तिरूपं स्तोत्रम्», a chant made of the repetition of a sāman.

Sandhi & sandhi-viccheda

  • तपश्च tapaśca → तपः + च

    visarga sandhi

    visarga before c becomes ś (P. 8.3.34, 8.4.40); so throughout: -aś ca, -iś ca, -uś ca, -āś ca

  • म ऋतुश्च ma ṛtuśca → मे + ऋतुः

    vowel sandhi (ayādi, y dropped)

    final e before a vowel other than a becomes ay (P. 6.1.78), and the y is dropped (P. 8.3.19), leaving the hiatus a ṛ

  • व्रतञ्च vratañca → व्रतम् + च

    anusvāra sandhi

    final m before a consonant becomes anusvāra, written as the nasal of the next consonant’s class (ñ before c; P. 8.3.23, 8.4.59)

  • मेऽहोरात्रयोर्वृष्ट्या me'horātrayorvṛṣṭyā → मे + अहोरात्रयोः + वृष्ट्या

    vowel sandhi (pūrvarūpa) and visarga sandhi

    final e before short a: the a is absorbed into the e, shown by the avagraha (eṅaḥ padāntād ati, P. 6.1.109); and -oḥ before a voiced consonant becomes -or (the visarga is r before voiced sounds)

Vyākaraṇa

  • अहोरात्रयोः ahorātrayoḥ

    The dvandva ahan + rātri takes the stem ahorātra; -ayoḥ is gen. or loc. du. Sāyaṇa’s संबन्धिनी, ‘belonging to’, shows that he takes it as genitive, with vṛṣṭyā.

  • वृष्ट्या vṛṣṭyā

    i-stem vṛṣṭi, f. instr. sg. There is no verb for it in the text; Sāyaṇa supplies one with its subject (see the traditional note).

  • बृहद्रथन्तरे bṛhadrathantare

    n. nom. du. of the dvandva bṛhat + rathantara; inside the compound the t of bṛhat is voiced to d before r. A dual in -e is pragṛhya and would stand in hiatus before a vowel; here it stands before ca, so no junction arises.

  • कल्पेताम् kalpetām

    Imperative 3rd du. ātmanepada of kḷp, agreeing with the dual bṛhadrathantare.

  • व्रतम् vratam

    Sāyaṇa’s «व्रतमेकस्तनादि» names the vow by its first instance, ‘one teat’; this is taken to be the measured milk-diet of the consecrated sacrificer (see the flags).

  • मे me

    The me after bṛhadrathantare ca Sāyaṇa reads with yajñena as ‘my’ (मदीयेन). The anuvāka ends with kalpetām (॥ [16]).

Translation

LiteralConsecration and for me, austerity and for me, the season and for me, the vow and for me [may they be ready]; by the rain of day and night [may my crop be ready]; the Bṛhat and Rathantara too — by my sacrifice may the two be fit.

In readable EnglishMay consecration be mine, and austerity, the season and the vow; by the rain of day and night [may my crop be ready]; and may the Bṛhat and the Rathantara be made fit by my sacrifice.

Interpretation: ahorātrayor vṛṣṭyā has no verb of its own. Sāyaṇa supplies one with a subject not in the text, ‘may my crop be ready’ (मदीयं सस्यं कल्पताम्, marked इति शेषः); the translation follows him and brackets the supplement. Keith instead joins the phrase to the items before it: ‘may the Ṛc … the vow (prosper) through the rain of day and night’.

Uncertain: vrata: Sāyaṇa’s «एकस्तनादि» is very brief; reading it as the regulated milk-diet of the consecrated sacrificer (milk from one teat, then more) is our explanation, not his words.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa: «दीक्षा यजमानसंस्कारः» — consecration is the sanctifying of the sacrificer; «तपः पापक्षयार्थमनशनादि» — austerity is fasting and the like, for the wearing away of sin; «ऋतुर्यज्ञाङ्गभूतः कालः» — the season is time as a part of the sacrifice; «व्रतमेकस्तनादि» — the vow is the [observance] beginning with ‘one teat’. For the rain he supplies the rest of the sentence: «अहोरात्रयोः संबन्धिनी या वृष्टिस्तया मदीयं सस्यं कल्पतामिति शेषः» — ‘by the rain that belongs to day and night may my crop be ready’ is to be understood. The last pair are the two chants, «बृहद्रथंतरे सामनी», and «ते च मदीयेन यज्ञेन कल्पेतां स्वस्वव्यवहारसमर्थे भवेताम्» — may they, by my sacrifice, be fit, each able for its own use.
