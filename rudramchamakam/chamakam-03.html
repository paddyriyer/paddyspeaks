चमकम् · Chamakam · Anuvāka 3

तृतीयोऽनुवाकःHappiness here and hereafter; health, long life and a fair day

Taittirīya Saṃhitā 4.7.3 · 6 sense-groups for study · 108 words

✲

The third anuvāka asks for happiness and what makes it: delight and what is dear, good fortune, fame and wealth; a guide and a supporter, security and steadiness; knowledge, the plough and freedom from what hinders it; the rite and its undying fruit, freedom from disease, long life, freedom from enemies and from fear; and at the last good going, rest, a fair dawn and a fair day. Sāyaṇa reads several pairs as the good of this world and of the next (śam and mayas, bhadra and śreyas), and supplies kalpatām, ‘may it be ready’, to each item.

Samāsa & samāsa-vigraha

Vyākaraṇa

  • शम् śam

    śam is an indeclinable, ‘happily, well’, used in the Veda as a noun, ‘welfare’ (MW: often to be translated by a substantive, as in śaṃ yoḥ). Its m becomes ñ before ca.

  • अनुकामः anukāmaḥ

    anu-kāma, m. ‘desire’ (MW, citing VS); as an adjective ‘according to one’s desire, agreeable’ (RV), the sense Sāyaṇa’s अनुकूलत्व- takes up.

  • सौमनसः saumanasaḥ

    saumanasa, a derivative (taddhita, with vṛddhi) of sumanas ‘well-minded, cheerful’; MW: m. ‘cheerfulness, enjoyment, comfort’ (VS, AV). The text shows it masculine (-aḥ).

  • मयः mayaḥ

    mayas, n. s-stem, ‘refreshment, delight’; nom. sg. mayaḥ. Here it is paired with śam.

Translation

LiteralWelfare and for me, delight and for me, what is dear and for me, what is agreeable to desire and for me, desire and for me, ease of mind and for me [may they be ready].

In readable EnglishMay welfare be mine, and delight; what is dear and what is wished for; the object of desire, and a mind at ease.

Interpretation: saumanasa: the dictionaries give ‘cheerfulness, ease of mind’; Sāyaṇa makes it the kinsfolk who cause that ease (मनःस्वास्थ्य करो बन्धुवर्गः). The word meaning gives the first, the context Sāyaṇa’s.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa divides the goods between this world and the next: «शंशब्द ऐहिकसुखवाची», śam names happiness here, and mayas happiness in the world beyond. Priya is «प्रीतिकारणं वस्तु», a thing that gives pleasure, and anukāma a thing desired because it is agreeable; both belong to this world, differing in degree («एतदुभयमैहिकामेव तारतम्योपेतम्»). Kāma is the desired thing of the next world, heaven and the like («आमुष्मिकः स्वर्गादिः»). Saumanasa is the circle of kin who give ease of mind: «सौमनसो मनःस्वास्थ्य करो बन्धुवर्गः».

Vyākaraṇa

  • श्रेयः śreyaḥ

    śreyas, comparative ‘better’, as a neuter noun ‘the better, welfare’; nom. sg. śreyaḥ.

  • वस्यः vasyaḥ

    vasyas, the Vedic comparative of vasu ‘good’ (= vasīyas, MW), as a neuter noun ‘increasing wealth or prosperity, welfare’ (RV, AV).

Translation

LiteralGood fortune and for me, the better and for me, welfare and for me, fame and for me, fortune and for me, wealth and for me [may they be ready].

In readable EnglishMay good fortune be mine, and what is better still; may welfare be mine, and fame, and fortune, and wealth.

Interpretation: vasyas: Monier-Williams ‘better; n. welfare’, a comparative beside śreyas; Sāyaṇa connects it with dwelling (निवासहेतुः), ‘a house and the like’.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa again pairs this world and the next: bhadra is «कल्याणमिह लोके रमणीयम्», the good that is pleasing in this world, and śreyas what is good for the world beyond (परलोकहितम्). Vasyas he takes from ‘dwelling’: «वस्यो निवासहेतुर्गृहादिः», a house and the like. Yaśas is renown (कीर्तिः), bhaga good fortune (सौभाग्यम्), draviṇa wealth (धनम्).

Vyākaraṇa

  • यन्ता yantā

    yantṛ and dhartṛ are agent nouns in -tṛ from √yam ‘to hold, restrain’ and √dhṛ ‘to hold, support’; the nom. sg. ends in -tā.

  • महः mahaḥ

    mahas, n. s-stem, ‘greatness, might’ and ‘joy, festivity’ (MW); nom. sg. mahaḥ.

Translation

LiteralA guide and for me, a supporter and for me, security and for me, steadiness and for me, all and for me, greatness and for me [may they be ready].

In readable EnglishMay I have one to guide me and one to sustain me; may security be mine, and steadiness; may all be mine, and honour.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa makes the first two persons: the yantṛ is «नियामक आचार्यदिः», one who disciplines, a teacher and the like, and the dhartṛ «पोषकः पित्रादिः», one who nourishes, a father and the like. Kṣema is «विद्यमानधनस्य रक्षणशक्तिः», the power to keep the wealth one has; dhṛti firmness that does not waver even in misfortune («धैर्यमापद्यपि निश्चलत्वम्»). Viśva he reads as the goodwill of all people («सर्वजनानुकूल्यम्»), and mahas as honour (पूजा).

    saṃvid, f. root noun from sam-√vid ‘to know together’: ‘knowledge, understanding’ (MW). Final d becomes t in pausa and c before ca. The text writes the nasal of sam as the Taittirīya v̐ before v; the padaccheda uses anusvāra.

    sū and pra-sū are root nouns (f. nom. sg. -ūḥ). Two roots sū meet here: √sū ‘to impel’ (suvati), which Sāyaṇa follows (प्रेरण), and √sū ‘to beget’ (sūte), which the dictionaries give for sū and prasū.

Translation

LiteralKnowledge and for me, the faculty of knowing and for me, impelling and for me, impulse and for me, the plough and for me, rest and for me [may they be ready].

In readable EnglishMay knowledge be mine, and the power to know; the power to set others to work, and to direct them; the plough, and freedom from what hinders it.

Interpretation: sū, prasū: Sāyaṇa reads them as powers of directing others (from √sū ‘to impel’); Monier-Williams gives ‘begetting, bringing forth’ (√sū ‘to beget’), as Keith’s ‘begetting, procreation’. laya: Sāyaṇa ‘the removal of what hinders farming’; MW ‘clinging; lying down; rest; dissolution’. The word meanings give the dictionary senses, the contexts Sāyaṇa’s.

Reading: The printed bhāṣya’s lemma for jñātra reads शात्रं; the Saṃhitā has ज्ञात्रम्, and his gloss ज्ञापयितृत्व- shows the same word is meant.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa: saṃvid is knowledge of the Veda, the śāstras and the like («वेदशास्त्रादिविज्ञानम्»), and jñātra the capacity to make known («ज्ञापयितृत्वसामर्थ्यम्»). He takes sū and prasū from impelling: «सूः पुत्रादिप्रेरणसामर्थ्यम्», the capacity to direct sons and others, and «प्रसूर्भृत्यादिप्रेरणसामर्थ्यम्», the capacity to direct servants and others. Sīra is the means of farming, the plough and the rest, and laya «तत्प्रतिबन्धनिवृत्तिः», the removal of what hinders it.

ऋतं यज्ञादिकर्म ।

अमृतं तत्फलम् ।

Bhāṣya

अयक्ष्मं राजयक्ष्मादिप्रबलव्याधिराहित्यम् ।

अनामयज् ज्वराद्य्-अल्प-व्याधि-राहित्यम् ।

Bhāṣya

जीवातुर्जीवनकारणं व्याधिपरिहारार्थमौषधम् ।

दीर्घायुत्वमपमृत्सुराहित्यम् ।

Bhāṣya

अनमित्रं वैरिराहित्यम् ।

अभयं भयराहित्यम् ।

Bhāṣya

सुगं शोभनगमनं सर्वैरङ्गीकृताचरणमित्यर्थः ।

शयनं शय्योपधानदिसंपत्तिः ।

Bhāṣya

सूषा स्नानसंध्यावन्दनादियुक्तः शोभनः प्रातःकालः ।

सुदिनं यज्ञदानाध्ययनादियुक्तं कृत्स्नं दिनम् ॥

इति श्रीमत्सायणाचार्यविरचिते माधवीये वेदार्थप्रकाशे कृष्णयजुर्वेदीयतैत्तिरीयसंहिताभाष्ये चतुर्थकाण्डे सप्तमप्रपाठके तृतीयोऽनुवाकः ॥

३ ॥
