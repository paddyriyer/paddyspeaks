चमकम् · Chamakam · Anuvāka 8

अष्टमोऽनुवाकःThe implements and places of the sacrifice

Taittirīya Saṃhitā 4.7.8 · 4 sense-groups for study · 66 words

✲

Twenty-two items, each followed by ca me: the materials, vessels and places of the Soma sacrifice — kindling-wood, strewn grass, the altar and the side-hearths, the ladles and the Soma cups; the pressing-stones, the strips of the post, the sounding-holes, the pressing-boards and the Soma vessels; the vessels for the strained and the stirred Soma, the Agnīdh’s place, the shed of the Soma carts, the halls and the sadas; the cakes, the cooking-fires, the closing bath and the svagā-call. Sāyaṇa calls them parts of the sacrifice well known from the sacrificial sections and glosses only three: the halls (the wife’s hall and the like), the pacatāḥ (the śāmitra and other cooking-fires) and the svagā-call (the śaṃyuvāka). The verb kalpatām is supplied from anuvāka 10.

Samāsa & samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this unit; every word is a simple word.

Vyākaraṇa

  • मे me

    Enclitic me (for mama / mahyam). The verb is supplied from anuvāka 10 (kalpatām), as for every item of the Chamakam: ‘may [it] be ready for me’.

  • बर्हिः barhiḥ

    s-stem barhis, n. nom. sg.: the grass ‘plucked up’ and strewn over the vedi as a seat for the gods and a surface for the offerings (Monier-Williams).

  • धिष्णियाः dhiṣṇiyāḥ

    Monier-Williams gives dhiṣṇiya as another form of dhiṣṇya, the subordinate altars or hearths — heaps of earth on which fire is placed — in the Āgnīdhra and the Sadas, belonging to particular priests.

  • स्रुचः srucaḥ

    c-stem sruc, f. nom. pl. Monier-Williams names three: juhū, upabhṛt and dhruvā.

Translation

LiteralKindling-wood and for me, the strewn grass and for me, the altar and for me, the side-hearths and for me, the ladles and for me, the [Soma] cups and for me [may they be ready].

In readable EnglishMay the kindling-wood be mine, and the strewn grass, the altar, the side-hearths, the ladles and the Soma cups.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa introduces the anuvāka with «इध्मश्च म इति» and covers its whole list in one sentence: «इध्मादीनि यज्ञाङ्गद्रव्याणि यज्ञप्रकरणे प्रसिद्धानि» — kindling-wood and the rest are materials that are parts of the sacrifice, well known from the sections on the sacrifice. He glosses only gṛhāḥ, pacatāḥ and svagākāra (units 3 and 4).

    karmadhāraya

    Members

Vyākaraṇa

  • ग्रावाणः grāvāṇaḥ

    an-stem grāvan, m. nom. pl.: the stones with which the Soma shoots are pressed.

  • स्वरवः svaravaḥ

    u-stem svaru, m. nom. pl. (guṇa of the stem vowel before -aḥ). Monier-Williams: a stake, especially the sacrificial post or a strip of wood cut from it.

  • उपरवाः uparavāḥ

    upa + rava (from ru ‘to resound’). Monier-Williams: the holes over which the Soma is ground, so called because they increase the sound of the stones.

  • अधिषवणे adhiṣavaṇe

    n. nom. du. of adhiṣavaṇa (adhi + savana, from su ‘to press’): the pair of boards on which the Soma is pressed; the word is generally used in the dual. A dual in -e is pragṛhya and would stand in hiatus before a vowel; here it stands before ca, so no junction arises.

  • वायव्यानि vāyavyāni

    From vāyu with the suffix -ya: ‘belonging to Vāyu’. Monier-Williams: (with pātra understood) Soma vessels shaped like mortars. Neuter plural, agreeing with the vessels understood.

Translation

LiteralThe pressing-stones and for me, the stakes and for me, the sounding-holes and for me, the two pressing-boards and for me, the wooden Soma vessel and for me, the vāyavya [vessels] and for me [may they be ready].

In readable EnglishMay the pressing-stones be mine, and the strips of the post, the sounding-holes, the two pressing-boards, the great wooden Soma vessel and the vessels for Vāyu.

Uncertain: droṇakalaśa: Monier-Williams records the compound as ‘a large wooden vessel for the Soma’, and droṇa alone as ‘a wooden vessel, trough’; the analysis as a karmadhāraya (‘a jar that is a wooden trough’) is ours, and the relation of the members could also be read otherwise. The meaning is not in doubt.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa treats this yajus as clear and adds nothing beyond his opening statement that these are materials of the sacrifice known from the sacrificial sections: «यज्ञाङ्गद्रव्याणि यज्ञप्रकरणे प्रसिद्धानि».

Vyākaraṇa

  • पूतभृत् pūtabhṛt

    pūta ‘purified, strained’ + bhṛt, the root bhṛ ‘to bear, hold’ as a final member with no visible suffix. Monier-Williams: the vessel that receives the Soma juice after it has been strained.

  • आधवनीयः ādhavanīyaḥ

    ā + dhū ‘to shake, stir’, with -anīya: ‘that in which [the Soma] is stirred’. Monier-Williams: a vessel in which the Soma plant is stirred and cleansed.

  • आग्नीध्रम् āgnīdhram

    A vṛddhi derivative of agnīdh, the priest who kindles the fire: ‘belonging to the Agnīdh’. Neuter, taken here (with Keith, ‘the Agnidh’s altar’) as his place in the sacrificial ground; see the flags.

  • हविर्धानम् havirdhānam

    havis ‘oblation’ + dhāna ‘receptacle’; the s of havis becomes r before the voiced dh inside the compound. Monier-Williams: the vehicle in which the Soma plants are carried to be pressed (generally dual), and the shed for those vehicles.

  • गृहाः gṛhāḥ

    m. pl.: in the Veda the plural is used of a house as having several rooms (Monier-Williams). Sāyaṇa gives the sacrificial sense.

  • सदः sadaḥ

    s-stem sadas, n. nom. sg. sadaḥ. Monier-Williams: a shed erected in the sacrificial enclosure, east of the Prācīnavaṃśa.

Translation

LiteralThe pūtabhṛt and for me, the ādhavanīya and for me, the Āgnīdhra and for me, the havirdhāna and for me, the halls and for me, the sadas and for me [may they be ready].

In readable EnglishMay the vessel for the strained Soma be mine, and the vessel in which it is stirred, the Agnīdh’s place, the shed of the Soma carts, the halls and the assembly shed.

Uncertain: āgnīdhram (n.): Monier-Williams records āgnīdhra as an adjective ‘belonging to the Agnīdh’ and as the name of the priest; the neuter here is taken, with Keith (‘the Agnidh’s altar’), as the Agnīdh’s place. Sāyaṇa does not gloss it.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa glosses only gṛhāḥ: «गृहाः पत्नीशालादयः» — the ‘houses’ are the hall of the sacrificer’s wife and the like. The other vessels and places of this group fall under his opening statement, «यज्ञाङ्गद्रव्याणि यज्ञप्रकरणे प्रसिद्धानि».

Vyākaraṇa

  • पुरोडाशाः puroḍāśāḥ

    m. nom. pl. of puroḍāśa. Monier-Williams: a sacrificial cake of ground rice.

  • पचताः pacatāḥ

    pacata, a participial adjective of pac ‘to cook’: ‘cooked’; as a noun Monier-Williams records ‘fire’ (lexicographers) and ‘cooked food’. Sāyaṇa takes the plural as the cooking-fires such as the śāmitra. On the second witness’s पचन्ताः see the flags.

  • अवभृथः avabhṛthaḥ

    ava + bhṛtha (from bhṛ ‘to carry’): ‘carrying off’. Monier-Williams: the purification or ablution of the sacrificer and the sacrificial vessels after a sacrifice.

  • स्वगाकारः svagākāraḥ

    svagā, a ritual exclamation, + kāra ‘making, utterance’, as in svāhākāra and vaṣaṭkāra. Monier-Williams: the exclamation svagā.

  • मे me

    The anuvāka ends here (॥ [15]); the list runs on into the next.

Translation

LiteralThe sacrificial cakes and for me, the cooking-fires and for me, the closing bath and for me, the svagā-call and for me [may they be ready].

In readable EnglishMay the sacrificial cakes be mine, and the cooking-fires, the closing bath and the call svagā.

Reading: corrections.json (C8.4): the second witness reads पचन्ताः for प॒च॒ताश्च॑; classified as a W2 error. Sāyaṇa reads pacatāḥ: «पचताः शामित्रादयः».

Interpretation: pacatāḥ: as an adjective pacata is ‘cooked’ (Monier-Williams), and Keith translates ‘the cooked (offerings)’; Sāyaṇa takes the word as the cooking-fires, the śāmitra and the like. The translation follows Sāyaṇa; the word table gives both.

Uncertain: Sāyaṇa’s reason for calling the svagā-call the śaṃyuvāka reads in the witness «तेन हि यथास्यं देवतानां हविर्नमनं क्रियते»; यथास्यं (for यथास्वम्, ‘each to its own’?) and हविर्नमनं are not secure, so the sentence is reported but not translated in full. The witness also prefixes a stray vowel sign to स्वगाकारः.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa: «पचताः शामित्रादयः» — the pacatāḥ are the śāmitra and the like, that is, the fire on which the flesh of the victim is cooked and the other cooking-fires. And «स्वगाकारः शंयुवाकः» — the svagā-call is the śaṃyuvāka. He adds a reason, «तेन हि …», ‘for by it …’, which concerns the oblation and the deities but whose wording in this witness is not secure (see the flags). The cakes and the closing bath he leaves as known.
