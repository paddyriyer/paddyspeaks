नमकम् · Namakam · Anuvāka 4

चतुर्थोऽनुवाकःHomage on both sides: bands, crafts and hunters

Taittirīya Saṃhitā 4.5.4 · 17 yajus · 102 words

✲

Seventeen yajus, each with homage on both sides (namaḥ … ca vo namaḥ), completing the series begun in the third anuvāka. Pairs of beings are saluted and then addressed as ‘you’: women who wound and who crush, troops and their lords, the misshapen and the many-formed, great and small, those with and without chariots, then armies, charioteers and the crafts — carpenters, chariot-makers, potters, smiths — and the hunters, fowlers, fishermen, arrow- and bow-makers, dog-leaders and dogs. Sāyaṇa sums up the anuvāka as praise of Rudra chiefly in the forms of beings of many kinds, after the divine forms of the second and the thieves of the third.

Vyākaraṇa

  • नमः namaḥ

    A yajus with homage on both sides (ubhayato-namaskāra): namaḥ opens it and ca vo namaḥ closes it. The enclitic vaḥ (for yuṣmabhyam, P. 8.1.21) is a dative plural, ‘to you’, and takes up the two datives before it: ‘homage to A and to B — to you homage’.

  • आव्याधिनीभ्यः āvyādhinībhyaḥ

    ā + vyādhin ‘piercing’ (from √vyadh ‘to pierce’ with the agent suffix -in), feminine in -ī. A prefixed derivative, not a compound. Dative with namaḥ.

  • विविध्यन्तीभ्यः vividhyantībhyaḥ

    vi + vidhyat-, present participle of √vyadh, whose present stem is vidhya- with samprasāraṇa (P. 6.1.16); feminine -antī (the 4th-class stem keeps the n). The preverb stays on the participle.

  • वः vaḥ

    Enclitic: it never opens a clause. Here dative plural, governed by the closing namaḥ.

Translation

LiteralHomage to [the women] who wound from every side, and to [the women] who pierce in many ways — to you homage.

In readable EnglishHomage to you who wound from every side and to you who pierce in many ways; to you, homage.

Uncertain: Sāyaṇa’s gloss on the women’s forms is printed as स्रीमूर्तय, evidently for strīmūrtayaḥ ‘female forms’, and the participle as विवध्यन्त्यः for the mantra’s vividhyantyaḥ; the reading of the mantra is not in doubt.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa begins by placing the anuvāka: the third anuvāka gave yajus with homage on both sides, and the fourth gives others of the same kind («कानिचिदुभयतोनमस्काराणि यजूंषि»). He takes the āvyādhinīs as female forms able to pierce all round («आ समन्ताद्वेद्धुं शक्ताः») and the vividhyantīs as those able to pierce in a special way («विशेषेण वेद्धुं शक्ता»), and ends each gloss with the homage to them («ताभ्यो नमः»).

Contemplative reflection · modern, not a translation

The yajus salutes the one who wounds before it names anything else. The address turns at the end to ‘you’: what is feared is spoken to, not only spoken of.

    The Taittirīya text, and Sāyaṇa’s gloss, have -ṣṭa. Monier-Williams enters the word as puñji-ṣṭha (from the Vājasaneyi recension), ‘a fisherman or a bird-catcher’, a compound with -stha. Because the form here differs, no samāsa analysis is given.

  • निषादेभ्यः niṣādebhyaḥ

    ni-ṣāda, from ni + √sad; the name of a non-Āryan people described as hunters and fishermen (Monier-Williams). A prefixed derivative, not a compound.

LiteralHomage to the fowlers, and to the Niṣādas — to you homage.

In readable EnglishHomage to you, the bird-catchers, and to you, the Niṣāda fishermen; to you, homage.

Uncertain: Sāyaṇa’s gloss is printed धातकाः; the sense ‘killers’ (ghātaka) is inferred from the context and from his gloss on niṣāda in the next sentence.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa treats the ninth to the seventeenth yajus as clear («स्पष्टार्थानि») but glosses two words here: the puñjiṣṭas are killers of flocks of birds («पक्षिपुञ्जानां धातकाः», where धातकाः evidently stands for ghātakāḥ, ‘killers’), and the Niṣādas are killers of fish («मत्स्यघातिनो निषादाः»).
