नमकम् · Namakam · Anuvāka 8

अष्टमोऽनुवाकःSoma and Rudra, the makers of weal, and the lord of the fords

Taittirīya Saṃhitā 4.5.8 · 17 yajus · 80 words

✲

Seventeen yajus, each a namaḥ with one or two names in the dative. Sāyaṇa introduces them as further yajus with namaḥ on one side, continuing anuvāka 7. They name Rudra as Soma and Rudra, the red sun, the bringer of weal and lord of creatures, the fierce slayer near and far, the green-haired trees and the deliverer; then as the source and maker of happiness (śambhu, mayobhu, śaṅkara, mayaskara), the auspicious and more auspicious; and last as present at fords and banks, at the far and near shore, at the crossing over and out, and in the river's grass, foam, sand and stream, which Sāyaṇa reads as the crossing of saṃsāra and as Rudra in the form of the faithful who live by the rivers.

Sandhi & sandhi-viccheda

  • नमस्सोमाय namassomāya → नमः + सोमाय

    visarga sandhi

    visarga before s becomes s, the doubled sibilant the Taittirīya text writes (P. 8.3.36 allows visarga or the sibilant)

Samāsa & samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this unit; every word is a simple word.

Vyākaraṇa

  • सोमाय somāya

    Dative with namaḥ (‘homage to …’), as is every name in this anuvāka. Sāyaṇa does not take soma here as the soma plant or its god: he analyses it as sa + umā, ‘he who is together with Umā’ (a vigraha of the saha-bahuvrīhi kind). The word itself is the ordinary soma, and the text gives no compound to divide.

  • च ca

    The pattern of the anuvāka is namaḥ X ca Y ca: one namaḥ and two names, each followed by ca, ‘both to X and to Y’. Sāyaṇa counts each such line as one yajus.

Translation

LiteralHomage both to Soma and to Rudra.

In readable EnglishHomage to Soma and to Rudra.

Interpretation: soma is the name Soma in the dictionaries (Keith: ‘Homage to Soma’); Sāyaṇa reads it as sa + umā, ‘with Umā’. The translation keeps the name; the word table gives his reading.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa introduces the anuvāka as further yajus with namaḥ on one side only, continuing those of anuvāka 7 (कानिचिदन्यतरतोनमस्काराण्यष्टमेऽनुवाके कथ्यन्ते), and counts seventeen of them (सप्तदश यजूंष्याह). He explains soma as ‘he who is together with Umā’, and rudra by derivation: rud is weeping, its cause is sorrow, and Rudra is the one who drives that sorrow away.

Translation

LiteralHomage both to [him] whose striking is in front and to [him] whose striking is far off.

Samāsa & samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this unit; every word is a simple word.

Vyākaraṇa

  • हन्त्रे hantre

    Agent noun in -tṛ from the root han ‘to strike, slay’; dat. sg. hantre.

  • हनीयसे hanīyase

    Comparative in -īyas formed directly from the root han (as garīyas, jyāyas): ‘slaying more, the greater slayer’; dat. sg. hanīyase.

Translation

LiteralHomage both to the slayer and to the greater slayer.

In readable EnglishHomage to the slayer, and to the one who slays still more.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa: in the world too, whoever slays an opponent anywhere, it is Rudra in that form who is the slayer (hantṛ). For this he cites the Lord's words to Arjuna: by me they have already been slain; be only the occasion, Savyasācin. He is hanīyas, the greater slayer, because at the time of dissolution he slays all, in the highest degree.

  • ताराय tārāya

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa: tāra is the one made known by the praṇava, the syllable om.

Reading: corrections.json (N8.15): the second witness reads आलाट्याय; the text keeps -lādya (W2 error). The note there cites Sāyaṇa's alāda, ‘one who eats the fruit of action’.

Uncertain: The padaccheda is अलाद्याय, after Sāyaṇa's derivation from alāda; the Saṃhitā चालाद्याय would equally be ca + ālādya. Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa: even when the knowledge of reality that carries one out of saṃsāra is at hand, a man may set it aside and, by performing rites for desired ends, come back again into saṃsāra; that return is ātāra, and the one who merits it is ātārya — that is, the giver of the fruits of desire-prompted rites. alāda is the jīva, which eats the fruit of action to the full, as the śruti he quotes says: ‘one of the two eats the sweet fig’. Rudra, being its impeller and so connected with it, is alādya.

Vyākaraṇa

  • शष्प्याय śaṣpyāya

    śaṣpa ‘young, sprouting grass’ + -ya; phenya from phena ‘foam’. Sāyaṇa reads the -ya of these four names as ‘who is worthy of’ (arhati) the thing named.

Translation

LiteralHomage both to the [one] in the young grass and to the [one] in the foam.

In readable EnglishHomage to him in the young grass, and to him in the foam.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa: śaṣpa is young grass, the shoots of kuśa and the like that grow on the banks of the Gaṅgā and other rivers, and śaṣpya is the one worthy of it (tad arhati, he says, for all four names); phenya, the one worthy of the foam in the middle of the river.

    sikatā ‘sand’ + -ya (the final ā dropped before the suffix); pravāhya from pravāha ‘current, stream’. The anuvāka ends here (॥) at the pañcāśat mark [18].

Translation

LiteralHomage both to the [one] in the sand and to the [one] in the current.

In readable EnglishHomage to him in the sand, and to him in the flowing stream.

Interpretation

Traditional interpretation · after Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa: sikatya is the one worthy of the sand, pravāhya the one worthy of the current. The purport of the four names śaṣpya, phenya, sikatya and pravāhya, he says, is Rudra in the form of the person of faith, intent on bathing and the like, who lives always on the banks of the Gaṅgā and other rivers.

Sāyaṇa’s bhāṣya on this anuvāka (Sanskrit, in full)

Bhāṣya

(अथ चतुर्थकाण्डे पञ्चमप्रपाठकेऽष्टमोऽनुवाकः)।

सप्तमोऽनुवाके यान्यन्यतरतोनमस्काराणि यजूंष्युक्तानि तेभ्योऽन्यानि कानिचिदन्यतरतोनमस्काराण्यष्टमेऽनुवाके कथ्यन्ते।

तत्र विद्यमानानि सप्तदश यजूंष्याह—

Bhāṣya

नमः सोमाय चेति। उमया सह वर्तत इति सोमः। रुद्रोद्रनहेतुर्दुःखं तद्‍ब्रावयति विनाशयतीति रुद्रः। आदित्यरूपेणोदयकालेऽत्यन्तं रक्तस्ताम्नः। उदयादूर्ध्वमपिद्रक्तोऽरुणः। शं सुखं गमयति प्रापयतीति शंगः पशूनां पालयिता पशुपतिः। विरोधिनो नाशयितुं क्रोधयुक्त उग्रः। दर्शनमात्रेण विरोधिनां भयहेतुर्भीमः। अग्रे पुरतो वधोऽस्येत्यग्रेवधः। एवं दूरेवधः। पुरतो दूरे वा वर्तमानं विरोधिनमना-यासेनैव हन्तीत्यर्थः। लोकेऽपि यो यत्र विरोधिनं हन्ति तत्र तद्रूपेणायमेव हन्ता। अत एवेश्वरेणार्जुनं प्रत्युक्तम्—

‘मयैवैते निहताः पूर्वमेव निमित्तमात्रं भव सव्यसाचिन्, इति।

संहारकालेऽतिशयेन च सर्वेषां हन्ता हनीयान्। हरितवर्णानि केशसदृशानि षर्णानि येषां ते हरिकेशास्तथाविधा ये वृक्षाः कल्पतरुप्रभृतयस्तद्रूपोऽयं रुद्र इत्यर्थः। तारः प्रणवप्रतिपाद्यः। शं सुखं भावयत्युत्पादयतीति शंभुः। मयः सुखं भावयतीति मयोभूः। एकं विषयसुखमपरं मोक्षसुखमिति तयोर्विवेकः । पित्रादिरूपेण शं लौकिक-सुखं करोतीति शंकरः। आचार्यशास्त्रादिरूपेण मोक्षसुखं करोतीति मयस्करः। साक्षात्सुखकारित्वमेताभ्यां पदाभ्यामुक्तमतेन्मुखेन कारयितृत्वं पूर्वाभ्यां पदाभ्यामिति विवेकः। शिव कल्याणरूपः स्वयं निष्कल्मष इत्यर्थः। अतिशयेन शिवः शिवतरः स्वभक्तानपि निष्कल्मषान्करोती त्यर्थः। तीर्थे प्रयागादौ संनिहितस्तीर्थ्यः। कूले नदीतीरे प्रतिष्ठापितलिङ्गरूपेणावतिष्ठत इति कूल्यः। पारे संसारसमुद्रस्य परतीरे मुमुक्षुभिर्ध्येयत्वेनावति-

Bhāṣya

ष्ठत इत्यवार्यः। प्रकृष्टेन मन्त्रजपादिरूपेण पापतरणहेतुः प्रतरणः। तत्त्वज्ञानरूपेण कृत्स्‍नसंसारोत्तरणहेतुरुत्तरणः। संभवत्यपि संसारोत्तरणहेतौ तत्त्वज्ञाने तदुपेक्ष्य काम्य-कर्मानुष्ठानेन संसारे पुनरागमनमातारस्तमर्हतीत्यातार्यः, काम्यफलप्रद इत्यर्थः। अलं संपूर्णं यथा भवति तथा कर्मफलमत्तीत्यलादो जीवः “तयोरन्यः पिप्पलं स्वाद्वत्ति” इति श्रुतेः। तस्य प्रेरकत्वेन तत्संबन्धित्वा दालद्यः। शष्पं वालतृणं गङ्गातीरादावुत्पन्नं कुशा-ङ्‍करादि तदर्हतीति शष्प्यः। नदमिध्यगतं फेनमर्हतीति फेन्यः। सिकतामर्हतीति सिकत्यः। प्रवाहमर्हतीति प्रवाह्यः। य पुरुषः श्रद्धालुः सन्स्‍नानादितत्परो निरन्तरं गङ्गादितीरे वर्तते तद्रूप इति शष्प्यादिशब्दानां चतुर्णां तात्पर्यर्थः।।

इति श्रीमत्सायणाचार्यविरचिते माधवीये वेदार्थप्रकाशे कृष्णयजुर्वे-दीयतैत्तिरीयसंहिताभाष्ये चतुर्थकाण्डे पञ्चम-

प्रपाठकेऽष्टमोऽनुवाकः ।। ८।।
