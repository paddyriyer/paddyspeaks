Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 35 of 80

Ojo ’si and the gāyatrī with its head: the invitation completed and the breath-control mantra

The invitation of the gāyatrī is completed with her praises and her seer, deity and limbs; then come the seven vyāhṛtis, the gāyatrī and its ‘head’, recited together for breath control.

5 Faithful English translation

You are vigour, you are conquering power, you are strength, you are radiance; the splendour of the gods [is your] name; you are the all, the life of all; you are everything, the life of everything, the overpowering. Om. I invite the gāyatrī, I invite the sāvitrī, I invite Sarasvatī, I invite the metres and seers, I invite Śrī. Of the gāyatrī: the metre gāyatrī, the seer Viśvāmitra, the deity Savitṛ; Agni the mouth, Brahmā the head, Viṣṇu the heart, Rudra the crest, the earth the womb; having prāṇa, apāna, vyāna, udāna and samāna, with the vital powers, white in colour, of the lineage of Sāṅkhyāyana — the gāyatrī of twenty-four syllables, three feet, six bellies, five heads; [its] application in the upanayana. Om bhūḥ. Om bhuvaḥ. Om suvaḥ. Om mahaḥ. Om janaḥ. Om tapaḥ. Om satyam. Om, may we meditate on that excellent radiance of the god Savitṛ, who may impel our thoughts. Om, the waters, light, essence, the immortal, brahman; bhūḥ bhuvaḥ suvaḥ, Om.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

You are vigour and conquering power, strength and radiance; the splendour of the gods is your very name; you are the universe and its life, all things and the life of all, the one who prevails. Om — I call here the gāyatrī, the sāvitrī, Sarasvatī, the metres and the seers, and Śrī. The gāyatrī: its metre gāyatrī, its seer Viśvāmitra, its deity Savitṛ; Agni its mouth, Brahmā its head, Viṣṇu its heart, Rudra its crest, the earth its womb; bearing the five breaths, white, of the lineage of Sāṅkhyāyana; twenty-four syllables in three feet; used at the upanayana. Om earth, Om mid-space, Om heaven, Om the great, Om janaḥ, Om tapaḥ, Om the true. Om — let us contemplate the excellent radiance of the god Savitṛ, who impels our minds. Om — the waters, light, essence, the immortal: Brahman; earth, mid-space, heaven — Om.

7 Explain it to a beginner

This section continues the welcome of the gāyatrī and then gives the formula for breath control. First the worshipper praises her — you are vigour, strength, radiance, the life of all — and invites her, together with Sarasvatī, the metres and seers, and Śrī. Then comes the standard heading of the gāyatrī verse: its metre, its seer Viśvāmitra, its deity Savitṛ, and the deities placed on its mouth, head, heart and crest, ending with its use at the initiation of a student. The last lines are the formula recited while holding the breath: Om with each of seven sacred utterances naming the worlds, the gāyatrī verse itself (‘may we contemplate the excellent radiance of the god Savitṛ, who impels our minds’), and its ‘head’, which declares the waters, light, essence and the immortal to be Brahman. The commentators explain the procedure; the edition makes no claim about its effects.

An analogy Like a musician who names the piece, the key and the composer before playing it, then plays it through without pause.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It praises the goddess as vigour, strength, radiance, the all and its life; invites the gāyatrī, the sāvitrī, Sarasvatī, the metres and seers, and Śrī; states the gāyatrī’s metre, seer, deity, the deities of her limbs, her breaths, colour, lineage, syllables, feet, bellies and heads and her application at the upanayana; and gives the seven vyāhṛtis each with Om, the gāyatrī verse with Om, and its head, ‘the waters, light, essence, the immortal, brahman’.

Traditional Interpretation

How does it divide in the rite?

The anonymous commentator begins: गायत्र्या आवाहनमन्त्रमाह — this is the mantra of invitation; then, at the vyāhṛtis: गायत्र्यावाहनादूर्ध्वं प्राणायामार्थं मन्त्रमाह — after the invitation, the mantra for breath control. Sāyaṇa opens his comment on the breath-control mantra with the same words (गायत्र्या आवाहनादूर्ध्वं प्राणायामार्थं मन्त्रमाह). Bhāskara likewise comments on the invitation (‘ojo ’si’) with the preceding anuvāka and on the breath-control mantra separately.

Traditional Interpretation

What is the breath-control mantra?

Sāyaṇa: the seven vyāhṛtis name the worlds from bhūḥ to satyam (भूरादयः सत्यान्ता लोकप्रतिपादिकाः सप्त व्याहृतयः), each said with Om because each world is Brahman named by Om; then the gāyatrī, with Om before it; then its head. He cites a verse he attributes to the Amṛtanāda Upaniṣad: सव्याहृतिं सप्रणवां गायत्रीं शिरसा सह। त्रिः पठेदायतप्राणः प्राणायामः स उच्यते — reciting this three times while holding the breath is called prāṇāyāma. Bhāskara: प्रणवव्याहृतिशिरोभिः सहिता सावित्री प्राणायामार्था, and defines the practice as the stopping of in- and out-breath for the time of three recitations (अस्यास्त्रिरावर्तनकालं प्राणापानवृत्तिनिरोधः प्राणायामः). The verse is reported as cited by them; the edition has not verified it in that Upaniṣad.

Traditional Interpretation

What does the gāyatrī verse mean to the commentators?

Sāyaṇa: Savitṛ is the god who impels from within, his excellent radiance is to be meditated on, and he is asked to impel our minds to the knowledge of truth — सवितुः प्रेरकस्यान्तर्यामिणो देवस्य वरेण्यं वरणीयं श्रेष्ठं तद्भर्गस्तेजो धीमहि ध्यायेम … प्रचोदयात्प्रकर्षेण तत्त्वबोधे प्रेरयतु. Bhāskara records its metre, seer and deity: विश्वामित्रस्यार्षेयम् । सविता देवता.

Traditional Interpretation

What is the ‘head’ of the gāyatrī?

The anonymous commentator: ‘āpo jyotiḥ …’ is the gāyatrī’s head (आपो ज्योतिरित्यादि गायत्र्याः शिरः), framed by Om. Sāyaṇa: whatever light there is, whatever essence of six tastes, whatever nectar the gods drink — all that is Brahman named by Om: तत्सर्वमों प्रणवप्रतिपाद्यं ब्रह्म; the three worlds named by bhūr bhuvaḥ suvaḥ are that Brahman too.

Traditional Interpretation

How are the gāyatrī’s limbs and lineage explained?

The anonymous commentator explains each: the earth is her womb as the cause of all birth; she is white because pure goodness predominates in her; Sāṅkhyāyana is the supreme self, reached by those who enumerate and set aside all qualities (सांख्यैरीयते गम्यते प्राप्यते सर्वगुणनिरासात्स सांख्यायनः परमात्मा); her six bellies are the six auxiliary sciences of the Veda (षड्वेदाङ्गानि कुक्षयोऽस्याः); her five heads the four Vedas’ Upaniṣad portions and the epics and Purāṇas as a fifth. The application is the initiation of a student (वटोरुपनयन उपनीतकरणे).

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • ओजोऽसि ojo'si → ओजः + असि

    aḥ before a

    ojaḥ + asi → ojo ’si: -aḥ becomes -o and the following a is elided (P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति)).

  • विश्वायुस्सर्वमसि viśvāyussarvamasi → विश्वायुः + सर्वम् + असि

    visarga before s, written doubled

    The Taittirīya recitation writes a visarga before s as s: viśvāyuḥ + sarvam → viśvāyus sarvam.

  • सर्वायुरभिभूरों sarvāyurabhibhūroṃ → सर्वायुः + अभिभूः + ओम्

    visarga sandhi (after u, ū)

    A visarga after a vowel other than a or ā becomes r before a vowel: sarvāyur abhibhūr om.

  • गायत्रीच्छन्दो gāyatrīcchando → गायत्रीच्छन्दः

    ch doubled after a long vowel (inside the compound); aḥ before a voiced sound

    gāyatrī + chandas → gāyatrīcchandas (P. 6.1.75 (दीर्घात्)); -aḥ before v becomes -o.

  • विश्वामित्र ऋषिः viśvāmitra ṛṣiḥ → विश्वामित्रः + ऋषिः

    aḥ before a vowel

    The visarga of -aḥ is dropped before a vowel and the vowels stay apart.

  • देवताऽग्निर्मुखं devatā'gnirmukhaṃ → देवता + अग्निः + मुखम्

    ā + a; visarga after i

    devatā + agniḥ → devatāgniḥ, the merged a marked by avagraha as printed; agniḥ + mukham → agnir mukham.

  • पञ्चशीर्षोपनयने pañcaśīrṣopanayane → पञ्चशीर्षा + उपनयने

    vowel sandhi (guṇa)

    ā + u → o (P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः)).

  • ओमापो ज्योती रसोऽमृतं omāpo jyotī raso'mṛtaṃ → ओम् + आपः + ज्योतिः + रसः + अमृतम्

    m before a vowel; aḥ before a voiced sound; iḥ before r; aḥ before a

    om āpaḥ → om āpo; jyotiḥ + rasaḥ → jyotī rasaḥ (the visarga dropped before r and the i lengthened, P. 6.3.111 (ढ्रलोपे पूर्वस्य दीर्घोऽणः)); rasaḥ + amṛtam → raso ’mṛtam.

  • भूर्भुवस्सुवरोम् bhūrbhuvassuvarom → भूः + भुवः + सुवः + ओम्

    r-visarga before voiced sounds; visarga before s written doubled

    bhūḥ + bhuvaḥ → bhūr bhuvaḥ; bhuvaḥ + suvaḥ → bhuvas suvaḥ; suvaḥ (from suvar) + om → suvar om.

Morphology

गायत्रियाः
The text prints गायत्रिया before गायत्रीच्छन्दः; a genitive gāyatriyāḥ (Vedic, with the visarga dropped before the voiced g) heads the statement ‘of the gāyatrī: the metre is gāyatrī, the seer Viśvāmitra …’. An instrumental gāyatriyā is also possible in form; the commentators do not gloss the word.
षटकुक्षिः
Printed without the virāma of ṣaṭ (षट्कुक्षिः is the expected form, and the anonymous commentator writes षट्कुक्षिः); the other witnesses agree with the base or show their own errors (sources page).
धीमहि
A Vedic form of √dhī (‘to think, to contemplate’) with the sense of a wish; Sāyaṇa glosses it ध्यायेम, ‘may we meditate’.
प्रचोदयात्
Subjunctive of the causative of √cud with pra, ‘may he impel’; the anonymous commentator gives it as a statement (प्रेरयति), Sāyaṇa as a wish (प्रेरयतु).

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

Three parts. (1) Nominal predicates with asi, addressed to the goddess, and five parallel clauses with āvāhayāmi. (2) A ritual heading in nominal sentences — seer, deity, the limbs (mouth, head, heart, crest, womb) and qualities of the gāyatrī — ending with its application (upanayane viniyogaḥ). (3) The breath-control mantra: Om with each of seven vyāhṛtis; the gāyatrī verse (a relative clause yo naḥ pracodayāt dependent on the genitive savituḥ); and its ‘head’, a series of nominatives identified with brahman, framed by Om.

Ambiguities

whom ‘you are vigour …’ addresses
The anonymous commentator: the gāyatrī (हे गायत्रि त्वमोजोऽसि); Bhāskara: the same, explaining why she is to accept the recitation (कस्मादेवमुच्यत इति चेत्).
dhāma nāma
The anonymous commentator: the splendour of the gods is your name; Bhāskara: you are the gods’ abode, and nāma as ‘bowing, subduing’ (नाम नमनं वशीकरणम्).
the end of the invitation
In the Ānandāśrama print the passage from Viśvāmitra to the gāyatrī’s lineage, and the first five vyāhṛtis, stand inside the commentary rather than the text; the collation therefore lists them as ‘review’ items. The base and the Tirupati e-text have them in the text.

Metre

mixed · syllables counted as written: 177 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 6 + 16 + 8 + 17

Prose formulas, then the gāyatrī verse. The lines count 177, 3, 3, 3, 3, 6, 16, 8 and 17 syllables. The gāyatrī’s first foot, tat savitur vareṇyam, has seven syllables, so the line with Om counts 16; Bhāskara therefore calls the verse a nicṛd gāyatrī, one syllable short (तत्सवितुरिति सावित्री त्रिपदा निचृद्गायत्रीछन्दस्का अक्षरोनत्वादस्याः).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Attention

The rite gathers the whole of the gāyatrī — her seer, her deity, her parts — before the verse is said, then fixes attention on one sentence asking that the mind be impelled. Preparing fully and then attending to a single sentence is a shape of attention that does not depend on any belief. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin The gāyatrī asks that ‘our thoughts’ be impelled. What do you hope your thinking is moved towards?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why might the text recite the seven worlds, each with Om, before and around the gāyatrī rather than the verse alone?

A contemplation exercise Read the English of the gāyatrī slowly three times, attending only to the words.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.

Further notes

  • Sāyaṇa comments on ojo ’si in his anuvāka 26 and on the breath-control mantra in his anuvāka 27. The source prints the colophon of 27 as ‘26’; the edition keys the file by its heading, so both are cited here by his own numbers.
  • The gāyatrī verse is Ṛgveda 3.62.10; the edition’s Ṛgveda source covers only the first maṇḍala, so it is not cited as a parallel here.

Uncertainties recorded

  • review The ‘review’ items of the collation for this anuvāka mostly reflect the Ānandāśrama print setting part of the mūla inside its commentary; they should be checked against the printed book.
  • variant षटकुक्षिः as printed (षट्कुक्षिः expected); the Tirupati e-text has ष्ट्कुक्षिः.
  • interpretation gāyatriyā as a genitive heading (‘of the gāyatrī’) is the reading adopted; an instrumental is possible in form.
