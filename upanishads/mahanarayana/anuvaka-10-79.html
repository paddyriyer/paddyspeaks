Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 79 of 80

Āruṇi asks Prajāpati what is highest: the twelve means told again, and renunciation above them

Āruṇi, son of Prajāpati, asks his father what the venerable ones call highest. Prajāpati praises truth, austerity, restraint, calm, giving, dharma, procreation, the fires, the agnihotra, sacrifice and mental worship, each in turn — then renunciation, the self in the sun and the chain from food to knowing the self; a verse and a formula of meditation on Om close the teaching.

5 Faithful English translation

Āruṇi, son of Prajāpati, son of Suparṇā, approached [his] father Prajāpati: ‘What do the venerable ones call highest?’ To him he declared: By truth the wind blows; by truth the sun shines in the sky; truth [is] the foundation of speech; in truth everything is established; therefore they call truth highest. By austerity the gods in the beginning went to godhood; by austerity the seers found heaven; by austerity we drive away rivals [and] foes; in austerity everything is established; therefore they call austerity highest. By restraint the restrained shake off sin; by restraint the celibate students went to heaven; restraint [is] hard for beings to overcome; in restraint everything is established; therefore they call restraint highest. By calm the calm practise the good; by calm the sages found the vault of heaven; calm [is] hard for beings to overcome; in calm everything is established; therefore they call calm highest. Giving [is] the shelter of sacrifices, the sacrificial fee; in the world all beings live upon the giver; by giving they drove away foes; by giving those who hate become friends; in giving everything is established; therefore they call giving highest. Dharma [is] the foundation of the whole world; in the world people approach the most righteous; by dharma one drives away evil; in dharma everything is established; therefore they call dharma highest. Procreation indeed [is] a foundation in the world; extending rightly the thread of offspring one becomes free of debt to the ancestors; that alone [is] the freedom from its debt; therefore they call procreation highest. The fires indeed [are] the threefold knowledge, the path leading to the gods: the householder’s fire [is] the Ṛg verse, the earth, the Rathantara; the southern fire [is] the Yajus, the mid-air, the Vāmadevya; the offering fire [is] the Sāman, the heavenly world, the Bṛhat; therefore they call the fires highest. The agnihotra, evening and morning, [is] the discharge of the household, well offered, well poured, the beginning of the sacrifices and soma rites, the light of the heavenly world; therefore they call the agnihotra highest. ‘Sacrifice’: for by sacrifice the gods went to heaven; by sacrifice they drove away the asuras; by sacrifice those who hate become friends; in sacrifice everything is established; therefore they call sacrifice highest. The mental [worship] indeed [is] Prajāpati’s purifier; by the mental [worship], with the mind one sees rightly; the mind-born seers created creatures; in the mental [worship] everything is established; therefore they call the mental [worship] highest. ‘Renunciation’, the wise say, [is] Brahmā; Brahmā [is] all, which (?), self-existent, Prajāpati, the year. The year [is] yonder sun; the person who [is] in the sun, he [is] the one in the highest place, brahman, the self. With the rays by which the sun gives heat, with those the rain-cloud rains; by the rain-cloud plants and trees are born; by plants and trees food comes to be; by food the breaths; by the breaths strength; by strength austerity; by austerity faith; by faith intelligence; by intelligence understanding; by understanding reflection; by reflection peace; by peace awareness; by awareness remembrance; by remembrance continual remembering; by continual remembering discerning knowledge; by discerning knowledge one comes to know the self. Therefore one giving food gives all these; from food the breaths of beings come to be; from the breaths the mind, and from the mind discerning knowledge; from discerning knowledge bliss, brahman, the source. He indeed [is] this person, fivefold, having five selves, by whom all this is woven through — earth and mid-air and sky and the directions and the intermediate directions; he indeed [is] all this world; he [is] what has been, he what is to be. Made certain by inquiry, born of the right order, abiding in wealth, faith, true, full of splendour, [beyond] the most excellent austerity (?) — having known him thus with mind and heart, do not, knowing, go again to death. Therefore they call renunciation the exceeding [one] of these austerities. You are the imparter of the true thing, pervading; you are the joiner in the breath; O Brahman, you are the upholder of all; you are the giver of radiance, you are Agni; you are the giver of lustre of the sun; you are the giver of brightness of the moon; you are taken up with the support; you for brahman, for greatness. With “Om” one should fix the self. This indeed [is] the great secret teaching, the secret of the gods. Who knows thus attains the greatness of brahman; therefore the greatness of brahman — thus the secret teaching.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Āruṇi, Prajāpati’s son, went to his father and asked: “What do the wise call the highest?” Prajāpati answered him, praising each means in turn. By truth the wind blows and the sun shines; truth is the ground of speech. By austerity the gods became gods and the seers reached heaven. By restraint the self-controlled shake off their faults; by calm the calm do good and the sages reached heaven. Giving shelters the sacrifices, and all beings live on the giver; by giving even enemies become friends. Dharma holds up the whole world. Procreation keeps the family line and pays the debt to the ancestors. The three sacred fires are the three Vedas and the path to the gods; the daily fire offering begins every rite; sacrifice took the gods to heaven; worship in the mind purifies and lets one see truly. For each, “everything rests on it, so they call it highest.” But renunciation, say the wise, is Brahmā himself — the all, the self-born, the year, the person in the sun. From the sun’s rays come rain, plants, food, breath, strength, austerity, faith, intelligence, understanding, reflection, peace, awareness, memory, steady recollection and discerning knowledge, by which one knows the self; so whoever gives food gives all these. That person, fivefold, runs through the earth, sky and every direction; he is all that was and will be. Knowing him with mind and heart, do not go to death again. Renunciation surpasses all these austerities. Then comes a prayer to the inner Lord, and the instruction: with “Om”, fix the self in meditation. This is the great secret teaching; whoever knows it reaches the greatness of brahman.

7 Explain it to a beginner

This is the same teaching as the previous section, told as a story. Āruṇi asks his father Prajāpati, the creator, “What do wise people call the highest?” Prajāpati does not give one answer at once. He praises truth, then austerity, then self-control, calm, generosity, good works, raising a family, the sacred fires, the daily fire offering, sacrifice and inner worship — and says of each, “everything rests on it, so people call it the highest.” The commentators say Āruṇi was not satisfied with any of these, so his father went on. Finally he names renunciation, and links it to the creator himself. He then traces a long chain: sunlight brings rain, rain brings plants and food, food brings breath and strength, and so on up to faith, memory and the knowledge of the self. Whoever gives food, he says, gives all of this. The section ends with a short prayer and an instruction to settle the mind on the syllable Om.

An analogy Like a guide who walks a traveller up a long staircase, stopping on each landing to praise the view, before opening the door at the top.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

Āruṇi asks his father Prajāpati what is called highest. Prajāpati answers with eleven praises — truth, austerity, restraint, calm, giving, dharma, procreation, the fires, the agnihotra, sacrifice, mental worship — each ending ‘therefore they call it highest’. Then he names renunciation, identifies Brahmā with the year and the person in the sun, traces a chain from the sun’s rays through food, breath and the mind to knowing the self, praises the giving of food, describes the fivefold person, gives a verse on knowing him, says renunciation exceeds all the austerities, and ends with a prayer and the instruction to fix the self with Om.

Traditional Interpretation

Who are the speakers?

The anonymous commentator: Āruṇi is the son of Prajāpati (प्रजापतेः पुत्रः प्राजापत्यः) and is called Sauparṇeya as the son of a woman named Suparṇā (सुपर्णाख्यायाः स्त्रिया अपत्यत्वात्सुपर्णेय इत्युच्यते). Bhāskara reports a view that he had two fathers, Prajāpati and Aruṇa, and takes Suparṇā as his mother (सुपर्णाया मातुः पुत्रः). He adds that the son rejected each answer in turn, and that the father therefore praised each one at length.

Traditional Interpretation

How do the commentators read the eleven praises?

For the anonymous commentator, the gods and seers gained their places in earlier births by these means: the seers by austerity reached heaven step by step (स्वर्गलोकमनुक्रमेण लब्धवन्तः). He takes the agnihotra as the price that redeems the house (गृहाणां निष्कृतिः क्रयसाधनं मूल्यम); Bhāskara as the cleansing of the household’s faults (गृहसम्बन्धिनां पापानां शोधनं). Bhāskara explains mental worship as Prajāpati’s through a Vedic identification he quotes, मनो वै प्रजापतिः.

Traditional Interpretation

What is said of renunciation?

The anonymous commentator: renunciation is called Brahmā, that is Hiraṇyagarbha, because it is the nearest means of reaching him — न्यास इत्युक्तो [यो] मोक्षहेतुस्तं ब्रह्माणं हिरण्यगर्भरूपं — and cites a smṛti line, संन्यासाद्ब्रह्मणः स्थानम्, reported here as cited by him. At the end of the chain he concludes: संन्यासमतिरिक्तमत्युत्कृष्टं साधनं मनीषिण आहुः. Bhāskara: तस्मान् मुक्तिसाधनत्वान् न्यासम् एव एषां सत्यादीनां तपसामतिरिक्तं प्रकृष्टम् आहुस् तत्त्वविदः.

Traditional Interpretation

What is the meditation at the end?

The anonymous commentator: after renunciation, the renunciant praises the inner ruler, to remove obstacles, and then fixes himself in meditation, pronouncing the three-measured Om — त्रिमात्रप्रणवमुच्चारयन्सर्ववेदान्तेषु निर्णीतमात्मानं स्वात्मरूपेण चित्ते समादध्यात्. He takes ‘the great secret teaching’ as Om, the word all the Upaniṣads teach, and cites the Kaṭha: सर्वे वेदा यत्पदमामननन्ति. The one who knows gains the greatness of brahman first while living and then, when the body falls, fully (ततो जीवन्मुक्तो भवतीत्यर्थः, विदेहमुक्तिर्भवतीत्यर्थः). Bhāskara takes the prayer as addressed to the meditator’s own self, and the repeated last sentence either as two stages or, with others, as marking the end of the teaching (केचित्तु विद्यासमाप्तिसूचनार्थ्य् आवृत्तिरित्याहुः).

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Which readings are doubtful?

Three places. (1) तपसो वरिष्ठात् (base, Ānandāśrama) against तमसोपरिष्टात् (Tirupati), which both commentators read as ‘beyond darkness’ (Bhāskara: तमस उपरिष्टाद् अज्ञानमतीत्य उपरिस्थितः). (2) अग्निरसि ‘you are Agni’ (base, Ānandāśrama) against अग्नेरसि (Tirupati), which Bhāskara reads: त्वमग्नेरसि — ‘you are [the giver of radiance] of fire’, matching the genitives of sun and moon that follow. (3) विश्वधृत् (base) against विश्वसृत् (Tirupati); Bhāskara has विश्वसृक् विश्वस्य भर्ता. The edition keeps the base and lists all three for review.

Morphology

सुपर्णेयः
A metronymic from Suparṇā; classical grammar expects the first vowel lengthened (सौपर्णेयः). Bhāskara: आदिवृद्ध्यभावश्छान्दसः — the missing vṛddhi is Vedic.
दमः
In the refrain तस्माद्दमः परमं वदन्ति the word stands where an accusative is expected (as in तस्माद्धर्मं परमं वदन्ति). Bhāskara: द्वितीयायास्स्वादेशः — the nominative ending replaces the accusative. The same holds for शमः.
ददन्
Present participle of √dā ‘give’; the classical form is ददत्. Bhāskara explains the n as a Vedic insertion (व्यत्ययेन नुम्); the anonymous commentator glosses ददत्.
उपयाहि
Imperative of upa-√yā with न: ‘do not go to (death again)’; the knower is addressed. The anonymous commentator glosses मा प्राप्नुहि.
वेदयति
A causative of √vid ‘know’, used in the sense ‘comes to know, experiences’: Bhāskara takes the active ending as a Vedic interchange (व्यत्ययेन परस्मैपदम्).
महोपनिषदम्
An a-stem neuter beside the consonant stem upaniṣad. Bhāskara: मत्वर्थीयोकारः — an a with possessive sense.
स्वयंभुः
Written with a short u; the usual form is स्वयम्भूः (as the Tirupati e-text has). The anonymous commentator reads it as the first member of a compound with प्रजापतिः.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

A frame narrative (उपससार … प्रोवाच) encloses a long answer in twelve paragraphs on one pattern: an instrumental of the means repeated in two or three clauses (सत्येन …, तपसा …, दमेन …), a closing clause X-e सर्वं प्रतिष्ठितम्, and the refrain तस्मात् X परमं वदन्ति. The eleventh paragraph is followed by renunciation, defined by identifications in nominal sentences (न्यास इत्याहुर्मनीषिणो ब्रह्माणम्; ब्रह्मा विश्वः …; संवत्सरोऽसावादित्यः …), then a chain of fifteen instrumentals each producing the next noun (अन्नेन प्राणाः प्राणैर्बलं …), a second chain of ablatives (अन्नात्प्राणाः …), a praise of the person, a triṣṭubh verse (जिज्ञासक्लृप्त …), the conclusion on renunciation, a prayer in the second person (… त्वमसि), an optative instruction (ओमित्यात्मानं युञ्जीत), and the closing formula.

Ambiguities

what is said to be highest
For the anonymous commentator, the best means to release (मोक्षसाधनेषु किं साधनं परममुत्कृष्टं वदन्ति); for Bhāskara, the best austerity (परमं प्रकृष्टं तपो वदन्तीति).
कतमः
An interrogative (‘which?’) standing among predicates of Brahmā. The anonymous commentator: the one most full of happiness (कतमोऽतिशयेन सुखस्वरूपः); Bhāskara: the one who cannot be determined (कतमो ऽशक्यनिर्धारणः).
ब्रह्मात्मा
The anonymous commentator separates two predicates of the person in the sun — brahman, the cause of the world, and the self of all (स च परमेष्ठी ब्रह्म जगतकारणवस्तु, तथैवाऽऽत्मा सर्वेषां प्रत्यगात्मभूतः); Bhāskara reads one: परब्रह्मात्मा परमात्मा च. The padaccheda keeps the joined form.
पञ्चधा पञ्चात्मा
The anonymous commentator: the person who is the twenty-five principles, five groups of five, or one standing fivefold as the self of the elements, the senses, matter, the individual and the supreme; Bhāskara: the five selves from the one made of food to the one made of bliss (पञ्चप्रकारो ऽन्नमयाद्यानन्दमयान्तात्मकपञ्चकभावेन वर्तते).
तपसो वरिष्ठात्
The base reads तपसो वरिष्ठात्, which yields no clear sense (‘from the most excellent austerity’). Both commentators read तमस उपरिष्टात् ‘beyond darkness’ (Bhāskara: तमस उपरिष्टाद् अज्ञानमतीत्य उपरिस्थितः; the anonymous commentator: तमसा संसारकारणाज्ञानेन वियुक्तत्वादुपरिष्टाद्वर्तते), and the Tirupati e-text has तमसोपरिष्टात्. The edition keeps the base and marks it for review.
वसु रण्वः
Both commentators divide vasu raṇvaḥ, not vasur aṇvaḥ: the anonymous commentator, the one who tells the true thing (वसुनो वस्तुतत्त्वस्य रण्वो वशिता कथयिता); Bhāskara, the one who leads to the best knowledge. Whom the prayer addresses also differs: the inner ruler for the anonymous commentator, the meditator’s own self for Bhāskara.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Dependence

The chain from sunlight to knowledge makes every higher capacity rest on a lower one: no thought without breath, no breath without food, no food without rain. Read today, it is a reminder that reflection and faith depend on very ordinary conditions, and that feeding someone sustains all of these at once. This is a modern reflection, not part of the traditional reading.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Why do you think Prajāpati praises every answer before naming renunciation?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry What does it mean that the giver of food ‘gives all these’ — breath, strength, faith, memory and knowledge?

A contemplation exercise Trace one thing you understand well back through the conditions that made it possible, step by step, as the passage does.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.

Further notes

  • Sāyaṇa’s comment on this anuvāka is his anuvāka 63; Bhāskara’s is his anuvāka 63. The anonymous commentator follows Sāyaṇa closely here and adds a long discussion of renunciation from the Brahmasūtra and from Upaniṣadic and smṛti texts, which this record does not quote.
