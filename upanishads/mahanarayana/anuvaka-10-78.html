Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 78 of 80

Truth, austerity and the rest: renunciation surpasses them all

Twelve answers to “what is highest” — truth, austerity, restraint, calm, giving, dharma, procreation, the fires, the agnihotra, sacrifice, mental worship — each with its adherents; renunciation, held by Brahmā, surpasses them all.

1 Original Sanskrit

सत्यं परं परं सत्यं सत्येन न सुवर्गाल्लोकाच्च्यवन्ते कदाचन सतां हि सत्यं तस्मात्सत्ये रमन्ते तप इति तपो नानशनात्परं यद्धि परं तपस्तद्दुर्धर्षं तद्दुराधर्षं तस्मात्तपसि रमन्ते दम इति नियतं ब्रह्मचारिणस्तस्माद्दमे रमन्ते शम इत्यरण्ये मुनयस्तमाच्छमे रमन्ते दानमिति सर्वाणि भूतानि प्रशंसन्ति दानान्नातिदुश्चरं तस्माद्दाने रमन्ते धर्म इति धर्मेण सर्वमिदं परिगृहीतं धर्मान्नाति दुष्करं तस्माद्धर्मे रमन्ते प्रजन इति भूयांसस्तस्माद्भूयिष्ठाः प्रजायन्ते तस्माद्भूयिष्ठाः प्रजनने रमन्तेऽग्नय इत्याह तस्मादग्नय आधातव्या अग्निहोत्रमित्याह तस्मादग्निहोत्रे रमन्ते यज्ञ इति यज्ञो हि देवास्तस्माद्यज्ञे रमन्ते मानसमिति विद्वांसस्तस्माद्विद्वांस एव मानसे रमन्ते न्यास इति ब्रह्मा ब्रह्मा हि परः परो हि ब्रह्मा तानि वा एतान्यवराणि परांसि न्यास एवात्यरेचयद्य एवं वेदेत्युपनिषत् ॥ ९९ ॥

5 Faithful English translation

Truth [is] highest; the highest [is] truth. By truth they never fall from the heavenly world, for truth [is that] of the good; therefore they delight in truth. ‘Austerity’ [say others]: there is nothing higher than not eating; for what [is] the highest austerity, that [is] hard to bear, that [is] hard to overcome; therefore they delight in austerity. ‘Restraint’ [say] the celibate students, constantly; therefore they delight in restraint. ‘Calm’ [say] the sages in the forest; therefore they delight in calm. ‘Giving’: all beings praise [it]; nothing [is] harder to carry out than giving; therefore they delight in giving. ‘Dharma’: by dharma all this is held together; nothing [is] harder to do than dharma; therefore they delight in dharma. ‘Procreation’ [say] the many; therefore the greatest number beget offspring; therefore the greatest number delight in procreation. ‘The fires’, one says; therefore the fires are to be established. ‘The agnihotra’, one says; therefore they delight in the agnihotra. ‘Sacrifice’: for [by] sacrifice [are] the gods [in heaven]; therefore they delight in sacrifice. ‘The mental [worship]’ [say] the knowing; therefore the knowing delight only in the mental [worship]. ‘Renunciation’ [says] Brahmā; Brahmā indeed [is] highest, the highest indeed [is] Brahmā. These indeed are lower — the high ones; renunciation alone surpassed [them]. Who knows thus — this is the secret teaching.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Truth is the highest, say some: by truth no one falls from the heavenly world, for truth belongs to the good, and so they delight in truth. Others name austerity, for nothing is higher than fasting, and the highest austerity is hard to bear. The celibate students name restraint; the sages in the forest name calm. Giving is praised by all beings, and nothing is harder; dharma holds the whole world together; most people name procreation and have many children. One says the sacred fires, another the daily fire offering, others sacrifice, by which the gods reached heaven; the knowing delight in worship done in the mind. But Brahmā names renunciation — and Brahmā is the highest. All these others are lower; renunciation alone surpasses them. Whoever knows this has the secret teaching.

Key concepts संन्यास renunciation सत्य truth, the real तपस् austerity; disciplined effort धर्म dharma ब्रह्मचारिन् brahmacārin, Vedic student अग्नि Agni, fire त्याग giving up, relinquishment

7 Explain it to a beginner

People have always disagreed about what matters most in life. This passage lists twelve answers. Some say truthfulness, because the truthful never fall. Some say austerity — above all fasting — because it is so hard. Students living under vows prize self-control; hermits in the forest prize a calm mind. Some prize generosity, some good works for the community, most people prize raising a family. Followers of the Vedic ritual prize keeping the sacred fires, the daily fire offering, or the great sacrifices; thoughtful people prize inner worship. Each group delights in its own answer. Then the passage gives its verdict: Brahmā, the creator, names renunciation — giving up the whole round of works — and all the other answers are lower than that. The next anuvāka tells the same teaching as a story.

An analogy Like a teacher who writes every student’s answer on the board, explains why each one is attractive, and only then circles the one answer that includes and surpasses the rest.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It names twelve things held to be highest — truth, austerity, restraint, calm, giving, dharma, procreation, the fires, the agnihotra, sacrifice, mental worship and renunciation — gives a reason for each, and says that each has those who delight in it. It ends by calling the first eleven lower and saying that renunciation alone surpassed them.

Traditional Interpretation

Why does the list stand here?

The anonymous commentator marks a new section, on renunciation, after the sections on food and on rites: अथेदानीं सर्वकर्ममयसंसारबीजदाहार्थं संन्यासप्रकरणमारभ्यते — renunciation, to burn the seed of the round of existence made of all works. To show that renunciation is the highest of the means to knowledge, the other eleven are set against it: सत्यादीन्येकादशोत्कृष्टसाधनानि प्रतियोगित्वेन वक्तव्यानि. Sāyaṇa opens his comment with the same sentence.

Traditional Interpretation

How does each commentator read the list?

For the anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa, each item is someone’s view of the best means to release, with its adherents: तपः परं मोक्षसाधनमिति केषांचिन्मतम्. Bhāskara reads the list as kinds of austerity (tapas), each held by some to be the highest, so that renunciation itself is the highest austerity: सत्यादितपोव्युदासेन सन्न्यासाख्यस्य तपसः प्रकृष्टत्वं प्रतिपादगति; for the second item he says तप इति यत्प्रसिद्धं यमनियमादिकं तदेव परं तप इति पक्षान्तरम् इत्यर्थः. In support of truth he cites a verse: अश्वमेधसहस्रं च सत्यं च तुलया धृतम् — a thousand horse sacrifices weighed against truth — reported here as cited by him.

Traditional Interpretation

What is the renunciation meant here?

Sāyaṇa: the giving up of the rites of the ritual portion of the Veda, such as the agnihotra, in the way the Āruṇi and Jābāla Upaniṣads teach — पूर्वकाण्डोक्तानामग्निहोत्रादिकर्मणामारुणिजाबालाद्युपनिषदुक्तप्रकारेण परित्यागो न्यासः — and it alone surpassed all the rest: संन्यास एक एव तानि सर्वाण्यत्यरेचयत्. Bhāskara: the giving up of the fruit of all works — सर्वकर्मफलत्यागो न्यासः स एव परमं तपः.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What does the text read at two doubtful places?

In the sentence on calm the base and the Tirupati e-text print तमात्, the Ānandāśrama text तस्मात्; every other paragraph has तस्मात्, and the translation follows it. In the closing sentence the base reads एतान्यवराणि परांसि, ‘these are lower — the high ones’, while Bhāskara’s text reads तपांसि ‘austerities’ (तानि वा एतान्यवराणि तपांसि); the edition keeps the base.

Morphology

तमात्
The base, and the Tirupati e-text, print तमात्; the Ānandāśrama text reads तस्मात्, which the rest of the anuvāka uses in every other clause (‘therefore they delight in …’) and which both commentators read (Bhāskara: तस्माच्छमे रमन्ते सर्वेऽपि). The edition keeps the base and marks the place for review; the translation reads ‘therefore’.
परांसि
A Vedic neuter plural of an s-stem paras- beside para- ‘high’; it stands in apposition to अवराणि: ‘these are lower — the (so-called) high ones’. Bhāskara’s text has तपांसि ‘austerities’ in this place.
अत्यरेचयत्
Imperfect of the causative of ati-√ric ‘to exceed’: ‘made (them) left behind, surpassed’. The anonymous commentator glosses अतिक्रान्तवान्.
आह
A perfect form of √ah with present meaning: ‘says’. The one who says it is not named; the commentators supply a follower of the Veda (कश्चिद्वेदार्थपर आह).

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

A catalogue of twelve short paragraphs built on one pattern: the name of a means, often marked by इति (‘“austerity”’), a reason in a nominal sentence or with हि, and the refrain तस्मात् … रमन्ते ‘therefore they delight in …’. The holders of each view are named in the nominative without a verb of saying (ब्रह्मचारिणः, मुनयः, भूयांसः, विद्वांसः, ब्रह्मा). The last paragraph turns the catalogue into a ranking: तानि … अवराणि … न्यास एवात्यरेचयत्, closed by the formula य एवं वेद … इत्युपनिषत्.

Ambiguities

who says “the fires” and “the agnihotra”
आह has no subject. The commentators supply a follower of the Veda for each view (कश्चिद्वेदार्थपर आह); the text itself does not name one.
यज्ञो हि देवाः
Literally ‘for sacrifice is the gods’. The anonymous commentator reads यज्ञः as an instrumental by Vedic interchange of cases and supplies ‘went to heaven’ (यज्ञो व्यत्ययेन तृतीया यज्ञेन), matching 10.79’s यज्ञेन हि देवा दिवं गताः; Bhāskara calls the clause plain (यज्ञेन हीत्यादि स्पष्टम्).
what ‘Brahmā’ is
For the anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa, Hiraṇyagarbha, who may be called the supreme because his knowledge equals it (परो हि परमात्मैव ब्रह्मा हिरण्यगर्भ इति वक्तुं शक्यते). For Bhāskara, the one placed above all (ब्रह्मा हि सर्वस्मात् परम उपरि स्थितः), and renunciation is in the place of Brahmā.

    The Bṛhadāraṇyaka names giving, sacrifice and austerity as ways the self is sought, and the wandering renunciants as those who seek it alone.

Contemporary Reflection

Theme Values

Each group in the passage delights in the value it has chosen, and each has a good reason. The passage does not mock them; it ranks them. One can read it as a question about what one would keep if every other good had to be held loosely. This is a modern reflection, not part of the traditional reading.
