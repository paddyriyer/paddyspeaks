Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 15 of 80

Ādityo vai tejaḥ: the sun as all things, and the fruit of knowing it

The sun is identified with brilliance, the senses, the gods, breath, truth, food and the self-existent brahman; whoever knows this reaches union with Brahmā or with these deities.

1 Original Sanskrit

5 Faithful English translation

The sun, truly, [is] brilliance, vigour, strength, fame, eye, ear, self, mind, anger, Manu, Death, Satya, Mitra, wind, space, breath, the guardian of the worlds, Ka, what, joy, that, truth, food, the immortal, the living being, the all, Katama, the self-existent brahman; [all] this [is he]. Immortal [is] this person; this [is] the overlord of beings. He attains union with Brahmā, sharing his world; he attains union with these very deities, equal station, living in the same world — who knows thus. Thus the secret teaching.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

The sun is all of these: brilliance, vigour, strength and fame; eye, ear, body and mind; anger, Manu and Death; Satya, Mitra, wind, space and breath; the guardian of the worlds; Prajāpati, the unnameable, joy, the hidden, truth, food, the immortal, every living being, the whole, the most joyful — the self-existent brahman. This person is immortal; he rules all beings. Whoever knows this attains union with Brahmā and shares his world, or union with these deities, their station and their world. This is the secret teaching.

7 Explain it to a beginner

This passage follows the meditation on the sun as the three Vedas and widens it: the sun is everything. It lists brilliance, vigour, strength and fame; the eye, the ear, the body and the mind; anger and death; gods such as Mitra; wind, space and breath; and, at the end, the mysterious names Ka, ‘what’, and ‘joy’ — all of them ending in the self-existent Brahman. Then it states a promise: whoever knows this reaches union with Brahmā, or lives in his world, or shares the station of the gods named. The old commentators explain the different results by how intensely one meditates. The closing words, ‘thus the Upaniṣad’, mark the end of a section; the commentator says the secret teaching of the preceding anuvākas is complete here.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

That the sun is brilliance, vigour, strength, fame, the senses, the body, mind, anger, Manu, Death, several deities, wind, space, breath, the guardian of the worlds, Ka, what, joy, that, truth, food, the immortal, the living being, the all, Katama and the self-existent brahman; that this person is immortal and the overlord of beings; and that the one who knows this attains union with Brahmā and with these deities.

Traditional Interpretation

Why is this said?

The anonymous commentator: it shows the remaining quality to be meditated on in the person in the sun, that he is everything, पूर्वोक्तस्यैवाऽऽदित्यपुरुषस्य सर्वात्मकत्वलक्षणमवशिष्टमुपास्यगुणं दर्शयति. Bhāskara: each item is how the god favours the world in that form — as brilliance he gives brilliance, तेजो भूत्वा तेजयति, and so for all; they are the deities presiding over each, देवताविशेषा एते तत्तदभिमानिनः.

Traditional Interpretation

What are the rewards?

Sāyaṇa distinguishes two meditations, on Hiraṇyagarbha and on the deities that are his parts, द्विविधमुपासनं हिरण्यगर्भोपासनं तदवयवभूतदेवतोपासनं चेति, and grades the result by the intensity of meditation: with the greatest, union with Hiraṇyagarbha, तत्र हिरण्यगर्भोपास्तौ भावनाधिक्ये सति ब्रह्मणो हिरण्यगर्भस्य सायुज्यं सहभावं तादात्म्यं प्राप्नोति; with less, sharing his world, भावनामान्द्ये सलोकतां हिरण्यगर्भेण सहैकलोकनिवासं प्राप्नोति; and among the deities, equal power with middling meditation and the same world with the least, मान्द्ये तु समानलोकतां प्राप्नोति.

Traditional Interpretation

What does ‘thus the Upaniṣad’ close?

The anonymous commentator: the knowledge told in the preceding anuvākas, the secret of the Upaniṣad, is here complete, इत्येवं पूर्वोक्तैरनुवाकैरुक्तोपनिषद्रहस्यभूता विद्या समाप्तेति शेषः.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What do the witnesses show?

The Tirupati e-text adds after this anuvāka the passage beginning ghṛṇiḥ sūrya ādityaḥ, which in the base is a separate anuvāka; the sources page lists it as a variant. Sāyaṇa’s text, printed with his commentary, has a longer list (life, Prajāpati, the year) and reads svayaṃbhūḥ.

Where they disagree

No Vedānta-school commentary on this text is in the edition’s sources (Raṅgarāmānuja’s commentary on the Mahānārāyaṇa is not available here). Sāyaṇa and the anonymous commentator read the passage as meditation on Hiraṇyagarbha in the sun, with graded rewards; Bhāskara as the god’s favour in many forms. None of them discusses the relation of the meditator’s self to brahman here.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • तेज ओजो teja ojo → तेजः + ओजः

    -aḥ before a vowel other than a

    tejaḥ + ojaḥ → teja ojaḥ: the visarga is dropped and the vowels stay apart (P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि) with the y lost).

  • यशश्चक्षुः yaśaścakṣuḥ → यशः + चक्षुः

    visarga before c

    yaśaḥ + cakṣuḥ → yaśaś cakṣuḥ (P. 8.4.40 (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः)).

  • ब्रह्मैतदमृत brahmaitadamṛta → ब्रह्म + एतत् + अमृतः

    a + e; t before a vowel

    brahma + etat → brahmaitat (P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि)); etat + amṛtaḥ → etad amṛtaḥ.

  • सलोकतामाप्नोत्येतासामेव salokatāmāpnotyetāsāmeva → सलोकताम् + आप्नोति + एतासाम् + एव

    m before a vowel; i before a dissimilar vowel

    m joins the next vowel; āpnoti + etāsām → āpnoty etāsām (P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि)).

  • वेदेत्युपनिषत् vedetyupaniṣat → वेद + इति + उपनिषत्

    a + i; i before u

    veda + iti → vedeti (P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः)); iti + upaniṣat → ity upaniṣat.

Morphology

स्वयंभु
As printed with a short u; Sāyaṇa’s text and gloss have svayaṃbhūḥ, स्वयंभूः कारणान्तरादनुत्पन्नः प्रजापतिः. The anonymous commentator reads it with brahma as ‘brahman free of origin’.
कः
Ka, the interrogative used as a name of Prajāpati (as in the hymn kasmai devāya); the next words kiṃ and kaṃ play on the same root.
वेद
The perfect of √vid with present meaning, ‘knows’, regular in the formula ya evaṃ veda.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

A list of some thirty predicate nominatives with the single subject ādityaḥ (‘the sun [is] …’), closed by etat (‘[all] this’), then two short nominal sentences on the person (amṛta eṣa puruṣaḥ, eṣa bhūtānām adhipatiḥ), then two sentences of reward with āpnoti, whose subject is given at the end by the relative ya evaṃ veda. The last words, ity upaniṣat, close the section.

Ambiguities

whose union
brahmaṇaḥ can be brahman (neuter) or Brahmā (masculine). Sāyaṇa and the anonymous commentator take it as Brahmā, Hiraṇyagarbha, and explain the grades of reward by the strength of meditation (see commentary).
the short list
Sāyaṇa’s text and gloss give more items than the base: āyuḥ, ‘life’, आयुः शतसंवत्सरादिकम्, and the year, संवत्सर आदित्यस्वरूपः. Bhāskara’s gloss also runs to the year: तेजआदिसंवत्सरपर्यन्यः पदार्थवर्गः.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Interdependence

Brilliance, breath, food, the senses and the mind are all traced to the sun. Modern science also traces most of what lives on earth to sunlight; the text’s way of saying it is devotional, not physical, but the sense of one source behind many things is shared. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Which things in your life depend, in the end, on sunlight?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry The list ends with ‘Ka, what, joy, that’ — a question, the unnameable, joy and the hidden. Why might a list of the sun’s forms end there?

A contemplation exercise Eat one meal today remembering where the food’s energy came from.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
