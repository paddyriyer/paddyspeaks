Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 60 of 80

Yad vo devāś cakṛma: setting down offences against the gods

A verse of offering asks the gods to set aside the wrongs done against them by speech or by a wandering mind, and the hostility that turns against the speaker.

5 Faithful English translation

O gods, whatever grave offence to the gods we have done against you with the tongue, or by inattention of the mind — [and] whatever hostile [thing], O Vāyu, behaves malevolently against us — that sin, O Vasus, set down in him. Svāhā.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Gods, whatever grave offence we have given you, by careless speech or a wandering mind, and whatever ill-will turns against us — Vāyu, Vasus, lay that wrong down elsewhere and do not hold it against us. Svāhā.

Key concepts देवता deity वायु breath, air मन्त्र mantra

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse belongs to a series of offerings near the end of the Upaniṣad. The speaker admits that, in dealing with the gods, he may have given offence: by careless words, or by a mind that wandered when it should have attended. He also names a hostility that turns against him, which the old commentator compares to a vicious dog. He asks the gods, the wind god Vāyu and the Vasus to set that wrong down and not hold it against him, and offers butter into the fire with the word svāhā. The commentator reads the request as: keep our fault in your own deep mind and do not expose it, that is, forgive it. The verse states a prayer for pardon; the edition makes no claim about what reciting it achieves.

An analogy Like asking a wronged friend to let the matter rest with them rather than bring it up again.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

The speakers admit an offence against the gods done by the tongue or by the mind’s inattention, and a malevolence turned against them, and ask the gods, Vāyu and the Vasus to set that sin down. Svāhā marks it as an offering.

Traditional Interpretation

How does the anonymous commentator read the request?

He makes the offence a mockery or belittling of the gods, यद्देवहेडनं देवविडम्बनम्, committed by speech, जिह्वया वाचा वयं चकृम, and reads the request as a plea for forbearance: तदेनो यूयं तस्मिन्पूर्वोक्ते गम्भीरे युष्मदीयमनसि निधेतन — keep that sin in your deep mind — अस्मदपराधं सहध्वमिति यावत् — that is, bear with our fault. He takes Vāyu as the chief of the Maruts, हे वायो सप्तमरुत्प्रधाना हे मरुत उपवायवो, and the hostility as behaving ‘like a vicious dog’, दुष्टशुनवदाचरति.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Do Sāyaṇa and Bhāskara comment on it?

No comment on this verse is found in the edition’s copies of Sāyaṇa’s or Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara’s commentaries, which follow 64-anuvāka recensions; the computed concordance assigns none. The anonymous bhāṣya is the only commentary here.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Why does the printed line read no abhi and tasmin tad?

Both are Vedic junctions kept as printed: no abhi leaves the a of abhi unelided, which keeps the third pāda at twelve syllables, and tasmin tad keeps the n with no inserted sibilant. They are declared as exceptions in the analysis rather than normalised.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • यद्वो yadvo → यत् + वः

    final t voiced before a voiced consonant

    yat + vaḥ → yad vaḥ; then vaḥ + d → vo.

  • देवाश्चकृम devāścakṛma → देवाः + चकृम

    visarga before c

    devāḥ + cakṛma → devāś cakṛma: the visarga becomes ś before c (P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः), with ś by assimilation).

  • मनसो वा manaso vā → मनसः + वा

    -aḥ before a voiced consonant

    manasaḥ + vā → manaso vā.

  • वायो नो अभि vāyo no abhi → वायो + नः + अभि

    -aḥ before a, unjoined (Vedic)

    naḥ + abhi → no abhi: the visarga goes to o, but the following a is not elided, as printed; the pāda keeps twelve syllables (see sandhi_exceptions).

  • तस्मिन्तदेनो tasmintadeno → तस्मिन् + तत् + एनः

    final n kept before t; t voiced before a vowel

    tasmin + tat → tasmin tat, the Taittirīya recitation’s usual form, with no sibilant inserted (classical tasmiṃs tat, P. 8.3.7 (नश्छव्यप्रशान्)); tat + enaḥ → tad enaḥ.

  • वसवो निधेतन vasavo nidhetana → वसवः + निधेतन

    -aḥ before a voiced consonant

    vasavaḥ + nidhetana → vasavo nidhetana.

Junctions outside the general rules

  • नः · अभि — vedic-abhinihita-not-applied: naḥ + abhi is printed no abhi: the visarga becomes o, but the a of abhi is not elided (classical no ’bhi). Counting abhi’s first syllable keeps the pāda at twelve syllables.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • देवहेडनम् devaheḍanam

    ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa · देव + हेडन

    Vigraha देवानां हेडनम्

    offence against the gods

Morphology

चकृम
Perfect 1st plural of √kṛ with the Vedic ending -ma: ‘we have done’.
प्रयुती
Instrumental singular of prayuti with the Vedic ending -ī for -yā. The anonymous commentator instead takes it as an adjective ‘countless’, from prayuta, a large number, and calls the long ī Vedic: दीर्घश्छान्दसः.
अरा
As printed, arā is a separate word, which the commentator explains as ara with a changed ending, ‘what brings death’ (his explanation is partly bracketed in the print). The same verse elsewhere is read with arāvā yo, ‘the hostile one who’; that text is not among the edition’s witnesses (see references).
दुच्छुनायते
Denominative from ducchunā, ‘misfortune, malice’ (literally ‘ill-luck’); the commentator hears śuna, ‘dog’, in it: दुष्टशुनवदाचरति.
निधेतन
Imperative 2nd plural of ni + √dhā with the Vedic ending -tana for -ta; the commentator names the suffix tanap: स्वार्थे तनप्.

Anvaya

[हे] देवाः यत् वः जिह्वया मनसः प्रयुती वा गुरु देवहेडनम् चकृम [यत् च] अरा नः अभि दुच्छुनायते हे वायो हे वसवः तत् एनः तस्मिन् निधेतन स्वाहा

he devāḥ yat vaḥ jihvayā manasaḥ prayutī vā guru devaheḍanam cakṛma yat ca arā naḥ abhi ducchunāyate he vāyo he vasavaḥ tat enaḥ tasmin nidhetana svāhā

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A relative clause (yat … cakṛma) with two instrumentals of the means joined by vā, followed by a second relative idea (… abhi ducchunāyate) whose subject the commentator takes as the sin itself; both are resumed by tad enaḥ, the object of the imperative nidhetana, addressed to the gods, Vāyu and the Vasus. Svāhā closes the offering.

Ambiguities

guru manasaḥ
Read as two words, guru ‘grave’ qualifies the offence and manasaḥ goes with prayutī, ‘by the mind’s inattention’. The anonymous commentator first joins them as one vocative, गुरु गम्भीरं मनो येषां ते गुरुमनसो — ‘you whose minds are deep’ — and then offers a second reading, agurumanasaḥ, ‘we whose minds are not devoted’, as a description of the speakers.
tasmin, ‘in him’ or ‘in that’
The commentator makes tasmin the gods’ own deep mind, in which the sin is to be kept and not spoken of: तस्मिन्पूर्वोक्ते गम्भीरे युष्मदीयमनसि निधेतन and स्थापयत, न तु वाचोद्धाट्यत — set it down in your deep mind, do not expose it in words. The verse alone does not say where.
arā
See morphology: the printed arā and the commentator’s explanation differ from the form of the line known from other texts.

Metre

jagatī · syllables counted as written: 24 + 26

A jagatī verse of four twelve-syllable pādas; as printed the lines count 24 and 26 syllables, the extra two being svāhā. The third pāda reaches twelve only with no abhi read unjoined, as printed.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Accountability

The verse separates two sources of fault — what is said carelessly and what slips by when attention wanders — and asks that the matter be set down rather than held. It is a model of naming a lapse without dramatising it. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Which costs you more: careless words, or moments when your attention was elsewhere?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry The commentator reads ‘set it down in him’ as ‘keep it in your own mind, do not expose it’. What kind of forgiveness does that describe?

A contemplation exercise Name one lapse of speech and one of attention from this week, plainly, and decide what you will do differently.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
