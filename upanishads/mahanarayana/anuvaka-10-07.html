Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 7 of 80

Pāhi no agna ekayā: protection by one, two, three and four

A counting prayer to Agni: protect us with one, a second, a third and four songs of praise.

5 Faithful English translation

Protect us, O Agni, with one; protect [us] also with the second; protect [our] strength with the third; protect [us] with four songs, O good one — svāhā.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Agni, protect us with one song of praise, and with a second; with a third protect our nourishment; with all four songs protect us, good one. Svāhā.

Key concepts अग्नि Agni, fire वेद Veda मन्त्र mantra

7 Explain it to a beginner

A short counting prayer to Agni, the fire: protect us with one, protect us with a second, protect our strength with a third, protect us with four songs of praise, good one. Only the last line says what is being counted, songs of praise. The anonymous commentator identifies the four with the four Vedas: the Ṛgveda, the Yajurveda, the Sāmaveda and the Atharvaveda, so that the god is protecting “us” as one praised by each Veda in turn and then by all. He says these are four offerings for the same purpose as the previous anuvāka, protection for one who seeks knowledge, and that svāhā belongs to each. The record reports that reading and claims nothing about its effect.

An analogy Like a child counting on its fingers, one, two, three, and then holding up the whole hand.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It asks Agni to protect us with one, with a second, to protect our strength with a third, and to protect us with four songs of praise, ending with svāhā.

Traditional Interpretation

What are the four?

The anonymous commentator: four offerings for the same purpose as the last anuvāka (पुनरपि पूर्वोक्तप्रयोजनकाहुतिचतुष्टयमन्त्रा उच्यन्ते), with Agni praised by the Ṛgveda (एकयर्ग्वेदलक्षणया गिराऽभिष्ठुतः सन्), the Yajurveda, the Sāmaveda, and by all four (चर्ग्यजुः सामाथर्वणरूपाभिश्चतसृभि[र्गीर्भिरभि]ष्टुतः). Svāhā goes with every line (स्वाहाशब्दः सर्वत्रानुषज्यते).

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What do the witnesses show?

In पाह्यूर्जं the Ānandāśrama text writes an extra anusvāra (पाह्यूंर्ज), and both it and the Tirupati e-text lack the anusvāra the base gives ūrjaṃ before tṛtīyayā; the sources page lists these for review. Neither Sāyaṇa’s nor Bhāskara’s copy used here comments on this anuvāka.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • अग्न एकया agna ekayā → अग्ने + एकया

    e before a vowel other than a

    agne before e becomes agna, the y dropped, a gap left.

  • पाह्युत pāhyuta → पाहि + उत

    i before a dissimilar vowel

    i becomes y before u: pāhy uta.

  • पाह्यूर्जं pāhyūrjaṃ → पाहि + ऊर्जम्

    i before a dissimilar vowel; m before a consonant

    pāhi + ūrjam → pāhy ūrjaṃ (the m becomes anusvāra before t).

  • गीर्भिश्चतसृभिर्वसो gīrbhiścatasṛbhirvaso → गीर्भिः + चतसृभिः + वसो

    visarga before c; visarga after i before a voiced consonant

    gīrbhiḥ before c becomes gīrbhiś; catasṛbhiḥ before v becomes catasṛbhir.

Junctions outside the general rules

  • नः · अग्ने — unjoined hiatus (as printed): no · agne: the a of agne is not elided after no, as in 10.6; every witness prints the two words apart.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this anuvāka.

Morphology

नः
Vedic: before a, naḥ is written no and the a of agne is not elided (classical no ’gne); the record declares the gap as a sandhi exception.
एकया
Feminine instrumentals of the ordinals, agreeing with an understood gir (song, word), which the last line supplies as gīrbhiḥ.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

Four imperative clauses with pāhi; the instruments are feminine ordinals (ekayā, dvitīyayā, tṛtīyayā) and finally gīrbhiś catasṛbhiḥ, which supplies the noun the others leave understood.

Ambiguities

one, two, three, four what?
The text supplies gīrbhiḥ, songs, only in the last line. The anonymous commentator identifies them with the Ṛg-, Yajur-, Sāma- and Atharvaveda; the text itself does not name the Vedas.

Metre

gāyatrī · syllables counted as written: 8 + 7 + 7 + 12

Counted 8, 7, 7 and 12 syllables: a gāyatrī-like triplet whose second and third pādas are one syllable short (pāhy uta dvitīyayā; pāhy ūrjaṃ tṛtīyayā), and a closing line of 10 + svāhā.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Many ways of saying one thing

The prayer asks the same thing four times, each time with one more voice, until all four speak together. Saying something important in more than one way, and then together, is how agreement is built. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Why might a prayer count up — one, two, three, four — instead of asking once?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry The text names only “songs”; the commentator names the four Vedas. What does the identification add, and what does it assume?

A contemplation exercise Say one thing you value in four different ways today: in words, in an action, in a gift of time, in silence.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
