Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 69 of 80

Śraddhāyāṃ prāṇe niviṣṭaḥ: the meal as an offering to the five breaths

The prāṇāgnihotra: the first mouthfuls of a meal offered, in faith, into the five breaths; the water sipped before and after the meal as the spread beneath and the cover over the ‘immortal’ food.

5 Faithful English translation

In faith, settled in prāṇa, I offer the immortal. In faith, settled in apāna, I offer the immortal. In faith, settled in vyāna, I offer the immortal. In faith, settled in udāna, I offer the immortal. 6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

In faith, entering into the out-breath, I offer the nectar — and so into the down-breath, the diffused breath, the up-breath and the breath that equalises. My self is in Brahman, for immortality. [To the water before the meal:] you are the spread beneath the nectar. [Or, offering each mouthful:] in faith, entering into the out-breath, I offer the nectar; come into me gently, so as not to burn; to the out-breath, svāhā — and so for the other four. My self is in Brahman, for immortality. [To the water after the meal:] you are the covering over the nectar.

Key concepts प्राण breath, life-force श्रद्धा faith, trust अमृत immortality, the deathless अन्न food ब्रह्मन् Brahman आत्मन् self, Self

7 Explain it to a beginner

This anuvāka treats a meal as a fire offering made inside the body; the tradition calls it the prāṇāgnihotra. Before eating, a little water is sipped with the words ‘you are the spread beneath the nectar’. Then the first five mouthfuls are offered, one to each of the five vital breaths: ‘In faith, entering into the out-breath, I offer the nectar’, and so for the other four. A second set of words asks the food to enter gently, so as not to burn. Between them comes ‘my self is in Brahman, for immortality’. After the meal, water is sipped again: ‘you are the covering over the nectar’. The commentators explain the ‘nectar’ as the food itself. The edition reports the rite as they describe it and makes no claim about its effects.

An analogy Like laying a cloth on a table before the food is served and covering the dish when the meal is done: the water before and after frames the meal as something cared for.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

Five times: in faith, settled in a breath, I offer the immortal. Then: my self in Brahman, for immortality; you are the under-spread of the immortal. Then the five again, each with ‘kindly enter me, for not burning’ and svāhā to the breath; again the line on Brahman; and: you are the covering of the immortal.

Traditional Interpretation

What is the rite?

Bhāskara: अथ प्राणाग्निहोत्रमन्त्रा आत्मयाजिनाम् — now the mantras of the fire offering into the breath, for those who sacrifice in the self. The anonymous commentator: the mantras of the offerings to the breaths, and the mantra for sipping water at the start of the meal; Sāyaṇa likewise (अथ भोजनादावपां प्राशने मन्त्रमाह, जलप्राशनादूर्ध्वं प्राणाहुतिमन्त्रान्दर्शयति).

Comparative Vedānta

What is the ‘immortal’ that is offered?

The anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa: the food, a sweet oblation (स्वादुभूतमिदं हविर्जुहोमि प्रक्षिपामि). Bhāskara: अमृतम् अमरणहेतुम् अमृतात्मकब्रह्मस्वरूपं वा इदमन्नं जुहोम्य् आत्मज्योतिषि प्रक्षिपामि — I offer this food, the cause of not dying, or of the nature of Brahman, which is immortal; I cast it into the light within.

Traditional Interpretation

What are the five breaths?

Sāyaṇa and the anonymous commentator call them the kinds of wind within the body (पञ्चसु शरीरगतवायुभेदेषु). Bhāskara describes them: prāṇa moves upward and leaves by mouth and nose (प्राण उर्ध्ववृत्तिर् मुखनासिकनिस्सरणः); vyāna is diffused (व्यानः विष्वग्वृत्तिः); udāna makes sound (उदानः शब्दकारी); apāna moves downward; samāna evens out what is eaten and drunk.

Comparative Vedānta

My self in Brahman?

The anonymous commentator: अमृतत्वाय मोक्षाय मे मदीय आत्मा जीवो ब्रह्मणि परमात्मन्येको भवत्विति शेषः — for immortality, that is liberation, may my self, the individual, become one with Brahman, the supreme self (‘may become one’ is supplied). Bhāskara: इदानीमनेनान्नहोमेन मे आत्मा शरीरं ब्रह्मण्य् एव स्थितम् — now, by this offering of food, my body stands in Brahman itself; or Brahman is the food, on which the body depends (ब्रह्मण्य् अन्ने मम शरीरं स्थितं तदधीनस्थितिकत्वात्).

Traditional Interpretation

Spread beneath, covering over?

The water sipped before the meal is the spread beneath, as a cloth is spread on a couch for one lying down; the water after is the covering (पीयमान हे जल त्वममृतमविनश्वरमपिधानमाच्छादकमसि, Sāyaṇa). The anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa cite the Vājasaneyins, who in the teaching on the breath make water the breath’s clothing (तथा च वाजसनेयिनः प्राणविद्यायां प्राणदेवताया जलवस्त्रत्वमामनन्ति). Bhāskara: उपस्तरणम् आस्तरणं यत्र अमृतम् अन्नं ब्रह्म सीदतीति — the spread on which the immortal, the food, Brahman, sits; and the covering: अपिधानं छादनं येनामृतं पिधीयते तत्त्वमसि हे उदक.

Traditional Interpretation

Why ‘not to burn’?

Sāyaṇa: अप्रदाहाय क्षुत्संपादितदाहशान्त्यर्थम् — to quell the burning that hunger produces. Bhāskara: इतरथा जाठरो वह्निः शरीरं दहेत् — otherwise the fire of the stomach would burn the body. These are the commentators’ explanations of the words.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • निविष्टोऽमृतं niviṣṭo'mṛtaṃ → निविष्टः + अमृतम्

    visarga after a before a

    niviṣṭaḥ + amṛtam → niviṣṭo ’mṛtam: -aḥ becomes -o and the a that follows is elided (avagraha).

  • श्रद्धायामपाने śraddhāyāmapāne → श्रद्धायाम् + अपाने

    final m before a vowel

    śraddhāyām + apāne: the m is joined to the vowel.

  • ब्रह्मणि म आत्माऽमृतत्वाय brahmaṇi ma ātmā'mṛtatvāya → ब्रह्मणि + मे + आत्मा + अमृतत्वाय

    e before a vowel; ā + a

    me + ātmā → ma ātmā: e becomes a before a vowel and the two stay apart; ātmā + amṛtatvāya → ātmā ’mṛtatvāya, ā and a fusing into ā.

  • अमृतोपस्तरणमसि amṛtopastaraṇamasi → अमृतोपस्तरणम् + असि

    a + u inside the compound; final m before a vowel

    amṛta + upastaraṇam → amṛtopastaraṇam; the m is joined to asi.

  • शिवो मा विशाप्रदाहाय śivo mā viśāpradāhāya → शिवः + मा + विश + अप्रदाहाय

    visarga before a voiced consonant; a + a

    śivaḥ + mā → śivo mā; viśa + apradāhāya → viśāpradāhāya.

  • अमृतापिधानमसि amṛtāpidhānamasi → अमृतापिधानम् + असि

    a + a inside the compound; final m before a vowel

    amṛta + apidhānam → amṛtāpidhānam; the m is joined to asi.

  • अमृतोपस्तरणम् amṛtopastaraṇam

  • अमृतत्वाय amṛtatvāya

    taddhita (abstract suffix -tva) · अमृत + त्व

    Vigraha अमृतस्य भावः

    for the state of being immortal

Morphology

निविष्टः
Past participle of √viś with ni-, ‘entered, settled’. Bhāskara reads it two ways: intent on the breath, striving to keep it; or dwelling in the breath, since one’s life depends on it (see commentary).
मा
Enclitic accusative of the first person, ‘me’ (= mām); not the prohibitive particle. Sāyaṇa and the anonymous commentator paraphrase मां प्रविश, Bhāskara माम् आविश.
मे
Enclitic genitive of the first person; before आत्मा it is written म (e → a before a vowel, the two left unjoined).
अप्रदाहाय
Dative of purpose of a-pradāha, ‘not burning’. Sāyaṇa: क्षुत्संपादितदाहशान्त्यर्थम् — to quell the burning that hunger produces; Bhāskara: otherwise the fire of the stomach would burn the body.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

The offering formula has one shape: a locative of faith, a locative of the breath, the participle niviṣṭaḥ agreeing with the unexpressed ‘I’, the object amṛtam and the verb juhomi. brahmaṇi ma ātmā ’mṛtatvāya is nominal, with ‘is’ (or ‘be’) understood. The two water formulas are second-person nominal sentences with asi. The alternative set adds an imperative (śivo mā viśa …) and a dative with svāhā.

Ambiguities

brahmaṇi ma ātmā
The anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa supply ‘be one’: by these five offerings may my self, the individual self, become one with the supreme self, for liberation. Bhāskara offers three readings: by this offering of food my body (ātmā) stands in Brahman and that is for my immortality; or, may my self stand in Brahman; or, Brahman is the food, on which my body depends.
who is addressed in śivo mā viśa
Sāyaṇa and the anonymous commentator: the substance being offered (हूयमान … द्रव्यविशेष); Bhāskara: the food (हे अन्न).
one set or two
The base gives the five offering formulas twice: first alone, then each with śivo mā viśāpradāhāya and a svāhā to the breath. Sāyaṇa calls the second set alternatives (प्राणाहुतिष्वेव विकल्पितानि मन्त्रान्तराणि दर्शयति), and Bhāskara says the same (पूर्वेण विकल्प्यते).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Attention

The passage turns the start of an ordinary meal into a deliberate act: a sip of water, a few mouthfuls taken with words, a sip of water at the end. Whatever one believes about the breaths, beginning and ending a meal on purpose, rather than eating while doing something else, is a choice anyone can make. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

  • review The edition’s computed map gives no Bhāskara anuvāka for 10.69; by content his comments are bhaskara:32–35, cited explicitly. Sāyaṇa 32–35 are right.
  • review Order: the base gives the five offerings before amṛtopastaraṇam asi; in the texts of Sāyaṇa and Bhāskara the sipping formula comes first, and Sāyaṇa says the offerings follow the sipping (जलप्राशनादूर्ध्वं). The edition keeps the base order.
  • interpretation मा in शिवो मा विश is read as the enclitic ‘me’, as all three commentators paraphrase it; the base spaces मा विश.
