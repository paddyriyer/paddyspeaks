Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 17 of 80

Sadyojātaṃ prapadyāmi: the first of the five ‘brahma’ mantras

Refuge in Sadyojāta, the first of the five faces of Śiva, with the plea to be led beyond birth after birth.

5 Faithful English translation

I go [for refuge] to Sadyojāta; to Sadyojāta truly homage, homage. In birth after birth — not [so]; in what is beyond birth bring me. To the one who lifts [us] out of becoming, homage.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

I take refuge in Sadyojāta; homage, homage to Sadyojāta. Do not keep me in birth after birth; lead me beyond birth. Homage to the one who raises us out of becoming.

Key concepts उपासना worship, meditation संसार saṃsāra, the round of births मोक्ष liberation मन्त्र mantra

7 Explain it to a beginner

This short mantra is the first of five that address five forms, or ‘faces’, of Śiva, each looking in a different direction; together they are called the five ‘brahma’ mantras. This one names Sadyojāta, ‘the one born at once’, whom the old commentators identify with the western face. The speaker says ‘I take refuge’ and bows twice. Then comes the request: not to be kept going from one birth to the next, but to be led beyond birth altogether. The last line bows to Śiva as the one who lifts people out of ‘becoming’ — the cycle of being born again. The commentators read it as a prayer for the knowledge that ends that cycle. The same five mantras open many forms of Śiva worship.

An analogy Like asking a ferryman not for another lap around the lake but to take you across to the far shore.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

The speaker takes refuge in Sadyojāta and offers homage, asks not to be kept in birth after birth but to be brought beyond it, and bows to the one who lifts out of becoming.

Traditional Interpretation

What is Sadyojāta?

The anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa place the mantra among the five that describe the five faces of Mahādeva, and this one as the western face. Sāyaṇa: मेधाविनः पुरुषस्य ज्ञानोत्पादनाय महादेवसंबन्धिषु पञ्चवक्त्रेषु मध्ये पश्चिमवक्त्रप्रतिपादकं मन्त्रमाह. Bhāskara explains the name: the one who appears in the very moment the god wishes to create, the cause of the world’s unfolding: सद्योजातं यत्क्षणे सिसृक्षा देवस्य तदानीमेवाविर्भूतं प्रपञ्चोन्मेषहेतुं देवं त्वां प्रपद्यामि.

Traditional Interpretation

What is asked for?

All three read the request as release from rebirth. Sāyaṇa: तत्तज्जन्मनिमित्तं मां न भजस्व न प्रेरयेत्यर्थः, and then अतिभवे जन्मातिलङ्घननिमित्तं भजस्व तत्त्वज्ञानाय प्रेरय — impel me toward the knowledge of reality, for passing beyond birth. The anonymous commentator gives the same explanation on the reading bhavasva.

Traditional Interpretation

Who is this for?

The commentators differ on the person meant. Sāyaṇa: for the person of intelligence, मेधाविनः पुरुषस्य ज्ञानोत्पादनाय. The anonymous commentator says it is for the three twice-born classes entitled to the śāstra, त्रैवर्णिकानां शास्त्राधिकारिणां ज्ञानोत्पादनाय; that restriction is his, and the text itself names no one.

Traditional Interpretation

What are the five ‘brahma’ mantras?

Bhāskara introduces this anuvāka and the next four together: अथ पञ्चब्रह्माख्याः पञ्चानुवाका ईश्वराराधनादिषु उपासनादिषु च विनियुक्ताः — five anuvākas called the five brahmas, used in the worship of the Lord and in meditation. He calls them the five faces of the Lord whose body is all knowledge: सर्वविद्यादेहस्य सद्योजातादिनामानि पञ्चविद्यास्त्रोतःप्रवर्तकतदीयपञ्चमुखभूतानि पञ्चब्रह्माणि.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

How is the text written?

Two differences from the commentators’ texts: bhavasva for bhajasva, and namo namaḥ where Sāyaṇa’s text has a single namaḥ. The Ānandāśrama print of the 80-anuvāka text sets this mantra inside its commentary rather than as a separate line; the sources page lists it as a place to verify.

Where they disagree

No Vedānta-school commentary on this text is in the edition’s sources (Raṅgarāmānuja’s commentary on the Mahānārāyaṇa is not available here). The three commentaries present — the anonymous bhāṣya, Sāyaṇa and Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara — read the mantra as worship of Śiva’s five faces and a prayer for release; they differ on whom it is for and on the reading bhavasva or bhajasva, not on doctrine.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • सद्योजातं प्रपद्यामि sadyojātaṃ prapadyāmi → सद्योजातम् + प्रपद्यामि

    final m before a consonant

    sadyojātam + prapadyāmi → sadyojātaṃ prapadyāmi: m becomes anusvāra (P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः)).

  • नमो नमः namo namaḥ → नमः + नमः

    visarga after a before a voiced consonant

    namaḥ + namaḥ → namo namaḥ: -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced sound.

  • नातिभवे nātibhave → न + अतिभवे

    a + a

    na + atibhave → nātibhave: like vowels merge long (P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः)).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • सद्योजातम् sadyojātam

    tatpuruṣa (adverbial first member) · सद्यस् + जात

    Vigraha सद्यः जातः

    born at once, on the instant

  • भवोद्भवाय bhavodbhavāya

    tatpuruṣa · भव + उद्भव

    Vigraha भवात् उद्भवः (उद्धर्ता) यः

    the one who raises [one] out of becoming

  • अतिभवे atibhave

    prādi (avyayībhāva-like) · अति + भव

    Vigraha भवम् अतिक्रान्तः

    what has passed beyond becoming

Morphology

भवेभवे
An āmreḍita: the locative repeated means ‘in each birth’; written as one word, as Vedic repetitions are.
भवस्व
As printed, a middle imperative of √bhū, which is rare. Sāyaṇa’s and Bhāskara’s texts read bhajasva (√bhaj, ‘resort to, assign’); see flags.
सद्योजातम्
The first member is the adverb sadyas, ‘at once’; its final s becomes o before the voiced j.

Anvaya

सद्योजातम् प्रपद्यामि । सद्योजाताय वै नमः नमः । भवेभवे [माम्] न [भवस्व], अतिभवे माम् भवस्व । भवोद्भवाय नमः ।

sadyojātam prapadyāmi | sadyojātāya vai namaḥ namaḥ | bhavebhave mām na bhavasva, atibhave mām bhavasva | bhavodbhavāya namaḥ |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A first-person declaration (prapadyāmi), then a nominal sentence of homage with the dative (sadyojātāya … namaḥ), then a request in the imperative (bhavasva mām) set between two locatives, bhave bhave and atibhave, with na. The commentators construe na with the first locative — not in birth after birth, but beyond birth — supplying the verb twice. The last line repeats the homage with a new epithet.

Ambiguities

where na belongs
Read as written, na stands before atibhave. All three commentators move it to bhave bhave: ‘do not [bring] me to birth after birth, [bring] me to what is beyond birth’. The anonymous commentator: भवे भवे तत्तज्जन्मनिर्मितं मां न भवस्व न प्रेरयेत्यर्थः.
bhavasva or bhajasva
The base reads bhavasva and the anonymous commentator explains that reading; Sāyaṇa and Bhāskara comment on bhajasva (‘resort to me’, ‘take me up’). The sense of the request is the same.

Metre

mixed · syllables counted as written: 18 + 12 + 7

Bhāskara: सद्योजातम् इति परोष्णिक् त्रिपदा मामन्ता — three pādas up to mām, in the paroṣṇik metre, and the rest a yajus. As printed the lines count 18, 12 and 7 syllables; with the single namaḥ of Sāyaṇa’s text the first line would have 16.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Endings and beginnings

The request is not for a better turn of the wheel but for leaving the wheel. Many people know the difference between wanting more of the same and wanting to be done with a pattern altogether. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.
