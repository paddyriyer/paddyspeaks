Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 43 of 80

Ā māṃ medhā: may understanding come

A further verse asking Medhā, described as golden, many-formed and nourishing like a cow full of milk, to come to the speaker and favour him.

5 Faithful English translation

Hither to me [let] Medhā [come] — fragrant, of every form, golden-coloured, the world, to be sought; full of strength, swelling with milk — may she, Medhā, fair of face, favour me.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

May Medhā come to me — fragrant, taking every form, golden, present everywhere, the goal of all who seek; strong and nourishing like a cow full of milk. May that understanding, gracious of face, favour me.

Key concepts मेधा medhā, retentive intelligence देवता deity मन्त्र mantra

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse continues the prayer for medhā, understanding and memory. It pictures her as a goddess: fragrant, able to take every form, golden in colour, present everywhere in the world, the goal of everyone who seeks something worth having. She is strong, and she nourishes the way a cow full of milk nourishes those who depend on her. The verse asks that this understanding, with a gracious face, come and favour the one who prays. The commentators explain her many forms as her ability to hold every branch of learning. Read together with the verses before it, it shows how highly this text values the power to learn and to keep what has been learned.

An analogy Like describing a beloved teacher by all the good things they give, before asking them to stay.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It asks Medhā — fragrant, many-formed, golden, the world, to be sought, strong, swelling with milk, fair of face — to come to the speaker and favour him.

Traditional Interpretation

How do the commentators read the epithets?

The anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa: many-formed because she can hold every science, golden in the bodies of the deities, present everywhere as the world, to be eagerly sought by those who desire the aims of life, gladdening us with milk and its juices: सा तथाविधगुणयुक्ता मेधा सुप्रतीका सुमुखी भूत्वा मां जुषतां सेवताम् (Sāyaṇa).

Plural in form with a singular subject (medhā); the anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa gloss the singular juṣatām.
आ
The preverb stands first with its verb unexpressed; the commentators supply ‘may she come’.

Syntax

One verse: the preverb ā with its verb supplied and a string of epithets of medhā in the nominative, resumed by sā … medhā with the imperative juṣantām.

Ambiguities

jagatī
The anonymous commentator’s text reads जहती in the gloss where Sāyaṇa reads जगती सर्वत्र वर्तमानत्वेन जगदात्मिका — ‘the world, being present everywhere’; the verse’s jagatī may also name the metre, as other goddesses are hailed as metres.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Understanding as nourishment

The verse pictures understanding as food that sustains, not as a possession. Learning that keeps feeding later thought is different from facts kept for their own sake. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate
