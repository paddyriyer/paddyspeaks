Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 12 of 80

Aṇor aṇīyān: the self in the cave of the heart

The self hidden in the heart, source of the senses, worlds and waters; the unborn one and the two unborn; the four-horned bull; Rudra and the golden germ; immortality by renunciation; the small space in the lotus of the heart; the Lord beyond the syllable.

अणोरणीयान्महतो महीयानात्मा गुहायां निहितोऽस्य जन्तोः ।तमक्रतुं पश्यति वीतशोको धातुः प्रसादान्महिमानमीशम् ।सप्त प्राणाः प्रभवन्ति तस्मात्सप्तार्चिषस्समिधस्सप्त जिह्वाः ।सप्त इमे लोका येषु चरन्ति प्राणा गुहाशयां निहितास्सप्तसप्त ।अतस्समुद्रा गिरयश्च सर्वेऽस्मात्स्यन्दन्ते सिन्धवस्सर्वरूपाः ।अतश्च विश्वा ओषधयो रसाच्च येनैष भूतस्तिष्ठत्यन्तरात्मा ।ब्रह्मा देवानां पदवीः कवीनामृषिर्विप्राणां महिषो मृगाणाम् ।श्येनो गृद्ध्राणाग् स्वधितिर्वनानां सोमः पवित्रमत्येति रेभन् ।अजामेकां लोहितशुक्लकृष्णां बह्वीं प्रजां जनयन्तीं सरूपाम् ।अजो ह्येको जुषमाणोऽनुशेते जहात्येनां भुक्तभोगामजोऽन्यः ॥ २६ ॥

हंसः शुचिषद्वसुरन्तरिक्षसद्धोता वेदिषदतिथिर्दुरोणसत् ।नृषद्वरसदृतसद्व्योमसदब्जा गोजा ऋतजा अद्रिजा ऋतं बृहत् ।घृतं मिमिक्षिरे घृतमस्य योनिर्घृते श्रितो घृतमुवस्य धाम ।अनुष्वधमावह मादयस्व स्वाहाकृतं वृषभ वक्षि हव्यम् ।समुद्रादूर्मिर्मधुमां उदारदुपांशुना सममृतत्वमानट् ।घृतस्य नाम गुह्यं यदस्ति जिह्वा देवानाममृतस्य नाभिः ।वयं नाम प्रब्रवामा घृतेनास्मिन्, यज्ञे धारयामा नमोभिः ।उप ब्रह्मा शृणवच्छस्यमानं चतुः शृङ्गोऽवमीद्गौर एतत् ।चत्वारि शृङ्गा त्रयो अस्य पादा द्वे शीर्षे सप्त हस्तासो अस्य ।त्रिधा बद्धो वृषभो रोरवीति महो देवो मर्त्यां आविवेश ॥ २७ ॥

त्रिधा हितं पणिभिर्गुह्यमानं गवि देवासो घृतमन्वविन्दन् ।इन्द्र एकं सूर्य एकं जजान वेनादेकग्ग् स्वधया निष्टतक्षुः ।यो देवानां प्रथमं पुरस्ताद्विश्वाधियो रुद्रो महर्षिः ।हिरण्यगर्भं पश्यत जायमानं स नो देवः शुभया स्मृत्यास्संयुनक्तु ।यस्मात्परं नापरमस्ति किञ्चिद्यस्मान्नाणीयो न ज्यायोऽस्ति कश्चित् ।वृक्ष इव स्तब्धो दिवि तिष्ठत्येकस्तेनेदं पूर्णं पुरुषेण सर्वम् ।न कर्मणा न प्रजया धनेन त्यागेनैके अमृतत्वमानशुः ।परेण नाकं निहितं गुहायां विभ्राजदेतद्यतयो विशन्ति ।वेदान्तविज्ञानसुनिश्चितार्थाः सन्न्यासयोगाद्यतयः शुद्धसत्त्वाः ।ते ब्रह्मलोके तु परान्तकाले परामृतात्परिमुच्यन्ति सर्वे ।दह्रं विपापं परमेश्मभूतं यत्पुण्डरीकं पुरमध्यसंस्थम् ।तत्रापि दह्रं गगनं विशोकस्तस्मिन्, यदन्तस्तदुपासितव्यम् ।यो वेदादौ स्वरः प्रोक्तो वेदान्ते च प्रतिष्ठितः ।तस्य प्रकृतिलीनस्य यः परस्स महेश्वरः ॥ २८ ॥

7 Explain it to a beginner

This anuvāka gathers some of the best-known verses about the self. The self is smaller than the smallest thing and larger than the largest, and it sits hidden in the heart. Whoever sees it, by the grace of the Lord, is free of grief. Everything comes from it: the senses, the worlds, the oceans, the rivers, the plants. A riddle follows about one unborn mother of three colours and two unborn ones: one clings to her, the other, having had enough, lets her go. Then a hymn on ghee and a mysterious four-horned bull, which the commentators read either as the syllable om or as the sacrifice. The text then says that immortality is reached not by ritual, children or wealth but by renunciation, and it points to a small space inside the lotus of the heart as the place for meditation. It ends with the Lord who lies beyond the syllable om.

An analogy Like a house with a small inner room: the house is the body, the room is the lotus of the heart, and the space inside that room, small only because of the walls around it, is the same space that fills the sky.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It describes the self hidden in the heart and seen by grace; everything arises from it; the unborn one of three colours and the two unborn; the swan who dwells everywhere; a hymn on ghee and the four-horned bull; Rudra who saw Hiraṇyagarbha born; the One than whom nothing is greater or smaller; immortality through renunciation; the small space in the heart-lotus to be meditated on; and the Lord beyond the syllable that begins and ends the Veda.

Traditional Interpretation

Why does the anuvāka come here?

Sāyaṇa: the previous anuvākas praised meritorious action and blamed forbidden action; this one teaches the truth to a person whose mind has been purified by them (शुद्धान्तःकरणस्य पुरुषस्य तत्त्वमुपदेष्टुमयमनुवाक आरभ्यते). Bhāskara: this and the next anuvāka describe the supreme self and are used in its worship (परमात्मोपवर्णनत्वान् महापुरुषा राधनायां विनियुक्तौ).

Traditional Interpretation

How is the self in the cave explained?

Sāyaṇa: the cave is the intellect, enclosed by the heart-lotus; to be ‘set’ there means to be known there, not to be contained (विद्ययोपलभ्यमानत्वं नतु बुद्धावाधेयत्वम्), since what supports the whole world cannot be held. The fitness to see him comes from the Lord’s grace (धाता जगतो विधाता परमेश्वरः, तस्य प्रसादोऽनुग्रहः). Bhāskara compares the space within the heart to the space outside (यावान् वा अयमाकाशस्तावानेषोऽन्तर्हृदय आकाशः).

Traditional Interpretation

Who are the unborn female and the two unborn?

Sāyaṇa: the unborn female is māyā in the form of root nature (न जायत इत्यजा मूलप्रकृतिरूपा माया); her colours are fire, water and earth, or rajas, sattva and tamas. Of the two unborn selves, the attached one cherishes her and goes round in births and deaths; the dispassionate one sets her aside by discerning the truth (तां मायां तत्त्वविवेकेन बाधत इत्यर्थः). Bhāskara reads her as prakṛti, the three guṇas in balance (लोहितं रजः शुक्लं सत्त्वं कृष्णं तम), and the one who leaves her as the freed self.

Traditional Interpretation

What is the swan verse doing here?

Sāyaṇa: it lists the world as the sun, wind, fire, guest and the rest, to show that for one who has given up māyā the whole world appears as brahman (अज्ञानिदृष्ट्या जगद्रूपेण भासमानं सर्वं ज्ञानिदृष्ट्या ब्रह्मैवैत्यर्थः). Bhāskara says the one supreme self exists in many forms by its different works (कृत्यभेदेन परमात्मैव बहुधा वर्तत इति योज्यम्).

Traditional Interpretation

What are the ghee and the four-horned bull?

Sāyaṇa and the anonymous commentator: ghee, from the root ‘shine’, is self-luminous brahman (घृतं दीप्तं स्वप्रकाशं ब्रह्मेत्यर्थः), its secret name is om, and the four-horned bull is the praṇava with a, u, m and the resonance as horns. Bhāskara reads the verses of the sacrifice: the four horns are the four priests, the three feet the three fires, and the bull roars at the three pressings (त्रिषु सबनेष्व् ऋम्भिर्यजुभिस्सामभिः प्रयुज्यमानैर्महान्तं शब्दं करोति).

Traditional Interpretation

Not by action, by offspring or by wealth — what then?

Sāyaṇa: by giving up all worldly and Vedic activity, some inward-turned people attain immortality (केचिदेवान्तर्मुखा अमृतत्वमानशुः). Bhāskara disagrees about what is given up: obligatory rites cannot be abandoned, so it is the fruit of action that is renounced (तस्मात् फलत्यागिनोऽमृतत्वमश्नुवते), and he cites the Gītā: यस्तु कर्मफलत्यागी स त्यागीत्यभिधीयते.

Traditional Interpretation

How do knowledge and renunciation fit together?

Sāyaṇa raises the objection that elsewhere knowledge alone is said to free, and answers that each has its own use: knowledge removes ignorance by revealing the truth; renunciation purifies the mind by turning it from objects (ज्ञानं तत्त्वप्रकाशनेनाविद्यानिवर्तकं त्यागस्तु विषयभोगवृत्तिद्वारा चित्तशुद्धिहेतुरिति पृथगुपयोग उक्तो भवति). He takes yoga as the stilling of the mind’s activity and cites Patañjali (योगश्चित्तवृत्तनिरोधः). Bhāskara: sannyāsa is offering the fruits of action to the Lord, and yoga is samādhi (ब्रह्महुताशने आत्महविःप्रक्षेपलक्षणः समाधिसञ्ज्ञो योगः).

Traditional Interpretation

What is the small space in the heart?

Sāyaṇa: the meditation is for one not yet able to know the truth directly (तत्त्वं ज्ञातुमसमर्थस्यात्रोपासनमुच्यते). The heart is an eight-petalled lotus; the space within it is brahman, called small only because of its place, like the space in a pot; he cites the Chāndogya and the dahara section of the Brahmasūtra (दहराकाशस्य च ब्रह्मत्वं दहराधिकरणे निर्णीतम्). Bhāskara: the body is a city, the heart its palace, the space within its innermost room, and the supreme brahman within that is the king to be worshipped.

Traditional Interpretation

What is the sound at the beginning and end of the Veda?

Sāyaṇa: om, which opens the Vedas and is taught in the Upaniṣads; in meditation a is dissolved into u, u into m, and what lies beyond, meditated on as the fourth measure in the resonance, is the great Lord (सोऽयं महेश्वरो विज्ञेयः). Bhāskara: what is beyond the three measures, the half-measure, is the meaning to be worshipped (यः परो ऽर्धमात्राकालः स प्रतिपाद्योऽर्थ उपास्यः).

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Which readings differ?

The commentators read some words differently from the base: वरवेश्मभूतं (Sāyaṇa) and परवेश्मभूतं (Bhāskara) for परमेश्मभूतं; विश्वाधिक (both) for विश्वाधियः; स्मृत्या for स्मृत्यास्; विभ्राजते (Sāyaṇa) for विभ्राजत्. Sāyaṇa’s anuvāka also has verses not in this base between the swan verse and the ghee verse (yasmāj jātā, vidhartāram, madhu vātā), commented on as his seventh to thirteenth verses.

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

Both read the anuvāka as Advaita. Seeing the Lord depends on his grace, and they quote an earlier teacher: ईश्वरानुग्रहादेषां पुंसामद्वैतवासना. Morphology

10.12.26 गुहाशयाम्
Printed गुहाशयां. The Muṇḍaka parallel reads गुहाशया (nominative plural, with the ‘seven-by-seven’). Bhāskara explains a nominative here with a Vedic ending (‘सुपां सुलुक्’ इति जस आदेशः), and offers ‘set in the cave so as to lie there’ (गुहायां शयनमभिलक्ष्य निहिता); Sāyaṇa reads guhāśaya as the Lord who lies in the intellect (गुहाशयः परमेश्वरस्तस्मादुत्पन्नां).
10.12.26 रसात्
Printed रसाच्च. Sāyaṇa and Bhāskara gloss the plural rasāḥ, ‘and the saps’ (रसाश्च मधुरादयो); the Muṇḍaka parallel has रसश्च. The printed form is kept.
10.12.26 पदवीः
A Vedic -ī stem with nominative -īḥ (as in rathīḥ). Sāyaṇa derives it: पदं तद्वेति गच्छतीति पदवीः.
10.12.26 अजाम्
ajā means ‘unborn’ and also ‘she-goat’; aja ‘unborn’ and ‘he-goat’. Both commentators take the first sense.
10.12.27 उव्
The Vedic particle u (‘indeed’), written uv before a vowel in the Taittirīya texts. Sāyaṇa paraphrases with eva (घृतमेवास्याग्नेर्धाम); the anonymous commentator repeats घृतमु घृतमेवास्याग्नेर्धाम.
10.12.27 प्रब्रवामा
Subjunctive 1st plural prabravāma, with the long final ā of the saṃhitā; so dhārayāmā. Bhāskara: सांहितिकम् आख्यातस्य दीर्घत्वं छान्दसम्.
10.12.27 वक्षि
Second singular of √vah without the tense sign. Bhāskara: वहेर्लटि शपो लुक्.
10.12.27 आनट्
Bhāskara derives it either from √aś with an exchanged ending, or from √naś ‘reach’ with the aorist sign dropped (नशेर्गतिकर्मणो लङि ‘मन्त्रे घस’ इति च्लेर्लुक्).
10.12.28 परमेश्मभूतम्
As printed. The anonymous commentator divides para + meśma and says m stands for v by Vedic licence (वकारस्थाने मकारश्छान्दसः), so ‘become the house of the highest’; Sāyaṇa reads वरवेश्मभूतं, Bhāskara परवेश्मभूतं.
10.12.28 विशोकः
Masculine as printed, though gaganam is neuter. Sāyaṇa glosses the neuter विशोकं शोकरहितं गगनशब्दवाच्यं ब्रह्म; Bhāskara takes viśokaḥ as the supreme self within the space (विशोकः परमात्मेति यावद्).
10.12.28 परिमुच्यन्ति
An active ending on the passive stem (classical parimucyante); Bhāskara glosses सर्वतो मुच्यन्ते.
10.12.28 पश्यत
Imperfect without the augment, Vedic; Bhāskara: पश्यत अपश्यद्. Sāyaṇa glosses the present पश्यत साक्षात्करोति.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

Three units of verse. 10.12.26 states the self in the cave (aṇor aṇīyān … ātmā guhāyāṃ nihitaḥ), names what arises from it with tasmāt, atas, asmāt, lists what it is among each kind (brahmā devānām …), and ends on the ajā verse. 10.12.27 begins with a string of epithets in apposition (haṃsaḥ śuciṣad …) and moves into an Agni hymn on ghee (imperatives āvaha, mādayasva, vakṣi; subjunctives prabravāmā, dhārayāmā, śṛṇavat) and the riddle of the bull (catvāri śṛṅgā …). 10.12.28 completes the ghee riddle, prays to Rudra (yo … sa no devaḥ … saṃyunaktu), describes the One with relatives in the ablative (yasmāt), states the means (na karmaṇā … tyāgena), and closes with two meditations: the space in the heart-lotus (tad upāsitavyam) and the Lord beyond the syllable (yaḥ … sa maheśvaraḥ).

Ambiguities

dhātuḥ prasādāt
Here two words: ‘through the grace of the Ordainer’, as Sāyaṇa and Bhāskara read it (Bhāskara cites यमेवैष वृणुते तेन लभ्यः). The Kaṭha has the single compound धातुप्रसादात्, which can be read as ‘through the clarity of the body’s elements (senses and mind)’; that reading is not taken by the commentators here.
ghṛta, the bull and the four horns
Two different readings run through 10.12.27–28. Sāyaṇa and the anonymous commentator read the ghee as self-luminous brahman and the four-horned bull as the syllable om (a, u, m and the resonance), with three feet as waking, dream and deep sleep. Bhāskara reads the same verses of the sacrifice: ghee as water and rain, the four horns as the four priests, the three feet as the three fires, the seven hands as the seven metres (सप्त छन्दांसि गायत्र्यादीनि हस्तासः).
parāmṛtāt and parāntakāle
Sāyaṇa: at the fall of the body after knowledge, the knowers are released even from the unmanifest (‘the highest deathless’), which the ignorant are not freed from even at dissolution. Bhāskara: having attained the supreme immortal state they are released in every way, and he records others who say all are released together at the end of the world-age (परान्तकाले महार्थोपसंहारकाले सर्वे युगपन् मुच्यन्त इति).
pareṇa nākam … vibhrājad etat
Sāyaṇa makes immortality the subject: set in the one-pointed intellect, it shines beyond heaven, and ascetics enter it. Bhāskara: what shines above the world of brahman, set in the heart, the ascetics enter.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Letting go and attention

The anuvāka puts two practical ideas side by side: what lasts is not reached by accumulating — results, heirs, money — but by letting go; and attention can be turned to a small, quiet place within, where the commentators locate what is greatest. Many people today meet the same pair as a contrast between achievement and inner stillness. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What might it mean for something to be both smaller than the smallest and larger than the largest?

A philosophical inquiry Sāyaṇa reads the four-horned bull as the syllable om; Bhāskara reads it as the sacrifice. What in the verse supports each reading?

A contemplation exercise Sit for a few minutes with attention resting in the centre of the chest, and notice what the mind does when given so small a place to rest.
