Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 26 of 80

Yasya vaikaṅkatī: the ladle of vikaṅkata wood

A single ritual rule: the agnihotra ladle should be of vikaṅkata wood, so that the offerings stand firm, and the offerer with them.

5 Faithful English translation

He whose agnihotra ladle is made of vikaṅkata — his offerings indeed stand firm; and [it is] for firm standing.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

If the ladle with which a man makes the agnihotra offering is made of vikaṅkata wood, his offerings stand firm; and it gives him a firm footing too.

Key concepts कर्मन् action, ritual work अग्नि Agni, fire

7 Explain it to a beginner

Among the hymns and prayers of this Upaniṣad stands one plain ritual rule. The agnihotra is the daily offering of milk into the sacred fire, poured with a ladle. The rule says: if that ladle is made from the wood of the vikaṅkata tree, the offerings ‘stand firm’, and the one who offers gains a firm footing too. Why is a rule about a ladle here? The old commentators answer in two ways. One says that in a text about release, this tree stands for all the rites of the Veda, which, done rightly, purify the mind and so prepare it for knowledge. Another connects the tree with an old story in which the vikaṅkata grew where the first offering stood firm. The text states the rule; it does not claim any further effect.

An analogy Like a craftsman’s rule about which wood to use for a handle: a small choice that is meant to make the whole work hold.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

That for one whose agnihotra ladle is made of vikaṅkata, the offerings stand firm, and it serves for firm standing.

Traditional Interpretation

Why does a ritual rule stand in this text?

The anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa: the rule prescribes a tree for an instrument of the agnihotra, अग्निहोत्रकर्मणि होमसाधनद्रव्यस्य कारणभूतं वृक्षविशेषं विधत्ते, and the tree stands for all the rites of the Veda as means to release, through purity of mind: मुमुक्षोश्चित्तशुद्धिद्वारा वेदोक्तानां कृत्स्नकर्मणां मोक्षसाधनत्वं द्योतयितुमुपलक्षणत्वेन कर्माङ्गभूतो विकङ्कतवृक्षो विहितः.

Traditional Interpretation

Why vikaṅkata?

Bhāskara quotes, without naming its source, a passage that the vikaṅkata arose where the first offering stood firm, and from it creatures were made: सा यत्राहुतिः प्रत्यतिष्ठत्ततो विकङ्कत उदतिष्ठत्ततः प्रजा असृजत. Since the tree is tied to creation, the rule here honours the god who makes the world grow in the form of fire; knowing this, he says, one should worship that god: एवं ज्ञात्वाऽग्न्यात्मानं देवमाराधयेद् उपासीतेति.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • वैकङ्कत्यग्निहोत्रहवणी vaikaṅkatyagnihotrahavaṇī → वैकङ्कती + अग्निहोत्रहवणी

    ī before a dissimilar vowel

    ī becomes y before another vowel (P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि)): vaikaṅkatī + agnihotra° → vaikaṅkaty agnihotra°.

  • प्रत्येवास्याहुतयस्तिष्ठन्त्यथो pratyevāsyāhutayastiṣṭhantyatho → प्रति + एव + अस्य + आहुतयः + तिष्ठन्ति + अथो

    i before a vowel; like vowels merge; visarga before t

    prati + eva → praty eva (P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि)); eva + asya and asya + āhutayaḥ merge into long ā (P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः)): evāsyāhutayaḥ; the visarga becomes s before t (P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः)); tiṣṭhanti + atho → tiṣṭhanty atho.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • अग्निहोत्रहवणी agnihotrahavaṇī

    ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa · अग्निहोत्र + हवणी

    Vigraha अग्निहोत्रस्य हवणी

    the ladle of the agnihotra

Morphology

वैकङ्कती
A derivative meaning ‘made of vikaṅkata’, feminine to agree with havaṇī. Bhāskara explains the suffix by P. 4.3.141 (पलाशादिभ्यो वा), which allows it for the names of certain trees.
प्रति
The preverb prati stands apart from its verb tiṣṭhanti, as Vedic prose allows (tmesis): prati … tiṣṭhanti, ‘stand firm’. The last word, pratiṣṭhityai, is the noun from the same verb.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

A relative clause (yasya … bhavati, ‘whose … is’) followed by the main clause in which the preverb prati is separated from its verb tiṣṭhanti by eva asya āhutayaḥ. A last phrase, atho pratiṣṭhityai (‘and for firm standing’), states a further purpose.

Ambiguities

pratiṣṭhityai
Whose firm standing? Bhāskara: the sacrificer’s, तस्यापि यजमानस्य प्रतिष्ठित्यै सा भवति. The anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa: the performer’s establishment in knowledge of reality, reached through a purified mind.
Why a ritual rule here?
A rule about a ladle sits among hymns and mantras of worship. The commentators explain its place differently: as standing for all Vedic rites as aids to release (the anonymous commentator, Sāyaṇa), or as praise of the god in the form of fire (Bhāskara).

Metre

prose · syllables counted as written: 30

A Brāhmaṇa-style rule in prose, 30 syllables in one line.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Small things done carefully

The rule is about a ladle, yet the text places it among its hymns. Whatever one makes of the ritual, the thought that a small, exact choice of means can steady a whole practice is familiar from any craft. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.
