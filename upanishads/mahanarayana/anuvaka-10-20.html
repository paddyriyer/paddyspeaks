Tatpuruṣāya vidmahe: the fourth of the five ‘brahma’ mantras

A gāyatrī to Tatpuruṣa, ‘that Person’, the eastern face, by the commentators’ account: we know him, we meditate on him, may he impel us.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

We know Tatpuruṣa; we meditate on the Great God. So may Rudra urge us on.

Key concepts गायत्री gāyatrī मन्त्र mantra उपासना worship, meditation

7 Explain it to a beginner

This is the fourth of the five ‘brahma’ mantras to the faces of Śiva, and it has the shape of the famous Gāyatrī: ‘we know …, we meditate on …, may he urge us on’. Here the one known is Tatpuruṣa, ‘that Person’, whom the old commentators identify with the eastern face; the one meditated on is the Great God; and the one asked to inspire is Rudra. Sāyaṇa explains the order: first we come to know him from a teacher and the scriptures, then we meditate on what we know, and so we ask him to move us toward knowledge and meditation. The Upaniṣad uses the same three-step form for many deities, and the first anuvāka of this text already contains this very gāyatrī.

An analogy Like a student’s three steps with a great teacher: learn who they are, keep them in mind, and ask to be set in motion.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

We know Tatpuruṣa, we meditate on the Great God; may Rudra impel us.

Traditional Interpretation

Which face is this?

The anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa: the mantra of the eastern face, whose god is named Tatpuruṣa. Sāyaṇa: प्राग्वक्त्रेदेवस्तत्पुरुषनामकः.

Traditional Interpretation

How is it read?

Sāyaṇa: we know him from the mouth of teacher and scripture, then meditate on him, and therefore ask Rudra to impel us toward knowledge and meditation: विद्महे गुरुशास्त्रमुखाज्जानीमः। ज्ञात्वा च महादेवाय तं महादेवं धीमहि ध्यायेम, and नोऽस्मान्प्रचोदयाज्ज्ञानध्यानार्थं प्रेरयतु.

Traditional Interpretation

Why does Bhāskara not explain it here?

He says it was explained at ‘ambhasy apāre’, the first anuvāka, where the same gāyatrī stands among others: तत्पुरुषायेति व्याख्यातं — at ambhasy apāre. There he calls it the first of five gāyatrīs used in the worship of the Lord, तत्पुरुषायेत्याद्याः पञ्च गायत्र्यः, and here adds that some part of the god pervading Rudra is meant: अत्रापि पुरुषेश्वर रुद्रव्यापको देवांशः कश्चित्प्रतिपाद्यः.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • तन्नो tanno → तत् + नः

    t before a nasal; -aḥ before a voiced sound

    tat + naḥ → tan no: t becomes n before n (P. 8.4.45 (यरोऽनुनासिकेऽनुनासिको वा)); naḥ becomes no before r.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • तत्पुरुषाय tatpuruṣāya

    karmadhāraya (name) · तद् + पुरुष

    Vigraha सः पुरुषः

    that Person

  • महादेवाय mahādevāya

    karmadhāraya · महत् + देव

    Vigraha महान् देवः

    the great god

Morphology

तत्पुरुषाय
The dative stands where an accusative is meant (‘we know Tatpuruṣa’), as Sāyaṇa says; the same holds for mahādevāya. This is the regular form of the gāyatrīs of this Upaniṣad.
प्रचोदयात्
A subjunctive (leṭ) with the augment a, as Bhāskara notes (लेट्याडागमः): ‘may he impel’. Bhāskara explains tatpuruṣāya by a dropped dative ending of tad: तदिति चतुर्थ्या लुक्.

Anvaya

तत्पुरुषाय विद्महे महादेवाय धीमहि । तत् रुद्रः नः प्रचोदयात् ।

tatpuruṣāya vidmahe mahādevāya dhīmahi | tat rudraḥ naḥ pracodayāt |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two first-person plural verbs, vidmahe and dhīmahi, each with its object in the dative, followed by a third-person wish (pracodayāt) with Rudra as subject and naḥ as object. The form is that of the gāyatrī: know, meditate, may he impel.

Ambiguities

tat
Sāyaṇa takes tat as ‘therefore’; Bhāskara, in his first anuvāka, takes it as ‘there’, अथ तत् तत्र नो ऽस्मान् रुद्रः.

Metre

gāyatrī · syllables counted as written: 16 + 8

Three pādas of eight syllables (tatpuruṣāya vidmahe · mahādevāya dhīmahi · tan no rudraḥ pracodayāt), printed in two lines of 16 and 8.

Textual variations between witnesses

The witnesses differ here only in spelling (listed in section 14).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Learning

Know, then reflect, then ask to be moved: the order of the mantra is also an order of learning anything well. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.
