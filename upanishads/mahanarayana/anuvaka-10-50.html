Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 50 of 80

Śalkair agnim: crossing beyond death

The speaker, kindling the fire with wood, asks to win both worlds and, having won them, says: I cross beyond death. It ends the six verses on death.

Kindling the fire with chips of wood, may I win both worlds. Having prospered in both worlds, I cross beyond death.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Kindling the fire with dry sticks, may I gain both this world and the next; and having gained them both, I pass beyond death.

Key concepts अग्नि Agni, fire लोक world, realm मृत्यु death अमृत immortality, the deathless

7 Explain it to a beginner

This short verse closes the group of six verses about turning death away. It pictures the speaker at the ritual fire, feeding it with chips of dry wood. While kindling it, the speaker asks to gain both worlds, this one and the next. The second line then says, in the present tense: having prospered in both worlds, I cross beyond death. The old commentator explains that this shows how good action bears fruit in both worlds. He reads crossing beyond death as living out a full life, enjoying the good things of this world, and at its end becoming a god. He does not take it here as liberation.

An analogy Like tending a small fire through the night until the dawn comes.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

That the speaker, kindling the fire, asks to win both worlds, and having prospered in both, crosses beyond death.

Traditional Interpretation

How does the commentator read it?

The anonymous commentator: the speaker asks this of the Lord; it shows that good action bears fruit in both worlds (एतेन लोकद्वयं सत्कर्मणा फलतीत्युक्तं भवति), and crossing death means becoming deathless (मृत्युमहमतितराम्यमृतो भवामि), which he explains as a full life and then the state of a god.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • शल्कैरग्निमिन्धान उभौ śalkairagnimindhāna ubhau → शल्कैः + अग्निम् + इन्धानः + उभौ

    visarga after ai before a vowel; -aḥ before a vowel other than a

    The visarga of śalkaiḥ becomes r before a vowel; indhānaḥ + ubhau → indhāna ubhau, the visarga dropped (P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य)).

  • सनेमहम् sanemaham → सनेम् + अहम्

    m before a vowel

    sanem + aham → sanem aham, written together.

  • उभयोर्लोकयोर्ऋध्वाऽति ubhayorlokayorṛdhvā'ti → उभयोः + लोकयोः + ऋध्वा + अति

    visarga after o before a voiced sound; ā + a → ā

    The visargas of ubhayoḥ and lokayoḥ become r before l and ṛ; ṛdhvā + ati → ṛdhvāti, the avagraha marking the merged a (P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः)).

  • तराम्यहम् tarāmyaham → तरामि + अहम्

    i before a dissimilar vowel

    tarāmi + aham → tarāmy aham (P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि)).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this anuvāka.

Morphology

सनेम्
A Vedic optative first person singular of √san ‘to win’; the commentator, as printed, writes सनें and glosses prāpnuyām.
ऋध्वा
A Vedic gerund of √ṛdh. The base and the Tirupati e-text write ऋध्वा; the Ānandāśrama text writes ऋद्ध्वा (corrections.json, a witness error), the spelling the commentator also uses (ऋद्ध्वा).
तरामि
With ati, separated from it by mṛtyum (tmesis): ati-tarāmi, ‘I cross beyond’.

Anvaya

अहम् शल्कैः अग्निम् इन्धानः उभौ लोकौ सनेम् । अहम् उभयोः लोकयोः ऋध्वा मृत्युम् अति तरामि ।

aham śalkaiḥ agnim indhānaḥ ubhau lokau sanem | aham ubhayoḥ lokayoḥ ṛdhvā mṛtyum ati tarāmi |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A wish in the optative (sanem) whose subject is described by a participle (indhānaḥ) with its instrumental and object; then a statement in the present (ati … tarāmi) preceded by a gerund (ṛdhvā).

Ambiguities

ubhayor lokayoḥ
Locative ‘in both worlds’ (prospering in both); the commentator reads it, by a change of case, as an accusative object of ṛdhvā: ‘having gained both worlds’.
crossing death
The commentator explains it as enjoying this world’s goods for a full life and at its end becoming a god (यावदायुरैहिकान्भोगान्भुक्त्वाऽन्ते देवो भवामीत्यभिप्रायः), not as liberation.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 15 + 15

One anuṣṭubh verse printed in two lines; the checker counts 15 and 15 syllables, the first and last pādas a syllable short as written (indhāna ubhau is kept in hiatus, and ṛdhvā ’ti merges the a of ati).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Small, steady acts

The verse sets its largest hope, passing beyond death, beside a small daily act: feeding a fire with sticks. Large hopes are often carried by small and repeated actions. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.
