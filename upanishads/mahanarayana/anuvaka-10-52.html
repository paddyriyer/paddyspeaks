Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 52 of 80

Mā no mahāntam: spare our great and our small

Rudra is asked not to strike any of the household: the elder and the infant, the young man and the child unborn, father and mother, and the bodies the speakers hold dear.

5 Faithful English translation

Do not [strike] our great one, nor our small one; do not [strike] our sprinkling one, nor our sprinkled one; do not strike our father, nor [our] mother; do not harm our dear bodies, O Rudra.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Do not strike the old among us or the very young, the man in his strength or the child not yet born; do not strike our father or our mother; Rudra, do not harm the lives we hold dear.

Key concepts मन्त्र mantra देवता deity

7 Explain it to a beginner

This prayer is addressed to Rudra, a god feared for his power to strike and prayed to for protection. It asks him not to strike anyone in the family, and it names them in pairs: the old and the very young, the man in his strength and the child still in the womb, the father and the mother. It ends by asking him not to harm "the bodies we hold dear". The same verse is found in the Ṛgveda and in the Śrī Rudram, the great hymn to Rudra in the Yajurveda, with small differences of wording. In the Mahānārāyaṇa it stands among short prayers for protection that come before the offering formulas. The old commentator explains each word: the great are elders and teachers, the small one is a nursing infant. The edition reports this; it makes no claim about what reciting it does.

An analogy Like a roll call of a whole household read out at the door: every generation is named so that none is left out.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It asks Rudra not to strike the great one and the small one, the sprinkling one and the sprinkled one, the father and the mother, and not to harm the dear bodies of the speakers.

Traditional Interpretation

How does the commentator explain the pairs?

The anonymous commentator explains Rudra as the one who makes the wicked weep, हे रुद्र दुष्टरोदनकर्तः. The great one is the class of elders and teachers to be honoured, गुरुप्रभृतिपूज्यवर्गं; the small one is the suckling, स्तनंधयम्; the sprinkled one is the embryo, योनिष्वासिक्तं गर्भस्थं पिण्डं. He gives the sense of the whole: सर्वानस्मानस्मदीयांश्च सुखयेति भावः — make all of us and ours happy.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

How does it differ from the Ṛgveda?

The Ṛgveda has the verse at 1.114.7, with the first line as here. Its last clause reads मा नः प्रियास्तन्वो रुद्र रीरिषः, with the enclitic before priyāḥ and the classical tanvaḥ; the Mahānārāyaṇa has प्रिया मा नस्तनुवो रुद्र रीरिषः, with the Vedic tanuvaḥ. The words are the same; their order and one spelling differ.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • नो महान्तमुत no mahāntamuta → नः + महान्तम् + उत

    visarga after a before a voiced consonant; final m before a vowel

    naḥ before m becomes no; mahāntam is joined to uta.

  • न उक्षन्तमुत na ukṣantamuta → नः + उक्षन्तम् + उत

    visarga after a before a vowel other than a

    naḥ before u drops its visarga and stands in hiatus: na ukṣantam.

  • नो अर्भकं no arbhakaṃ → नः + अर्भकम्

    visarga after a before a; the a kept

    naḥ before a becomes no, and here the following a is kept, not elided (no arbhakam, not no ’rbhakam): see sandhi exceptions.

  • मोत mota → मा + उत

    guṇa

    ā + u becomes o: mā uta → mota.

  • प्रिया मा priyā mā → प्रियाः + मा

    visarga after ā before a voiced consonant

    āḥ before a voiced sound drops its visarga: priyāḥ mā → priyā mā.

  • नस्तनुवो रुद्र nastanuvo rudra → नः + तनुवः + रुद्र

    visarga before t; visarga after a before a voiced consonant

    naḥ before t becomes nas; tanuvaḥ before r becomes tanuvo.

Junctions outside the general rules

  • नः · अर्भकम् — vedic-prakṛtibhāva: naḥ arbhakam is written no arbhakam: the visarga becomes o, but the initial a of arbhakam is kept, as in the Ṛgveda’s text of this verse, where classical sandhi would elide it (no ’rbhakam).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this anuvāka.

Morphology

वधीः
The one verb governs the whole first line and the first half of the second: ‘do not strike our great one … nor our mother’. After mā the aorist has no augment, P. 6.4.74 (न माङ्योगे).
तनुवः
Accusative plural of tanū written with the Vedic -uv- (tanuvaḥ) where classical Sanskrit writes tanvaḥ.
उक्षन्तम्
Present participle of √ukṣ, ‘sprinkle’; the commentator glosses it as the young man able to beget. Its pair ukṣitam, the past participle, is ‘what has been sprinkled in’, the child in the womb.

Anvaya

हे रुद्र नः महान्तम् मा [वधीः] उत नः अर्भकम् मा [वधीः] नः उक्षन्तम् मा उत नः उक्षितम् मा नः पितरम् मा वधीः मा उत मातरम् नः प्रियाः तनुवः मा रीरिषः ।

he rudra naḥ mahāntam mā vadhīḥ uta naḥ arbhakam mā vadhīḥ naḥ ukṣantam mā uta naḥ ukṣitam mā naḥ pitaram mā vadhīḥ mā uta mātaram naḥ priyāḥ tanuvaḥ mā rīriṣaḥ |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A list of objects, each with mā and the enclitic naḥ, all depending on the one verb vadhīḥ, which comes only in the second line; a last clause has its own verb rīriṣaḥ with the vocative rudra. The pairs are built on contrast: great and small, the one who begets and the one begotten, father and mother.

Ambiguities

ukṣantam and ukṣitam
The two participles of √ukṣ are read by the commentator as the young man and the embryo. The words themselves say only ‘the sprinkling’ and ‘the sprinkled’; the pairing with great and small supports a sequence of the generations.

Metre

jagatī · syllables counted as written: 24 + 24

Two lines of 24 syllables (12 + 12 each), a regular jagatī as printed.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Care for others

The prayer asks nothing for the speaker alone; it names the whole household across the generations. Concern widened to the oldest and the not yet born is a measure of what a person counts as their own. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Who would you name, in pairs, if you made such a list for your own family?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why might the verse be built on pairs of opposites rather than a simple list?

A contemplation exercise Do one thing today for someone of a generation other than your own.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
