Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 73 of 80

Vayaḥ suparṇāḥ: the seers came to Indra

A Vedic verse in which the seers, like fair-winged birds, come to Indra and ask him to drive away the darkness, fill their sight and set them free.

5 Faithful English translation

The birds, fair-winged, approached Indra — the seers to whom the rite is dear, beseeching: Uncover the darkness, fill the eye, release us, bound to a treasure.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Like fair-winged birds the seers came to Indra, longing, devoted to the rite, and asked: drive the darkness away, fill our sight, and set us free — us who are bound.

Key concepts ऋषि seer तमस् darkness

7 Explain it to a beginner

This is an old Vedic verse placed here among the prayers. It tells a short scene: the seers, like birds with beautiful wings, came to the god Indra and asked him for something. Their request is in three parts: drive away the darkness, fill our eyes, set us free. The anonymous commentator, the only one in this edition who explains the verse, reads it as a mantra for repetition and tells a fuller story: seven seers went up to Indra in heaven, asked for garments, beauty and treasure, received them, and returned to their hermitages. Other readers have taken the darkness and the binding in a more inward sense; the edition reports the commentator’s reading as his.

An analogy Like travellers who arrive at a door at night and ask for three things at once: a light, a clear view, and the untying of their burdens.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

The seers, fair-winged like birds, came to Indra, beseeching; they asked him to uncover the darkness, fill the eye and release them, who are bound.

Traditional Interpretation

How does the commentator use it?

The anonymous commentator calls it a mantra for repetition, a dialogue between Indra and the seers (सप्तर्षिसंवादकं मन्त्रं), said for the removal of all sins and for gaining wealth. He completes the story: the seers, made happy by what Indra gave them, came down to the earth and stayed in their hermitages (एवमृषियाचितेनेन्द्रेण दत्तैर्भाग्यैः सुखिताः सन्त ऋषयो भूमिमेत्य स्वाश्रमेषु स्थितवन्त इत्याद्युपाख्यानं पूरयितव्यम्).

Traditional Interpretation

What do they ask for, in his reading?

Garments, beauty and treasure: ‘cover us’ with divine cloth (दिव्यदुकूलादिप्रदानेनाऽऽच्छादय), ‘fill our eye’ by showing and giving beautiful things, ‘release us from all sins’, and make us ‘attached to treasure’ (अनेकधनरत्नवन्निधियुक्तानस्मान्कुर्वित्यर्थः). He takes apadhvāntam as describing Indra: अपध्वान्तमपगतहृदयान्धकारम्.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

How are the words joined?

The base writes अप ध्वान्तमूर्णुहि and निधयेऽव बद्धान् with spaces; the letters can be divided more than one way (see ambiguities). The base doubles the d of pūrdhi (पूर्द्धि), where both other witnesses and the commentator write पूर्धि.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • वयस्सुपर्णा vayassuparṇā → वयः + सुपर्णाः

    visarga before s

    The Taittirīya recitation writes the visarga before s as ss: vayaḥ suparṇāḥ → vayas suparṇāḥ.

  • सुपर्णा उपसेदुरिन्द्रं suparṇā upasedurindraṃ → सुपर्णाः + उपसेदुः + इन्द्रम्

    visarga after ā and after u before a vowel

    suparṇāḥ + upaseduḥ → suparṇā upaseduḥ: after ā the visarga is dropped before a vowel and the vowels stay apart; upaseduḥ + indram → upasedur indram: after u it becomes r.

  • ऋषयो नाधमानाः ṛṣayo nādhamānāḥ → ऋषयः + नाधमानाः

    visarga after a before a voiced consonant

    ṛṣayaḥ + nādhamānāḥ → ṛṣayo nādhamānāḥ.

  • मुमुग्ध्यस्मान् mumugdhyasmān → मुमुग्धि + अस्मान्

    i before a vowel

    mumugdhi + asmān → mumugdhy asmān.

  • निधयेऽव nidhaye'va → निधये + अवबद्धान्

    e before a

    nidhaye + avabaddhān → nidhaye ’vabaddhān: the a after e is elided, marked by the avagraha.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this anuvāka.

Morphology

पूर्द्धि
Imperative 2nd singular of √pṛ, ‘fill’ (classically पूर्धि). The base writes the d doubled after r, a spelling of the recitation text; the Ānandāśrama pariśiṣṭa text and the Tirupati e-text write पूर्धि (see flags).
मुमुग्धि
A reduplicated (perfect-stem) imperative of √muc, ‘release’.
अवबद्धान्
The base prints अव बद्धान् with a space; the participle with its preverb is given as one word.
उपसेदुः
Perfect 3rd plural of √sad with upa-, ‘they sat near, approached’ (with the s of the root after the preverb).

Anvaya

सुपर्णाः वयः प्रियमेधाः नाधमानाः ऋषयः इन्द्रम् उपसेदुः । ध्वान्तम् अप ऊर्णुहि । चक्षुः पूर्द्धि । निधये अवबद्धान् अस्मान् मुमुग्धि

suparṇāḥ vayaḥ priyamedhāḥ nādhamānāḥ ṛṣayaḥ indram upaseduḥ | dhvāntam apa ūrṇuhi | cakṣuḥ pūrddhi | nidhaye avabaddhān asmān mumugdhi

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A narrative sentence in the perfect (upaseduḥ), whose subject ṛṣayaḥ is described as vayaḥ suparṇāḥ, priyamedhāḥ and nādhamānāḥ; then the seers’ request, three imperatives in a row (apa … ūrṇuhi, pūrddhi, mumugdhi), the last governing asmān … avabaddhān.

Ambiguities

apa dhvāntam ūrṇuhi
With apa as the separated preverb of ūrṇuhi the words mean ‘uncover the darkness’. The anonymous commentator instead reads apadhvāntam as describing Indra, ‘free of darkness’ (अपध्वान्तमपगतहृदयान्धकारम्), and ūrṇuhi as ‘cover us’ with garments.
nidhaye ’va baddhān
The letters allow nidhaye ava-baddhān (‘bound to a treasure’), as the base’s avagraha and the anonymous commentator take them, and also nidhayā iva baddhān (‘bound as by a snare’). The edition follows the printed avagraha.
vayaḥ
Birds in apposition to the seers, or a simile; the anonymous commentator makes it a simile, ‘swift like birds’.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 21

As printed the two lines count 22 and 21 syllables. The second line is one short because mumugdhy asmān joins two vowels; read apart (mumugdhi asmān) it has 22, the regular 11 + 11.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Asking for help

The seers’ request names exactly what they lack: light, sight, release. Being able to say precisely what one needs is often the first part of receiving it. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin If you could ask for three things in the words of this verse, what would ‘darkness’, ‘eye’ and ‘bound’ mean for you?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry The commentator reads the requests as worldly goods. What would change if they were read as requests for understanding?

A contemplation exercise Write down one thing you need help with, as plainly as the seers ask.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
