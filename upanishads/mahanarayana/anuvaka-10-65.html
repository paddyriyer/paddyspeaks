Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 65 of 80

Prāṇāpāna … śudhyantām: the purification of the person, breath to body

Offerings asking that the breaths, the faculties, the tissues and the limbs be purified, each ending ‘may I become light, free of stain, free of evil’; then the Person within is asked to rise and give.

5 Faithful English translation

May my out-breath, in-breath, diffused, up-going and equalising breaths be purified; may I become light, free of stain, free of evil. Svāhā. May my speech, mind, eye, ear, tongue, smell, seed, understanding, intention [and] resolves be purified; may I become light … Svāhā. May my skin, hide, flesh, blood, fat, marrow, sinews [and] bones be purified; may I become light … Svāhā. May my head, hands, feet, sides, back, thighs, belly, shanks, organs of generation and excretion be purified; may I become light … Svāhā. Rise, O Person, tawny, reddish-brown, red-eyed; give, give; [be] the one who causes [it] to be given; may my [faculties] be purified; may I become light, free of stain, free of evil. Svāhā.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

May my five breaths be made pure; may I become light, free of stain, free of wrong. Svāhā. May my speech and mind, my senses, my seed, my understanding, my intentions and resolves be made pure; may I become light … Svāhā. May my skin and flesh, my blood, fat, marrow, sinews and bones be made pure … Svāhā. May my whole body, head to foot, be made pure … Svāhā. 7 Explain it to a beginner

This anuvāka is a series of short offerings into the sacred fire, each ending with the same wish: may I become light, free of stain, free of wrong. Each offering names one layer of the person to be made pure: first the five breaths, then speech, mind, the senses and the will, then the body’s tissues from skin to bone, then the limbs from head to foot. The last offering turns to the Person dwelling within and asks him to rise and give. The old commentator Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara says these offerings belong to the rite of one taking up renunciation, when everything that makes up the person is offered and cleansed. The edition reports that use and makes no claim about what the rite achieves.

An analogy Like cleaning a house room by room — the air, the instruments, the walls, the frame — and then opening the door to the owner.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It asks, layer by layer, that the five breaths, the faculties from speech to resolve, the seven tissues and bones, and the parts of the body be purified, each time adding ‘may I become light, free of stain, free of evil’ and svāhā. Then it addresses the Person — rise, give, give — before the same refrain.

Traditional Interpretation

In what rite are these formulas used?

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara, on the Drāviḍa text, opens the series: अथ सन्न्यासविषया विरजाहोमा एकपञ्चाशप्रभृतय एकादशानुवाकाः — the virajā offerings concerning renunciation, eleven anuvākas from the fifty-first. He glosses the refrain: ततो ऽहं परं ज्योतिर् भूत्वा विरजा विरजस्कः प्रवर्तक रहितः विपाप्मा विगतत्रिविधमलश्च भूयासम्, and the svāhā as an offering to the highest light: अनेन होमेन स्वाहा हुतं चेदमस्तु परस्मै ज्योतिषे.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Bhāskara group the four lists?

He treats each list as its own anuvāka and names what it purifies: the ten instruments of knowing, वागादिसङ्कल्पान्तज्ञानसाधनदशकशुद्धिर् द्विपञ्चाशे; the ten instruments of action, शिरःप्रभृतिपाय्वन्तकर्मसाधनदशकशुद्धिस् त्रिपञ्चाशे; the seven tissues, त्वगादिमज्जान्तसप्तधातुशुद्धिश् चतुःपञ्चाशे. In his text the limbs come before the tissues; the 80-anuvāka text has the tissues first.

Comparative Vedānta

How do the commentators read ‘rise, O Person’?

Sāyaṇa: the Supreme, dwelling in the body of fire, is asked to give up indifference and take up the speaker’s cause, हे परमात्मान्नुत्तिष्ठौदासीन्यं परित्यज्य मदनुग्रहार्थमुद्युक्तो भव, to give purity again and again, देहि देहि पुनः पुनः शुद्धिं मे प्रयच्छ, and to have knowledge given through the teacher, ददापयिताऽऽचार्यमुखात्तत्त्वज्ञानस्यातिशयेन दापयिता भवेति शेषः. Bhāskara: the one lying in the city is asked not to stay contracted, न सङ्कुचितेनासितव्यं वैश्वरूप्यं भज, and reads the epithets as day and night, लोहिताक्षमहः and पिङ्गाक्षी रात्रिः. The anonymous commentator, whose comment here runs close to Sāyaṇa’s wording, takes the epithets in the 80-anuvāka text’s order: हे हरित प्रतिबन्धहरणकुशल, हे पिङ्गल पिङ्गलवर्ण.

No Vedānta school commentary on this anuvāka is in the edition’s sources. Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara reads the formulas as the offerings of one taking up renunciation, Sāyaṇa and the anonymous bhāṣya as purifications and offerings addressed to the Supreme; none argues a school position here. Raṅgarāmānuja’s commentary is not in the sources. No school position is given.

Morphology

वाङ्मनश्चक्षुःश्रोत्रजिह्वाघ्राणरेतोबुद्ध्याकूतिः
The base prints this series with spaces (वाङ्मनश्चक्षुः श्रोत्रजिह्वाघ्राणरेतो बुद्ध्याकूतिः) and a visarga after ākūti, so that ākūtiḥ ends a word and saṅkalpāḥ stands apart; the analysis follows the printed division. Bhāskara counts the whole series as ten instruments of knowing, from speech to resolve: वागादिसङ्कल्पान्तज्ञानसाधनदशकशुद्धिर् द्विपञ्चाशे.
शिरःपाणिपादपार्श्वपृष्ठोरूदरजङ्घशिश्नोपस्थपायवः
The base prints the members with spaces (शिरःपाणि पाद पार्श्व पृष्ठोरूदर जङ्घ शिश्नोपस्थ पायवो); they form one dvandva in the plural. Bhāskara calls it the ten instruments of action, from the head to the anus: शिरःप्रभृतिपाय्वन्तकर्मसाधनदशकशुद्धिस् त्रिपञ्चाशे.
त्वक्चर्ममांसरुधिरमेदोमज्जास्नायवः
A dvandva of seven body tissues, printed with spaces; Bhāskara: त्वगादिमज्जान्तसप्तधातुशुद्धिश् चतुःपञ्चाशे — the seven tissues from skin to marrow.
लोहिताक्षि
Sāyaṇa takes it as a vocative, ‘red-eyed’ (his text reads lohita piṅgalākṣi: हे लोहित रक्तवर्ण हे पिङ्गलाक्षि पिङ्गलनयन); the anonymous commentator, on the base order, glosses it the same way: लोहिताक्षि रक्तनयन. Bhāskara reads lohitapiṅgalākṣi as a dual, day and night, whose eyes are the red sun and the tawny fire, with the final vowel shortened by Vedic licence: छान्दसं ह्रस्वत्वम्.
ददापयिता
Agent noun from the causative of √dā with reduplication, ‘the one who causes [it] to be given’. Bhāskara explains the doubled देहि देहि by the rule of repeated action: ‘क्रियासमभिहारे लोट्’.
विरजाः
Nominative singular masculine of the s-stem virajas, ‘without rajas (dust, stain; the quality that drives to action)’. Bhāskara: विरजा विरजस्कः.
भूयासम्
Benedictive (precative) 1st singular of √bhū: ‘may I become’.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

Five offering formulas with one refrain. The first four each give a subject (a compound list of the breaths, the faculties, the tissues, the limbs) to the third-person imperative śudhyantām, ‘may they be purified’, then the first-person benedictive jyotir ahaṃ virajā vipāpmā bhūyāsam, ‘may I become light …’, and svāhā. The fifth replaces the subject with an address to the Person (vocatives, two imperatives and an agent noun) before the same refrain.

Ambiguities

what is purified in the fifth formula
The fifth formula has no subject for śudhyantām. Sāyaṇa supplies the workings of the mind, for the rise of knowledge: तस्य दापयितव्यस्य ज्ञानस्योत्पत्तये मे मदीयाश्चित्तवृत्तयः शुध्यन्ताम्. Bhāskara takes it of the Person’s powers named before: एतेऽपि शुद्धयन्ताम्.
who is the Person
Sāyaṇa: the Supreme dwelling in the city of the body of fire, asked to give up indifference, हे परमात्मान्नुत्तिष्ठौदासीन्यं परित्यज्य मदनुग्रहार्थमुद्युक्तो भव. Bhāskara: the one lying in the city, asked to take on his universal form, वैश्वरूप्यं भज, with day and night as the ‘red and tawny-eyed’ pair.
the epithets
The base reads harita piṅgala lohitākṣi; Sāyaṇa’s text reads harita lohita piṅgalākṣi; Bhāskara’s reads lohitapiṅgalākṣi as a dual. The words are the same, the order and grouping differ.

Metre

prose · syllables counted as written: 30 + 39 + 36 + 41 + 44

Prose formulas (yajus): the five printed lines count 30, 39, 36, 41 and 44 syllables, the refrain ‘jyotir ahaṃ … svāhā’ (16) included.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Self-knowledge

The formulas take stock of a person in layers — breath, faculties, tissues, limbs — and treat each as something that can be cleared. Whatever one believes about the rite, the inventory is a careful map of what a human being is made of. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Which of the four lists — breath, faculties, tissues, limbs — do you most rarely think about?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Bhāskara reads the fifth formula as asking the Person ‘not to stay contracted’. What would it mean for something within you to stop being contracted?

A contemplation exercise Sit for a minute and attend to each layer in turn — breath, senses, body — without trying to change it.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.

Further notes

  • The anonymous bhāṣya is quoted on the fifth formula from vishvAsa’s assembled page for this anuvāka. vishvAsa labels its blocks सायण-टीका, but the page closes with the pariśiṣṭa bhāṣya’s own colophon, naming the 65th anuvāka of the bhāṣya on the Yājñikī Upaniṣad, where Sāyaṇa’s colophons name his Vedārthaprakāśa; their numbering in the 80-anuvāka count and their wording are the anonymous bhāṣya’s, so the edition files them as the anonymous commentator’s. Sāyaṇa is quoted only from his own numbered comment (his anuvāka 60). For the first formula the page gives only a link to the section page 51-57_virajA_vipApmA, which has not been imported; the second to fourth formulas have no comment on the page. Sāyaṇa’s own comments on the first four formulas (his anuvākas 52–57) are on that section page.
  • The Ānandāśrama pariśiṣṭa text prints each formula twice and lacks part of the fifth (पुरुष हरित पिङ्गल लोहिताक्षि देहि देहि दद…); these differences are listed on the sources page as variants.

Uncertainties recorded

  • review The Ānandāśrama text reads पृष्टो- for पृष्ठो- and the Tirupati e-text उरूधर for उरूदर; both are left for review by the collation.
  • review The edition’s computed concordance maps Sāyaṇa’s 51 and 60 to this anuvāka and Bhāskara’s none; by content Sāyaṇa’s 51 is the trisuparṇa summary, his 60 is the fifth formula here, and Bhāskara’s 51–54 and 60 comment on this anuvāka. They are cited explicitly.
  • interpretation Who the Person is, and what is purified in the fifth formula, are read differently by Sāyaṇa and Bhāskara; the text does not decide.
