Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 55 of 80

Svastidā viśaspatiḥ: may Indra, giver of well-being, go before us

Indra — giver of well-being, lord of the people, slayer of Vṛtra, repeller of foes, the controller, the bull — is asked to go before the speakers as the maker of fearlessness.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

May Indra go before us: giver of well-being, lord of the people, slayer of Vṛtra, driver-off of enemies, master of all, the showering bull, the one who makes us unafraid.

Key concepts मन्त्र mantra देवता deity

7 Explain it to a beginner

This prayer asks Indra, the king of the gods, to go in front of the speakers, as a leader goes before a band of travellers. It piles up his titles: giver of well-being, lord of the people, the one who killed the serpent Vṛtra, the one who drives off enemies, the one who rules everything, the bull who brings rain, and the one who makes people unafraid. "Giver of well-being" comes twice; the old commentator says the first time means well-being in this world and the second in the next. He explains the request as protection on every side, so that, guarded by Indra, the travellers may be without fear. The edition reports what the words and the commentator say; it makes no claim about what reciting the prayer brings.

An analogy Like a guide walking ahead of a group through unknown country: those behind can go without fear because someone they trust goes first.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It asks Indra to go before the speakers, naming him giver of well-being, lord of the people, slayer of Vṛtra, repeller of foes, controller, bull and maker of fearlessness.

Traditional Interpretation

How does the commentator read ‘go before us’?

The anonymous commentator: for those who travel here and there, may Indra come before them to protect them, and by extension in every direction: इतस्ततो गच्छतां नोऽस्माकं पुरः पुरस्तादौपलक्षण्येन प्रागवाक्प्रत्यगुदगूर्ध्वाधोदिक्षु परिपालनायेन्द्र एत्वागच्छतु. The point: सर्वतो दिक्षु वज्रहस्तेन पालिता वयं निर्भयाः स्यामेति भावः — guarded on every side by the one who holds the thunderbolt, may we be without fear.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • स्वस्तिदा विशस्पतिर्वृत्रहा svastidā viśaspatirvṛtrahā → स्वस्तिदाः + विशस्पतिः + वृत्रहा

    visarga after ā before a voiced consonant; after i before a voiced consonant

    svastidāḥ before v drops its visarga; viśaspatiḥ before v becomes viśaspatir.

  • विमृधो वशी vimṛdho vaśī → विमृधः + वशी

    visarga after a before a voiced consonant

    vimṛdhaḥ before v becomes vimṛdho.

  • वृषेन्द्रः vṛṣendraḥ → वृषा + इन्द्रः

    guṇa

    ā + i becomes e: vṛṣā indraḥ → vṛṣendraḥ.

  • पुर एतु pura etu → पुरः + एतु

    visarga after a before a vowel other than a

    puraḥ before e drops its visarga and stands in hiatus.

  • नस्स्वस्तिदा अभयङ्करः nassvastidā abhayaṅkaraḥ → नः + स्वस्तिदाः + अभयङ्करः

    visarga before s; visarga after ā before a vowel

    naḥ before s is written nas s (the recitation text’s doubled sibilant); svastidāḥ before a drops its visarga and stands in hiatus.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • स्वस्तिदाः svastidāḥ

    upapada-tatpuruṣa · स्वस्ति + दा

    Vigraha स्वस्तिं ददातीति

    giver of well-being

  • विशस्पतिः viśaspatiḥ

    upapada-tatpuruṣa · वृत्र + हन्

    Vigraha वृत्रं हतवान्

    slayer of Vṛtra

  • अभयङ्करः abhayaṅkaraḥ

    upapada-tatpuruṣa · अभय + कर

    Vigraha अभयं करोतीति

    maker of fearlessness

Morphology

विशस्पतिः
A compound that keeps the genitive of its first member, viśaḥ, with the visarga written s before p: viśas-patiḥ, ‘lord of the people’. The edition keeps it as one word.
अभयङ्करः
The first member abhaya keeps a nasal before kara (abhayaṃ-kara), written ṅ before k.

Anvaya

स्वस्तिदाः विशस्पतिः वृत्रहा विमृधः वशी वृषा अभयङ्करः स्वस्तिदाः इन्द्रः नः पुरः एतु ।

svastidāḥ viśaspatiḥ vṛtrahā vimṛdhaḥ vaśī vṛṣā abhayaṅkaraḥ svastidāḥ indraḥ naḥ puraḥ etu |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A single sentence: the subject indraḥ with eight epithets in the nominative, svastidāḥ framing the verse at its beginning and near its end, and the imperative etu with the adverb puraḥ and the genitive naḥ.

Ambiguities

svastidāḥ twice
The epithet is repeated. The commentator gives each its own sense: well-being in this world and in the other, स्वस्तिदा भूलोकसुखकृत्। पुनः स्वस्तिदा आमुत्रिकसुखकृत्.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 15 + 16

As printed 15 + 16 syllables: svastidā viśaspatir (7) vṛtrahā vimṛdho vaśī (8); vṛṣendraḥ pura etu nas (8) svastidā abhayaṅkaraḥ (8). The first pāda has seven; reading svasti with su as two syllables (suvasti) would give eight, but the edition only reports the count.

Textual variations between witnesses

The witnesses differ here only in spelling (listed in section 14).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Courage

The prayer does not ask for the road to be safe; it asks for someone to walk ahead. Courage often comes less from removing danger than from not facing it alone. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Who has ‘gone before’ you, making a hard road easier to walk?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why might the prayer name so many titles before making its single request?

A contemplation exercise Go ahead of someone in one small difficulty today, so that they can follow without fear.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
