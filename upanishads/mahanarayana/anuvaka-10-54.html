Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 54 of 80

Prajāpate na tvad: none but you encompasses all that is born

Prajāpati is told that no one but he encompasses all created things; the worshippers ask that what they desire in offering to him be theirs, and that they become lords of riches.

5 Faithful English translation

O Prajāpati, no other than you has encompassed all these born things. With whatever desire we offer to you, let that be ours; may we be lords of riches.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Prajāpati, none but you holds all that has come to be. Whatever we desire as we make our offering to you, let it be ours; may we become masters of wealth.

Key concepts मन्त्र mantra देवता deity जगत् world, ‘the moving’

7 Explain it to a beginner

This prayer is addressed to Prajāpati, the "lord of creatures", the creator. Its first half is a statement: no one other than you holds all that has been born. Its second half is a request: whatever we wish for when we make our offering to you, let it come to us, and may we become masters of wealth. The old commentator explains that only this lord creates, sustains and withdraws everything, and that the offering is made because of that. He adds that the same verse is used when water is offered at a meal given to brāhmaṇas. The verse is also known from the Ṛgveda. The edition reports what the words and the commentator say; it makes no claim about what making the offering brings.

An analogy Like a household acknowledging the one who keeps the whole estate before asking him for its share.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It tells Prajāpati that no one other than he encompasses all created things, and asks that the object of the worshippers’ desire in offering to him be theirs, and that they become lords of riches.

Traditional Interpretation

How does the commentator read it?

The anonymous commentator takes pari-bhū as overpowering, परिपूर्वो भवतिस्तिरस्कारार्थः, and draws the sense: in creation, in sustaining and in withdrawal it is this lord alone who acts, अपि तु सृष्टिकाले त्वमेव सृजसि जीवनकाले त्वमेव जीवयसि प्रलये त्वमेव त्वय्येवोपसंहरसि. The riches asked for are of two kinds, of learning and of enjoyment: विद्याविनयसंपदामिहपरोपभोग्यभोगसंपदां च.

Traditional Interpretation

Where is it used?

Besides the offering into fire, the commentator names a second use: अयमेव मन्त्रो ब्राह्मणभोजनादौ पार्वतीपतिप्रीतिकृदुदकदाने विनियुज्यते — the same mantra is used when water is given at the start of a meal for brāhmaṇas, to please the lord of Pārvatī; then ‘we offer’ means offering food into the brāhmaṇa’s mouth as into fire.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • त्वदेतान्यन्यो tvadetānyanyo → त्वत् + एतानि + अन्यः

    final t voiced before a vowel; i before a vowel; visarga after a before a voiced consonant

    tvat before e becomes tvad; etāni before a becomes etāny; anyaḥ before v becomes anyo.

  • यत्कामास्ते yatkāmāste → यत्कामाः + ते

    visarga before t

    yatkāmāḥ before t becomes yatkāmās.

  • जुहुमस्तन्नो juhumastanno → जुहुमः + तत् + नः

    visarga before t; t before a nasal

    juhumaḥ before t becomes juhumas; tat before n becomes tan.

  • नो अस्तु no astu → नः + अस्तु

    visarga after a before a; the a kept

    naḥ before a becomes no, and the initial a of astu is kept, not elided: see sandhi exceptions.

  • पतयो रयीणाम् patayo rayīṇām → पतयः + रयीणाम्

    visarga after a before a voiced consonant

    patayaḥ before r becomes patayo.

Junctions outside the general rules

  • नः · अस्तु — vedic-prakṛtibhāva: naḥ astu is written no astu: the visarga becomes o, but the initial a is kept, where classical sandhi would elide it (no ’stu).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • प्रजापते prajāpate

    ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa · प्रजा + पति

    Vigraha प्रजानां पतिः

    lord of creatures

  • यत्कामाः yatkāmāḥ

    bahuvrīhi · यद् + काम

    Vigraha यत् कामः येषां ते

    those whose desire is what

Morphology

परि
The preverb pari stands apart from its verb babhūva (tmesis), as often in the Veda: pari … babhūva, ‘has encompassed, has surpassed’.
विश्वा
Vedic neuter plural in -ā (viśvā for viśvāni), like tā for tāni in the same line.

Anvaya

हे प्रजापते त्वत् अन्यः एतानि विश्वा जातानि ता न परि बभूव । यत्कामाः [वयम्] ते जुहुमः तत् नः अस्तु । वयम् रयीणाम् पतयः स्याम ।

he prajāpate tvat anyaḥ etāni viśvā jātāni tā na pari babhūva | yatkāmāḥ vayam te juhumaḥ tat naḥ astu | vayam rayīṇām patayaḥ syāma |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A negative statement (na … anyaḥ … pari babhūva) with the ablative tvat governed by anyaḥ, and the object stated twice (etāni viśvā jātāni, resumed by tā). Then a relative clause whose subject is described by the bahuvrīhi yatkāmāḥ, resumed by tat in a third person imperative (astu), and a closing wish in the optative (syāma).

Ambiguities

pari babhūva
‘Encompassed’ (held within) or ‘surpassed, overpowered’. The commentator takes bhū with pari as overpowering, परिपूर्वो भवतिस्तिरस्कारार्थः, and explains it as creating, sustaining and withdrawing the worlds: none but Prajāpati does so.
who Prajāpati is
The text says only Prajāpati. The commentator addresses him as Brahman and as the husband of Pārvatī, हे प्रजापते ब्रह्मन्पार्वतीवल्लभ; that identification is his.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 21 + 21

As printed each half has 21 syllables: prajāpate na tvad etāny anyo (10) viśvā jātāni pari tā babhūva (11); yatkāmās te juhumas tan no astu (11) vayaṃ syāma patayo rayīṇām (10). Two pādas fall one short of eleven; reading tvad and syāma with two syllables each (tuvad, siyāma), as Vedic metre often requires, would make them regular, but the edition only reports the count.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Witness error Tirupati e-text (eBhāratī): यत्कामस्ते जुहुमस्तन्नो — The Tirupati e-text alone reads — for ा; the base and the Ānandāśrama pariśiṣṭa text agree.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Gratitude

The verse acknowledges before it asks: first that all that exists is held by another, then what the worshippers want. Acknowledging what one depends on before asking for more is a discipline in its own right. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What do you depend on that you rarely acknowledge?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry The commentator reads ‘riches’ as both learning and enjoyment. Why might a prayer for wealth be read as including learning?

A contemplation exercise Before asking anyone for something today, first say what you are grateful for in them.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
