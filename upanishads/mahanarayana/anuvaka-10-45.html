Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 45 of 80

Apaitu mṛtyuḥ: let death go away

The first of six verses that turn death away: death is to depart, the deathless to come, Yama to grant fearlessness, harm to fall away like a ripe leaf and wealth to stay.

5 Faithful English translation

Let death go away; immortality has come to us; may the son of Vivasvat make fearlessness for us. Like the leaf of a tree, let [it] fall away from us on every side; may wealth attend us, the lord of Śacī.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

May death go far from us and the deathless come to us; may Yama, the son of Vivasvat, give us freedom from fear. Like a ripe leaf from a tree, may what harms us fall away on every side; may wealth, such as Indra enjoys, stay with us.

Key concepts मृत्यु death अमृत immortality, the deathless मोक्ष liberation

7 Explain it to a beginner

This is the first of a short group of verses that ask death to go away. It makes five wishes, one after another. Death should leave; the deathless, which the old commentator explains as liberation, should come; Yama, the god who rules over the dead and is called the son of Vivasvat, should grant freedom from fear. Then comes a picture from nature: like a ripe leaf that drops from a tree, may harm fall away from us on every side. The commentator says that what falls away is our sin. Last, the verse asks that wealth stay with the speakers. The commentator reads the final word, lord of Śacī, a name of Indra, as describing that wealth: the kind of prosperity a king of the gods enjoys.

An analogy Like an autumn tree letting go of its dry leaves: they are not torn off, they simply fall away.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse ask?

That death depart, the deathless come, Yama grant fearlessness, harm fall away like a leaf, and wealth remain.

Traditional Interpretation

How does the commentator read it?

The anonymous commentator: with this verse the speaker asks the Lord for what he desires (अनयर्चा परमात्मनः सकाशात्स्वाभीष्टं याचते); amṛta is liberation, Yama’s gift of fearlessness is the absence of the sufferings of hell, and what falls like a ripe leaf is our sin.

Comparative Vedānta

Is there a parallel?

The movement from death to the deathless is the prayer of the Bṛhadāraṇyaka (1.3.28), ‘from death lead me to the deathless’: मृत्योर्मामृतं.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • अपैतु apaitu → अप + एतु

    a + e → ai with a preverb before eti

    apa + etu → apaitu: vṛddhi, not the pararūpa a preverb otherwise takes (P. 6.1.89 (एत्येधत्यूठ्सु)).

  • मृत्युरमृतं mṛtyuramṛtaṃ → मृत्युः + अमृतम्

    visarga after u before a vowel

    The visarga of mṛtyuḥ becomes r before a vowel.

  • न आगन्वैवस्वतो na āganvaivasvato → नः + आगन् + वैवस्वतः

    -aḥ before a vowel other than a; -aḥ before a voiced consonant

    naḥ + āgan → na āgan, the visarga dropped (P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य)); vaivasvataḥ + naḥ → vaivasvato naḥ (P. 6.1.114 (हशि च)).

  • पर्णं वनस्पतेरिवाभि parṇaṃ vanaspaterivābhi → पर्णम् + वनस्पतेः + इव + अभि

    m before a consonant; visarga after e; a + a

    parṇam → parṇaṃ (P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः)); vanaspateḥ + iva → vanaspater iva; iva + abhi → ivābhi (P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः)).

  • रयिस्सचतां rayissacatāṃ → रयिः + सचताम्

    visarga before s

    The recitation text writes the visarga before s as s (P. 8.3.36 (वा शरि) allows either); the other two witnesses write ः स (spelling only).

Junctions outside the general rules

  • नः · अभयम् — unjoined hiatus (as printed): naḥ · abhayam: -aḥ before a written -o with the a kept (classical no ’bhayam); a Vedic hiatus, printed so in every witness.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • शचीपतिः śacīpatiḥ

    tatpuruṣa · शची + पति

    Vigraha शच्याः पतिः

    lord of Śacī, or lord of might

  • वनस्पतेः vanaspateḥ

    tatpuruṣa · वनस् + पति

    Vigraha वनस्य पतिः

    lord of the forest, a tree

Morphology

आगन्
A root aorist of √gam with ā (ā-agam-t, the t lost after m, and m → n at the end of a word). The commentator renders it as a wish.
शीयताम्
√śad ‘to fall off’ makes its present stem śīya- (śīyate ‘falls away’); here the middle imperative.
रयिः
The anonymous bhāṣya as printed reads नयिः (नोऽस्मान्नयिर्महदैश्वर्यं), a misprint for रयिः; it then asks रयिः कीदृशः।.

Anvaya

मृत्युः अपैतु । अमृतम् नः आगन् । वैवस्वतः नः अभयम् कृणोतु । वनस्पतेः पर्णम् इव नः [पापम्] अभि शीयताम् । शचीपतिः रयिः नः सचताम् ।

mṛtyuḥ apaitu | amṛtam naḥ āgan | vaivasvataḥ naḥ abhayam kṛṇotu | vanaspateḥ parṇam iva naḥ pāpam abhi śīyatām | śacīpatiḥ rayiḥ naḥ sacatām |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Five short wishes in the imperative (apaitu, kṛṇotu, śīyatām, sacatām) and one aorist (āgan) read as a wish. The fourth clause has no subject in the verse; the commentator supplies pāpam, our sin.

Ambiguities

what falls away
The verse says only ‘let [it] fall away from us like a leaf’. The commentator supplies our sin; read with the first line, death itself could be meant.
śacīpatiḥ
The commentator takes the word as describing the wealth, ‘such as Indra enjoys’. Read on its own it is a nominative, and may be a second subject: ‘may wealth, may the lord of might, be with us’.
āgan
Grammatically ‘has come’; the commentator reads it as a wish, ‘may it come’ (āgacchatu).

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 23

One triṣṭubh verse (four pādas of 11) printed in two lines; the checker counts 22 and 23 syllables, the extra one in the second line from reading rayis sacatāṃ with its doubled s.

Textual variations between witnesses

The witnesses differ here only in spelling (listed in section 14).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Letting go

The image of the ripe leaf asks for no struggle: what is finished simply drops. Many people find that what they fear loosens when it is named and let go rather than fought. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.
