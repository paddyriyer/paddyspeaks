Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 19 of 80

Aghorebhyo ’tha ghorebhyaḥ: the third of the five ‘brahma’ mantras

Homage to all the forms of Rudra — the gentle, the terrible and the most terrible: the southern face, by the commentators’ account.

5 Faithful English translation

To the not-terrible, and to the terrible, to the more-terrible-than-terrible, to all, O Śarva, to the Śarva [forms] — homage be to your forms of Rudra.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

To your gentle forms and your terrible forms, and to those more terrible still — to all your forms of Rudra, O Śarva, everywhere, be homage.

Key concepts मन्त्र mantra उपासना worship, meditation

7 Explain it to a beginner

This is the third of the five ‘brahma’ mantras to the faces of Śiva. It names the face called Aghora, ‘not terrible’, and bows to every form the god takes: the gentle ones, the terrible ones, and those more terrible still. The old commentators say these are the three kinds of form: calm, fierce and darkest, matching the three qualities that Indian philosophy finds in all things. The point is that all of them are his, and all receive the same homage. The commentators also note that the words for ‘all’ and ‘Śarva’ (a name of Śiva) have changed places in the text as printed here; their own texts read ‘O Śarva, to all your forms’. The prayer does not ask for anything; it acknowledges that the frightening is as much the god’s as the gentle.

An analogy Like greeting a mountain in fair weather and in storm with the same respect: it is one mountain.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It offers homage to the forms of Rudra, gentle, terrible and more terrible, to all of them, addressing the god.

Traditional Interpretation

Which face is this, and what are the three kinds of form?

The anonymous commentator: the mantra of the southern face, दक्षिणवक्त्रप्रतिपादकं मन्त्रमाह. Of its forms, some are gentle, विग्रहा अघोराः सात्विकत्वेन शान्ताः सौम्याः, others fierce, अन्ये तु घोरा राजसत्वेनोग्राः, and others more terrible still, अपरे तु तामसत्वेन घोरादपि. Sāyaṇa gives the same account.

Traditional Interpretation

To whom is the homage offered, and where?

Sāyaṇa reads the god addressed as Śarva and the homage as offered everywhere and always: हे शर्व परमेश्वर ते त्वदीयेभ्यः पूर्वोक्तेभ्यस्त्रिविधेभ्यः सर्वेभ्यो रुद्ररूपेभ्यः, then सर्वतः सर्वेषु देशेषु सर्वेषु च कालेषु नमोऽस्तु. Bhāskara: the parts of the god, with body, speech and mind: हे शर्व! ते त्वदीयेभ्यो ऽंशेभ्यः सर्वतः सर्वप्रकारं शिरसा वचसा मनसा च नमोस्तु.

Traditional Interpretation

What is meant by the god here?

Bhāskara: here too brahman is meant, as the totality of the Rudras: अत्रापि रुद्रसमष्ट्यात्मकं ब्रह्म प्रतिपाद्यम्.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What do the witnesses show?

The base reads sarvebhyas sarva śarvebhyo; the Tirupati e-text reads sarvataḥ śarva sarvebhyo, which is also Sāyaṇa’s text and the reading every commentator explains. The sources page classes the place ‘verification required’. The hiatus te astu is kept by every witness.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • अघोरेभ्योऽथ aghorebhyo'tha → अघोरेभ्यः + अथ

    -aḥ before a

    aghorebhyaḥ + atha → aghorebhyo ’tha: -aḥ becomes -o and the following a is elided (P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति)).

  • घोरेभ्यो घोरघोरतरेभ्यः ghorebhyo ghoraghoratarebhyaḥ → घोरेभ्यः + घोरघोरतरेभ्यः

    -aḥ before a voiced consonant

    ghorebhyaḥ + ghora- → ghorebhyo ghora-.

  • सर्वेभ्यस्सर्व sarvebhyassarva → सर्वेभ्यः + सर्व

    visarga before s (Taittirīya spelling)

    sarvebhyaḥ + sarva → sarvebhyas sarva (P. 8.3.36 (वा शरि)); the recitation writes the double sibilant.

  • नमस्ते namaste → नमः + ते

    visarga before t

    namaḥ + te → namas te (P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः)).

Junctions outside the general rules

  • ते · अस्तु — unjoined hiatus (as printed): te · astu: classical te ’stu; every witness, and Sāyaṇa’s text, keeps the vowels apart.

Morphology

घोरघोरतरेभ्यः
ghoratara is the comparative of ghora; prefixed by ghora it means ‘more terrible than the terrible’. Bhāskara: घोरेभ्योपि घोरतरेभ्यस् त्वत्तोपि घोरतरेभ्यः.
सर्व
As printed the line reads sarvebhyas sarva śarvebhyo. The texts of Sāyaṇa and the Tirupati e-text read sarvataḥ śarva sarvebhyo, and all three commentators explain śarva as a vocative (‘O Śarva’) and sarvataḥ (‘everywhere’). The base appears to have the two words in each other’s places.

Anvaya

[हे] सर्व, ते अघोरेभ्यः अथ घोरेभ्यः घोरघोरतरेभ्यः सर्वेभ्यः रुद्ररूपेभ्यः शर्वेभ्यः नमः अस्तु ।

he sarva, te aghorebhyaḥ atha ghorebhyaḥ ghoraghoratarebhyaḥ sarvebhyaḥ rudrarūpebhyaḥ śarvebhyaḥ namaḥ astu |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A single sentence: the subject namaḥ with the imperative astu, and a string of datives naming whom the homage is for — the forms (rūpebhyaḥ) of Rudra, described as not-terrible, terrible and more terrible, and as all. The vocative (sarva as printed; śarva in the commentators’ texts) addresses the god himself; te (‘your’) makes the forms his.

Ambiguities

sarvebhyas sarva śarvebhyo
As printed the text addresses ‘O all’ and names ‘the Śarvas’. The commentators all read ‘O Śarva … to all’: Sāyaṇa हे शर्व परमेश्वर and सर्वेभ्यो रुद्ररूपेभ्यः; Bhāskara हे शर्व! ते त्वदीयेभ्यो ऽंशेभ्यः.
three kinds of form
The anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa match the three adjectives to the three guṇas: gentle as sattva, fierce as rajas, most terrible as tamas. The text gives the three degrees, not the guṇas.

Metre

irregular · syllables counted as written: 15 + 18

Close to an anuṣṭubh: the lines count 15 and 18 syllables (the first pāda of each has a syllable fewer or more than eight). Sāyaṇa’s order (sarvataḥ śarva sarvebhyo) has the same count.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Acceptance

The mantra bows equally to the gentle and to the terrible. Much of life asks for that kind of even regard toward what we would rather not meet. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.
