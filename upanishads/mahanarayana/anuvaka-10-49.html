Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 49 of 80

Hariṃ harantam: the gods follow Hari

Hari, whom the gods follow, the ruler of all and chief among minds, is asked to let the Veda come whole to the speaker, to spare the path the speaker has made, and to stride forth.

5 Faithful English translation

The gods follow Hari, who carries off, the ruler of all, the bull of thoughts. The sacred word, whole in its form, this, has come after me; do not destroy the course; stride forth.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

The gods follow Hari, who takes away, the ruler of all, chief among minds. May this sacred word come to me whole; do not destroy the path we have made; stride forth.

Key concepts वेद Veda मोक्ष liberation देवता deity

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse begins with praise. The gods follow Hari, a name the old commentator explains as Viṣṇu, the one who takes away the wrongs of those devoted to him. He rules everything, and he is the chief of all minds; the commentator adds that he moves the thoughts of every being, and quotes the Gāyatrī mantra’s line about the one who inspires our thoughts. Then come three requests. May the sacred word, the Veda, come to me whole and correct. Do not destroy the path I have made. Stride forth. The commentator explains the Veda as the four Vedas, the path as the way to liberation, and the last word as a request that Hari make the effort to give.

An analogy Like a student asking a teacher that the whole lesson, not a garbled piece of it, come through to them.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

That the gods follow Hari, ruler of all and chief of minds; it asks that the sacred word come to the speaker, that the path not be destroyed, and that Hari stride forth.

Traditional Interpretation

How does the commentator read it?

The anonymous commentator: Hari is Viṣṇu, who takes away his devotees’ sin; the gods follow him as servants; brahma is the four Vedas, correct in every form, and the speaker asks that by his grace they come to him; ayanam is the path to liberation.

Traditional Interpretation

A gloss to report as the commentator’s

The anonymous commentator describes this Veda as studied openly by all of the three varṇas (सर्वैस्त्रैवर्णिकैः प्रत्यक्षत्वेनाधीयमानम्). That is his gloss, reported as his; the verse itself says only ‘this’.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • विश्वस्येशानं viśvasyeśānaṃ → विश्वस्य + ईशानम्

    a + ī → e

    viśvasya + īśānam → viśvasyeśānam (P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः)).

  • अनु मेदमागादयनं anu medamāgādayanaṃ → अनु + मा + इदम् + आगात् + अयनम्

    ā + i → e; m and t before a vowel

    mā + idam → medam (P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः)); idam āgāt joins unchanged; āgāt + ayanam → āgād ayanam (P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते)).

  • विवधीर्विक्रमस्व vivadhīrvikramasva → विवधीः + विक्रमस्व

    visarga after ī before a voiced consonant

    The visarga of vivadhīḥ becomes r before v.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • सरूपम् sarūpam

    bahuvrīhi · स + रूप

    Vigraha समानानि रूपाणि यस्य तत्

    whose forms are even, whole in form

Morphology

मा
Two words are written मा in this line: the first is the enclitic pronoun ‘me’ (joined with idam to medam); the second is the prohibitive particle with vivadhīḥ.
आगात्
A root aorist of √gā with ā, separated from its preverb anu (tmesis); the commentator renders it as a wish (anvāgacchatu).
विवधीः
An aorist of the root vadh (supplying the aorist of √han), with vi, used with mā as a prohibition.

Anvaya

देवाः हरन्तम् विश्वस्य ईशानम् मतीनाम् वृषभम् हरिम् अनुयन्ति । इदम् सरूपम् ब्रह्म मा अनु आगात् । अयनम् मा विवधीः । विक्रमस्व ।

devāḥ harantam viśvasya īśānam matīnām vṛṣabham harim anuyanti | idam sarūpam brahma mā anu āgāt | ayanam mā vivadhīḥ | vikramasva |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A statement (anuyanti) with Hari as object and three descriptions; then a statement or wish with brahma as subject and the separated verb anu … āgāt; then a prohibition (mā vivadhīḥ) and an imperative (vikramasva), both addressed to Hari.

Ambiguities

harantam
The verse does not say what Hari carries off. The commentator: the sin of his devotees.
ayanam
Literally ‘course, path’. The commentator: the path to liberation that we have prepared.
vikramasva
‘Stride forth’, a word often used of Viṣṇu’s strides; the commentator explains it as ‘make the effort to give’.
vṛṣabhaṃ matīnām
The commentator: the chief of the minds of all beings, who moves them to merit and demerit, and he quotes the Gāyatrī: धियो यो नः प्रचोदयात्.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 22

One triṣṭubh verse printed in two lines of 22 syllables.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Receiving whole

The verse asks that what is handed down arrive whole, its forms intact. Anyone who has learnt something from a teacher knows how easily a lesson can be half-heard. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.
