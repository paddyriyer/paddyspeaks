Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 29 of 80

Āpo vā idaṃ sarvam: all this is water

A litany identifying the waters with everything — beings, breath, food, the cosmic sovereign, the Veda, truth, the gods and the three worlds — closed by Om.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

All this is water. Water is every being; water is breath; water is cattle, food and the undying. Water is the sovereign, the widely shining and the self-shining. Water is the metres, the lights and the sacred formulas; water is truth; water is all the gods. Earth, sky and heaven are water. Om.

Key concepts अमृत immortality, the deathless सत्य truth, the real व्याहृति the sacred calls ओम् Om

7 Explain it to a beginner

This passage is a hymn to water. Its first words say: all this is water. Then it goes through the world one item at a time and calls each one water: every living being, the breath, cattle, food, the undying, three names of the cosmic ruler, the metres and formulas of the Veda, truth, all the gods, and finally the three worlds — earth, sky and heaven. It ends with Om. The commentators explain each step: bodies come from seed, breath is refreshed by drinking, cattle turn water into milk, rain makes food grow. They also say why the hymn stands here: it is a prayer to the waters to turn away the hardships of drought. One commentator reads the whole as praise of the highest reality under the name of water. The text praises; it makes no promise.

An analogy Like tracing every dish on a table back to the same rain that fell on the fields.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

That water is all this; and then that water is each of a long list — beings, breaths, cattle, food, the immortal, the sovereign, the widely and the self-shining, the metres, the lights, the yajus formulas, truth, all the deities, and the three worlds — closing with Om.

Traditional Interpretation

Why is it recited?

The anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa call it a mantra to the waters that helps by warding off the troubles that come when rain fails: वृष्ट्यभावकृतोपद्रवपरिहारेणोपकारिणमब्देवताकं मन्त्रमाह. Bhāskara reads it as praise of Brahman as water, आपो वा इत्यादिनाऽप्त्वेन ब्रह्म स्तूयते, concluding ‘therefore let us worship them’, तस्मात्ता उपास्मह इति शेषः.

Traditional Interpretation

How can breath be water?

Sāyaṇa: because drinking nourishes the breaths, and he cites the Chāndogya, introducing it as what the Chāndogya reciters teach: अत एव च्छन्दोगा आमनन्ति.

Traditional Interpretation

Why the closing Om?

The anonymous commentator: Om is recited to show that the waters, as the supreme self, the root cause, are what the praṇava names: एताश्चाऽऽपो मूलकारणपरमात्मरूपेण प्रणवप्रतिपाद्या इति वक्तुमोंकारः पठितः. Bhāskara takes Om as the supreme self among the things named as water: ओं परमात्मा आप एव.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • आपो वा इदं āpo vā idaṃ → आपः + वै + इदम्

    -aḥ before v; ai before a vowel

    āpaḥ + vai → āpo vai; vai + idam: ai becomes āy before a vowel (P. 6.1.78 (एचोऽयवायावः)) and the y is dropped (P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य)): vā idam.

  • भूतान्यापः bhūtānyāpaḥ → भूतानि + आपः

    i before a dissimilar vowel

    bhūtāni + āpaḥ → bhūtāny āpaḥ (P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि)).

  • पशव आपो paśava āpo → पशवः + आपः

    -aḥ before a vowel other than a

    paśavaḥ + āpaḥ → paśava āpaḥ: the visarga becomes y and the y is dropped (P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य)).

  • आपोऽन्नमापोऽमृतमापः āpo'nnamāpo'mṛtamāpaḥ → आपः + अन्नम् + आपः + अमृतम् + आपः

    -aḥ before a; final m before a vowel

    āpaḥ + annam → āpo ’nnam, the a elided (P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति)); annam āpaḥ, with the m joined to the vowel.

  • सम्राडापो samrāḍāpo → सम्राट् + आपः

    final ṭ before a vowel

    A final ṭ becomes ḍ before a vowel (P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते)): samrāḍ āpaḥ.

  • आपश्छन्दाग्स्यापो āpaśchandāgsyāpo → आपः + छन्दाग्सि + आपः

    visarga before ch; i before a vowel

    The visarga becomes ś before ch (P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः), P. 8.4.40 (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः)): āpaś chandāṃsi; the recitation text writes the anusvāra before s as ग् (chandāgsi); chandāṃsi + āpaḥ → chandāṃsy āpaḥ.

  • सुवराप ओम् suvarāpa om → सुवः + आपः + ओम्

    suvaḥ before a vowel; -aḥ before o

    suvaḥ, whose final is r, keeps it before a vowel: suvar āpaḥ; āpaḥ + om → āpa om, the visarga dropped before the vowel.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this anuvāka.

Morphology

विश्वा
The Vedic neuter plural in -ā, for classical viśvāni; Bhāskara glosses विश्वा विश्वानि.
छन्दाग्सि
The Taittirīya recitation writes the anusvāra before a sibilant as ग् (chandāgsi, jyotīgṣi, yajūgṣi). The word is chandāṃsi; the padaccheda keeps the recitation text’s own spelling so that it rejoins to the text as printed.
सम्राट्
From samrāj, as virāj and svarāj: the final j becomes ṭ in the pausal form and ḍ before the following vowel.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

A chain of nominal identifications without a verb, each pairing āpaḥ (‘the waters’) with one thing: first āpo vai idaṃ sarvam (‘water, truly, is all this’), then one item after another, each with its āpaḥ, ending with the three worlds bhūr bhuvaḥ suvaḥ and Om.

Ambiguities

Why water?
The anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa give a reason for each identification — water as the seed of bodies, as what nourishes breath, as milk in cattle, as the cause of food. Bhāskara gives one reason for all: everything depends on water, सर्वमाप एव तदधीनत्वात्.
bhūtāni
Living bodies (Sāyaṇa) or the elements (Bhāskara).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Ecology and dependence

The litany follows water into everything: bodies, breath, milk, food, the gods themselves. Its commentators read it as a prayer against drought. A list that traces so much back to one resource is a sober reminder of how much depends on it. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Pick three items from the list. How would you explain, in your own words, that each one ‘is water’?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry The commentators give either a separate reason for each identification or one reason for all (dependence). Which kind of explanation suits a litany better?

A contemplation exercise Notice every use of water in one ordinary day, from waking to sleep.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
