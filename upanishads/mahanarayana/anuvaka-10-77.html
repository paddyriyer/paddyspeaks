Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 77 of 80

Śivena me santiṣṭhasva: stay with me

The one addressed as ‘sacrifice’ is asked to stay with the speaker, bringing welfare, comfort, prosperity and the radiance of sacred learning, and is given homage three times.

5 Faithful English translation

Abide with me with welfare; abide with me with comfort; abide with me with prosperity; abide with me with the lustre of sacred knowledge; abide following the success of the sacrifice. Close to you, O sacrifice, homage; close to you, homage; close to you, homage.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Stay with me, bringing welfare; stay with me, bringing comfort; stay with me, bringing prosperity; stay with me, bringing the radiance of sacred learning; stay, as the sacrifice prospers. Homage to you, O Sacrifice, close at hand — homage to you, homage to you.

Key concepts ब्रह्मन् Brahman कर्मन् action, ritual work

7 Explain it to a beginner

This is a request to stay. The speaker asks ‘you’ to remain with him, bringing welfare, comfort, prosperity and the radiance that comes with sacred learning, and to stay as the sacrifice succeeds. Then he offers homage three times, each time saying ‘close to you’. The one addressed is called ‘sacrifice’. The anonymous commentator, the only one in this edition who explains the line, takes this as the Lord whose form is every sacrifice, and reads the request as asking him to dwell in the speaker’s house so that the speaker may share those qualities. He explains the threefold homage as a sign of great respect. The edition reports this as his reading.

An analogy Like a host at the door asking a valued guest not to leave yet, and naming the good things the guest has brought into the house.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the formula say?

It asks the one addressed to stay with the speaker with welfare, comfort, prosperity and the lustre of sacred knowledge, and to stay following the success of the sacrifice; then it says three times ‘homage, close to you’, the first time addressing ‘sacrifice’.

Traditional Interpretation

Who is addressed, and what is asked?

The anonymous commentator: the Lord, whose form is all sacrifice (हे यज्ञ सर्वयज्ञस्वरूप), asked to dwell in the house with welfare and the rest (शिवेन कल्याणप्रदानेन सह मे गृहे), so that the speaker may share those qualities (एवंगुणवति त्वयि मद्गृह उपस्थिते सत्यहमप्येवंविधगुणवान्भूयासमिति प्रार्थयितुराशयः).

Traditional Interpretation

Why the threefold homage?

The repetition, says the anonymous commentator, is for great respect: अत्यादरार्थमाम्रेडितम्.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • यज्ञस्यर्द्धिमनु yajñasyarddhimanu → यज्ञस्य + ऋद्धिम् + अनु

    a + ṛ; final m before a vowel

    yajñasya + ṛddhim → yajñasyarddhim: a and ṛ fuse into ar; ṛddhim anu: the m is joined to the vowel.

  • सन्तिष्ठस्वोप santiṣṭhasvopa → सन्तिष्ठस्व + उप

    a + u

    santiṣṭhasva + upa → santiṣṭhasvopa: a and u fuse into o.

  • नम उप nama upa → नमः + उप

    visarga after a before a vowel other than a

    namaḥ + upa → nama upa: the visarga is dropped and the vowels stay apart.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • ब्रह्मवर्चसेन brahmavarcasena

    ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa · ब्रह्मन् + वर्चस्

    Vigraha ब्रह्मणः वर्चः

    the lustre that comes of sacred knowledge

Morphology

सन्तिष्ठस्व
Middle imperative of √sthā with sam-, ‘stay, remain, be established’; the root’s present stem is tiṣṭha-.
यज्ञ
Vocative: the sacrifice is addressed as a person; the anonymous commentator takes it as the Lord whose form is every sacrifice.

Anvaya

यज्ञ शिवेन मे सन्तिष्ठस्व । स्योनेन मे सन्तिष्ठस्व । सुभूतेन मे सन्तिष्ठस्व । ब्रह्मवर्चसेन मे सन्तिष्ठस्व । यज्ञस्य ऋद्धिम् अनु सन्तिष्ठस्व । ते उप नमः । उप ते नमः । उप ते नमः

yajña śivena me santiṣṭhasva | syonena me santiṣṭhasva | subhūtena me santiṣṭhasva | brahmavarcasena me santiṣṭhasva | yajñasya ṛddhim anu santiṣṭhasva | te upa namaḥ | upa te namaḥ | upa te namaḥ

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Five commands with the same verb, santiṣṭhasva: four with an instrumental of what is to accompany the one addressed and the enclitic me, the fifth with the postposition anu. Then three nominal clauses, upa te … namaḥ, the first with the vocative yajña.

Ambiguities

who is the ‘sacrifice’
The vocative yajña may address the rite itself or a deity identified with it; the anonymous commentator addresses the Lord whose form is every sacrifice, the supreme self (हे भगवन्).
me
Genitive or dative ‘with me / for me’; the anonymous commentator takes it as ‘in my house’.

Metre

prose · syllables counted as written: 62

A single yajus formula of 62 syllables.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Hospitality

The formula is phrased as an invitation to stay rather than a demand to give: it names what the guest brings and asks for its presence. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Which of the four things named — welfare, comfort, prosperity, the radiance of learning — would you most want to ‘stay’ in your home?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why might the text address ‘the sacrifice’ as someone who can stay or leave?

A contemplation exercise Name one good quality you would like to have stay with you, and one thing you could do to keep it.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
