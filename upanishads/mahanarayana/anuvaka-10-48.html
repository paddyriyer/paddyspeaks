Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 48 of 80

Amutrabhūyāt: freed from Yama’s claim

Bṛhaspati is praised for freeing the speaker from Yama’s claim and from disgrace, and Agni is told that the Aśvins, physicians of the gods, have pushed death away.

5 Faithful English translation

From being in the yonder world, then, from what is Yama’s, O Bṛhaspati, from curse you freed [me]. The two Aśvins pushed death back from us, the two physicians of the gods, O Agni, with their powers.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Bṛhaspati, you set me free from going to the other world, from what belongs to Yama, and from disgrace. Agni, the two Aśvins, physicians of the gods, drove death away from us by their skill.

Key concepts मृत्यु death अग्नि Agni, fire देवता deity

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse speaks to two gods. First it thanks Bṛhaspati, the lord of sacred speech, for having set the speaker free from going to the other world, from what belongs to Yama, the ruler of the dead, and from disgrace. Then it turns to Agni, the fire, and says that the two Aśvins, the twin physicians of the gods, have pushed death away from us. The old commentator reads these past tenses as requests: free me, push death away. He also reads one word as describing Agni himself as the gods’ physician, because by carrying offerings to them Agni takes away their hunger. Like the verses before it, this one asks that death keep its distance.

An analogy Like thanking a friend for a past rescue while asking for the same help again.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

That Bṛhaspati freed the speaker from the other world, from what is Yama’s and from disgrace, and that the Aśvins, physicians of the gods, pushed death away.

Traditional Interpretation

How does the commentator read it?

The anonymous commentator: Bṛhaspati, the lord of the Veda’s great words, is the supreme self; the speaker asks to be freed from the fear that belongs to Yama (यमस्य संबन्धि यद्भयं तस्मान्माममुञ्चो मोचय) and from disgrace, and then to be joined with the happiness of the other world.

Traditional Interpretation

Why is Agni a physician?

The anonymous commentator: because, by carrying the oblations, Agni removes the hunger of the gods (हविर्नयनद्वारा देवताक्षुन्निवारकत्वादग्नेर्भिषक्त्वं द्रष्टव्यम्).

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • अमुत्रभूयादध यद्यमस्य amutrabhūyādadha yadyamasya → अमुत्रभूयात् + अध + यत् + यमस्य

    t before a vowel and before y

    amutrabhūyāt + adha → amutrabhūyād adha; yat + yamasya → yad yamasya (P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते)).

  • अभिशस्तेरमुञ्चः abhiśasteramuñcaḥ → अभिशस्तेः + अमुञ्चः

    visarga after e before a vowel

    The visarga of abhiśasteḥ becomes r before a vowel.

  • प्रत्यौहताम् pratyauhatām → प्रति + औहताम्

    i before a dissimilar vowel

    prati + auhatām → pratyauhatām (P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि)).

  • मृत्युमस्मद्देवानामग्ने mṛtyumasmaddevānāmagne → मृत्युम् + अस्मत् + देवानाम् + अग्ने

    m before a vowel; t before d

    mṛtyum asmat join unchanged; asmat + devānām → asmad devānām (P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते)).

Junctions outside the general rules

  • बृहस्पते · अभिशस्तेः — unjoined hiatus (as printed): bṛhaspate · abhiśasteḥ: the e of the vocative kept before a without elision (classical bṛhaspate ’bhiśasteḥ); a Vedic hiatus (prakṛtibhāva, P. 6.1.115 (प्रकृत्यान्तःपादमव्यपरे)), printed so in every witness.

Morphology

अश्विना
The Vedic dual in -ā for -au (P. 7.1.39 (सुपां सुलुक्पूर्वसवर्णाऽऽच्छेयाडाड्यायाजालः)); the commentator glosses it aśvinau.
औहताम्
An augmented past of √ūh ‘to push’ (a + ūh → auh); with prati, ‘they pushed back’. The commentator renders it as a wish.

Anvaya

बृहस्पते [त्वम्] अमुत्रभूयात् यत् यमस्य [तस्मात्] अध अभिशस्तेः [माम्] अमुञ्चः । अग्ने देवानाम् भिषजा अश्विना शचीभिः मृत्युम् अस्मत् प्रति औहताम् ।

bṛhaspate tvam amutrabhūyāt yat yamasya tasmāt adha abhiśasteḥ mām amuñcaḥ | agne devānām bhiṣajā aśvinā śacībhiḥ mṛtyum asmat prati auhatām |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two sentences, each addressed to a god: Bṛhaspati, with the verb amuñcaḥ and three ablatives of what one is freed from; Agni, with the Aśvins as subject of praty-auhatām, death as object and asmat as the ablative ‘from us’.

Ambiguities

past or wish
The verbs are past (amuñcaḥ, auhatām): the verse states what the gods have done. The commentator renders both as requests (mocaya, dūrī kurvātām).
bhiṣajā
A dual, ‘the two physicians of the gods’, in apposition to the Aśvins; or, as the commentator takes it, an instrumental singular describing Agni, ‘by you, the physician of the gods’. He explains Agni as physician because, carrying the oblations, he takes away the gods’ hunger (हविर्नयनद्वारा देवताक्षुन्निवारकत्वादग्नेर्भिषक्त्वं द्रष्टव्यम्).
śacībhiḥ
In Vedic usage ‘with powers, with skill’; the commentator reads Śacī, Indra’s consort, in the plural.
amutrabhūyāt
An ablative, ‘from being in the other world’, that is, from death; the commentator, by a change of case, reads ‘with the happiness of the other world’.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Gratitude and asking

The verse remembers help already given before it asks again. Recalling what has already carried us through can steady us when we need help once more. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.
