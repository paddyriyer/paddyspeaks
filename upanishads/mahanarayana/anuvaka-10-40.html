Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 40 of 80

Brahma medhavā: the third trisuparṇa

The third trisuparṇa: brahman is reached by sacrifice; two Ṛgvedic verses praising Soma and the swan who dwells everywhere; the streams of ghee and the golden reed on which the fine-winged bird sits; and the brāhmaṇa lines.

1 Original Sanskrit

ब्रह्म मेधवा ।मधु मेधवा ।ब्रह्ममेव मधु मेधवा ।ब्रह्मा देवानां पदवीः कवीनामृषिर्विप्राणां महिषो मृगाणाम् ।श्येनो गृद्ध्राणाग् स्वधितिर्वनानां सोमः पवित्रमत्येति रेभन् ।हंसः शुचिषद्वसुरन्तरिक्षसद्धोता वेदिषदतिथिर्दुरोणसत् ।नृषद्वरसदृतसद्व्योमसदब्जा गोजा ऋतजा अद्रिजा ऋतं बृहत् ।ऋचे त्वा रुचे त्वा समित्स्रवन्ति सरितो न धेनाः ।अन्तर्हृदा मनसा पूयमानाः ।घृतस्य धारा अभिचाकशीमि ।हिरण्ययो वेतसो मद्ध्य आसाम् ।तस्मिन्त्सुपर्णो मधुकृत्कुलायी भजन्नास्ते मधु देवताभ्यः ।तस्यासते हरयः सप्त तीरे स्वधां दुहाना अमृतस्य धाराम् ।य इदं त्रिसुपर्णमयाचितं ब्राह्मणाय दद्यात् ।वीरहत्यां वा एते घ्नन्ति ।ये ब्राह्मणास्त्रिसुपर्णं पठन्ति ।ते सोमं प्राप्नुवन्ति ।आसहस्रात्पङ्क्तिं पुनन्ति ।ओम् ॥ ५८ ॥

5 Faithful English translation

Brahman by sacrifice; sweetness by sacrifice; brahman itself, sweetness, by sacrifice. The brahman among the gods, the path-finder among poets, the seer among the inspired, the buffalo among wild beasts, the falcon among birds of prey, the axe among woods — Soma goes through the filter, singing. The swan dwelling in the bright, the Vasu dwelling in the mid-air, the offerer seated at the altar, the guest dwelling in the house, dwelling among men, in the choicest place, in the right order, in the sky; born of the waters, of the cows, of the right order, of the rock — the right order, great. For the verse, you; for brightness, you; the fuel-stick. They flow like rivers, streams, purified within, by heart and mind; I behold the streams of ghee. A golden reed in the middle of them; on it the fine-winged one, honey-maker, nesting, sits apportioning honey to the deities; on its bank sit the seven bay ones, milking the svadhā, the stream of the deathless. Whoever gives this trisuparṇa unasked to a brāhmaṇa — these truly destroy [the sin of] killing a hero. The brāhmaṇas who recite the trisuparṇa attain soma. They purify the row up to a thousand. Om.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Brahman is reached through sacrifice; so is the sweet; so is Brahman itself, the sweet. As the chief priest among the gods, the path-finder among poets, the buffalo among beasts, the falcon among birds, the axe among the trees, so Soma passes singing through the filter. The swan in the bright sky, the god in mid-air, the priest at the altar, the guest in the house, at home among men and in the highest place, in the true order and in the sky, born of water, earth, truth and rock — he is the great order. For the verse and for its brightness, this fuel-stick. The streams of ghee, made pure within by the heart and the mind, flow like rivers, and I watch them. In their midst stands a golden reed; on it sits the fine-winged bird that makes honey and shares it with the gods; on its bank sit the seven, drawing out the stream of the deathless. The text then promises, as before, that those who teach this unasked destroy the sin of killing a hero, attain soma, and purify a row of a thousand. Om.

7 Explain it to a beginner

The third trisuparṇa says that Brahman, the sweet, is reached through sacrifice. One commentator, Bhāskara, sees an order in the three: the first spoke of meditation, the second of knowledge, the third of action. Two old verses follow. The first lists the best of every kind — the chief priest among the gods, the falcon among birds, the axe among the trees — and says Soma passes singing through the filter. The second calls the divine swan the one who lives everywhere: in the bright sky, at the altar, in the house, among people. Then comes a picture: streams of ghee, made pure by the heart and mind, flowing like rivers, a golden reed in their midst, and on it a bird that makes honey and shares it with the gods. The text ends with its usual promise to those who teach and recite it.

An analogy Like three ways to the same house — studying the map, knowing the road, and walking it — each called by the same name.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

That brahman, the sweet, is reached by sacrifice; it praises Soma among the best of all kinds and the swan who dwells everywhere; it offers the fuel-stick to the verse and to brightness; it describes the streams of ghee, the golden reed in their midst and the fine-winged bird on it, with the seven on its bank; and it states the reward of the trisuparṇa.

Traditional Interpretation

How do the three trisuparṇa differ?

Bhāskara: the first taught meditation as the means of reaching the highest, the second knowledge, and the third now teaches action: तत्र प्रथमे उपासना परप्राप्तिहेतुरुक्ता । द्वितीये ज्ञानम् । इदानीं कर्मणः परप्राप्तिसाधनत्वमुच्यते. Sāyaṇa: medha is sacrifice, and the knowledge of brahman comes after the desire to know, which sacrifice and giving produce (यज्ञदानादिनाध्यविविदिषा).

Traditional Interpretation

What is the trisuparṇa itself?

The anonymous commentator: the supreme self, from whom the three ‘fine-winged ones’ arise — Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Maheśvara, or other triads — and the text is called by its name because it states his greatness: तन्महिमप्रतिपादकत्वादयं ग्रन्थोऽपि त्रिसुपर्णशब्देनोपचर्यते.

Traditional Interpretation

Who are the seven on the bank?

The anonymous commentator: the seven ṛṣis around the Lord, called hari because remembering them takes away sin: हरन्ति पापानि स्मरणमात्रेणेति हरयः.

Traditional Interpretation

Who is the ‘hero’ whose killing is named?

Sāyaṇa: a brāhmaṇa devoted to the Veda and its practice, or an anointed king (vīra); Bhāskara: a virtuous son, or, as others say, an anointed kṣatriya (गुणवान् पुत्रो वीरः । अभिषिक्तः क्षत्रिय इत्यन्ये). The anonymous commentator ranks the three sins of the three trisuparṇa in ascending gravity (10.41).

Traditional Interpretation

Where are the two Ṛgvedic verses explained?

Sāyaṇa says the verses brahmā devānām and haṃsaḥ śuciṣat were explained in the anuvāka aṇor aṇīyān: इत्यादिके द्वे ऋचावणोरणीयानित्यनुवाके व्याख्याते. The anonymous commentator says the same of them: ब्रह्मा देवानां ह ँसः शुचिषदिति द्वे ऋचावणोरणीयानित्यस्मिन्ननुवाके व्याख्याते.

Where they disagree

No Vedānta-school commentary on this text is in the edition’s sources. Bhāskara’s ordering of meditation, knowledge and action, and his insistence that brahman is not reached without action, is his own reading; the anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa read medhavā of sacrifice that leads to the desire to know. 10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • कवीनामृषिर्विप्राणां kavīnāmṛṣirviprāṇāṃ → कवीनाम् + ऋषिः + विप्राणाम्

    final m before a vowel; i-visarga before a voiced sound

    m joined to the vowel; ṛṣiḥ + viprāṇām → ṛṣir viprāṇām.

  • गृद्ध्राणाग् स्वधितिर् gṛddhrāṇāg svadhitir → गृद्ध्राणाम् + स्वधितिः

    final m before a sibilant

    The m becomes anusvāra before s (P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः)); the Taittirīya recitation writes it ग्.

  • पवित्रमत्येति pavitramatyeti → पवित्रम् + अत्येति

    final m before a vowel; i + e

    ati + eti → atyeti (P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि)), a preverb joined to its verb.

  • अन्तरिक्षसद्धोता antarikṣasaddhotā → अन्तरिक्षसत् + होता

    final t before h

    t becomes d before a voiced sound, and the h that follows may become dh (P. 8.4.62 (झयो होऽन्यतरस्याम्)).

  • अन्तर्हृदा antarhṛdā → अन्तः + हृदा

    r-visarga before a voiced sound

    antaḥ stands for antar, whose r shows before h.

  • मद्ध्य आसाम् maddhya āsām → मद्ध्ये + आसाम्

    e before a vowel

    maddhye + āsām → maddhya āsām: e becomes ay and the y drops (P. 6.1.78 (एचोऽयवायावः), P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य)).

  • तस्मिन्त्सुपर्णो tasmintsuparṇo → तस्मिन् + सुपर्णः

    n before s

    A t may be inserted between final n and s (P. 8.3.30 (नश्च)).

  • भजन्नास्ते bhajannāste → भजन् + आस्ते

    n after a short vowel before a vowel

    The n is doubled (P. 8.3.32 (ङमो ह्रस्वादचि ङमुण्नित्यम्)).

  • ब्राह्मणास्त्रिसुपर्णं brāhmaṇāstrisuparṇaṃ → ब्राह्मणाः + त्रिसुपर्णम्

    visarga before t

    brāhmaṇāḥ + trisuparṇam → brāhmaṇās trisuparṇam: the visarga becomes s before t (P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः)).

  • आसहस्रात्पङ्क्तिं āsahasrātpaṅktiṃ → आ + सहस्रात् + पङ्क्तिम्

    words written together

    ā and sahasrāt are written as one; final t stays before p.

Morphology

मेधवा
From medhavat ‘having sacrifice’ (Sāyaṇa); Bhāskara explains the ending ā as the case ending of the vat-stem replaced by the rule P. 7.1.39 (सुपां सुलुक्पूर्वसवर्णाऽऽच्छेयाडाड्यायाजालः).
रुचे
The Ānandāśrama print marks this reading as adopted from two manuscripts, where others read ṛce again (see flags).
अत्येति
The preverb ati written with its verb; Soma ‘goes through’ the filter.

Anvaya

Syntax

Three verbless statements with medhavā; a verse listing the best of each kind (ṣaṣṭhī of the group with a nominative) and ending in a finite verb (atyeti); a verse of nominative epithets ending in ṛtaṃ bṛhat; two datives with tvā and a nominative samit, the verb supplied; a sequence of clauses with stream-imagery: a plural verb (sravanti), a first-person verb (abhicākaśīmi), a verbless statement (hiraṇyayo vetasaḥ …), and two sentences with āste / āsate. Then the brāhmaṇa.

Ambiguities

medhavā
Sāyaṇa and the anonymous commentator: medha is sacrifice, so brahman is reached by those who perform sacrifice; Bhāskara: by action such as the jyotiṣṭoma, and not without it (न विना कर्मणा). He sets the three trisuparṇa in order: meditation, knowledge, action (see commentary).
the golden reed and the bird
The anonymous commentator reads the reed as the fire in which the streams are offered and the fine-winged one as the trisuparṇa, the supreme self, apportioning the offerings to the gods, with the seven ṛṣis around him; the text leaves the image without a gloss of its own.

Metre

Prose formulas; two Ṛgvedic verses (Bhāskara: ब्रह्मा देवानामिति त्रिष्टुप्, हंसश्शुचिषदित्य् अतिजगती); verse lines of the streams and the reed; and the brāhmaṇa in prose.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Many paths

Bhāskara reads the three passages as three means to one end — attention, understanding and action. Whether one approaches a goal by contemplating it, by learning about it or by working for it, each can be honoured. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin The verse names the best of each kind. What would you name as ‘the best’ of a few things you know well?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Bhāskara says brahman is not reached without action. How does doing something change what one understands about it?

A contemplation exercise Pick one thing you understand well and do one small act that puts it into practice today.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.

Further notes

  • Sāyaṇa (his 50) and Bhāskara (his 50) comment on this anuvāka; the concordance maps both correctly. The verse brahmā devānām is also Ṛgveda 9.96.6, and the lines from ghṛtasya dhārā to the golden reed recall Ṛgveda 4.58.5; the edition’s parallel sources do not contain those maṇḍalas.
  • The effects claimed for giving and reciting the trisuparṇa are the text’s own and are reported as such.

Uncertainties recorded

  • review ṛce tvā ruce tvā: the Tirupati e-text reads ṛce tvā ṛce tvā; the Ānandāśrama print marks ruce as adopted from two manuscripts while others read ṛce (corrections.json, review). The base reading ruce is kept.
  • review maddhye / madhye and gṛddhrāṇām / gṛdhrāṇām: the two other witnesses agree on the single consonant where the base doubles it (corrections.json, review); a spelling of the recitation, the words unchanged.
  • interpretation The image of the streams, the reed and the bird is read by the anonymous commentator as the fire-offering and the supreme self; other readings are possible.
