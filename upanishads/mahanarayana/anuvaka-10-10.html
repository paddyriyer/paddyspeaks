Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 10 of 80

Ṛtaṃ tapaḥ: what austerity is

A list of virtues and rites, each called austerity, ending with meditation on Brahman as the austerity.

5 Faithful English translation

Right is austerity; truth is austerity; learning is austerity; calm is austerity; restraint is austerity; tranquillity is austerity; giving is austerity; sacrifice is austerity. Earth, mid-air, heaven: this Brahman is to be meditated on — this is austerity.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

True thought is austerity; true speech, learning, calm, self-restraint, quiet of mind, giving and sacrifice are each austerity. But to meditate on Brahman, which is earth, mid-air and heaven, that is austerity.

Key concepts तपस् austerity; disciplined effort ऋत the right; cosmic and moral order सत्य truth, the real ब्रह्मन् Brahman व्याहृति the sacred calls उपासना worship, meditation

7 Explain it to a beginner

A list that redefines a word. Tapas usually means harsh discipline or self-denial. Here eight ordinary goods are each called tapas: true thinking, true speaking, learning, calm, self-restraint, quiet of mind, giving and sacrifice. Then the list turns: Brahman, which is earth, the space between and heaven, is to be meditated on, and that meditation is tapas. Sāyaṇa explains each term: true thinking is seeing things as they are, giving is truly letting go of what was yours. He ranks the eight as a lower discipline and the meditation as the highest; Bhāskara treats the eight as the limbs of the meditation. The anonymous commentator adds that the mind’s one-pointedness is the highest tapas. The record reports these readings and claims nothing about effects.

An analogy Like a coach who lists drills — running, lifting, stretching — and then says that what all of them are for is the game itself.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It calls right, truth, learning, calm, restraint, tranquillity, giving and sacrifice each tapas, and ends: earth, mid-air, heaven — this Brahman is to be meditated on; this is tapas.

Traditional Interpretation

What is tapas here?

The anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa define tapas as the one-pointedness of mind and senses, the means to knowledge, citing a verse they call śruti: मनसश्चेन्द्रियाणां च ह्यैकाग्र्यं परमं तपः. The anuvāka praises that tapas as present in all the rites of śruti and smṛti, or gives a japa to achieve it.

Traditional Interpretation

How do the eight relate to the meditation?

Sāyaṇa calls the eight a lower austerity and the meditation the highest: तदेतत्सर्वमर्वाचीनं तपः and तदेतदुपासनमुत्तमं तपः; the Brahman meditated on is Virāj, the three worlds (भूरादिलोकत्रयात्मकं विराहरूपं यद्ब्रह्मास्ति). Bhāskara makes the eight the limbs of the meditation and quotes the Taittirīya, ‘seek to know Brahman by tapas; tapas is Brahman’: तपसा ब्रह्म विजिज्ञासस्व । तपो ब्रह्म.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What do the witnesses show?

The base writes सत्त्यं; the Ānandāśrama text and the Tirupati e-text have सत्यं, and the base’s spacing differs from theirs (listed for review). The Tirupati e-text prints the whole anuvāka a second time with उपास्व for उपास्य; the sources page records it as an addition. The base’s upāsya is kept.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • ऋतं तपस्सत्त्यं ṛtaṃ tapassattyaṃ → ऋतम् + तपः + सत्त्यम्

    m before a consonant; visarga before s

    ṛtam becomes ṛtaṃ before t; tapaḥ before s is written tapas s (Taittirīya doubling).

  • दमस्तपः damastapaḥ → दमः + तपः

    visarga before t

    A visarga becomes s before t.

  • सुवर्ब्रह्मैतदुपास्यैतत्तपः suvarbrahmaitadupāsyaitattapaḥ → सुवः + ब्रह्म + एतत् + उपास्य + एतत् + तपः

    suvaḥ before b; a + e; t before a vowel; a + e; t before t

    suvar brahma; brahma + etat → brahmaitat (a + e = ai); etad upāsya (t → d before a vowel); upāsya + etat → upāsyaitat; etat tapaḥ.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this anuvāka.

Morphology

उपास्य
The base reads upāsya, the gerundive ‘to be meditated on’ (or a gerund, ‘having meditated’); the Tirupati e-text, in a second copy of the anuvāka it prints, has उपास्व, and both Sāyaṇa and Bhāskara gloss the imperative upāssva, ‘meditate!’. The meaning is close; the base is kept.
सत्त्यम्
The base writes the double tt (सत्त्यं); the other witnesses have सत्यं. The word is satyam.
यज्ञम्
Neuter in form here, matching the neuter nouns of the list; yajña is usually masculine.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

Eight equations of the form X tapaḥ (‘X is austerity’), then the vyāhṛtis with brahma etad upāsya and a final equation, etat tapaḥ, which the commentators read as the climax.

Ambiguities

upāsya or upāssva
The base’s upāsya can be a gerundive (‘is to be meditated on’) or a gerund (‘having meditated’); the commentators gloss the imperative upāssva. The final etat tapaḥ then means ‘this meditation is the austerity’.
how the list relates to the end
Sāyaṇa ranks the first eight as lesser austerity and the meditation as the highest (तदेतत्सर्वमर्वाचीनं तपः and तदेतदुपासनमुत्तमं तपः); Bhāskara makes them its limbs (ऋतादीनि त्वस्याङ्गतया कुरु). The text only lists them side by side.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Discipline redefined

The list takes a word that usually means hardship and attaches it to honesty, learning, generosity and calm. It suggests that the hardest discipline is not pain but steady goodness. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Which item on the list would be the hardest discipline for you, and why?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry If every virtue is tapas, what makes meditation on Brahman the tapas? Do the commentators answer differently?

A contemplation exercise Choose one item — true speech, giving, calm — and practise it deliberately for one day.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
