महानारायणोपनिषत्Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad

The tenth prapāṭhaka of the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka: hymns, prayers, the mantras of daily worship, and the teaching of renunciation

Unlike the Upaniṣads that set out one argument, this one gathers what a householder and a renunciant recite: the hymn to the one who stands ‘beyond the waters’, the gāyatrīs of the gods, the Nārāyaṇa hymn, the five ‘brahma’ mantras, the mantras of the morning, midday and evening worship, homa formulas, and at the end the teaching that for one who knows, life itself is the sacrifice.

One prapāṭhaka, several recensions

Academic History

The Mahānārāyaṇa is the tenth and last prapāṭhaka of the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka. Its commentators call it by more than one name: Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara closes each of his chapters इति याज्ञिकीषूपनिषत्सु प्रथमोऽनुवाकः, ‘in the Yājñikī Upaniṣads’, the Upaniṣads of the sacrifice.

It has come down in recensions that differ in length and order. The text given here has eighty anuvākas; Sāyaṇa comments on a text of sixty-four, and Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara on another of sixty-four (the Drāviḍa), which order some sections differently and lack the long block of homa and japa mantras found here in anuvākas 51–64. Another recension, in twenty-five khaṇḍas, is transmitted among the Upaniṣads of the Atharvaveda. This edition does not choose among the recensions’ readings silently: it gives one text and lists every difference of its witnesses.

The text and how it was verified

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

The base is the 80-anuvāka recitation text with svara, divided by its own marks into 101 units. Each anuvāka is collated letter by letter with the same text as the Ānandāśrama edition (1927) prints it in the pariśiṣṭa of its Taittirīya Āraṇyaka, and with the e-text of the Rāṣṭriya Saṃskṛta Vidyāpīṭha, Tirupati.

Where the two other witnesses agree with each other and not with the base, the place is marked for verification against the printed books; where one stands alone, its reading is classed as its own error or variant; differences of spelling only — the visarga written as a doubled sibilant, the anusvāra written ग्ं in the Taittirīya manner — are listed as such. No reading of the base has been changed. The index line (anukramaṇī) that the base prints after each anuvāka is kept with the witness, not in the text.

The mantras of the sandhyā

Traditional Interpretation

Seven consecutive anuvākas hold mantras of the daily worship at the three junctions of the day. The anonymous commentator says what each is for: anuvāka 30 is माध्याह्निकसंध्यानुष्ठानेऽभिमन्त्रितजलपानार्थं मन्त्रमाह, the mantra for sipping consecrated water in the midday worship; anuvāka 31 सायंसंध्याकाले जलपानार्थं मन्त्रमाह, for the evening; anuvāka 32 प्रातःसंध्याकाले जलपानार्थं मन्त्रमाह, for the morning.

Anuvāka 33 states that Om is the one syllable; anuvākas 34 and 35 invite the gāyatrī — the commentator introduces 34 with गायत्र्या आवाहनमन्त्रमाह; and anuvāka 36, जपादूर्ध्वं गायत्रीविसर्जनमन्त्रमाह, bids it farewell after the japa. The other mantras of the sandhyā — the Gāyatrī itself, āpo hi ṣṭhā, the hymns to Mitra and Varuṇa — come from the Saṃhitās, not from this Upaniṣad.

Where the text begins and ends

Direct Textual Meaning

It opens with a hymn to the one who is अम्भस्य पारे भुवनस्य मध्ये, beyond the waters, in the middle of the world, and closes with a teaching of the one who knows, whose life is a sacrifice: तस्यैवं विदुषो यज्ञस्यात्मा यजमानः — of the one who knows thus, the sacrificer is the self of the sacrifice.

The commentaries

Academic History

The Ānandāśrama edition prints with the 80-anuvāka text an anonymous bhāṣya; its editor says he does not know who wrote it and that it largely follows Sāyaṇa. It is the only commentary here on this text in this order, and it is cited as anonymous. Sāyaṇa’s Vedārthaprakāśa and Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara’s Jñānayajña comment on the 64-anuvāka recensions; each anuvāka page names the anuvāka of theirs that corresponds, found by the wording the texts share. A commentary of Raṅgarāmānuja on this Upaniṣad is known but is not among the sources of this edition, so it is not cited.

A beginner’s reading guide

  1. Anuvākas 1–2: the opening hymn, the gāyatrīs and the hymn to Durgā. The first anuvāka is the longest: the hymn to the one beyond the waters, then the gāyatrīs of many deities and the mantras of the ritual bath; the second is the hymn ‘jātavedase sunavāma’.
  2. Anuvākas 13–21: the Nārāyaṇa hymn and the five ‘brahma’ mantras. The hymn ‘sahasraśīrṣaṃ devam’ and the mantras beginning sadyojātam, vāmadeva, aghora, tatpuruṣa and īśāna.
  3. Anuvākas 30–36: the mantras of the sandhyā. The mantras sipped at midday, in the evening and in the morning; the one syllable; the invitation and the farewell of the gāyatrī.
  4. Anuvākas 51–80: homa and japa mantras, the purifications, and the close. A block of mantras that the 64-anuvāka recensions lack, then the teaching that ends the Upaniṣad.
