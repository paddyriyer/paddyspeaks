Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 66 of 80

Pṛthivyāpas tejo vāyur ākāśā: the purification of the elements and the sheaths

The virajā offerings continue from the elements and the senses’ objects to the acts of mind, speech and body, the self, inner self and supreme self, short offerings to hunger and the creator, a casting-out of misfortune, and the five sheaths.

7 Explain it to a beginner

This long anuvāka continues the purifying offerings of the one before. Each short formula names something to be made pure — the five elements the body is made of; sound, touch, form, taste and smell; whatever one has done in thought, word and body — and repeats the wish: may I become light, free of stain, free of wrong. Then come brief offerings to hunger and thirst and to names of the creator, a line driving out misfortune, pictured as the elder sister of the goddess of fortune, and finally the five ‘sheaths’ of the self known from the Taittirīya Upaniṣad, from the body made of food to the self made of bliss. Bhāskara places these among the offerings of renunciation. The edition reports that use and makes no claim about what the rite achieves.

An analogy Like the last pages of an inventory that move from the furniture to the foundations and end with the owner’s name.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It asks that the elements, the objects of the senses, the acts of mind, speech and body, the self, inner self and supreme self, and the five sheaths be purified, each time with the refrain ‘may I become light, free of stain, free of evil, svāhā’; it makes offerings to hunger, hunger-and-thirst, the all-pervading, the maker of the verses and the one eager to make; and it casts out the stain of hunger and thirst, Misfortune, want and evil.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Bhāskara group these formulas?

As in 10.65, he gives each list an anuvāka of its own in his recension: the five objects of sense, शब्दादिगन्धान्तपञ्चविषयशुद्धिः पञ्चपञ्चाशे; the five elements, पृथिव्याद्याकाशान्तपञ्चभूतशुद्धिः षट्पञ्चाशे; the five sheaths, अन्नमयाद्यानन्दमयान्तपञ्चकोशशुद्धिः सप्तपञ्चाशे. He reads viviṭṭi as ‘pervade the universe, like Viṣṇu’, विविट्टि व्याप्नुहि विश्वं विष्णुरिवेति भावः, and kaṣotka as ghaṣotka, from the root of devouring, घस्ल् अदने. The refrain he glosses once, where the series begins (his 51; see 10.65).

Traditional Interpretation

How does Sāyaṇa read the names of the Supreme?

viviṭṭi is Brahman whose pervasion is complete, विशेषेण विष्टिर्व्याप्तिर्यस्य ब्रह्मणस्तद्विविष्टिः, with ṭ for ṣ by Vedic licence, छान्दसः षकारस्य टकारः; kaṣa is the made world, कष्यते क्रियत इति कषो नामरूपकर्मात्मकः कार्यप्रपञ्चः, and kaṣotka the Lord eager to make it.

Traditional Interpretation

What does the anonymous commentator say on the other formulas?

He reads the acts as those of mind, speech and body, मनोवाक्कायानां कर्माणि; avyaktabhāvair ahaṅkāraiḥ as a wish to be freed from empty pride, अहंकारैर्व्यर्थगर्वैर्विमुक्तो भूयासम्; ātmā as the body, with the plural verb explained as Vedic usage, प्रायःपाठात्सर्वत्र बहुवचनप्रयोगश्छान्दसः; the bare datives as offerings to the deities the words name, एतदादिवक्ष्यमाणपञ्चमन्त्रेषु लिङ्गोक्ता देवता हविर्भुजः; ṛgvidhāna as the Supreme who brings forth the verses, ऋचो विदधाति करोत्युत्पादयतीति ऋग्विधानः परमात्मा; and Alakṣmī as the elder sister of Lakṣmī, लक्ष्म्या ज्येष्ठामग्रजामलक्ष्मीरलक्ष्मीम्. See ambiguities for his answer to how the supreme self can be purified. On viviṭṭi and kaṣotka his wording runs close to Sāyaṇa’s.

Morphology

आकाशाः
Nominative plural of ākāśa, ‘space’, in a list of elements otherwise singular (pṛthivī, tejaḥ, vāyuḥ) and plural (āpaḥ); the plural matches the plural verb śudhyantām.
आत्मा
A singular subject with the plural verb śudhyantām; the anonymous commentator explains: प्रायःपाठात्सर्वत्र बहुवचनप्रयोगश्छान्दसः — the plural throughout is Vedic usage, following the recurrent wording.
क्षुत्पिपासाय
Dative in -āya from kṣutpipāsā (feminine), as printed; read as a Vedic or formulaic form.
विविट्ट्यै
Dative of viviṭṭi; Sāyaṇa derives it from √viṣ ‘pervade’ with ṭ for ṣ by Vedic licence: छान्दसः षकारस्य टकारः. Bhāskara reads it as an imperative of the same root, ‘pervade!’: विविट्टि व्याप्नुहि विश्वं विष्णुरिवेति भावः.
कषोत्काय
Sāyaṇa: kaṣa, the made world, + utka, ‘eager’: the Lord eager to create. Bhāskara reads ghaṣotka, from √ghas ‘devour’: the one who swallows the three worlds (घस्ल् अदने).
अलक्ष्मीः
Nominative form used as the object of nāśayāmi; the commentator glosses it with the accusative अलक्ष्मीम्.
विरजाः
Nominative singular masculine of the s-stem virajas, ‘without rajas (dust, stain; the quality that drives to action)’. Bhāskara: विरजा विरजस्कः.
भूयासम्
Benedictive (precative) 1st singular of √bhū: ‘may I become’.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

Fifteen formulas. Seven share the refrain of 10.65 (‘may my … be purified; may I become light … svāhā’) with a new subject each time; one (avyaktabhāvair ahaṅkārair …) replaces the subject with an instrumental of what one is to be freed from. Five are bare datives with svāhā. Then a first-person sentence (… nāśayāmy aham) and an imperative addressed to the deity (… nirṇuda me pāpmānam), and the last formula returns to the refrain with the five sheaths as subject.

Ambiguities

how the supreme self can be purified
The anonymous commentator raises it: ननु परमात्मनो नित्यशुद्धत्वात्कथं तच्छुद्धिः प्रार्थ्यत इति चेन्न — the supreme self is ever pure; how can its purification be asked? His answer: impurity means its not being recognised, owing to ignorance, and purity its being known as one’s own self: अविद्यादोषवशेन परमात्मनोऽप्रतिमानमशुद्धिरित्युच्यते, स्वात्मत्वेन प्रतिभानं शुद्धिरिति विशेषाङ्गीकारात्.
viviṭṭi and kaṣotka
Sāyaṇa and Bhāskara derive both words differently (see morphology); both read them as names of the Supreme.
the extra ‘om svāhā’
The Tirupati e-text adds ॐ स्वाहा after the bare datives; the base and the Ānandāśrama text lack it. Sāyaṇa (his 61) and Bhāskara (his 61) both comment on an ‘om svāhā’ formula in their recensions, so the addition may reflect a recension rather than a copying error.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Self-knowledge

The list moves from what a person is made of (elements) to what reaches them (sense objects), what they do (acts of mind, speech and body), what they are (self, inner self, highest self), and how a person can be described in layers (the five sheaths). It is an old and systematic map of human experience. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.
