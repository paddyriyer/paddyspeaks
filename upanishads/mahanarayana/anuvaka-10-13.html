Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 13 of 80

Sahasraśīrṣaṃ devam: the Nārāyaṇa hymn and the flame in the heart

A hymn to Nārāyaṇa as the all, the highest light, brahman, the meditator and the meditation, followed by the place of meditation: the downward-facing lotus of the heart, the fire within it, and the supreme Self in the middle of its flame.

1 Original Sanskrit

सहस्रशीर्षं देवं विश्वाक्षं विश्वशंभुवम् ।विश्वं नारायणं देवमक्षरं परमं पदम् ।विश्वतः परमान्नित्यं विश्वं नारायणं हरिम् ।विश्वमेवेदं पुरुषस्तद्विश्वमुपजीवति ।पतिं विश्वस्यात्मेश्वरं शाश्वतं शिवमच्युतम् ।नारायणं महाज्ञेयं विश्वात्मानं परायणम् ।नारायण परो ज्योतिरात्मा नारयणः परः ।नारायण परं ब्रह्मतत्त्वं नारायणः परः ।नारायण परो ध्याता ध्यानं नारायणः परः ।यच्च किंचिज्जगत्सर्वं दृश्यते श्रूयतेऽपि वा ॥ २९ ॥

अन्तर्बहिश्च तत्सर्वं व्याप्य नारायणस्स्थितः ।अनन्तमव्ययं कविं समुद्रेऽन्तं विश्वशम्भुवम् ।पद्मकोशप्रतीकाशं हृदयं चाप्यधोमुखम् ।अधो निष्ट्या वितस्त्यान्ते नाभ्यामुपरि तिष्ठति ।ज्वालमालाकुलं भाती विश्वस्यायतनं महत् ।सन्ततं शिलाभिस्तु लम्बत्याकोशसन्निभम् ।तस्यान्ते सुषिरं सूक्ष्मं तस्मिन्त्सर्वं प्रतिष्ठितम् ।तस्य मध्ये महानग्निर्विश्वार्चिर्विश्वतोमुखः ।सोऽग्रभुग्विभजन्तिष्ठन्नाहारमजरः कविः ।तिर्यगूर्ध्वमधः शायी रश्मयस्तस्य सन्तता ।सन्तापयति स्वं देहमापादतलमस्तकः ।तस्य मध्ये वह्निशिखा अणीयोर्ध्वा व्यवस्थितः ।नीलतोयदमध्यस्थाद्विद्युल्लेखेव भास्वरा ।नीवारशूकवत्तन्वी पीता भास्वत्यणूपमा ।तस्याः शिखाया मध्ये परमात्मा व्यवस्थितः ।स ब्रह्म स शिवः स हरिः सेन्द्रः सोऽक्षरः परमस्स्वराट् ॥ ३० ॥

7 Explain it to a beginner

This is one of the most widely recited passages of the Mahānārāyaṇa, often called the Nārāyaṇa Sūkta. It begins by praising Nārāyaṇa: he has a thousand heads and eyes everywhere, he is the source of the world’s good, the imperishable, the highest goal. Everything is that Person. He is the highest light, the highest reality, even the one who meditates and the meditation itself, and he fills whatever is seen or heard, inside and out. Then the hymn tells where to look for him: in the heart, which it pictures as a lotus bud hanging downward, a span above the navel. In the heart burns a great fire — the warmth that digests food and keeps the body warm — and in that fire a tiny flame, thin as the tip of a grain of rice and bright as lightning in a dark cloud. In the middle of that flame lives the supreme Self, who is called Brahmā, Śiva, Hari and Indra. The commentators read this as a guide to meditation.

An analogy Like a series of nested boxes, each opened in turn: the body, the heart, the fire, the flame — and at the centre, what all the boxes were made to hold.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It praises Nārāyaṇa as thousand-headed, all-eyed, the imperishable, the highest abode, the all, the Person on whom the all depends, the lord of selves, the highest light, brahman, the meditator and the meditation, pervading all that is seen or heard. Then it locates him: in the downward-facing lotus of the heart, in the fire within it, in the fine flame at the fire’s centre, where the supreme self dwells, who is Brahmā, Śiva, Hari, Indra, the imperishable, supreme and self-ruling.

Traditional Interpretation

How does it connect with what precedes?

The anonymous commentator: the previous anuvāka ended with the form of the Great Lord to be meditated on in the lotus of the heart; here its qualities are set out at length: पूर्वानुवाकान्ते ह्रदयपुण्डरीक उपास्यं यन्महेश्वरस्वरूपं निर्दिष्टं तस्मिन्नुपास्यगुणविशेषा अस्मिन्ननुवाके विस्तरेण प्रदर्श्यन्ते. Bhāskara: here too the supreme self is praised, अत्रापि परमात्मा स्तूयते.

Traditional Interpretation

What does the name Nārāyaṇa mean?

The anonymous commentator cites a verse which he says the Purāṇas give: आपो नारा इति प्रोक्ता आपो वै नरसूनवः — the waters are called nārāḥ, and they were his first dwelling; reported as cited by him. Bhāskara derives it from nara, the leader, whose own are the nārāḥ, of whom he is the ground: नरः नेता सर्वस्य स्वामी तस्य स्वभूताः पशवो नारास् तेषाम् अयनम् उपादानं नारायणम्.

Traditional Interpretation

Is the Self here a particular form?

The anonymous commentator denies it: Nārāyaṇa himself is the self, not some other embodied form, अतो नारायणः एवाऽऽत्मा न त्वपरो मूर्तिविशेषः. On the closing list he says the Self, though dwelling in a small place for the sake of meditation, is not small but all the gods, तस्योपासनार्थमल्पस्थानत्वेऽपि न स्वयमल्पः किंतु सर्वदेवात्मकः, and supreme as free of māyā, परमो मायारहितः शुद्धश्चिद्रुपः.

Traditional Interpretation

How is the heart described?

The anonymous commentator: like the hollow of an eight-petalled lotus, but facing down where an ordinary lotus faces up, लौकिकं पद्ममूर्ध्वाभिमुखं; below the base of the neck, निष्टिर्ग्रीवाबन्धस्तस्या अधस्ताद्वर्तते, a span of twelve finger-breadths above the navel. The fire in it he identifies with the digestive fire, quoting the Gītā’s अहं वैश्वानरो भूत्वा प्राणिनां देहमाश्रितः, and its warmth with the body’s: सोऽयं शरीरगतः संतापोऽग्निसद्धावे लिङ्गम्.

Comparative Vedānta

How do the commentators read the flame?

For the anonymous commentator the flame is the finest tongue of that fire, with the supreme self in its midst: तस्याः पूर्वोक्ताया वह्निशिखाया मध्ये जगत्कारणभूतः परमात्मा विशेषेणावस्थितः. Bhāskara reads the flame as the individual self, with Nārāyaṇa dwelling within it, contracted to its measure: तस्य अग्नेर् मध्ये वह्निशिखासदृशो जीवो व्यवस्थितः. The anonymous commentator thus places the supreme self directly in the flame, Bhāskara within the jīva.

Traditional Interpretation

What is the purpose of the description?

The anonymous commentator: the meaning of the whole is that one should meditate on the god described from sahasraśīrṣam on, in the manner described from padmakośapratīkāśam on: सहस्त्रशीर्षमित्यादिवाक्यप्रतिपादितं पद्मकोशप्रतीकाशमित्यादिवाक्योक्तप्रकारेण ध्यायेदिति तात्पर्यार्थः.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What do the witnesses show?

Several places differ: nārayaṇaḥ in the base against nārāyaṇaḥ in the Ānandāśrama text; śilābhiḥ against sirābhiḥ (Tirupati, Bhāskara); jvālamālā- against jvālāmālā- (Ānandāśrama); padam against prabhum (Tirupati, which Bhāskara also comments on); and in the last line the Tirupati text has brahmā. Morphology

10.13.29 नारायण
Three times the name stands without its visarga before para-. The anonymous commentator calls this a Vedic dropping of the visarga everywhere; Bhāskara instead reads a compound nārāyaṇa-para, ‘dependent on Nārāyaṇa’: नारायणपरतन्त्रं ब्रह्मपरं तद्भावापेक्षितत्वात् प्रपञ्चसृष्टेः.
10.13.29 नारयणः
As printed in the base, with a short a; the Ānandāśrama text reads nārāyaṇaḥ (see flags).
10.13.29 परमात्
An ablative with viśvataḥ, ‘higher than the all’; the anonymous commentator says the case is exchanged for the accusative and takes param as ‘highest’.
10.13.30 भाती
bhāti with a lengthened final vowel, which the anonymous commentator calls a Vedic licence: दीर्घश्छान्दसः.
10.13.30 वितस्त्या
As printed; the commentators explain ‘at the end of a span’ (the span of twelve finger-breadths). Bhāskara’s text reads vitastyāṃ tu: वितस्त्यां तु वितस्तिप्रमाणपरिमिते.
10.13.30 व्यवस्थितः
In line twelve a masculine participle agrees with the feminine vahniśikhā; Bhāskara calls this an exchange of gender, लिङ्गव्यत्ययः, and the Tirupati text reads vyavasthitā.
10.13.30 अणीयोर्ध्वा
Printed as one word. The anonymous commentator reads aṇīyā ūrdhvā, ‘very fine, rising upward’; Bhāskara either aṇīyo ’rdhvā, ‘whose path is very fine’, or aṇīyasām ūrdhvā, ‘finer than the finest’.
10.13.30 ब्रह्म
As printed; Brahmā is meant (the Tirupati text and the commentators read brahmā), set beside Śiva, Hari and Indra.

Syntax

The first unit is a string of accusatives describing the god, with no verb of its own: the commentators supply ‘meditate on’ (dhyāyet). Short nominal sentences follow, each with nārāyaṇa(ḥ) and para: Nārāyaṇa is the highest light, brahman, meditator. The relative clause yac ca kiṃcij jagat … closes in the second unit with its main clause, antar bahiś ca tat sarvaṃ vyāpya nārāyaṇas sthitaḥ. The second unit then turns from praise to description: a chain of short statements, each anchored by tasya (‘of it’) or tasyāḥ (‘of that [flame]’), moves inward from the heart to the hollow, the fire, the flame and the Self, and ends in a list of identifications with saḥ.

Ambiguities

who depends on whom
viśvam evedaṃ puruṣas tad viśvam upajīvati. The anonymous commentator: the supreme self relies on the world for its activity, स च परमात्मा तद्विश्वमुपजीवति, स्वस्य व्यवहारनिर्वाहार्थमाश्रयति. Bhāskara reverses it: the world lives on the Person, इदं विश्वं पुरुष एव पूरयिता पुरुषस् तत् तस्माद् विश्वं जगत् पुरुषम् उपजीवत्य् आत्मानं लभते तत्परिणामत्वाद्विश्वस्य.
nārāyaṇa para-
Read with the visarga restored (‘Nārāyaṇa is the highest …’, the anonymous commentator) or as a compound nārāyaṇa-para, ‘dependent on Nārāyaṇa’ (Bhāskara); see morphology.
the flame
For the anonymous commentator the flame is the finest tongue of the bodily fire, in which the supreme Self dwells. Bhāskara takes the flame-like thing as the individual self (jīva): तस्य अग्नेर् मध्ये वह्निशिखासदृशो जीवो व्यवस्थितः, and the supreme Self as dwelling within it, contracted to its measure: तस्यास् तादृश्या वह्निशिखाया मध्ये परमात्मा आत्मप्रकृतिभूतो नारायणो व्यवस्थितः, जीवानुरोधेन सङ्कुचितस्तिष्ठति.
śilābhiḥ
The base and the Ānandāśrama text read śilābhiḥ (‘with stones’), which makes little sense; the Tirupati text and Bhāskara read sirābhiḥ (‘with veins, channels’), and the anonymous commentator explains śirābhiḥ as nāḍīs: तञ्च शिराभिर्नाडीभिः.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Attention

The hymn moves from the vastest image, a god with a thousand heads, to the smallest, a flame thin as a grain’s awn, and says they are the same. Turning attention from the very large to the very near is a familiar discipline in many traditions of contemplation. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

Further notes

  • This anuvāka is Bhāskara’s anuvāka 11 (his comment covers both units). Sāyaṇa’s anuvākas 11–12, which would correspond, are missing from the edition’s copy.
  • The Nambūtiri (Kerala) recitation of this hymn is preserved in the vishvAsa sources as a separate text; it is not collated here.

Uncertainties recorded

  • review nārayaṇaḥ (base) against nārāyaṇaḥ (Ānandāśrama text); śilābhiḥ (base, Ānandāśrama) against sirābhiḥ (Tirupati; Bhāskara’s sirābhiḥ, the anonymous commentator’s śirābhiḥ); jvālamālā- against jvālāmālā-; padam against prabhum (Tirupati). All classed on the sources page.
  • variant Bhāskara’s text lacks ‘sa hariḥ’ in the last line and reads vitastyāṃ tu for vitastyānte.
  • review sendraḥ (saḥ + indraḥ) needs the sandhi engine to join saḥ with a following vowel (the rule of P. 6.1.134 (सोऽचि लोपे चेत्‌ पादपूरणम्)); reported as a shared-code need.
  • interpretation The commentators supply ‘meditate on’ for the accusatives of the first lines; the text has no verb there.
