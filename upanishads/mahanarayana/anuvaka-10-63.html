Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 63 of 80

Tilāñ juhomi: the sesame offering for good things

Sesame offered into the fire with requests for a mind delighting in the divine, for the goods of a household, and for faith and understanding.

5 Faithful English translation

I offer sesame seeds, moist, with meal; O fragrant one, may [they] delight in my mind. Svāhā. Cows, gold, wealth, food and drink, for the prosperity of all. Svāhā. Prosperity and fortune and nourishment and good name and freedom from debt, piety, having many children, faith and intelligence, offspring — may [he] grant. Svāhā.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

I offer moist sesame seeds mixed with meal. O fragrant one, may [your qualities] dwell gladly in my mind. Svāhā. Cows, gold, wealth, food and drink — for the flourishing of all. Svāhā. May prosperity and fortune, nourishment and a good name, freedom from debt, piety, many children, faith and understanding, and offspring be granted. Svāhā.

Key concepts अग्नि Agni, fire श्री śrī, prosperity श्रद्धा faith, trust मेधा medhā, retentive intelligence संन्यास renunciation

7 Explain it to a beginner

Here sesame seeds, moist and mixed with a little meal, are offered into the sacred fire with three short formulas, each ending with the call svāhā. The first asks that something divine take delight in the speaker’s mind. The second names the goods of a household — cows, gold, wealth, food and drink — and asks them for the flourishing of all. The third lists what a good life was thought to need: prosperity, a good name, freedom from debts, piety, children, faith and understanding. The old commentator says these mantras begin a fire ritual performed by someone about to renounce the world, to remove every fault first. The edition reports that use and makes no claim about what the offering brings.

An analogy Like a list made before a long journey: first the frame of mind, then the provisions, then what one hopes to carry inside.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

Sesame is offered with a wish that [the god’s qualities] delight in the speaker’s mind; cows, gold, wealth, food and drink are named ‘for the prosperity of all’; and prosperity, fortune, nourishment, good name, freedom from debt, piety, many children, faith, intelligence and offspring are asked to be granted. Each formula ends with svāhā.

Traditional Interpretation

In what rite are these mantras used?

The anonymous commentator says that from here on the mantras serve to remove all sin and belong to the virajā offering that forms part of taking up the fourth stage of life: अतः परं सर्वपापनिबर्हणार्थाः सर्वोत्कृष्टचतुर्थाश्रमकरणाङ्गभूते. The one about to renounce performs them, सर्वपापनिबर्हणद्वारा संन्यासं करिष्यमाणो, and the deity who receives every oblation is the Supreme Self: सर्वत्र हविर्ग्राहिणी देवता तु परमात्मैव.

Traditional Interpretation

How does he read the requests?

He reads each item with a gloss: the sesame is moist and mixed with meal, सरसान्न तु शुष्कान्सपिष्टान्सक्त्वादिपिष्टान्तरलेशसहितान्; the prosperity is royal fortune and the ‘fortune’ is that of release, श्रियं राज्यलक्ष्मीं and मोक्षलक्ष्मीं; freedom from debt is freedom from the three debts, आनृण्यतां देवर्षिपितॄणत्रयनिर्मुक्तत्वं; faith and intelligence are trust in the teacher and skill in the Veda, श्रद्धामेधे गुरुवेदान्तवाक्यविश्वासवेदशास्त्रार्थग्रहणधारणपटुत्वे. His gloss of brahmaṇyam as pre-eminence among brāhmaṇas is reported as his.

Comparative Vedānta

Where does the virajā series begin?

The anonymous commentator makes these sesame mantras the start of the virajā offering. Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara, on the Drāviḍa text, begins the virajā offerings later, with the series of purifications (our 10.65): अथ सन्न्यासविषया विरजाहोमा एकपञ्चाशप्रभृतय एकादशानुवाकाः.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • तिलाञ्जुहोमि tilāñjuhomi → तिलान् + जुहोमि

    n before j

    tilān + juhomi → tilāñ juhomi: n becomes the palatal ñ before j.

  • सरसां सपिष्टान्गन्धार sarasāṃ sapiṣṭāngandhāra → सरसान् + सपिष्टान् + गन्धार

    n before s and before g

    sarasān + sapiṣṭān: the n is written as anusvāra before s; sapiṣṭān + gandhāra: the n stays before g.

  • गावो हिरण्यं gāvo hiraṇyaṃ → गावः + हिरण्यम्

    -aḥ before a voiced consonant

    gāvaḥ + hiraṇyam → gāvo hiraṇyam.

  • धनमन्नपानं dhanamannapānaṃ → धनम् + अन्नपानम्

    final m before a vowel

    dhanam + annapānam → dhanam annapānam, written together.

  • सर्वेषाग् श्रियै sarveṣāg śriyai → सर्वेषाम् + श्रियै

    anusvāra before a sibilant (Taittirīya spelling)

    sarveṣām + śriyai → sarveṣāṃ śriyai, which the Taittirīya recitation writes सर्वेषाग् श्रियै.

  • चाऽऽनृण्यताम् cā''nṛṇyatām → च + आनृण्यताम्

    a + ā → ā

    ca + ānṛṇyatām → cānṛṇyatām, written with the doubled avagraha of the recitation text.

Morphology

लक्ष्मिम्
Accusative of lakṣmī, printed with a short i (लक्ष्मिं); the Tirupati e-text has लक्ष्मीं. The base and the Ānandāśrama text agree on the short vowel.
बहुपुत्रताम्
Abstract noun in -tā from the bahuvrīhi bahuputra, ‘having many children’.
सन्ददातु
Imperative 3rd singular of sam + √dā, written with its preverb; no subject is expressed. The commentator supplies ‘the Lord’.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

Three offering formulas, each closed by svāhā. The first has a main verb in the first person (juhomi) with an object and two adjectives, then a vocative and a wish in the third-person imperative (ramantu) without an expressed subject. The second is a list of nominatives with a dative of purpose (śriyai) and no verb. The third is a long list of accusatives joined by ca, the object of the imperative sandadātu, again without an expressed subject.

Ambiguities

who delights in the mind
ramantu has no subject in the text. The anonymous commentator supplies the qualities of the Supreme: त्वदीयाः परमपावना गुणा मम चित्ते रमन्तु.
gandhāra
A vocative, ‘O fragrant one’, which the commentator takes as an address to the Supreme Self (हे गन्धार परमात्मंस्त्वदर्थं तिलाञ्जुहोमि). The text does not say whom it addresses.
who grants
sandadātu has no subject here; in 10.64 the same list closes with jātavedaḥ sandadātu, addressing Agni Jātavedas. The commentator supplies the Lord.

Metre

mixed · syllables counted as written: 23 + 18 + 15 + 8 + 12

Prose formulas with anuṣṭubh-like phrases. The five printed lines count 23, 18, 15, 8 and 12 syllables (svāhā included).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Gratitude

The three formulas move from the inner (a mind that delights in what is good) to the outer (goods shared ‘for the prosperity of all’) and back to the inner (faith and understanding). It is a compact picture of what a well-ordered life was thought to need. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin If you made a list of what a good life needs, which items from this one would you keep?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry The commentator reads ‘fortune’ (lakṣmī) as the fortune of release, in a list of worldly goods. What does that reading do to the list?

A contemplation exercise Name one thing you have that is ‘for the prosperity of all’, not only for yourself, and use it so today.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
