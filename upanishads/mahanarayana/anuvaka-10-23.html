Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 23 of 80

Ṛtaṃ satyaṃ paraṃ brahma: truth, the supreme, in a person’s form

A verse of homage that names the supreme Brahman as truth and order, and then as a person, dark and tawny, chaste and three-eyed, whose form is the universe.

5 Faithful English translation

[The one who is] right order, truth, the highest brahman, the person dark and tawny, whose seed is drawn upward, of unusual eyes — to [him whose] form is the universe, truly, homage, homage.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Truth itself, the real, the supreme Brahman; the Person, dark and tawny, chaste, with eyes unlike any other: to him whose form is the whole universe, homage, and again homage.

Key concepts ऋत the right; cosmic and moral order सत्य truth, the real ब्रह्मन् Brahman पुरुष person

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse speaks of the highest reality in two ways at once. First it uses abstract words: the right order of things, truth, the supreme Brahman. Then it describes a person: dark and tawny in colour, chaste, with eyes unlike anyone else’s. One old commentator explains the two colours as the two halves of one figure, Śiva on the right and the goddess Umā on the left; the unusual eyes, he says, are three. The verse then bows to the one whose form is the whole universe. Read together, the two halves say that the same reality can be thought of as an abstract truth or pictured as a person, and the verse offers homage to it either way. It states this praise; it makes no claim about what reciting it does.

An analogy Like describing a mountain first by its height on a map and then by the colours of its two slopes at evening: two descriptions, one mountain.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It names right order, truth and the supreme Brahman, then a person — dark and tawny, with seed drawn upward, of unusual eyes — and offers homage to the one whose form is the universe.

Traditional Interpretation

How does the anonymous commentator read it?

He introduces it as a single verse of homage to the one worshipped, for the one who worships as taught before: पूर्वोक्तप्रकारेणोपासीनस्य पुरुषस्योपास्यनमस्कारार्थमेकामृचमाह. The supreme Brahman, he says, takes a person’s form for the sake of devotees, भक्तानुग्रहायोमामहेश्वरात्मकपुरुषरूपं भवति, a form that is Umā and Maheśvara together.

Traditional Interpretation

What is the verse used for?

Bhāskara: because it ends in homage, it is used in recitation, worship and offerings by those who want offspring, cattle, learning or prosperity, and by those who seek release: प्रजापशुविद्याविभूतिकामानाम् अपवर्गार्थिनां च जपोपासनहोमादिष्व् अयं विनियुज्यते.

Traditional Interpretation

Truth in two senses

The anonymous commentator explains why truth is named twice: there is a conventional truth and an ultimate one, सत्यत्वं च द्विविधं व्यावहारिकं पारमार्थिकं च, and the pair ṛtaṃ satyam marks the ultimate. Bhāskara instead divides the two words between mind and speech: मानस तथ्यम् ऋतम् and वाचिकं सत्यम्.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • ऋतं सत्यं परं ब्रह्म ṛtaṃ satyaṃ paraṃ brahma → ऋतम् + सत्यम् + परम् + ब्रह्म

    final m before a consonant

    A final m becomes anusvāra before a consonant (P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः)): ṛtam satyam param → ṛtaṃ satyaṃ paraṃ.

  • नमो नमः namo namaḥ → नमः + नमः

    -aḥ before a voiced consonant

    namaḥ + namaḥ → namo namaḥ.

  • कृष्णपिङ्गलम् kṛṣṇapiṅgalam

Morphology

ऊर्ध्वरेतम्
From the s-stem ūrdhvaretas the accusative would be ūrdhvaretasam; the text has ūrdhvaretam. Bhāskara: छान्दसस्सकारलोपः — a Vedic loss of the s.
विश्वरूपाय
After a row of accusatives the verse turns to the dative of homage, viśvarūpāya … namaḥ: the accusatives describe the one to whom the bow is then made.

Anvaya

ऋतम् सत्यम् परम् ब्रह्म पुरुषम् कृष्णपिङ्गलम् ऊर्ध्वरेतम् विरूपाक्षम् [अनुश्रुत्य] विश्वरूपाय वै नमः नमः

ṛtam satyam param brahma puruṣam kṛṣṇapiṅgalam ūrdhvaretam virūpākṣam anuśrutya viśvarūpāya vai namaḥ namaḥ

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Eight accusatives in apposition describe one being — first as abstract (ṛtam, satyam, param brahma), then as a person with attributes (puruṣam and its four qualifiers). No verb governs them; the anonymous commentator supplies ‘having heard [of him]’ (तादृशं परमेश्वरमनुश्रुत्येति शेषः), while Bhāskara reads them as a statement, ‘such is the supreme Brahman’. The verse ends with the dative of homage, viśvarūpāya … namo namaḥ.

Ambiguities

The accusatives
Nothing in the verse governs the accusatives. The anonymous commentator supplies a verb of hearing or recalling; Bhāskara turns the series into a statement about Brahman (ईदृशं परं ब्रह्म भवति). Either way they describe the one to whom homage is paid.
ṛtam and satyam
Bhāskara gives two readings of ṛtam: truth in the mind, set beside satyam as truth in speech; or, from a root of compassion, ‘the one who shows favour to the world’, ऋतीयते विश्वमनुगृह्णाति इत्यृतं. The anonymous commentator takes the pair as ‘utterly true’, the ultimate as against the conventional truth of Hiraṇyagarbha.
kṛṣṇapiṅgalam
For the anonymous commentator, the two colours of the two halves of the one form, Maheśvara and Umā. Bhāskara: the supreme as male and female together, स्त्रीपुंसशबलाकारं, and he reports another view (केचिदाहुः) that derives the colours from roots meaning to plough and to destroy.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 18

Bhāskara names the metre: ऋतमित्य् अनुष्टुप्. The checker counts 16 + 18 syllables: the first half is regular (8 + 8), the second has 8 + 10, the closing namo namaḥ adding two syllables beyond the pāda (viśvarūpāya vai namo namaḥ).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Abstraction and image

The verse moves without apology from abstract terms to a vivid figure, and back again in the word ‘universe’. People who think about what matters most often need both: a principle to hold and an image to turn toward. Neither replaces the other. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin The verse calls the highest reality both ‘truth’ and a ‘person’. Which of the two descriptions is easier for you to think about, and why?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry One commentator divides truth into the conventional and the ultimate; another into truth in mind and truth in speech. What does each division let him say about the verse?

A contemplation exercise Notice one moment today when what you thought and what you said were not the same, and one when they were.
