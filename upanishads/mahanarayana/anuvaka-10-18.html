Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 18 of 80

Vāmadevāya namaḥ: the second of the five ‘brahma’ mantras

Eleven names of Śiva as Vāmadeva, each with namaḥ: the northern face, by the commentators’ account.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Homage to Vāmadeva, to the eldest, to the best, to Rudra, to Time; to the one who makes the manifold world, who scatters and who crushes strength, to Strength itself; to the tamer of all beings, and to the one who lifts up the mind.

Key concepts मन्त्र mantra उपासना worship, meditation

7 Explain it to a beginner

This is the second of the five ‘brahma’ mantras that address five faces of Śiva. It is simply a list: eleven names, each followed by namaḥ, ‘homage’. The first name, Vāmadeva, means ‘the lovely god’; the old commentators identify him with the northern face. The other names describe what this god does: he is the eldest, because he existed before the world; the best; Rudra, who makes beings weep at the end of an age; Time, which wears away every life; the one who makes the world various; the one who breaks the strength of what is hostile; the tamer of all beings; and the one who lifts up the mind. Saying a list of names with ‘homage’ after each is a common Vedic form of praise. It needs no story; each name is a reason for the bow.

An analogy Like a roll call of a person’s titles before an audience, each one met with a bow.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It offers homage to Vāmadeva under eleven names: the eldest, the best, Rudra, Time, the maker of the manifold, the scatterer of strength, Strength, the crusher of strength, the tamer of all beings and the uplifter of the mind.

Traditional Interpretation

Which face is this?

The anonymous commentator: the mantra that describes the northern face, उत्तरवक्त्रप्रतिपादकमन्त्रमाह. He gives a reason for each name; Rudra, for example, प्रलये रोदनहेतुत्वाद्रुद्राय, and Time, सर्वप्राण्यायुःक्षयहेतुत्वात्कालाय.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Bhāskara read the list?

Bhāskara: these are not bodies of knowledge but the god with his powers, each called by its name: वामदेवादयो न विद्याशरीराश् चितिशक्तेस् तत्तन्नाम्नीभिः शक्तिभिः सहिताः. The whole group is named after Vāmadeva because he comes first: वामदेवप्राधान्याच्च न्यासादौ तेन समुदायस्याख्यानम्. The last name he explains as the one who wakes the conscious self: मनोन्मनः मन्ता जीवः मनस् तस्योन्मानयिता उद्बोधकश् चैतन्योद्दीपकः.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What do the witnesses show?

The Tirupati e-text reads balapramathāya for balapramathanāya (listed for review). Sāyaṇa’s own text, printed with his commentary, has no balāya namaḥ; his comment on this anuvāka is missing from the copy used, so only his text can be compared.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • नमो ज्येष्ठाय namo jyeṣṭhāya → नमः + ज्येष्ठाय

    visarga after a before a voiced consonant

    namaḥ + jyeṣṭhāya → namo jyeṣṭhāya: -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced sound; so namo before rudrāya, balavikaraṇāya, balāya, balapramathanāya, manonmanāya.

  • नमः श्रेष्ठाय namaḥ śreṣṭhāya → नमः + श्रेष्ठाय

    visarga before ś

    namaḥ stays before śreṣṭhāya (a visarga before a sibilant may stay); likewise before k (kālāya, kalavikaraṇāya).

  • नमस्सर्वभूतदमनाय namassarvabhūtadamanāya → नमः + सर्वभूतदमनाय

    visarga before s (Taittirīya spelling)

    namaḥ + sarva- → namas sarva-: the recitation writes the visarga as the sibilant, doubled (P. 8.3.36 (वा शरि)).

Morphology

कलविकरणाय
Bhāskara takes vikaraṇa as an agent noun in -ana (‘the one who makes various’), citing P. 3.3.113 (कृत्यल्युटो बहुलम्), or as a bahuvrīhi; the anonymous commentator splits kala (‘what takes happiness’) and vikaraṇa.
नमः
Each name in the dative is followed by namaḥ: the regular formula of homage, ‘homage [be] to …’, with no verb.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

A litany: eleven datives, each followed by namaḥ (‘homage [be] to …’). There is no verb; each pair is a complete sentence.

Ambiguities

whose names
For the anonymous commentator all eleven are names of the one god of the northern face. Bhāskara reads them as the god with his powers, the first three standing for the powers of will, knowledge and action: तत्रेच्छाज्ञानक्रियाशक्तिक्षोभका आद्यास्त्रयः.
kalavikaraṇa
The commentators explain the word differently: the anonymous commentator through kala, ‘what takes happiness’, and vikaraṇa, ‘that by which the various is made’; Bhāskara through kala as a stage of unmanifest change.

Metre

prose · syllables counted as written: 76

A yajus: a list of names with namaḥ, not in metre.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Naming

A list of names, each followed by a bow, is a way of seeing one thing from many sides before acknowledging it. Naming what someone does, one act at a time, can be a more honest form of respect than a single general word. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin If you listed eleven names for someone you respect, what would they be?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry The list moves from ‘the lovely god’ to Time and the tamer of beings, and ends with the uplifter of the mind. Why might it end there?

A contemplation exercise Write down three things a person in your life does, and thank them for one.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
