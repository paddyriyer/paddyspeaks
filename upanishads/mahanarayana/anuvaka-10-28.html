Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 28 of 80

Aditir devāḥ: the Earth, mother of all beings

A litany of the Earth as Aditi, mother of gods, men, ancestors and demons alike, ending with the words of the seer Vasiṣṭha: she is true, she is immortal.

5 Faithful English translation

Aditi — the gods, gandharvas, humans, ancestors and asuras: of them, of all beings, [she is] the mother; the earth, great, the great one, Sāvitrī, Gāyatrī, Jagatī, the wide, the broad, the abundant, the universal, what has come to be, the one hard to name, the embodied — she [is] ‘true’, thus, ‘immortal’, thus [says] Vasiṣṭha.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Aditi, the Earth, is mother of gods, gandharvas, humans, ancestors and asuras — of every being. She is the firm earth, great and worthy of honour; she belongs to Savitṛ, she is Gāyatrī and Jagatī; wide, broad, abundant, all-containing; she was there before all bodies, she cannot be pinned down, and she becomes every body. ‘She is true,’ says Vasiṣṭha, ‘she is immortal.’

Key concepts अदिति Aditi, ‘the eater’ सत्य truth, the real अमृत immortality, the deathless ऋषि seer

7 Explain it to a beginner

This passage praises the Earth under the name Aditi, ‘the unbounded’. It begins by listing the kinds of beings that live on her: gods, celestial musicians, humans, ancestors and the asuras, the gods’ rivals. She is mother of all of them, because every body is made from her. Then comes a long chain of names: the firm one, the great one, the wide, the broad, the abundant, the one that was here before any body was born, the one that becomes all bodies. Some names are those of Vedic metres, Gāyatrī and Jagatī. The passage ends by quoting the sage Vasiṣṭha: she is true, she is immortal. The commentators explain why it stands here: the earth gives food and fields, and so the means to live. It praises; it promises nothing.

An analogy Like the ground under a crowded market: everyone there stands on it, whatever they have come to buy or sell.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It names Aditi as mother of gods, gandharvas, humans, ancestors and asuras — of all beings — gives her many epithets, and reports that Vasiṣṭha calls her true and immortal.

Traditional Interpretation

Why a mantra to the Earth here?

The anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa: it is a mantra to the Earth that serves release by way of livelihood, the fields that sustain life: जीवनहेतुक्षेत्रलाभद्वारा मुक्तिहेतुं पृथिवीदेवताकं मन्त्रमाह (Sāyaṇa). Bhāskara: it is praise of Brahman in the form of the Earth, पृथ्वीरूपेण ब्रह्मणस्स्तुतिः, and its last words mean ‘therefore this is the form of Brahman to be worshipped’, तस्मादिदमेव ब्रह्मणो रूपम् उपास्यमिति वसिष्ठः प्रशस्त आह.

Traditional Interpretation

Mother of gods and asuras alike

The commentators explain that the five kinds named stand for every living body, of which the Earth is the material: एते च सर्वप्राणिदेहानामुपलक्षकाः (Sāyaṇa). Bhāskara adds that the five are named for their importance and that birds, snakes and the rest are included: तेषामन्येषामपि सर्वभूतानां विहगोरगादीनां माता.

Traditional Interpretation

‘True’ in what sense?

Sāyaṇa qualifies: true in the state of worldly dealing, सत्या व्यवहारदशायां बाधरहिता, and immortal because she endures through the cycle of the four ages: चतुर्युगपर्यावर्तनेऽप्यवस्थितेत्येतमप्यर्थं वसिष्ठ एवाऽऽह.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • अदितिर्देवा गन्धर्वा मनुष्याः aditirdevā gandharvā manuṣyāḥ → अदितिः + देवाः + गन्धर्वाः + मनुष्याः

    visarga after i; -āḥ before a voiced consonant

    aditiḥ + devāḥ → aditir devāḥ (the visarga after i becomes r before a voiced sound); -āḥ loses its visarga before a voiced consonant (P. 8.3.22 (हलि सर्वेषाम्)): devā gandharvā manuṣyāḥ.

  • पितरोऽसुरास्तेषां pitaro'surāsteṣāṃ → पितरः + असुराः + तेषाम्

    -aḥ before a; visarga before t

    pitaraḥ + asurāḥ → pitaro ’surāḥ (P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति)); the visarga becomes s before t (P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः)): asurās teṣām.

  • जगत्युर्वी jagatyurvī → जगती + उर्वी

    ī before a dissimilar vowel

    jagatī + urvī → jagaty urvī (P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि)).

  • सत्येत्यमृतेति satyetyamṛteti → सत्या + इति + अमृता + इति

    ā + i; i before a

    satyā + iti → satyeti and amṛtā + iti → amṛteti (a/ā + i gives e, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः)); iti + amṛtā → ity amṛtā (P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि)).

Morphology

महता
The base and the Ānandāśrama text print mahatā, in form an instrumental; the Tirupati e-text has mahatī, and all three commentators gloss mahatī (‘great’), the nominative that the list of epithets requires. The collation classes the Tirupati reading as a witness error because the other two agree; it may rather preserve the sense the commentators read.
काया
The anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa take kāyā as one word, ‘she who becomes all bodies’. Bhāskara divides it, kā yā: ‘who [she is], unmanifest; whatever she is, so she is, true’ (का अव्यक्तरूपा या यादृशी भवति सा तादृशी सती सत्या).
वसिष्ठः
For the anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa the sage Vasiṣṭha, the seer of the mantra: अतोऽस्य मन्त्रस्य वसिष्ठ ऋषिरित्यर्थः. Bhāskara takes it also as the superlative ‘most excellent’: the knower of Brahman is the truly excellent one, ब्रह्मज्ञो जनो वसिष्ठ एव भवति.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

A list of nominatives without a finite verb. Aditi is set at the head, then the five kinds of being whose mother she is (resumed by teṣāṃ sarvabhūtānām), then a long string of feminine epithets. The closing words sā satyā iti amṛtā iti vasiṣṭhaḥ report, with iti, what Vasiṣṭha says of her.

Ambiguities

mahatā
Printed as an instrumental, read by every commentator as the nominative mahatī. See the morphology note.
kāyā
One word (‘embodied’) or two, kā yā (‘who … whatever’): see the morphology note.
vasiṣṭhaḥ
The seer’s name, or the superlative ‘most excellent’; Bhāskara reads both.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Ecology and dependence

The passage lists gods, humans and their rivals side by side as children of one Earth, and calls her true and undying. Read today, its plainest lesson is dependence: every kind of being stands on the same ground and is made of it. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin The Earth is called mother of gods and of their rivals alike. What does it mean to have the same mother as one’s opponents?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Sāyaṇa says the Earth is ‘true’ in the world of experience. What is gained, and what lost, by qualifying ‘true’ in that way?

A contemplation exercise Before a meal, name three things the food passed through on its way to you from the ground.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
