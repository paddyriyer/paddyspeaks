Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 5 of 80

Mahate ca: the vyāhṛti offerings to the great one

The vyāhṛti offerings again, each adding ‘and to the great one’, and closing with the fourth vyāhṛti, mahaḥ.

5 Faithful English translation

Earth: to Agni and to the earth and to the great one, svāhā. Mid-air: to Vāyu and to the mid-air and to the great one, svāhā. Heaven: to Āditya and to the sky and to the great one, svāhā. Earth, mid-air, heaven: to the moon and to the constellations and to the quarters and to the great one, svāhā. Homage to the gods; svadhā to the fathers. Earth, mid-air, heaven, the great — om.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

The offerings of the last anuvāka are repeated, each now also made “to the great one”, and the moon is joined by the constellations. Homage to the gods, svadhā to the ancestors. Earth, mid-air, heaven and the great: so be it.

Key concepts व्याहृति the sacred calls अग्नि Agni, fire ओम् Om ब्रह्मन् Brahman हिरण्यगर्भ Hiraṇyagarbha, ‘golden embryo’

7 Explain it to a beginner

This is the third and fullest form of the offering formulas built on bhūḥ, bhuvaḥ and suvaḥ, the old words for earth, the space between, and heaven. Each offering is now made not only to a god and a region but also “to the great one”, and the moon is joined by the constellations. At the end a fourth word is added to the three: mahaḥ, “the great”. The commentators disagree about who the great one is. Sāyaṇa names Hiraṇyagarbha, the first-born creator; the anonymous commentator reads “great” as a description of each god; Bhāskara reads mahaḥ as Brahman, the whole of which the other three are limbs, quoting the Taittirīya Upaniṣad. The anonymous commentator says the offering is made by one who wishes for greatness; the record reports that and claims nothing about its effect.

An analogy Like adding, after each named guest, ‘and the host of the house’, until at the end the host is named alone.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It repeats the vyāhṛti offerings, adding to each ‘and to the great one’, adds the constellations to the moon and the quarters, greets the gods and fathers, and closes with four vyāhṛtis — bhūḥ, bhuvaḥ, suvaḥ, mahaḥ — and om.

Traditional Interpretation

Who is it for?

The anonymous commentator: offering formulas for one who wishes for greatness — महत्त्वकामस्य तत्फलका होममन्त्रा उच्यन्ते; the three deities should grant greatness, being honoured by all (महर्महत्त्वं सर्वपूज्यत्वं दद्युरिति शेषः). Sāyaṇa: a further reading of the same formulas for one who wishes to be honoured (पूजाकामस्य पुनरप्यन्यपाठमाह).

Traditional Interpretation

What is mahaḥ?

Bhāskara: here the fourth vyāhṛti, which names the supreme Brahman, is taken up in place of the praṇava (अत्र परस्य ब्रह्मणो वाचिका चतुर्थी व्याहतिः प्रणवपर्यायतयोपादीयत इति विशेषः), and he quotes the Taittirīya’s account of how Māhācamasya made it known: तासामु ह स्मैतां चतुर्थीम् । माहाचमस्यः प्रवेदयते । मह इति । तद्ब्रह्म.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What do the witnesses show?

In the closing भुर्भुवस्सुवर्महरोम् the base and the Tirupati e-text print भुर् with a short u, the Ānandāśrama text भूर्. The word is the vyāhṛti bhūḥ; the difference is listed on the sources page for checking. The Tirupati text also misprints महुते once.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • वायवे चान्तरिक्षाय vāyave cāntarikṣāya → वायवे + च + अन्तरिक्षाय

    a + a

    ca + antarikṣāya: two a’s merge into ā (cāntarikṣāya).

  • नक्षत्रेभ्यश्च nakṣatrebhyaśca → नक्षत्रेभ्यः + च

    visarga before c

    A visarga becomes ś before c.

  • सुवर्महरोम् suvarmaharom → सुवः + महर् + ओम्

    suvaḥ before m; mahar before a vowel

    suvaḥ keeps its r before a voiced consonant (suvar mahar); mahar joins om (mahar om).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this anuvāka.

Morphology

महर्
The fourth vyāhṛti, mahaḥ, here written with r before om; Bhāskara calls the r an optional form (महरोमिति रेफविकल्पः) and quotes the Taittirīya’s teaching that mahaḥ is Brahman: मह इति । तद्ब्रह्म. The word table gives it as printed, since a visarga form would not rejoin into the text.
भुः
The base and the Tirupati e-text print भुर्भुवस्सुवर्महरोम् with a short u; the Ānandāśrama text has भूर्. The word is the vyāhṛti bhūḥ; it is listed as printed, and the difference is flagged for review.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

The frame of 10.4 with each dative series extended by ca … mahate ca, the moon’s series adding nakṣatrebhyaś ca, and the closing vyāhṛtis extended by mahar.

Ambiguities

who ‘the great one’ is
Sāyaṇa: Hiraṇyagarbha, the great and worshipful (महते प्रौढाय पूज्याय वा हिरण्यगर्भाय); the anonymous commentator takes mahate as an adjective of each deity (‘to the great Agni’), adjusting number and gender (महत इत्यग्न्यादीनां विशेषणं वचनलिङ्गविपरिणामेना[न्यत्राप्य]न्वेतव्यम्). Bhāskara: the great god who is the whole of which the three are parts.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Wholes and parts

Each line names a part — a god, a region — and then adds “and the great one”, as if no part should be named without the whole it belongs to. Remembering the larger thing a task serves, each time one does the task, is a habit worth keeping. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Why might a fourth word be added to the three familiar vyāhṛtis only at the end?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Is ‘the great one’ a separate deity, an epithet of each deity, or the whole of which they are parts? Which reading does the grammar favour?

A contemplation exercise When you finish a piece of work today, name what larger thing it was for.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
