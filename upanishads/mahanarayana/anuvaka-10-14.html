Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 14 of 80

Āditya: the threefold knowledge in the sun

The sun’s disc, its radiance and the person within them are the three Vedas: a meditation on the sun as the Veda itself.

5 Faithful English translation

The sun, indeed, [is] this: this disc glows. There [are] those ṛcs; that disc [is] by the ṛc; it [is] the world of the ṛcs. Next, what radiance shines in this disc — those [are] the sāmans; it [is] the world of the sāmans. Next, the person who [is] in the radiance in this disc — those [are] the yajuses; he [is] the disc by the yajus; he [is] the world of the yajuses. That, this threefold knowledge itself, glows — the golden person who [is] within the sun.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

The sun is this glowing disc. The disc is the Ṛg verses: it is made of them, and it is their world. The radiance that shines in the disc is the Sāman chants, and their world. The person within that radiance is the Yajus formulas, and their world. So it is the threefold knowledge of the Veda itself that glows there: the golden person within the sun.

Key concepts सूर्य the Sun वेद Veda पुरुष person उपासना worship, meditation

7 Explain it to a beginner

The ancient Indian Veda has three main kinds of text: the Ṛg verses, the Sāman chants and the Yajus formulas used in ritual. This passage looks at the sun and finds all three in it. The glowing disc is the Ṛg verses; the light that shines from it is the Sāman chants; the person imagined within that light is the Yajus. So the sun is the whole Veda, the ‘threefold knowledge’, shining. The old commentators read it as a meditation: the Lord described in the previous section, Nārāyaṇa, is present in the sun, and one meditates on the disc, the radiance and the person as the three Vedas. The ‘golden person within the sun’ is a figure known from other Upaniṣads too, such as the Chāndogya, which describes him as golden down to the fingernails.

An analogy Like seeing in a single lamp the oil, the flame and the light, and naming each.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It identifies the sun’s disc with the ṛcs, the radiance in it with the sāmans and the person in the radiance with the yajuses, calls each the world of that Veda, and concludes that the threefold knowledge glows as the golden person within the sun.

Traditional Interpretation

What is this for?

The anonymous commentator: here begins the meditation on the supreme brahman in the sun’s disc, इदानीमादित्यमण्डले परब्रह्मण उपासनामाह. Sāyaṇa explains each step as something to meditate on: the radiance as the Bṛhat, Rathantara and other sāmans, तान्यर्चिःस्वरूपाणि बृहद्रथंतरादिसामानीति ध्यायेत्, and the whole as the threefold knowledge, सैषा मण्डलतदर्चिस्तत्रत्यपुरुषरूपं त्रय्येवर्ग्यजुःसामात्मिकैव विद्या तपति प्रकाशते.

Traditional Interpretation

Who is the person in the sun?

Sāyaṇa: the supreme lord named Nārāyaṇa in the previous anuvāka, present in the sun with the sun as his adjunct, नारायणशब्दवाच्यो यः परमेश्वरः पूर्वानुवाकेऽभिहितः स एष आदित्यो वै सोपाधिकः सन्नादित्यरूपेणैव वर्तते. For ‘golden’ both he and the anonymous commentator cite another branch of the Veda; the words are those of the Chāndogya (see parallels).

Traditional Interpretation

How does Bhāskara read it?

Bhāskara: the god’s modes of favouring the world are told again, पुनरपि जगदनुग्रहार्थं प्रकारा देवस्योच्यन्ते; the disc with its power to glow is the might of the ṛcs, तादृक् तपनशक्तियुक्तमण्डलम् ऋच ऋचां प्रभावः.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • आदित्यो वा ādityo vā → आदित्यः + वा

    -aḥ before a voiced consonant

    ādityaḥ + vā → ādityo vā.

  • वा एष एतन्मण्डलं vā eṣa etanmaṇḍalaṃ → वा + एषः + एतत् + मण्डलम्

    hiatus; eṣaḥ before a consonant; t before m

    vā eṣaḥ: ā before e is left apart here; eṣaḥ drops its visarga before a consonant (P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि)); etat + maṇḍalam → etan maṇḍalam (P. 8.4.45 (यरोऽनुनासिकेऽनुनासिको वा)).

  • ऋचस्तदृचा ṛcastadṛcā → ऋचः + तत् + ऋचा

    visarga before t; t before a vowel

    ṛcaḥ + tat → ṛcas tat (P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः)); tat + ṛcā → tad ṛcā.

  • लोकोऽथ loko'tha → लोकः + अथ

    -aḥ before a

    lokaḥ + atha → loko ’tha (P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति)).

  • मण्डलेऽर्चिर्दीप्यते maṇḍale'rcirdīpyate → मण्डले + अर्चिः + दीप्यते

    e before a; visarga after i before a voiced consonant

    maṇḍale + arciḥ → maṇḍale ’rciḥ (P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति)); arciḥ + dīpyate → arcir dīpyate.

  • लोकस्सैषा lokassaiṣā → लोकः + सा + एषा

    visarga before s; ā + e

    lokaḥ + sā → lokas sā (the Taittirīya doubled sibilant); sā + eṣā → saiṣā (P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि)).

  • त्रय्येव trayyeva → त्रयी + एव

    ī before a dissimilar vowel

    trayī + eva → trayy eva (P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि)).

  • य एषोऽन्तरादित्ये ya eṣo'ntarāditye → यः + एषः + अन्तरादित्ये

    -aḥ before a vowel; -aḥ before a

    yaḥ + eṣaḥ → ya eṣaḥ; eṣaḥ + antar- → eṣo ’ntar-.

Junctions outside the general rules

  • वा · एषः — unjoined hiatus (as printed): vā · eṣaḥ: ā before e is left apart, as in every witness and in Sāyaṇa’s text (classical vaiṣa).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • अन्तरादित्ये antarāditye

    avyayībhāva-like (locative) · अन्तर् + आदित्य

    Vigraha आदित्यस्य अन्तः

    within the sun

Morphology

वा
Here not ‘or’ but an affirming particle, as often in the Brāhmaṇas (ādityo vā eṣa); the next anuvāka opens with vai in the same place.
ऋचा
An instrumental: the disc is made by the ṛc, as the commentators explain. Bhāskara’s gloss reads the genitive plural ṛcām instead: तस्मात् तदृचाम् एव मण्डलं सङ्घातः.
हिरण्मयः
From hiraṇya with the suffix -maya, ‘made of gold’, with the a of hiraṇya dropped (a Vedic form).

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

A Brāhmaṇa-style identification in three steps, each with the same frame: a thing in the sun (the disc, the radiance in it, the person in the radiance) is identified (tāni …) with one Veda and called its world (sa … lokaḥ). The relative clauses (yaḥ eṣaḥ …) name the thing; the correlatives (tāni) give the Veda. The sentence sā eṣā trayy eva vidyā tapati gathers the three, and the final relative clause names the person.

Ambiguities

tad ṛcā maṇḍalam
The base reads the instrumental ṛcā (‘the disc is by the ṛc’), which the anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa explain as ‘made by the ṛcs’; Bhāskara’s gloss has the genitive plural ṛcām, ‘the disc is of the ṛcs’.
who the person is
The anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa say it is the supreme lord called Nārāyaṇa in the preceding anuvāka, present in the sun with a limiting adjunct: नारायणशब्दवाच्यो यः परमेश्वरः पूर्वानुवाकेऽभिहितः स एष आदित्यो वै सोपाधिकः सन्नादित्यरूपेणैव वर्तते (Sāyaṇa). Bhāskara speaks of the god’s modes of favouring the world.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Wonder

The passage looks at the most ordinary sight, the sun, and sees in it everything the tradition most values. Looking at something familiar until it means more than it did is an old habit of mind worth keeping. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Why might someone link the sun with knowledge?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry The text speaks of a ‘world’ of the ṛcs, the sāmans and the yajuses. What could it mean for words to have a place where they live?

A contemplation exercise At sunrise or sunset, look at the light for a minute without naming anything.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.

Further notes

  • This anuvāka is Sāyaṇa’s and Bhāskara’s anuvāka 13. The anonymous commentator’s text repeats Sāyaṇa’s almost word for word here.

Uncertainties recorded

  • interpretation The identification of the person in the sun with Nārāyaṇa is the commentators’ link to the previous anuvāka; the text says only ‘the golden person within the sun’.
