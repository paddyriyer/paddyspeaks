Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 44 of 80

Mayi medhām: understanding, offspring and splendour

A threefold prayer that Agni, Indra and Sūrya place in the speaker understanding and offspring, with radiance, strength and brilliance.

5 Faithful English translation

In me understanding, in me offspring, in me may Agni place radiance; in me understanding, in me offspring, in me may Indra place strength; in me understanding, in me offspring, in me may Sūrya place brilliance.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

May Agni place in me understanding, descendants and the lustre of learning; may Indra place in me understanding, descendants and strength; may the Sun place in me understanding, descendants and brilliance.

Key concepts मेधा medhā, retentive intelligence अग्नि Agni, fire सूर्य the Sun देवता deity

7 Explain it to a beginner

This short prayer has three parts that repeat a pattern. Each asks a god to place three things in the speaker: understanding, descendants, and a gift proper to that god. From Agni, the fire, it asks the radiance of a learned person; from Indra, king of the gods, strength and vigour; from the Sun, a brilliance that others notice. The words ‘in me’ come again and again. The old commentator says the repetition shows how earnestly the speaker asks. It closes the group of prayers for medhā that began two sections earlier.

An analogy Like asking three different mentors for the same two things, and one more that each is best at giving.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It asks Agni, Indra and Sūrya each to place in the speaker understanding and offspring, and, respectively, radiance, strength and brilliance.

Traditional Interpretation

How does the commentator read it?

The anonymous commentator: again, for the sake of medhā and the rest, Agni, Indra and Sūrya are entreated (पुनरपि मेधादिसिद्धयेऽग्नीन्द्रसूर्यान्प्रार्थयते); medhā is skill in grasping and retaining the Vedas and śāstras, prajā an unbroken line of descendants, tejas the lustre of sacred learning; the rest needs no explanation (निगदव्याख्यातमन्यत्).

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • मय्यग्निस्तेजो mayyagnistejo → मयि + अग्निः + तेजः

    i before a dissimilar vowel; visarga before t

    mayi + agniḥ → mayy agniḥ (P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि)); agniḥ + tejaḥ → agnis tejaḥ (P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः)).

  • मयीन्द्र इन्द्रियं mayīndra indriyaṃ → मयि + इन्द्रः + इन्द्रियम्

    i + i; aḥ before a vowel

    mayi + indraḥ → mayīndraḥ (P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः)); indraḥ + indriyam → indra indriyam (P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य)).

  • सूऱ्यो भ्राजो sūऱ्yo bhrājo → सूऱ्यः + भ्राजः

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    -aḥ becomes -o before bh and before d (P. 6.1.114 (हशि च)); the recitation writes the r of sūrya as the Vedic ऱ.

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Three parallel sentences, each with the locative mayi repeated three times, two shared objects (medhām, prajām), a subject (Agni, Indra, Sūrya), a third object proper to that god, and the imperative dadhātu.

Ambiguities

the repetition
The anonymous commentator: the repetition of mayi and the rest shows the speaker’s earnestness (आदराभिद्योतिका मयिपदाद्यावृत्तिः).

Metre

prose · syllables counted as written: 50

A prose formula (yajus) in three parallel clauses.

Textual variations between witnesses

The witnesses differ here only in spelling (listed in section 14).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Asking clearly

The prayer names exactly what it asks for, three times over. Saying clearly what one hopes for, and from whom, can itself clarify the hope. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.
