Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 47 of 80

Vātaṃ prāṇam: holding on to Prajāpati

Prajāpati, who is the wind outside and the breath within and guards the world, is held on to in thought and asked to keep the speakers from death and harm until old age.

5 Faithful English translation

The wind, the breath, we lay hold of with the mind: Prajāpati, who is the guardian of the world. May he protect us from death, may he guard us from distress; living long, may we reach old age.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

With our minds we hold on to Prajāpati, the wind and the breath, the guardian of the world. May he keep us from death and from harm, so that we live long and reach old age.

Key concepts प्राण breath, life-force वायु breath, air मनस् mind मृत्यु death

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse asks for a long life. It names Prajāpati, the lord of creatures, and says that he is both the wind that blows through the sky and the breath that moves in each body. The old commentator explains that this shows Prajāpati holding up both the single body and the whole universe. The speakers say that they hold on to him with their minds, the way one might take hold of a support. Then they ask him to protect them from death and from harm. The last words are a wish many people share: that we may live long and grow old. The commentator explains that this means living out a full human span of life.

An analogy Like keeping a hand on a railing in the dark: the support is the same one that holds up the whole stair.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse ask?

That Prajāpati, held on to in the mind, protect the speakers from death and distress, so that they live long and reach old age.

Traditional Interpretation

Who is Prajāpati here?

The anonymous commentator: the supreme self, the breath in every body and the wind of the mid-air, the protector of the universe: तं प्रजापतिं परमात्मानं.

Traditional Interpretation

What does reaching old age mean?

The anonymous commentator: living long, may we reach the state of old age, that is, live a full human span (ज्योग्जीवाश्विरंजीवनाः सन्तो वयं, वार्धकावस्थामशीमहि प्राप्नुमः).

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • मनसाऽन्वारभामहे manasā'nvārabhāmahe → मनसा + अन्वारभामहे

    ā + a → ā

    manasā + anvārabhāmahe → manasānvārabhāmahe; the avagraha marks the merged a (P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः)).

  • यो भुवनस्य yo bhuvanasya → यः + भुवनस्य

    -aḥ before a voiced consonant

    yaḥ → yo before bh (P. 6.1.114 (हशि च)).

  • स नो मृत्योस्त्रायतां sa no mṛtyostrāyatāṃ → सः + नः + मृत्योः + त्रायताम्

    saḥ before a consonant; visarga before t

    saḥ loses its visarga before a consonant (P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि)); naḥ → no before m (P. 6.1.114 (हशि च)); mṛtyoḥ + trāyatām → mṛtyos trāyatām (P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः)).

  • पात्वंहसो pātvaṃhaso → पातु + अंहसः

    u before a dissimilar vowel

    pātu + aṃhasaḥ → pātv aṃhasaḥ (P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि)).

  • ज्योग्जीवा जरामशीमहि jyogjīvā jarāmaśīmahi → ज्योक् + जीवाः + जराम् + अशीमहि

    k before a voiced consonant; -āḥ before a voiced consonant

    jyok → jyog before j (P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते)); jīvāḥ drops its visarga before j; jarām + aśīmahi joins without change.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • प्रजापतिम् prajāpatim

    tatpuruṣa · प्रजा + पति

    Vigraha प्रजानां पतिः

    lord of creatures

Morphology

अशीमहि
An optative of the root aorist of √aś ‘to reach’ (aś-ī-mahi), middle; the commentator renders it with a present, prāpnumaḥ.
गोपाः
A root noun in -ā (gopā, ‘cow-protector’, then any guardian); the nominative singular is gopāḥ.

Anvaya

यः भुवनस्य गोपाः [तम्] वातम् प्राणम् प्रजापतिम् मनसा अन्वारभामहे । सः नः मृत्योः त्रायताम् अंहसः पातु । ज्योक् जीवाः जराम् अशीमहि ।

yaḥ bhuvanasya gopāḥ tam vātam prāṇam prajāpatim manasā anvārabhāmahe | saḥ naḥ mṛtyoḥ trāyatām aṃhasaḥ pātu | jyok jīvāḥ jarām aśīmahi |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A main clause in the first person plural (anvārabhāmahe) with Prajāpati as object, described by two appositives (vātam, prāṇam) and a relative clause; then two imperatives with saḥ as subject, and an optative wish of the speakers.

Ambiguities

wind and breath
The commentator reads the two words as one being in two forms: the breath in every body and the wind in the mid-air, so that Prajāpati sustains both the body and the universe (एतेन पिण्डाण्डब्रह्माण्डोभयविधारकत्वं प्रजापतेरुक्तं भवति).

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 23 + 20

A triṣṭubh verse with irregular pādas; the checker counts 23 and 20 syllables. The first pāda (vātaṃ prāṇaṃ manasānvārabhāmahe) has 12, and the last (jyog jīvā jarām aśīmahi) only 8.

Textual variations between witnesses

The witnesses differ here only in spelling (listed in section 14).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Asking for ordinary things

The verse’s last wish is not for glory but to grow old. Wanting a full, ordinary span of life is one of the oldest hopes people have put into words. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What would you hope to be doing when you are old?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why might the same being be named as both the wind and the breath?

A contemplation exercise Sit for a minute and notice your breath going in and out, without changing it.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
