Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 75 of 80

Namo rudrāya viṣṇave: protection from death

A one-line formula of homage to Rudra and Viṣṇu with a request for protection from death.

7 Explain it to a beginner

This is a single line: homage to Rudra and Viṣṇu, and a request for protection. The last words, read word for word, say ‘death, protect me’. The only commentator in this edition who explains the line, the anonymous one, says that the word for death is to be understood as ‘from death’: the speaker asks the two gods to protect him from death. He takes Rudra and Viṣṇu as two deities and says the formula is meant to ward off death for as long as one lives. In the order of the text it follows the formula said while touching the heart after a meal. The edition reports how the commentator explains the line and makes no claim about what saying it does.

An analogy Like a short signature line under a letter: brief, but it names whom one is writing to and what one asks.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the formula say?

It offers homage to Rudra and Viṣṇu and asks for protection: the last words read, literally, ‘death, protect me’.

Traditional Interpretation

What is it for?

The anonymous commentator: it follows the address to the heart and is meant to ward off death for as long as one lives (यावज्जीवं मृत्युपरिहारार्थं). He names the two deities by their consorts: रुद्राय पार्वतीपतये नमोऽस्तु। विष्णवे लक्ष्मीपतये नमोऽस्तु.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

How can ‘death’ be the one asked?

The nominative mṛtyuḥ cannot be the subject of the second-person pāhi. The anonymous commentator explains it as the ablative by exchange of case: मृत्युर्व्यत्ययेन मृत्योः सकाशान्मे मां पाहि — protect me from death.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • नमो रुद्राय namo rudrāya → नमः + रुद्राय

    visarga after a before a voiced consonant

    namaḥ + rudrāya → namo rudrāya: -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced consonant.

  • मृत्युर्मे mṛtyurme → मृत्युः + मे

    visarga after u before a voiced consonant

    mṛtyuḥ + me → mṛtyur me: after u the visarga becomes r.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this anuvāka.

Morphology

मृत्युः
Grammatically a nominative, which does not fit the imperative pāhi addressed to ‘you’. The anonymous commentator explains it as standing for the ablative by exchange of case (vyatyaya): ‘protect me from death’.
मे
The enclitic of the first person, here used for the accusative ‘me’, as the anonymous commentator glosses it.

Anvaya

रुद्राय विष्णवे नमः [अस्तु] । मृत्युः मे पाहि

rudrāya viṣṇave namaḥ astu | mṛtyuḥ me pāhi

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two clauses. The first is nominal: namaḥ with two datives and the verb ‘be’ understood. The second is an imperative, pāhi, with the enclitic me as object; mṛtyuḥ does not agree with the second-person verb and is read by the commentator as an ablative.

Ambiguities

one deity or two
rudrāya viṣṇave may name two deities or one, the second dative in apposition to the first. The anonymous commentator takes two: Rudra, lord of Pārvatī, and Viṣṇu, lord of Lakṣmī, and addresses both.
mṛtyuḥ
Read by the anonymous commentator as ‘from death’, by exchange of case.

Metre

prose · syllables counted as written: 13

A yajus formula of 13 syllables.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Mortality

A daily formula that names death plainly and asks for protection keeps the fact of mortality within ordinary life rather than outside it. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Why might a short prayer name death directly instead of avoiding the word?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry The commentator reads ‘death’ as ‘from death’. What would the line mean if it were read as an address to Death itself?

A contemplation exercise Notice one moment today when you take your own safety for granted.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
