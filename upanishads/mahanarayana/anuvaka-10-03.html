Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 3 of 80

Bhūr annam: the vyāhṛti offerings with food

The three great utterances, each joined to the deity of its world, used in offerings for food; closed with homage to the gods and the fathers.

5 Faithful English translation

Earth: food to Agni, to the earth, svāhā. Mid-air: food to Vāyu, to the mid-air, svāhā. Heaven: food to Āditya, to the sky, svāhā. Earth, mid-air, heaven: food to the moon, to the quarters, svāhā. Homage to the gods; svadhā to the fathers. Earth, mid-air, heaven: food — om.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

With the words for earth, mid-air and heaven, food is offered to Agni and the earth, to the wind and the mid-air, to the sun and the sky, and with all three together to the moon and the quarters. Homage to the gods, svadhā to the ancestors. Earth, mid-air, heaven: may there be food — so be it.

Key concepts व्याहृति the sacred calls अन्न food अग्नि Agni, fire ओम् Om मन्त्र mantra

7 Explain it to a beginner

This anuvāka is a set of short offering formulas built on the three “great utterances”, bhūḥ, bhuvaḥ and suvaḥ, the old names for earth, the space between, and heaven. Each formula pairs one of these with the god of that world (fire for earth, wind for the space between, the sun for heaven) and adds the word for food and the call svāhā, said when something is placed into a sacred fire. A fourth formula uses all three together for the moon and the quarters. Then come a greeting to the gods and the word svadhā for the ancestors, and the three utterances once more with food and om, “so be it”. The commentators explain it as a fire-offering made by someone who wishes for food; this record reports that explanation and claims no result for it.

An analogy Like a letter addressed in turn to three households and then to all of them together, each line names the place, the one who lives there, and the gift.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It offers food, with svāhā, to Agni and the earth under bhūḥ, to Vāyu and the mid-air under bhuvaḥ, to Āditya and the sky under suvaḥ, and to the moon and the quarters under all three; it greets the gods and the fathers, and closes with the three vyāhṛtis, food and om.

Traditional Interpretation

What is it used for?

The anonymous commentator calls these homa mantras for one who wishes for food, offered as part of removing sin: अतः परं पापक्षयार्थद्वाराऽन्नकामस्य होममन्त्रानाह. He describes the procedure in the domestic fire according to one’s own gṛhya rules, and has the worshipper face east for namo devebhyaḥ and south for svadhā pitṛbhyaḥ. Sāyaṇa, whose order differs, treats the passage as a variant of the plain vyāhṛti offerings for one who wants plentiful food: अन्नसमृद्धिकामस्य तेष्वेव मन्त्रेषु पाठान्तरमाह.

Traditional Interpretation

What does the closing om mean here?

The anonymous commentator takes om as assent: the three deities agree to give the food prayed for — ओंकारोऽभ्यनुज्ञां कथयति and अस्मत्प्रार्थितमन्नं दातुं भूर्भुवः सुवस्तिस्रो देवता ओम्.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Bhāskara read the same words?

Bhāskara reads these anuvākas not as homa mantras for food but as meditation on the vyāhṛtis as Brahman. Here food is meditated on as Brahman in the form of bliss: आनन्दब्रह्मणा ताद्रूप्येण व्याहृत्यादीनामुपासनामात्रम् अत्रानुवाके विशेषः. He introduces this anuvāka and the two around it together as the great vyāhṛtis that set out Brahman: भूरग्नय इत्याद्यास्त्रयोऽनुवाका व्यष्टिसमष्टिरूपेण ब्रह्मण एव प्रतिपादका महाव्याहृतिनामानः. The two readings are reported side by side; the text itself only states the formulas.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What do the witnesses show?

The three witnesses agree on the words; the differences listed on the sources page for this anuvāka are spelling only (the doubled sibilant after a visarga against ः, and the like).

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • भूरन्नमग्नये bhūrannamagnaye → भूः + अन्नम् + अग्नये

    visarga after ū before a vowel; final m before a vowel

    A visarga after a vowel other than a/ā becomes r before a vowel (bhūr annam); a final m joins a following vowel (annam agnaye).

  • भुवोऽन्नं bhuvo'nnaṃ → भुवः + अन्नम्

    aḥ before a

    -aḥ before a becomes -o and the a is elided, marked by avagraha: bhuvaḥ + annam → bhuvo ’nnam (P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति)).

  • वायवेऽन्तरिक्षाय vāyave'ntarikṣāya → वायवे + अन्तरिक्षाय

    e before a

    After -e an initial a is elided (avagraha): vāyave ’ntarikṣāya (P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति)).

  • सुवरन्नमादित्याय suvarannamādityāya → सुवः + अन्नम् + आदित्याय

    suvaḥ before a vowel

    suvaḥ (svar) keeps its r before a vowel: suvar annam; then annam ādityāya.

  • दिग्भ्यस्स्वाहा digbhyassvāhā → दिग्भ्यः + स्वाहा

    visarga before s (Taittirīya spelling)

    The visarga before s is written as a doubled s in the Taittirīya recitation: digbhyas svāhā.

  • पितृभ्यो भूर्भुवस्सुवरन्नमोम् pitṛbhyo bhūrbhuvassuvarannamom → पितृभ्यः + भूः + भुवः + सुवः + अन्नम् + ओम्

    aḥ before a voiced consonant; ūḥ before a voiced consonant; visarga before s

    -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced consonant (pitṛbhyo bhūḥ); bhūḥ becomes bhūr before b; bhuvaḥ before s is written bhuvas s (Taittirīya doubling); suvaḥ keeps its r before a vowel (suvar annam), and annam joins om (annam om).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this anuvāka.

Morphology

भूः
The three vyāhṛtis bhūḥ, bhuvaḥ, suvaḥ are indeclinable words naming earth, mid-air and heaven. Bhāskara recalls that Prajāpati uttered them to make those worlds: स भूरिति व्याहरत् । स भूमिमसृजत.
स्वाहा
An indeclinable; it does not take the dative constructions around it as its object, which is why Bhāskara notes that the dative is not governed by it: स्वाहाकारसम्बन्धेऽपि निपातत्वाच् चतुर्थ्यभावः.
स्वधा
The exclamation used in offerings to the ancestors, as svāhā is used for the gods.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

A string of verbless offering formulas. Each has the same frame: a vyāhṛti, the word annam, two datives (a deity and its region) and svāhā. The fourth joins all three vyāhṛtis to the moon and the quarters. Two salutations follow (namo devebhyaḥ, svadhā pitṛbhyaḥ), and the three vyāhṛtis return with annam and om.

Ambiguities

what annam is
Annam may be the thing offered or the thing prayed for. The anonymous commentator reads both: the deity of the world should give food, and food in the form of the boiled offering is offered to it (मह्यमन्नं दद्यादिति शेषः । तदर्थं च चरुरूपमन्नं मयाऽस्मिन्स्मार्ताग्नौ स्वाहा सुहुतमस्तु).

Metre

prose · syllables counted as written: 68

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Gratitude and food

The formulas name, in order, the ground, the air and the sky before naming food: what nourishes is traced back to the whole world that produces it. Pausing before a meal to name where it came from is a habit that needs no rite. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.
