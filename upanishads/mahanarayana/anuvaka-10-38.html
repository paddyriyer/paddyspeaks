Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 38 of 80

Brahma metu mām: the first trisuparṇa

The first of three passages called trisuparṇa: a prayer that brahman, the sweet, come to the speaker; an offering of the breaths to Soma; and a brāhmaṇa passage on giving and reciting it.

5 Faithful English translation

May brahman come to me. May sweetness come to me. May brahman itself, the sweet, come to me. Which are your creatures, Soma — towards them I am a calf, that one, I. O slayer of evil dreams, overpower! Which are your breaths, Soma — those I offer. One should give the trisuparṇa unasked to a brāhmaṇa. These truly destroy [the sin of] killing a brāhmaṇa. The brāhmaṇas who recite the trisuparṇa attain soma. They purify the row up to a thousand. Om.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

May Brahman come to me; may the sweet come to me; may Brahman itself, the sweet, come to me. Soma, among your creatures I am your child. You who destroy the bad dream, overcome it. Soma, the breaths that are yours I offer back to you. This trisuparṇa should be taught to a brāhmaṇa even unasked. Those who recite it, the text says, destroy even the sin of killing a brāhmaṇa; they attain soma; they purify a row of a thousand at the meal. Om.

Key concepts ब्रह्मन् Brahman प्राण breath, life-force मन्त्र mantra ओम् Om स्वाध्याय recitation; study of one’s own Veda

7 Explain it to a beginner

This is the first of three short passages that share the name trisuparṇa, ‘the three fine-winged ones’. It begins with a simple prayer: may Brahman come to me, may the sweet come to me, may Brahman itself, the sweet, come to me. Then it speaks to Soma, whom the commentators take as the Supreme together with knowledge: among your creatures I am like your child; you destroy the bad dream; the breaths that are yours I give back to you. Sāyaṇa reads this last line as drawing the senses back from their objects. The passage ends with a few prose lines about the text itself: it should be taught even to one who has not asked, and the text promises those who recite it freedom from the gravest sin. The edition reports these claims; it does not make them.

An analogy Like a child who brings a gift back to the parent who gave it, and calls it an offering.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

A threefold prayer that brahman, the sweet, come to the speaker; a statement to Soma that the speaker is a child among his creatures and offers him the breaths; and a brāhmaṇa passage: the trisuparṇa is to be given unasked to a brāhmaṇa, its reciters destroy the sin of killing a brāhmaṇa, attain soma, and purify a row of a thousand.

Traditional Interpretation

What are the trisuparṇa?

Bhāskara: three anuvākas to be recited for the destruction of all sin, known and unknown: अज्ञातप्रज्ञातसमस्तपापक्षयार्थं जप्यास् त्रिसुपर्णाख्यास् त्रयोऽनुवाकाः. He derives the name from suparṇa, the fine-winged bird, and takes the bird as Om itself, the bird on which the self flies up to brahman (प्रणवः शकुन्तः सुपर्णः शोभनपतनः); that is why each of the three ends in Om (प्रणवप्राधान्यमेषां दर्शयितुं त्रयाणामनुवाकानां प्रणवान्तत्वम्). The anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa introduce them as mantras that remove the sins that obstruct knowledge: अथ ज्ञानप्रतिबन्धकब्रह्महत्यादिपापनिवृत्तिहेतवस्त्रिसुपर्णनामका मन्त्रा उच्यन्ते.

Traditional Interpretation

How is the offering of the breaths read?

Sāyaṇa: the movements of breath belong to the Lord, who made them, so the speaker withdraws them into him, holding the senses back from their objects and fixing the mind on him alone: विषयेभ्य इन्द्रियाणि निरुध्य त्वदेकचित्तो भवामीत्यर्थः. Bhāskara similarly: आददे प्रत्याहरामि विषयेभ्यः — I take them back from the objects.

Traditional Interpretation

Why ‘unasked’?

Sāyaṇa: other teachings follow the rule that one should not speak unless asked (नापृष्टः कस्यचिद्ब्रूयात्), and are given when the pupil requests them; this one is to be taught to a brāhmaṇa even without the request: इमं तु त्रिसुपर्णमन्त्रं शिष्ययाच्ञामन्तरेणैव ब्राह्मणायोपदिशेत्.

Traditional Interpretation

What is the Om at the end?

Sāyaṇa: it shows that the deity of the trisuparṇa is the supreme self named by Om: तस्मादों प्रणवप्रतिपाद्यः परमात्मैव त्रिसुपर्णमन्त्रस्य देवतेत्यर्थः.

Where they disagree

No Vedānta-school commentary on this text is in the edition’s sources. The anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa read the passage as a prayer to the supreme self with knowledge; Bhāskara speaks of becoming one with brahman (ब्रह्मीभूतस्तिष्ठामीति). None of them argues it against another school, and Raṅgarāmānuja’s commentary on the Mahānārāyaṇa is not among the sources.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • ब्रह्ममेतु brahmametu → ब्रह्मम् + एतु

    final m before a vowel

    The m is joined to the following vowel.

  • वत्सोऽभि vatso'bhi → वत्सः + अभि

    aḥ before a

    vatsaḥ + abhi → vatso ’bhi (P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति)).

  • ताञ्जुहोमि tāñjuhomi → तान् + जुहोमि

    n before j

    n becomes ñ before a palatal (P. 8.4.40 (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः)).

  • प्राणाग्स्ताञ्जुहोमि prāṇāgstāñjuhomi → प्राणान् + तान् + जुहोमि

    final n before t

    prāṇān + tān → prāṇāṃs tān (P. 8.3.7 (नश्छव्यप्रशान्)); the Taittirīya recitation writes the nasal as ग् (प्राणाग्स्ताञ्).

  • वा एते vā ete → वै + एते

    ai before a vowel

    vai + ete → vā ete: ai becomes āy and the y is dropped (P. 6.1.78 (एचोऽयवायावः), P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य)).

  • ब्राह्मणास्त्रिसुपर्णं brāhmaṇāstrisuparṇaṃ → ब्राह्मणाः + त्रिसुपर्णम्

    visarga before t

    brāhmaṇāḥ + trisuparṇam → brāhmaṇās trisuparṇam: the visarga becomes s before t (P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः)).

  • आसहस्रात्पङ्क्तिं āsahasrātpaṅktiṃ → आ + सहस्रात् + पङ्क्तिम्

    words written together

    ā and sahasrāt are written as one; final t stays before p.

Morphology

ब्रह्मम्
brahman is a neuter n-stem whose nominative is brahma; the text has brahmam, and madhum beside madhu. Sāyaṇa glosses the forms as they stand: ब्रह्मं परब्रह्मतत्त्वं and मधुं परमानन्दलक्षणमाधुर्योपेतं. Bhāskara divides differently, brahma metu mām, taking metu from a root mī ‘go’ (मी गतौ), and rejects the view that the m is an inserted Vedic sound (see ambiguities).
दुरुष्षह
Sāyaṇa takes it as an imperative of √sah with dur- and ut-, ‘overpower utterly’, the sounds changed in the Vedic way; the anonymous commentator as a request to destroy unbearable existence (दुःसहं भवं विनाशयेत्यर्थः); Bhāskara, with a different reading, as ‘hard to overcome’, said of the speaker who has won knowledge (ज्ञानलाभेन अनभिभवनीयः सम्पन्नोऽस्मि).
याः
In the second yās te soma line the relative is feminine plural beside the masculine prāṇān tān; the agreement is loose.

Anvaya

ब्रह्मम् माम् एतु । मधुम् माम् एतु । ब्रह्मम् एव मधुम् माम् एतु । सोम, याः ते प्रजाः [ताः] अभि सः अहम् वत्सः [अस्मि] । दुष्ष्वप्नहन्, दुरुष्षह । सोम, याः ते प्राणान् तान् जुहोमि । त्रिसुपर्णम् अयाचितम् ब्राह्मणाय दद्यात् । एते वै ब्रह्महत्याम् घ्नन्ति । ये ब्राह्मणाः त्रिसुपर्णम् पठन्ति ते सोमम् प्राप्नुवन्ति । [ते] आ सहस्रात् पङ्क्तिम् पुनन्ति । ओम् ।

brahmam mām etu | madhum mām etu | brahmam eva madhum mām etu | soma, yāḥ te prajāḥ tāḥ abhi saḥ aham vatsaḥ asmi | duṣṣvapnahan, duruṣṣaha | soma, yāḥ te prāṇān tān juhomi | trisuparṇam ayācitam brāhmaṇāya dadyāt | ete vai brahmahatyām ghnanti | ye brāhmaṇāḥ trisuparṇam paṭhanti te somam prāpnuvanti | te ā sahasrāt paṅktim punanti | om |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Three short imperative prayers with etu; then an address to Soma with a relative clause (yās te … prajāḥ) and a verbless statement about the speaker (vatsaḥ … so aham); two vocatives; a second relative clause with a first-person verb (juhomi). The brāhmaṇa part that follows uses an optative of instruction (dadyāt) and present statements in the plural, the subject of ghnanti (ete) left general.

Ambiguities

brahmam etu / brahma metu
Sāyaṇa (and the anonymous commentator, who glosses मामेतु प्राप्नोतु) read brahmam etu mām, ‘may brahman come to me’. Bhāskara divides brahma metu mām, metu from a root mī ‘go’, and rejects the view of ‘some’ that the m is an inserted Vedic sound (छान्दसो मकारोपजन इत्याहुः). The words of the text are the same either way.
Soma
The anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa read Soma as sa + Umā, the supreme self with knowledge; Bhāskara gives several derivations, among them the lord of all (ईष्टे सर्वस्येति सोमः) and the one with Umā, the power named by Om.
abhi so aham
Sāyaṇa: ‘such a one, I [am a child] towards your creatures’. Bhāskara also considers reading abhisaḥ as one word, ‘the one who presses [soma] all round’.

Metre

mixed · syllables counted as written: 5 + 5 + 9 + 12 + 8 + 10 + 14 + 9 + 11 + 7 + 9 + 1

Prose formulas and a brāhmaṇa passage, with an anuṣṭubh in the middle (Bhāskara: यास्त इत्यनुष्टुप्) that is irregular as printed: yās te soma prajā vatso ’bhi so aham has 12 syllables, duṣṣvapnahan duruṣṣaha 8, yās te soma prāṇāṃs tāñ juhomi 10.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Giving back

The prayer gives the breaths back to the one they came from. Treating what one has — time, attention, the ability to act — as received rather than owned changes how it is used. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What would it mean to offer back something you were given?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry The text says this teaching should be given even to one who has not asked. When is it right to teach what was not requested?

A contemplation exercise For one minute, notice your breath without changing it, as something received.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.

Further notes

  • Sāyaṇa comments on this anuvāka as his 48, Bhāskara as his 48; the edition’s concordance maps both correctly.
  • The claims about the effects of teaching and reciting the trisuparṇa (destroying the sin of killing a brāhmaṇa, attaining soma, purifying a row of diners) are the text’s own and are reported as such. The commentators’ restriction of the teaching to brāhmaṇas is reported as theirs.
