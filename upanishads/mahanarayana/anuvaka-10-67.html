Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 67 of 80

Agnaye svāhā: the vaiśvadeva and the bali offerings

The formulas of the household’s daily offering from the cooked food: oblations into the fire to Agni, the gods and the firm ones, and portions set down for dharma, the waters, the house, the boundaries, all beings, the fathers, people and the spirits who roam, with a prayer for undiminished grain.

1 Original Sanskrit

अग्नये स्वाहा ।विश्वेभ्यो देवेभ्यस्स्वाहा ।ध्रुवाय भूमाय स्वाहा ।ध्रुवक्षितये स्वाहा ।अच्युतक्षितये स्वाहा ।अग्नये स्विष्टकृते स्वाहा ।धर्माय स्वाहा ।अधर्माय स्वाहा ।अद्भ्यस्स्वाहा ।ओषधिवनस्पतिभ्यः स्वाहा ॥ ८५ ॥

रक्षोदेवजनेभ्यस्स्वाहा ।गृह्याभ्यस्स्वाहा ।अवसानेभ्यस्स्वाहा ।अवसानपतिभ्यस्स्वाहा ।सर्वभूतेभ्यस्स्वाहा ।कामाय स्वाहा ।अन्तरिक्षाय स्वाहा ।यदेजति जगति यच्च चेष्टति नाम्नो भागोऽयं नाम्ने स्वाहा ।पृथिव्यै स्वाहा ।अन्तरिक्षाय स्वाहा ॥ ८६ ॥

दिवे स्वाहा ।सूर्याय स्वाहा ।चन्द्रमसे स्वाहा ।नक्षत्रेभ्यस्स्वाहा ।इन्द्राय स्वाहा ।बृहस्पतये स्वाहा ।प्रजापतये स्वाहा ।ब्रह्मणे स्वाहा ।स्वधा पितृभ्यस्स्वाहा ।नमो रुद्राय पशुपतये स्वाहा ॥ ८७ ॥

देवेभ्यस्स्वाहा ।पितृभ्यस्स्वधाऽस्तु ।भूतेभ्यो नमः ।मनुष्येभ्यो हन्ता ।प्रजापतये स्वाहा ।परमेष्ठिने स्वाहा ।यथा कूपः शतधारः सहस्रधारो अक्षितः ।एवा मे अस्तु धान्यं सहस्रधारमक्षितम् ।धनधान्यै स्वाहा ।ये भूताः प्रचरन्ति दिवानक्तं बलिमिच्छन्तो वितुदस्य प्रेष्याः ।तेभ्यो बलिं पुष्टिकामो हरामि मयि पुष्टिं पुष्टिपतिर्दधातु स्वाहा ॥ ८८ ॥

Accented text · Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10, the recitation text (MD), as the witness keys it

अ॒ग्नये॒ स्वाहा॑ । विश्वे॑भ्यो दे॒वेभ्य॒स्स्वाहा॑ । ध्रु॒वाय॑ भू॒माय॒ स्वाहा॑ । ध्रु॒व॒क्षित॑ये॒ स्वाहा॑ । अ॒च्यु॒त॒क्षित॑ये॒ स्वाहा॑ । अ॒ग्नये॑ स्विष्ट॒कृते॒ स्वाहा॑ । धर्मा॑य॒ स्वाहा॑ । अध॑र्माय॒ स्वाहा॑ । अ॒द्भ्यस्स्वाहा॑ । ओ॒ष॒धि॒व॒न॒स्प॒तिभ्यः॒ स्वाहा॑

र॒क्षो॒दे॒व॒ज॒नेभ्य॒स्स्वाहा॑ । गृह्या॑भ्य॒स्स्वाहा॑ । अ॒व॒साने॑भ्य॒स्स्वाहा॑ । अ॒व॒सान॑पतिभ्य॒स्स्वाहा॑ । स॒र्व॒भू॒तेभ्य॒स्स्वाहा॑ । कामा॑य॒ स्वाहा॑ । अ॒न्तरि॑क्षाय॒ स्वाहा॑ । यदेज॑ति॒ जग॑ति॒ यच्च॒ चेष्ट॑ति॒ नाम्नो॑ भा॒गोऽयं नाम्ने॒ स्वाहा॑ । पृ॒थि॒व्यै स्वाहा॑ । अ॒न्तरि॑क्षाय॒ स्वाहा॑

दि॒वे स्वाहा॑ । सूर्या॑य॒ स्वाहा॑ । च॒न्द्रम॑से॒ स्वाहा॑ । नक्ष॑त्रेभ्य॒स्स्वाहा॑ । इन्द्रा॑य॒ स्वाहा॑ । बृह॒स्पत॑ये॒ स्वाहा॑ । प्र॒जाप॑तये॒ स्वाहा॑ । ब्रह्म॑णे॒ स्वाहा॑ । स्व॒धा पि॒तृभ्य॒स्स्वाहा॑ । नमो॑ रु॒द्राय॑ पशु॒पत॑ये॒ स्वाहा॑

दे॒वेभ्य॒स्स्वाहा॑ । पि॒तृभ्य॑स्स्व॒धाऽस्तु॑ । भू॒तेभ्यो॒ नमः॑ । म॒नु॒ष्ये॑भ्यो॒ हन्ता॑ । प्र॒जाप॑तये॒ स्वाहा॑ । प॒र॒मे॒ष्ठिने॒ स्वाहा॑ । यथा कू॑पः श॒तधा॑रः स॒हस्र॑धारो॒ अक्षि॑तः । ए॒वा मे॑ अस्तु धा॒न्यँ स॒हस्र॑धार॒मक्षि॑तम् । धन॑धान्यै॒ स्वाहा॑ । ये भू॒ताः प्र॒चर॑न्ति॒ दिवा॒नक्तं॒ बलि॑मि॒च्छन्तो॑ वि॒तुद॑स्य॒ प्रेष्याः॑ । तेभ्यो॑ ब॒लिं पु॑ष्टि॒कामो॑ हरामि॒ मयि॒ पुष्टिं॒ पुष्टि॑पतिर्दधातु॒ स्वाहा॑

7 Explain it to a beginner

This anuvāka gives the words for an old household rite done every day with the cooked food. First small portions are offered into the fire, each with ‘svāhā’: to Agni, to all the gods, to the firm and lasting ones, and to Agni who makes the offering whole. Then portions are set down on the ground for many others: right conduct and its opposite, the waters and plants, the deities of the house and its boundaries, all beings, the sky, sun, moon and stars, the great gods, the ancestors, and people. Each kind of recipient has its own call: svāhā for gods, svadhā for ancestors, namaḥ for beings, hanta for people. The prayer that grain be like a well that never runs dry, and the last offering to the spirits who roam, end it. The edition reports the rite as the commentator describes it.

An analogy Like a host who, before eating, sets aside a portion for every guest in the house, the neighbours and the stranger at the door.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It names, one by one, the recipients of the household’s daily offering: Agni, all the gods, the firm and unfailing ones, Agni the maker of the well-offered; dharma and adharma, the waters, plants and trees, rakṣases and the gods’ attendants, the deities of the house and its boundaries, all beings, Kāma, the mid-region, the Name, earth, sky, sun, moon and stars, Indra, Bṛhaspati, Prajāpati, Brahmā, the fathers, Rudra, the gods, beings, people, Parameṣṭhin and Dhanadhānī; it asks for grain like an inexhaustible well, and sets out a share for the spirits who roam.

Traditional Interpretation

What rite is this?

The anonymous commentator: the first formulas are इतः परं वैश्वदेवकर्मणि विनियुक्ताः षड्ढोममन्त्रा व्याख्यायन्ते — the six formulas of oblation used in the vaiśvadeva rite; from dharmāya on: इतः परं बलिहरणकर्मणि विनियुक्ता मन्त्रा व्याख्यायन्ते — the formulas used in setting out the bali offerings. Of the Name he says the offering is given on the ground (बलिहरणरूपमिदं हविर्भूमौ दत्तमस्तु).

Traditional Interpretation

Why four different calls?

The anonymous commentator: food is to be given to gods, fathers, beings and people with the four particles svāhā, svadhā, namaḥ and hanta; said with these words, the gift pleases them more, as kind words do with a meal. He adds that svāhā may be supplied with namaḥ for the beings, or namaḥ itself taken as the word of giving food.

Traditional Interpretation

Who is Agni the sviṣṭakṛt?

The anonymous commentator: कदाचित्प्रमादालस्यादिना दुरिष्टमपि कर्म स्विष्टं करोतीति स्विष्टकृत् — the one who makes well offered even what was ill offered through carelessness or sloth.

Traditional Interpretation

The well and the grain

The anonymous commentator: as a well, कूप उदकाधारविशेषः, with many streams stays undiminished however much water is drawn from it, so by the favour of the supreme self may my grain be inexhaustible, and filled in many granaries. The offering then goes to a goddess he calls Dhanadhānī: धनानि दधाति पोषयति भक्तानामिति धनधानी काचिद्देवता.

Traditional Interpretation

The spirits who roam

The anonymous commentator: तेभ्यो बलिं हरामि भूमौ निक्षिपामि — I set the offering on the ground for them, wishing for increase. They are a kind of seizing spirits, servants of Vituda, whom he identifies as Rudra in the form of the fire of time, dwelling in the cremation ground, who strikes the wrongdoers. These identifications are his.

10.67.85 अद्भ्यः
Dative plural of ap, ‘water’, which is used in the plural; the p becomes d before bh.
10.67.86 गृह्याभ्यः
Dative plural of the feminine gṛhyā, ‘belonging to the house’, with devatā understood (the commentator: कुलदेवताः).
10.67.86 नाम्नः
Genitive of nāman, ‘name’; with nāmne (dative) the line plays on the one word. The anonymous commentator takes the Name as the supreme self, whose portion is all that moves, to be taken back at the dissolution.
10.67.87 चन्द्रमसे
Dative of the s-stem candramas, ‘the moon’.
10.67.87 स्वधा
The call used in offerings to the fathers, as svāhā is to the gods; here both are said.
10.67.88 हन्ता
The interjection hanta, the call with which food is given to people, written in the base with a long final ā. The anonymous commentator names the four calls svāhā, svadhā, namaḥ and hanta as the particles with which food is given to gods, fathers, beings and people.
10.67.88 एवा
Vedic form of eva/evam, ‘so’, with a long final ā.
10.67.88 भूताः
Masculine plural of bhūta (classically neuter), ‘beings’; the commentator takes them as a kind of spirits.
10.67.88 वितुदस्य
Genitive of vituda, from vi-√tud, ‘to strike, pierce’. The anonymous commentator: विशेषेण तुदति पापिनः प्राणिनः पीडयतीति वितुदः — he who strikes especially the wrongdoers; for him, Rudra as the fire of time, who dwells in the cremation ground. This is the commentator’s reading.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

Almost every line is a dative of the recipient followed by a call of offering (svāhā, svadhā, namaḥ, hanta), with no verb. Two sentences are full: the relative clause yad ejati … ceṣṭati with nāmno bhāgo ’yam (86), and the comparison yathā kūpaḥ … evā me astu dhānyam (88). The last formula is a relative clause (ye bhūtāḥ … preṣyāḥ) taken up by tebhyaḥ, the verb harāmi and a wish in the imperative (dadhātu).

Ambiguities

the two antarikṣāya
Unit 86 has ‘to the mid-region’ twice. The anonymous commentator separates them: the first means the wind that dwells in the mid-region, the second the deity who presides over the middle world.
the Name
nāmno bhāgo ’yam nāmne svāhā: the anonymous commentator takes ‘Name’ as the whole of the Vedic word, and so the supreme self it makes known, whose portion everything that moves is, to be taken back at the dissolution. The text itself does not explain the word.
bhūma
dhruvāya bhūmāya: the commentator names dhruva as a well-known deity but does not explain bhūma; ‘the earth’ and ‘the abundant one’ are both possible.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Sharing

Before the household eats, the rite sets aside a share for a long list of others: the gods, the ancestors, the plants and waters, the house and its edges, all beings, people, even the spirits that roam. Whatever one makes of the recipients, the habit of thinking first of who else a meal depends on, and who else is hungry, is widely shared. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.
