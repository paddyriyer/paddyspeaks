Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 21 of 80

Īśānaḥ sarvavidyānām: the fifth face, Īśāna

The last of the five mantras on the faces of Śiva: Īśāna, ruler of all knowledge and all beings, is asked to be gracious, and the mantra closes on Sadāśiva and Om.

5 Faithful English translation

The ruler of all branches of knowledge, the lord of all beings, the overlord of brahman, the overlord of Brahmā — [that] Brahmā (or: brahman) — may [he be] gracious to me. Sadāśiva. Om.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Ruler of every kind of knowledge, lord of all that lives, guardian of the Veda, master even of Brahmā the creator: may that great one be gracious to me. He is Sadāśiva, the ever-auspicious. Om.

Key concepts ईश्वर the Lord ब्रह्मन् Brahman विद्या vidyā ओम् Om

7 Explain it to a beginner

This short prayer is the last of five that address the five faces of Śiva, each face with its own name. The fifth face looks upward and is called Īśāna, the Ruler. The prayer piles up titles: ruler of every kind of knowledge, lord of every living being, guardian of the sacred Veda, master even of Brahmā, the creator. Then it makes one simple request: may this great one be kind to me. It ends with the name Sadāśiva, the Ever-Auspicious, and the sacred syllable Om, run together into a single sound. The old commentators read that ending in two ways: one says the reciter declares, ‘I am that Sadāśiva’; another hears Om simply taking the place of the word ‘he’. The prayer itself states what it asks; it makes no claim about what reciting it brings.

An analogy Like addressing a letter with every title of its recipient before writing a one-line request: the titles say who is asked, the line says what for.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It names one being by five titles — ruler of all knowledge, lord of all beings, overlord of brahman, overlord of Brahmā, Brahmā (or brahman) — and asks that he be gracious to the speaker. It ends with the name Sadāśiva and Om.

Traditional Interpretation

Which face of Śiva is this?

The anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa both say the mantra sets out the upward face: ऊर्ध्ववक्त्रप्रतिपादकं मन्त्रमाह. In this text it closes the series of five mantras on the faces of Mahādeva that begins at 10.17 (sadyojātam), which the anonymous commentator introduces as being about महादेवसंबन्धिषु पञ्च faces. In Sāyaṇa’s own order the five stand at his anuvākas 43–47.

Traditional Interpretation

Who is ‘Brahmā’ here?

The anonymous commentator takes the brahman of brahmādhipatiḥ as the Veda and the brahman of brahmaṇaḥ as Hiraṇyagarbha: ब्रह्मणो हिरण्यगर्भस्याधिपतिः. The final brahmā is for him the supreme self: तादृशो यो ब्रह्मा प्रवृद्धः परमात्मा. Bhāskara reads the last word as the neuter, the supreme reality beyond the world, ब्रह्मा विश्वाधिक परं वस्तु, with a Vedic long vowel.

Traditional Interpretation

What does the closing ‘sadāśivom’ say?

For the anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa the reciter declares identity with Sadāśiva: स एव सदाशिव ओमहं भवामि — ‘that very Sadāśiva, Om, I become’. Bhāskara takes Sadāśiva as the god’s name, ‘the self of the praṇava’, and Om as standing where ‘he’ would: सस्थाने प्रणवः. He adds a grammatical alternative: the joined form by P. 6.1.95 (ओमाङोश्च), इति पररूपत्वं कृतम्.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

How is the joined text formed?

Two junctions are unusual. me astu keeps the e of me before a, as Vedic recitation does inside a pāda (P. 6.1.115 (प्रकृत्याऽन्तःपादमव्यपरे)), where classical Sanskrit would write me ’stu. And sadāśivaḥ + om gives sadāśivom: the visarga falls before the vowel and the o of om replaces the a (P. 6.1.95 (ओमाङोश्च)).

Where they disagree

No Vedānta-school commentary on this anuvāka is in the edition’s sources: the anonymous bhāṣya, Sāyaṇa and Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara explain the mantra and its use, not a school’s doctrine (Raṅgarāmānuja’s commentary on the Mahānārāyaṇa is not available here). The one real difference among them is the closing: identity with Sadāśiva for the anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa, a name with the praṇava for ‘he’ for Bhāskara.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • सर्वविद्यानामीश्वरः sarvavidyānāmīśvaraḥ → सर्वविद्यानाम् + ईश्वरः

    final m before a vowel

    A final m is joined to a following vowel: sarvavidyānām īśvaraḥ.

  • ब्रह्माधिपतिर्ब्रह्मणोऽधिपतिर्ब्रह्मा brahmādhipatirbrahmaṇo'dhipatirbrahmā → ब्रह्माधिपतिः + ब्रह्मणः + अधिपतिः + ब्रह्मा

    visarga after i before a voiced sound; -aḥ before a

    adhipatiḥ + brahmaṇaḥ → adhipatir brahmaṇaḥ (the visarga after i becomes r before a voiced sound); brahmaṇaḥ + adhipatiḥ → brahmaṇo ’dhipatiḥ: -aḥ becomes -o before a, and the a is elided (P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति)), marked by the avagraha.

  • शिवो मे śivo me → शिवः + मे

    -aḥ before a voiced consonant

    -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced consonant: śivaḥ + me → śivo me.

  • सदाशिवोम् sadāśivom → सदाशिवः + ओम्

    -aḥ before om: the a merges into o

    The visarga falls before a vowel, and a + om gives om, the second vowel replacing the first (P. 6.1.95 (ओमाङोश्च)): sadāśivaḥ + om → sadāśivom. Bhāskara names this rule as one way to explain the form.

Junctions outside the general rules

  • मे · अस्तु — vedic-prakṛtibhāva: me · astu: the e of me is not joined to the following a (classical me ’stu). In Vedic recitation e and o before a inside a pāda keep their form (P. 6.1.115 (प्रकृत्याऽन्तःपादमव्यपरे)); every witness prints me astu.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • सर्वविद्यानाम् sarvavidyānām

    karmadhāraya · सर्व + विद्या

    Vigraha सर्वाः विद्याः

    all branches of knowledge

  • सर्वभूतानाम् sarvabhūtānām

    karmadhāraya · सर्व + भूत

    Vigraha सर्वाणि भूतानि

    all beings

  • ब्रह्माधिपतिः brahmādhipatiḥ

    ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa · ब्रह्मन् + अधिपति

    Vigraha ब्रह्मणः अधिपतिः

    overlord of brahman (the Veda, for the commentators)

  • सदाशिवः sadāśivaḥ

    karmadhāraya (as a name) · सदा + शिव

    Vigraha सदा शिवः

    ever auspicious

Morphology

ब्रह्मा
Read as the masculine brahmā (Brahmā, or the supreme self ‘grown great’) by the anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa. Bhāskara reads the neuter brahma, ‘the supreme reality’, and explains the long final vowel as a Vedic lengthening in the joined text: छान्दसं सांहितिकं दीर्घत्वम्.
सदाशिवः
In the joined text the name and the following Om fuse into sadāśivom. The commentators do not agree on what the fusion means: see the commentary.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

A string of nominatives in apposition — īśānaḥ, īśvaraḥ, brahmādhipatiḥ, adhipatiḥ, brahmā — each with its genitive, followed by a single wish, śivo me astu (‘may [he] be gracious to me’). The name sadāśivaḥ and the syllable om close the formula, fused in the joined text.

Ambiguities

brahmādhipatiḥ
The anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa read one compound, ‘overlord of brahman’, taking brahman as the Veda. Bhāskara divides it, brahmā adhipatiḥ: ‘the great one, overlord of all’ (ब्रह्मा सर्वात्मना परिबृढो ऽधिपतिर्). The joined text allows both.
brahmā
The masculine Brahmā (the creator, or the supreme self as ‘grown great’) or the neuter brahma with a lengthened vowel (Bhāskara). The commentators differ; the text alone does not decide.
sadāśivom
Sadāśiva followed by Om; the anonymous commentator and Sāyaṇa make it a statement of identity by the reciter, Bhāskara a name with the praṇava standing for ‘he’.

Metre

prose · syllables counted as written: 38

A yajus formula in prose, one line of 38 syllables; no metrical division.

Textual variations between witnesses

The witnesses differ here only in spelling (listed in section 14).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Authority and gentleness

The mantra gives its addressee every title of power — over knowledge, over beings, over the Veda, over the creator — and then asks only that he be gentle. Power named in full and then asked for kindness is a pattern worth noticing wherever authority is held: the more is held, the more the request for gentleness matters. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Why might a prayer list so many titles before making a single, small request?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry The commentators hear ‘Sadāśiva, Om’ either as the reciter’s own identity or as a name with Om standing for ‘he’. What changes in the mantra between the two readings?

A contemplation exercise Read the five titles slowly, one per breath, and notice which one you linger on.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
