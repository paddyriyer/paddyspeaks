Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad · Prapāṭhaka 10 · Anuvāka 4 of 80

Bhūr agnaye: the plain vyāhṛti offerings

The three vyāhṛtis joined to Agni, Vāyu, Āditya and the moon, each with its region, in offering formulas that end with homage to the gods and fathers.

5 Faithful English translation

Earth: to Agni, to the earth, svāhā. Mid-air: to Vāyu, to the mid-air, svāhā. Heaven: to Āditya, to the sky, svāhā. Earth, mid-air, heaven: to the moon, to the quarters, svāhā. Homage to the gods; svadhā to the fathers. Earth, mid-air, heaven — O Agni, om.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

With the word for earth, an offering to fire and the earth; with the word for mid-air, to the wind and the mid-air; with the word for heaven, to the sun and the sky; with all three, to the moon and the quarters. Homage to the gods, svadhā to the ancestors. Earth, mid-air, heaven: Agni, so be it.

Key concepts व्याहृति the sacred calls अग्नि Agni, fire ओम् Om मन्त्र mantra

7 Explain it to a beginner

This anuvāka has the same shape as the one before it, but without the word for food. Each line takes one of the three great utterances (bhūḥ for earth, bhuvaḥ for the space between, suvaḥ for heaven), names the god and the region that belong to it, and ends with svāhā, the word said when an offering is put into a fire. All three together are then joined to the moon and the four quarters. A greeting to the gods and the word svadhā for the ancestors follow, and the passage closes by calling on Agni, the fire, with om, “so be it”. The anonymous commentator says these offerings are made with ghee for the removal of sin; Bhāskara reads the same words as a meditation on Brahman. The record reports both and claims no effect for the rite.

An analogy Like a roll-call that names each place and its keeper before naming them all together.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

It makes offerings with svāhā to Agni and the earth, Vāyu and the mid-air, Āditya and the sky, and, under all three vyāhṛtis, to the moon and the quarters; it greets the gods and the fathers and ends by addressing Agni with om.

Traditional Interpretation

What is it for?

The anonymous commentator: from here on come mantras purely for the removal of sin — इतः परं केवलपापक्षयार्था मन्त्रा उच्यन्ते — and because no other result is named, removal of sin is the result: फलान्तरानुक्तेः पापक्षय एव फलम्. The vyāhṛti deities are asked to accept the offering and remove sin (इदमाहुतिद्रव्यं स्वीकृत्यास्मत्पापं निवारयन्त्विति शेषः).

Traditional Interpretation

How does Bhāskara read it?

Bhāskara takes this anuvāka and the two around it as the great vyāhṛtis, which set out Brahman as the many and the one: भूरग्नय इत्याद्यास्त्रयोऽनुवाका व्यष्टिसमष्टिरूपेण ब्रह्मण एव प्रतिपादका महाव्याहृतिनामानः. The worlds and gods depend on the vyāhṛtis, and the formula is to be joined to a meditation, ‘I am the supreme Brahman that is undivided in these three’: परं ब्रह्म अहमस्मीत्य् उपासनाविधित्वेन योज्यम्.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What do the witnesses show?

The differences are spelling, except one: in भूर्भुवस्सुवश्चन्द्रमसे the Tirupati e-text writes भुर् with a short u. The base reads भूर्; the sources page lists the difference for review.

Where they disagree

These are offering formulas, and the commentaries available are ritual and Vedic; none takes a position on which the Vedānta schools divide. No Viśiṣṭādvaita or Dvaita commentary on the Mahānārāyaṇa is in the edition’s sources (Raṅgarāmānuja’s is not available here). The anonymous commentator reads a homa for the removal of sin; Bhāskara reads a meditation on Brahman as the self.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • भूरग्नये bhūragnaye → भूः + अग्नये

    visarga after ū before a vowel

    bhūḥ becomes bhūr before a vowel.

  • भुवो वायवेऽन्तरिक्षाय bhuvo vāyave'ntarikṣāya → भुवः + वायवे + अन्तरिक्षाय

    aḥ before a voiced consonant; e before a

    -aḥ becomes -o before v (bhuvo vāyave); after -e an initial a is elided, marked by avagraha (vāyave ’ntarikṣāya; P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति)).

  • सुवश्चन्द्रमसे suvaścandramase → सुवः + चन्द्रमसे

    visarga before c

    A visarga becomes ś before c: suvaś candramase.

  • सुवरग्न ओम् suvaragna om → सुवः + अग्ने + ओम्

    suvaḥ before a vowel; e before a vowel other than a

    suvaḥ keeps its r before a vowel (suvar agne); agne before o becomes agna, the y of agnay- being dropped and the gap left (agna om).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this anuvāka.

Morphology

अग्ने
Vocative of agni. Before the following om the final -e is resolved to -ay and the y dropped, so the text prints अग्न ओम्, two words with a gap.
स्वाहा
Indeclinable; the datives around it name to whom the offering goes. Bhāskara notes that svāhā, being a particle, does not itself govern the dative: स्वाहाकारसम्बन्धेऽपि निपातत्वाच् चतुर्थ्यभावः.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

The same frame as 10.3 without the word annam: vyāhṛti, two datives, svāhā; then the two salutations; then the three vyāhṛtis with the vocative agne and om.

Ambiguities

what is offered
No offering substance is named. The anonymous commentator infers ghee: no other substance is signalled by the words, and ghee is common to all homas — आज्यमेवात्र होतव्यं नान्यन्मन्त्रलिङ्गाभावात्.

Metre

prose · syllables counted as written: 61

Prose offering formulas, one printed line of 61 syllables; no metre is claimed.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Order and attention

The formula moves from the near to the far — ground, air, sky — and then gathers all three. Naming the layers of one’s world before addressing anything beyond them is a simple way of paying attention to where one stands. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What changes when the word for food (10.3) is left out of the same formula?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Is a formula that names gods and regions better understood as an offering or as a meditation? What would decide it?

A contemplation exercise Notice, once today, the ground under you, the air around you and the sky above you, in that order.
