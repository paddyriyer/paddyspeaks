Taittirīya Upaniṣad · Bhṛguvallī · Anuvāka 6 of 10

Bliss is Brahman: the end of Bhṛgu’s search

The fifth and last step: Bhṛgu finds that bliss meets every part of Varuṇa’s definition, and the text names this the knowledge of Bhṛgu and Varuṇa, resting in the highest space.

1 Original Sanskrit

5 Faithful English translation

He understood: ‘Bliss [is] Brahman.’ For from bliss alone truly these beings are born; born, [they] live by bliss; into bliss [they] go forth [and] enter. This is the knowledge of Bhṛgu, taught by Varuṇa; [it] is established in the highest space. He who knows thus is established. He becomes possessed of food, an eater of food. He becomes great — by offspring, by cattle, by the lustre of sacred learning; great by fame.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

At last Bhṛgu understood: bliss is Brahman. For it is from bliss that all beings are born, by bliss that they live once born, and into bliss that they pass and return. This is the wisdom Bhṛgu found and Varuṇa taught, and it rests in the highest space. Whoever knows this stands firm. Such a person has food and enjoys it, and grows great in family, in wealth, in the light of learning, and in good name.

Key concepts आनन्द bliss ब्रह्मन् Brahman तपस् austerity; disciplined effort विद्या vidyā आनन्दमय the self made of bliss उपासना worship, meditation

7 Explain it to a beginner

This is the end of the story. Bhṛgu had tried food, breath, mind and understanding as answers to his father’s question — what is that from which beings are born, by which they live, and into which they return? Each time something was missing, and each time his father told him to go back to his practice. Now he sees it: bliss. Beings come from bliss, live by it, and return to it. The text then speaks for itself and calls this the knowledge of Bhṛgu and Varuṇa, resting in the highest space. Whoever knows it stands firm. The final lines promise ordinary blessings too — food, family, learning, a good name — which the commentators read as the visible sign of an invisible attainment.

An analogy Like a diver who keeps going deeper until he reaches the still water at the bottom, where the surface waves can no longer reach.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the anuvāka say?

Bhṛgu came to know that bliss is Brahman: from bliss beings are born, by bliss they live, into bliss they go and enter. This is the knowledge of Bhṛgu and Varuṇa, established in the highest space. Whoever knows it is established, has food and eats it, and becomes great in offspring, cattle, the lustre of learning and fame.

Traditional Interpretation

What did Bhṛgu’s search teach?

Śaṅkara: purified by austerity, Bhṛgu went inward step by step until he reached the innermost, bliss, by austerity alone — तपसैव साधनेन भृगुः. The lesson for every seeker follows: तस्मात् ब्रह्म विजिज्ञासुना बाह्यान्तःकरणसमाधानलक्षणं परमं तपः साधनमनुष्ठेयमिति प्रकरणार्थः — the supreme austerity is the gathering of the outer and inner senses. The text then leaves the story and speaks in its own voice: सैषा भार्गवी भृगुणा विदिता वरुणेन प्रोक्ता वारुणी विद्या.

Traditional Interpretation

Why does the teaching stop here?

Rāmānanda: the knowledge passes beyond the selves of food, breath and the rest and comes to rest in the bliss that is the supreme Self; it does not go beyond that as it went beyond them — न तु आनन्दशब्दवाच्यपरमात्मानमपि अन्नमयादिकमिव समतिक्रम्य अन्यत्र स्थितिं प्राप्ता. Gopālānanda (modern) gives the reason bliss is accepted where the others were not: it alone meets the definition, जन्मादिकारणत्वात् उक्तलक्षणात्.

Traditional Interpretation

How do the Viśiṣṭādvaita commentators read ‘the highest space’?

Kūranārāyaṇa: the teaching culminates in the bliss-self who dwells in the supreme abode — एवं परमपदनिलये आनन्दमये विद्या पर्यवसिता. He supports this with a chain of passages, among them the Gītā’s ‘I am the origin and dissolution of the whole world’ (7.6; his text, broken by a page header, begins अहं कृत्स्नस्य जगतः), cited for the bliss as the source and end of all.

Traditional Interpretation

What does Madhva see in the five steps?

Madhva’s verses on the vallī read the steps as Bhṛgu’s gradual knowledge of Viṣṇu in his many forms — क्रमाद् भृगुर्व्यजानात् तमन्नादिबहुरूपिणम् — and austerity as knowledge itself: तपो ज्ञानस्वरूपत्वाद् विज्ञानं तु विवेचनात्. The fruits are those of the released soul: मुक्तो भूत्वाऽन्नवांश्च स्यादन्नादश्च सदैव तु.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Taittirīyopaniṣad-bhāṣya, Bhṛguvallī 6

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

Bliss is the innermost Brahman, reached by turning inward through the five selves; it is non-dual, and to know it is to become it — ब्रह्मैव भवतीत्यर्थः. The highest space is the cave of the heart, which is that same non-dual bliss.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Kūranārāyaṇa; Rāmānanda; Gopālānanda (modern), commentaries on Bhṛguvallī 6 (six-commentary edition)

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

Bliss is the supreme Self, the bliss-self of 2.5, cause of all and distinct from the selves it pervades; the knowledge rests in him in the supreme abode (एवं परमपदनिलये आनन्दमये विद्या पर्यवसिता, Kūranārāyaṇa). The knower reaches him and does not return (Gopālānanda); Rāmānanda names the place as Sāketa.

Dvaita · Madhva, Taittirīyopaniṣad-bhāṣya, on the Bhṛguvallī

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

Bhṛgu came to know Viṣṇu step by step in the forms named food, breath and the rest, क्रमाद् भृगुर्व्यजानात् तमन्नादिबहुरूपिणम्; the knower, released, abides in him and enjoys him as ‘food’.

Where they disagree

All agree that bliss is the last word of the search. They disagree on what knowing it achieves — identity with Brahman (Śaṅkara), reaching the Lord in his abode (the Viśiṣṭādvaita commentators), or abiding in Viṣṇu as a released soul (Madhva) — and on where ‘the highest space’ is.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • आनन्दो ब्रह्मेति ānando brahmeti → आनन्दः + ब्रह्म + इति

    visarga; vowel sandhi

    aḥ before a voiced consonant becomes o: the visarga (ru) becomes u, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च), and a + u gives o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः). brahma + iti: a + i gives e: P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • आनन्दाद्ध्येव ānandāddhyeva → आनन्दात् + हि + एव

    consonant sandhi; yaṇ

    t becomes d before a voiced sound, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते), and h after it may become dh, P. 8.4.62 (झयो होऽन्यतरस्याम्); hi + eva: i or u before an unlike vowel becomes y or v: P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि).

  • खल्विमानि khalvimāni → खलु + इमानि

    yaṇ sandhi

    i or u before an unlike vowel becomes y or v: P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि).

  • प्रयन्त्यभिसंविशन्तीति prayantyabhisaṃviśantīti → प्रयन्ति + अभिसंविशन्ति + इति

    yaṇ; savarṇa-dīrgha

    i before a becomes y, P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि); i + i gives ī, Like vowels merge into the long vowel: P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • सैषा saiṣā → सा + एषा

    vṛddhi

    a or ā + e gives ai: P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि).

  • स य एवम् sa ya evam → सः + यः + एवम्

    visarga

    saḥ and eṣaḥ lose their visarga before a consonant: P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि). yaḥ before e: aḥ before a vowel other than a loses its visarga and the hiatus stays: P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि) makes it y, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य) drops the y.

  • पशुभिर्ब्रह्मवर्चसेन paśubhirbrahmavarcasena → पशुभिः + ब्रह्मवर्चसेन

    visarga

    After i the visarga (from s, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः)) stays r before a voiced consonant.

  • अन्नवानन्नादो भवति annavānannādo bhavati → अन्नवान् + अन्नादः + भवति

    visarga

    n before a vowel is simply joined; aḥ before a voiced consonant becomes o: the visarga (ru) becomes u, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च), and a + u gives o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

Morphology

व्यजानात्
Imperfect of jñā with vi: ‘he came to know (by discrimination)’. The same verb closes each of the five steps of the vallī.
व्योमन्
A Vedic locative without its ending (for vyomani), P. 7.1.39 (सुपां सुलुक्पूर्वसवर्णाऽऽच्छेयाडाड्यायाजालः); the same phrase stands in 2.1.
भार्गवी
Feminine of bhārgava, ‘belonging to Bhṛgu’ (taddhita, with the vṛddhi of the first vowel); vāruṇī likewise from Varuṇa.
अभिसंविशन्ति
Present of viś with abhi and sam: ‘enter into and merge with’; with prayanti, ‘go forth’, it repeats the third term of Varuṇa’s definition in 3.1.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

A report with iti closes the fifth step (ānando brahmeti vyajānāt), followed by the reason in three short clauses that repeat Varuṇa’s definition from 3.1 (born from, live by, return to), with hi … eva khalu. Then the text speaks in its own voice: a nominal sentence naming the teaching (saiṣā … vidyā) with a participle (pratiṣṭhitā), a relative-correlative sentence (sa ya evaṃ veda pratitiṣṭhati), and the refrain of results shared with 3.7–3.10.

Ambiguities

Where is ‘the highest space’?
Śaṅkara: the cave of the heart, which is the supreme non-dual bliss — परमे व्योमन् हृदयाकाशगुहायां परमे आनन्दे अद्वैते प्रतिष्ठिता परिसमाप्ता. Kūranārāyaṇa: the supreme abode, and the phrase is chosen to show that the highest cause is not dissolved even when everything else is — परमे व्योमन्निति वचनं सर्वतत्त्वविलयेऽपि परमाकाशरूपस्य परमकारणस्य लयाभावदर्शनार्थम्. Rāmānanda places it in Sāketa: साकेतस्थितम् आनन्दम् अवाप्नोति इत्यर्थः.
What does ‘is established’ mean?
Śaṅkara: established in the supreme Brahman — ब्रह्मैव भवतीत्यर्थः. Gopālānanda (modern): reaching that bliss, one does not return — तमानन्दं प्राप्य पुनः न आवर्तते इति यावत्. Madhva: the released soul abides in Viṣṇu.
Why repeat the worldly fruits after the highest one?
Śaṅkara gives food, offspring and fame as the ‘seen’ fruit beside the unseen one, and insists on the word’s full force: everyone has some food, so it must mean abundance — सत्तामात्रेण तु सर्वो हि अन्नवानिति विद्याया विशेषो न स्यात्. Kūranārāyaṇa reads annavān of the enjoyments of the supreme abode: परमपदे हि भोग्यसमृद्धिः.

    यतो वा इमानि भूतानि जायन्ते

    yato vā imāni bhūtāni jāyante

    Varuṇa’s definition in 3.1, which the three clauses of this anuvāka now answer with ‘bliss’.

  • Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.1.1 Direct textual parallel

    यो वेद निहितं गुहायां परमे व्योमन्

    yo veda nihitaṃ guhāyāṃ parame vyoman

    The same ‘highest space’ in which the Brahmānandavallī placed Brahman hidden in the cave.

  • Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.3.32 Doctrinal comparison

    एतस्यैवानन्दस्यान्यानि भूतानि मात्रामुपजीवन्ति

    etasyaivānandasyānyāni bhūtāni mātrāmupajīvanti

    Other beings live on a portion of this bliss — the Bṛhadāraṇyaka’s form of ‘by bliss they live’.

  • Bhagavad Gītā 7.6 Doctrinal comparison

    अहं कृत्स्नस्य जगतः प्रभवः प्रलयस्तथा

    ahaṃ kṛtsnasya jagataḥ prabhavaḥ pralayastathā

    Cited by Kūranārāyaṇa on this anuvāka for the Lord as the origin and dissolution of the world.

Every quoted phrase is checked against the text of the passage cited (sources in section 14).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Ego and self-image

Bhṛgu’s first four answers were not wrong; they were incomplete — the body, the breath, the mind and the intellect are all part of who we are. What he was taught to do was keep going until nothing was left out. People often settle on a partial account of themselves: a role, a talent, a reputation. The passage suggests patience with the question rather than a quick answer. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What do you most often take yourself to be — your body, your thoughts, your work?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Varuṇa never gave the answer; he only said ‘go back and practise’. What can be learned that way that cannot be learned by being told?

A contemplation exercise Sit quietly and notice, one after another, the body, the breath, the moving mind, the one who decides. Then notice what remains when you are simply content.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
