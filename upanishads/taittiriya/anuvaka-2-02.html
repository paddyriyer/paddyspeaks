Taittirīya Upaniṣad · Brahmānandavallī · Anuvāka 2 of 9

Food, the eldest of beings, and the second self, made of breath

A verse praises food as the source, support and end of all creatures; then the text turns inward to a second self, made of breath, which fills the body and has the same shape.

5 Faithful English translation

From food, indeed, creatures are born — whatever [creatures] dwell on the earth. And also by food alone they live; and then into it they go at the end. For food is the eldest of beings; therefore it is called the remedy for all. They obtain all food who meditate on food as Brahman. For food is the eldest of beings; therefore it is called the remedy for all. From food beings are born; born, they grow by food. It is eaten, and it eats beings: therefore it is called ‘food’. From that, indeed, from this [self] made of the essence of food, [there is] another, inner self, made of breath. By it this [one] is filled. That, indeed, this [one] is of the form of a person. Following that one’s person-form, this one is of the form of a person. Of it, the out-breath indeed [is] the head; the diffused breath the right wing; the down-breath the left wing; space the trunk; earth the tail, the support. On that too there is this verse.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

All creatures that live on the earth are born of food, live by food, and return to food at the end. Food came first, before the beings; so it is called the medicine for all. Those who revere food as Brahman never lack food. Beings are born of food and grow by food; food is eaten by them and in the end eats them, and that is why it is called anna. Within the self made of food there is another self, made of breath, which fills it. It too has a person’s shape, following the shape of the first: the out-breath is its head, the diffused breath its right side, the down-breath its left, space its body, earth its tail and support. There is a verse on this too.

7 Explain it to a beginner

The verse in this passage is a hymn to food. Every creature on earth is born from food, lives on food, and in the end goes back into food when it dies. Food was there before any of the beings that eat it, so it is called the medicine for all. Then comes a word-play: food is called anna because it is eaten, and because in the end it eats those who ate it. After the verse the text takes a step inward. Inside the body made of food there is another self, made of breath, which fills the body the way air fills a bag. It has the same shape: the in-and-out breaths are its head and arms, and so on. This is the second of the five layers.

An analogy A leather bag takes its shape from the air that fills it, and the air takes the shape of the bag; Śaṅkara uses the image of air filling a bag (वायुनेव दृतिः) for the breath filling the body.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the passage say?

A verse says that creatures are born of food, live by it and go into it at the end; that food is the eldest of beings and the remedy for all; that those who meditate on food as Brahman obtain all food; and it explains the word anna as ‘eaten’ and ‘eating’. Prose then names a second self, made of breath, inside the self of food, filling it and having its shape, with the five breaths, space and earth as its head, wings, trunk and tail.

Traditional Interpretation

Why praise food?

For Śaṅkara the verse prepares the meditation on food as Brahman. Creatures arise from food, live by it and dissolve into it (अन्नं प्रति लीयन्त इत्यर्थः); food is the remedy because it allays the burning of the body. One who meditates ‘I am born of food, made of food, dissolve into food’ (अन्नजोऽन्नात्मान्नप्रलयोऽहम्) obtains all food, because a meditation brings what it meditates on. The etymology closes the account of the first sheath.

Traditional Interpretation

Why a second self?

Śaṅkara: scripture wants to show Brahman, the innermost, as one’s own Self, by removing the five sheaths made by ignorance, as one husks grain to reach the rice — अविद्याकृतपञ्चकोशापनयनेन अनेकतुषकोद्रववितुषीकरणेनेव तण्डुलान् प्रस्तौति. The self of breath is as much imagined as the self of food, and takes its shape from it like metal cast in a mould (मूषानिषिक्तप्रतिमावत्). Gopālānanda (modern) names the same method as the ‘gross-star’ way of showing the faint star Arundhatī (स्थूलारुन्धतीन्यायेन): the eye is led from the obvious to the subtle.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Madhva read food?

For Madhva every name in the passage finally names Viṣṇu. He is ‘food’ because he eats all the worlds (अत्तृत्वात् सर्वलोकानामन्नमित्युच्यते हरिः), and the ‘remedy’ because he is the refuge of those burning in saṃsāra (संसारे दह्यमानानामाश्रयत्वात् स औषधम्). The suffix maya means ‘abundant’: प्रचुरं मय इत्युक्तं.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What do the forms tell us?

The verb pair adyate / atti (passive and active of ad) explains anna. The particle atho is pragṛhya and stays unjoined. -maya in prāṇamaya is the taddhita of ‘made of’; Śaṅkara glosses प्राणः वायुः, तन्मयः तत्प्रायः. anu with the accusative means ‘after the pattern of’.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Taittirīyopaniṣad-bhāṣya, Brahmavallī 2

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

The self of breath is, like the body, falsely imagined as a self (पिण्डवदेव मिथ्यापरिकल्पित आत्मत्वेन प्राणमयः); the series of sheaths is taught only to be removed, so that the innermost Brahman is known as one’s own Self. Meditating on food as Brahman is a lower meditation with a fruit (all food); it is a step, not the goal.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Gopālānanda (modern); Kūranārāyaṇa, commentaries on Ānandavallī 1–2 (six-commentary edition)

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

Gopālānanda reads the sheaths as a teaching device leading the mind step by step to the inner Self, the supreme Brahman in the self of bliss; the self of breath ‘also has a person’s shape, but not in reality’. Kūranārāyaṇa (whose commentary has no separate note on this anuvāka in the edition) had already said that the sequence beginning ‘from that Self’ answers the question how Brahman abides in the cave of the heart — हृदयगुहायां कथमवस्थानम् इत्यपेक्षां पूरयति.

Dvaita · Madhva, Taittirīyopaniṣad-bhāṣya, on Ānandavallī 2

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

The ‘food’ of the verse is Viṣṇu, the creator of beings, superior in qualities (स एष भगवान् विष्णुर्भूतस्रष्टा गुणाधिकः), present in the body made of food and its essence. Meditation on ‘food as Brahman’ is meditation on him under that name.

Where they disagree

For Śaṅkara the selves are sheaths imagined on the one Self and removed in turn; for Gopālānanda they are a graded teaching leading to the inner Brahman, who is distinct from them; for Madhva the names in the verse are names of Viṣṇu present in each.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • अन्नाद्वै annādvai → अन्नात् + वै

    consonant sandhi

    A final t becomes d before a voiced consonant: P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

  • काश्च kāśca → काः + च

    visarga before c

    The visarga becomes ś before c (the sibilant of the same place as the following stop).

  • अथो अन्नेनैव atho annenaiva → अथो + अन्नेन + एव

    pragṛhya and vṛddhi

    atho ends in the particle u (atha + u), so its o is pragṛhya (P. 1.1.15 (ओत्)) and is not joined to the following vowel (P. 6.1.125 (प्लुतप्रगृह्या अचि नित्यम्)). annena + eva: a + e → ai.

  • अथैनदपि यन्त्यन्ततः athainadapi yantyantataḥ → अथ + एनत् + अपि + यन्ति + अन्ततः

    vṛddhi, voicing, semivowel

    atha + e → ai; enat → enad before a vowel (P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते)); yanti + a → yanty a: P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि).

  • तेऽन्नमाप्नुवन्ति te'nnamāpnuvanti → ते + अन्नम् + आप्नुवन्ति

    pūrvarūpa

    te + annam: the initial a after e is absorbed and shown by avagraha: P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति).

  • ब्रह्मोपासते brahmopāsate → ब्रह्म + उपासते

    vowel sandhi (guṇa)

    a + u → o: P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • अद्यतेऽत्ति adyate'tti → अद्यते + अत्ति

    pūrvarūpa

    e + a: the a is absorbed (P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति)).

  • तदुच्यत इति taducyata iti → तत् + उच्यते + इति

    e before a vowel

    ucyate + iti: e becomes ay (P. 6.1.78 (एचोऽयवायावः)) and the y is dropped (P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य)), leaving ucyata iti with hiatus.

  • अन्योऽन्तर आत्मा anyo'ntara ātmā → अन्यः + अन्तरः + आत्मा

    visarga before vowels

    anyaḥ + a → anyo ’ (as in so ’śnute: P. 6.1.113 (अतो रोरप्लुतादप्लुते), P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति)); antaraḥ before ā loses its visarga, hiatus kept (P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य)).

  • अन्वयं anvayaṃ → अनु + अयम्

    semivowel

    u + a → va: P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि); ayam → ayaṃ before a consonant (P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः)).

  • व्यानो दक्षिणः vyāno dakṣiṇaḥ → व्यानः + दक्षिणः

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    P. 6.1.114 (हशि च): the visarga becomes u and a + u → o.

Junctions outside the general rules

  • अथो · अन्नेन — pragṛhya: atho ends in the particle u; its o is pragṛhya (P. 1.1.15 (ओत्)) and is not joined to the following a of annena.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • सर्वौषधम् sarvauṣadham

    tatpuruṣa · सर्व + औषध

    Vigraha सर्वेषाम् औषधम् सर्वौषधम्

    the remedy for all. Śaṅkara: what allays the burning of the body of all creatures (सर्वप्राणिनां देहदाहप्रशमनमन्नमुच्यते).

  • अन्नरसमयात् annarasamayāt

    ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa with taddhita maya · अन्न + रस + मय

    Vigraha अन्नस्य रसः अन्नरसः, तन्मयः अन्नरसमयः

    made of the essence of food (see 2.1).

  • प्राणमयः prāṇamayaḥ

    taddhita (mayaṭ) · प्राण + मय

    Vigraha प्राणः प्रचुरः अस्मिन् इति प्राणमयः

    made of, abounding in, breath. Śaṅkara: तन्मयः तत्प्रायः; Madhva and Gopālānanda give maya the sense of abundance.

  • पुरुषविधः puruṣavidhaḥ

    bahuvrīhi · पुरुष + विधा

    Vigraha पुरुषस्य विधा इव विधा यस्य सः पुरुषविधः

    having the shape of a person.

Morphology

अथो
atha with the particle u: ‘and also’. Ending in the particle u, its o is pragṛhya and stays unjoined before annena (see the junction recorded in section 10).
एनत्
The unaccented anaphoric pronoun (enad), used for something just mentioned: here, food.
ज्येष्ठम्
Superlative of vṛddha/praśasya, ‘eldest’. Śaṅkara: प्रथमजम् — food arose before the beings that eat it.
अद्यते
Passive of ad, ‘to eat’; with atti it gives the play on the word anna, ‘what is eaten and what eats’ (Śaṅkara: अद्यते भुज्यते चैव यद्भूतैः अत्ति च भूतानि स्वयम्).
एषः
The pronoun eṣaḥ (pausal form). After tena it fuses (tena + eṣaḥ → tenaiṣa) and loses its visarga before the consonant of pūrṇaḥ, P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि).
पुरुषविधताम्
Abstract noun in -tā from puruṣavidha (P. 5.1.119 (तस्य भावस्त्वतलौ)): ‘the having-of-a-person’s-shape’.
अनु
Used as a postposition with the accusative (‘following, after the pattern of’); Śaṅkara: the self of breath takes the shape of the self of food as metal takes the shape of a mould (मूषानिषिक्तप्रतिमावत्).

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

Two parts. Lines 1–14 are the verse announced at the end of 2.1: short clauses with present verbs (prajāyante, jīvanti, yanti), the reason clauses with hi (annaṃ hi …), the result clauses with tasmāt, a relative-correlative sentence (sarvaṃ vai te ’nnam āpnuvanti ye ’nnaṃ brahmopāsate) with brahma as predicate accusative of upāsate, and an etymology (adyate ’tti ca … tasmād annaṃ tad ucyate). Lines 15–26 are prose, repeating a pattern that will recur for every self: ‘from that self, there is another, inner self, made of X; by it the outer one is filled; it too has a person’s shape, following the shape of the outer one; of it, A is the head, B the right wing, C the left wing, D the trunk, E the tail and support’.

Ambiguities

apiyanti
api is a preverb of yanti (‘go into’), separated from its verb. Śaṅkara: अपि शब्दः प्रतिशब्दार्थे, they go back into food, are dissolved into it at the end of life. The words can also be read ‘they go even into it’, i.e. into food as the body is eaten; the commentators take the first.
annam brahma upāsate
All the commentators read the clause as meditation on food as Brahman, not as a statement that food is Brahman. Śaṅkara supplies the content of the meditation: अन्नजोऽन्नात्मान्नप्रलयोऽहम् (‘I am born of food, made of food, dissolve into food’). Gopālānanda gives the ground: food shares qualities with Brahman (being eldest, being a remedy), so the meditation is fitting (अन्ने ब्रह्मदृष्टौ निमित्तं समानगुणयोगमाह).
ākāśa ātmā; pṛthivī puccham
In a self made of breath, ‘space’ and ‘earth’ need explaining. Śaṅkara: ‘space’ is the breath that stays in the middle, samāna (य आकाशस्थो वृत्तिविशेषः समानाख्यः), and ‘earth’ is the deity of earth that holds the breath down, citing the Praśna. Gopālānanda gives both this and a plainer reading (space as the body in which breath moves, earth as what it rests on).

Metre

mixed · syllables counted as written: 9 + 8 + 9 + 9 + 8 + 9 + 9 + 7 + 8 + 9 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 10 + 12 + 10 + 5 + 11 + 8 + 8 + 8 + 7 + 8 + 5 + 8 + 9

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Wealth and consumption

The verse looks at food from both sides: it feeds us, and in the end it ‘eats’ us — the body returns to the earth that grew it. A culture of plenty easily forgets the second half. The passage invites a sober gratitude: what sustains us is also what we are made of and what we go back to. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.
