Taittirīya Upaniṣad · Brahmānandavallī · Anuvāka 5 of 9

The self made of bliss

A verse praising understanding as the doer of every rite, and then the fifth and innermost self: the self made of bliss, whose tail and foundation is Brahman.

5 Faithful English translation

‘Understanding performs the sacrifice, and it performs the rites also. All the gods worship understanding [as] Brahman, the eldest. If one knows understanding [as] Brahman, if one does not swerve from that, having left the evils in the body, one attains all desires.’ Of that [previous one] this very [self] is the embodied self, [the self] which [is the self] of the previous [one]. From that, from this [self] made of understanding, [there is] another, inner self made of bliss. By it this [one] is filled. That indeed is person-shaped. Following the person-shape of that [one], this [one] is person-shaped. Of it the dear indeed [is] the head; delight [is] the right wing; great delight [is] the left wing; bliss [is] the trunk; Brahman [is] the tail, the support. On that also there is this verse.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘Understanding carries out the sacrifice, and all our acts. All the gods revere understanding as Brahman, the first. One who knows understanding as Brahman, and never strays from it, leaves every evil behind in the body and attains all that is desired.’ This is the embodied self of the one before. Within the self made of understanding there is another self, made of bliss, and it fills the one before. It too has the shape of a person: what is dear is its head, delight its right wing, great delight its left wing, bliss its body, and Brahman its tail and foundation. There is a verse about this too.

7 Explain it to a beginner

Here the Upaniṣad reaches the innermost of its five selves. First a verse praises understanding: it is what carries out every sacrifice and every act, and even the gods revere it. Then the text says that inside understanding there is a self made of bliss. Like the others it is drawn as a bird: its head is what is dear to us, its wings are delight and greater delight, its body is bliss itself, and its tail — the part that steadies it — is Brahman. Readers have argued for over a thousand years about this self. Is it a final layer of happiness that we still have to see through, with Brahman standing beneath it? Or is it Brahman itself, the joy the whole journey was heading for? The commentators give both answers, and this page sets out both.

An analogy Like a series of rooms leading to a lit courtyard: some say the courtyard is the last room, others that the light falling into it is what the rooms were built around.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the passage say?

A verse says that understanding performs the sacrifice and the rites; that the gods worship understanding as Brahman, the eldest; and that one who knows it so and does not swerve leaves evils behind in the body and attains all desires. The prose then names a further, inner self, made of bliss, which fills the understanding-self. Its head is the dear, its wings delight and great delight, its trunk bliss, and its tail and support Brahman.

Traditional Interpretation

What does the verse claim for understanding?

Śaṅkara: the one who has understanding performs the sacrifice with faith, so understanding is called its agent — विज्ञानवान्हि यज्ञं तनोति श्रद्धापूर्वकम्. Whoever ceases to take the outer sheaths as the self and rests in understanding leaves the evils that arise from identifying with the body: शरीराभिमाननिमित्ता हि सर्वे पाप्मानः, and they fall away as a shadow goes when the umbrella is taken away, छत्रापाय इव च्छायायाः. Bhāradvāja instead reads vijñāna as the individual self, the agent: नन्द्यादित्वम् आश्रित्य कर्तरि ल्युट्.

Traditional Interpretation

Why does Śaṅkara say the bliss-self is not Brahman?

He gives reasons from the context: the bliss-self is introduced in the same way as the food-self and the others, all of them products — आनन्दमय इति कार्यात्मप्रतीतिः, अधिकारात् मयट्शब्दाच्च; it is said later to be ‘passed into’, as the others are; a head and wings cannot be imagined for the cause of space; and Brahman is named separately as its tail. So the bliss-self is the fruit of knowledge and rites: आनन्द इति विद्याकर्मणोः फलम्. The bliss in it is nonetheless the supreme Brahman showing through a clear mind: आनन्द इति परं ब्रह्म, and Brahman, the tail, is the support, अद्वैतं ब्रह्म प्रतिष्ठा.

Traditional Interpretation

Why do the Viśiṣṭādvaita commentators say it is Brahman?

Kūranārāyaṇa: the bliss-self is other than the individual self and within it, जीवादन्यः विलक्षणः तदन्तरस्सूक्ष्म आत्मा, and he reads the Brahmasūtra’s eight sūtras on the ānandamaya as proving that it is the supreme self (‘आनन्दमयो अभ्यासात्’). The repeated measuring of bliss in anuvāka 8 could not apply to an individual self. Rāmānanda: as the knower is called ‘made of knowledge’ because he abounds in it, so the supreme self is ‘made of bliss’: आनन्दरूपः परमात्माऽपि धर्मभूतैः आनन्दैः आनन्दप्रचुरत्वात् आनन्दमयः उच्यत इति ध्येयम्. Gopālānanda (modern): the teaching shows the inmost self step by step, ‘like showing the star Arundhatī’, and rests in the bliss-self (स्थूलारुन्धतीन्यायेन).

Traditional Interpretation

What do head, wings and tail mean here?

Śaṅkara: the dear is the pleasure of seeing a loved son or friend; delight, the pleasure of getting it; great delight, that pleasure heightened; bliss, pleasure in general, which runs through them all. Rāmānanda gives the same three stages — seeing, getting, enjoying — as qualities of the supreme self. Both read the figure as a way of making the self graspable, not as anatomy.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Taittirīyopaniṣad-bhāṣya, Brahmānandavallī 5

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

The bliss-self is the last of the five sheaths and still a product: तस्मात्कार्यपतित एवानन्दमयः, न पर एवात्मा. Its bliss grows as the mind is purified, but the goal is Brahman, named here as its tail — real, knowledge, infinite, ‘within all of them, by which they all have a self’ — the non-dual support in which all duality ends: अद्वैतं ब्रह्म प्रतिष्ठा.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Kūranārāyaṇa; Raṅgarāmānuja; Kauśika Govindarāja; Rāmānanda; Gopālānanda (modern), commentaries on Brahmānandavallī 5 (six-commentary edition)

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

The bliss-self is the supreme self, distinct from and within the individual self; ‘-maya’ means abounding, not a product: आनन्दमयः आनन्दप्रचुरः, निरवधिकानन्दः परमात्मा (Gopālānanda). Kūranārāyaṇa reads the Brahmasūtra on the ānandamaya as settling the matter: अतः सूत्रकारानुमतत्वात् आनन्दमयः परमात्मैव. The ‘brahman’ of the tail is the same Lord, pictured as support. Raṅgarāmānuja (Prakāśikā) meets the obvious objection — how can the same Brahman be both the tail and the self of bliss? — by distinguishing aspects: एकस्यैव ब्रह्मणो ब्रह्मत्ववेषेण पुच्छत्वम्, the one Brahman is the tail as Brahman and the body as bliss; and he concludes अतः अत्र आनन्दमयः एव प्रक्रान्तं ब्रह्म, the bliss-self is the Brahman the vallī set out to teach. Kauśika Govindarāja glosses the word the same way: आनन्दमयः आनन्दप्रचुरः अपरिच्छिन्नानन्द इत्यर्थः.

Dvaita · Madhva, Taittirīyopaniṣad-bhāṣya, on the bliss-self (printed with anuvāka 6 in the digital text)

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

Nārāyaṇa, whose form is bliss, stands always in the bliss-self: तस्मिन्नानन्दरूपोऽसौ स्थितो नारायणस्सदा. The one Hari is present fivefold, as soul in a body: देहदेहिवदेवासौ पञ्चधाऽवस्थितो हरिः, and the tail named brahman is in Vāyu, the chief breath: पुच्छं प्रधानवायौ च ब्रह्माख्ये संस्थितं सदा.

Where they disagree

All three read this anuvāka as the turning point of the five sheaths, but they place Brahman differently. For Śaṅkara the bliss-self is still a sheath, and Brahman is the tail beneath it. For Kūranārāyaṇa, Rāmānanda and Gopālānanda the bliss-self is Brahman, the inner self of the individual. For Madhva the Lord stands within each sheath, and in this one as bliss itself. The dispute continues in the commentaries on the Brahmasūtra (1.1.12–19), which the Viśiṣṭādvaita commentators cite.

Morphology

आनन्दमयः
ānanda + the suffix -maya. Pāṇini teaches -maya both for ‘made of, a product of’, P. 4.3.143 (मयड्वैतयोर्भाषायामभक्ष्याच्छादनयोः), and for ‘abounding in’, P. 5.4.21 (तत्प्रकृतवचने मयट्). Śaṅkara takes the first sense here, as in annamaya (मयट् चात्र विकारार्थे दृष्टः, यथा अन्नमय इत्यत्र); Kūranārāyaṇa the second, since nothing can be a product of Brahman’s bliss (तस्मिन् ब्रह्मणि विकारासम्भवात् प्राचुर्येऽपि मयड्विधिसम्भवात्).
विज्ञानम्
An action noun in -ana. Bhāradvāja and Kūranārāyaṇa read it as an agent noun, ‘the knower’, by treating it as a word of the nandi group: Bhāradvāja cites P. 3.1.134 (नन्दिग्रहिपचादिभ्यो ल्युणिन्यचः), नन्द्यादित्वम् आश्रित्य कर्तरि ल्युट्.
तनुते
Present middle of tan ‘stretch’, an eighth-class verb (tan-u-te): of a sacrifice, ‘to spread, carry out’.
वेद
Perfect of vid ‘know’, used with present meaning: ‘he knows’.
प्रमाद्यति
Present of mad with pra, a fourth-class verb: ‘to be heedless, to swerve’.
हित्वा
Absolutive of hā ‘leave’ (hi-tvā): ‘having left behind’.
समश्नुते
Present middle of aś ‘reach’ (fifth class: aś-nu-te) with sam: ‘attains fully, enjoys’.
प्रमोदः
mud ‘rejoice’ with pra: delight carried further. Śaṅkara: स एव च प्रकृष्टो हर्षः प्रमोदः.
एषः
The pronoun eṣaḥ (pausal form). After tena it fuses (tena + eṣaḥ → tenaiṣa) and loses its visarga before the consonant of pūrṇaḥ, P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि).

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

An eight-line verse (two anuṣṭubh stanzas) illustrates the self made of understanding: two indicative sentences with vijñānam as subject (‘understanding performs…’), a third with the gods as subject and vijñānam as object, then a conditional (cet … cet) whose result is the absolutive hitvā and the verb samaśnute. The prose then repeats the formula of the previous anuvākas — identification of the embodied self, the ablatives of comparison introducing the inner self (ānandamayaḥ), and five nominal sentences assigning head, wings, trunk and tail.

Ambiguities

Is the self made of bliss a product, or Brahman itself?
The decisive question of this anuvāka. Śaṅkara: the bliss-self belongs with the other sheaths — it is a product, the fruit of knowledge and rites — and Brahman is named separately as its tail: तस्मात्कार्यपतित एवानन्दमयः, न पर एवात्मा. Kūranārāyaṇa, Rāmānanda, Gopālānanda and Bhāradvāja: the bliss-self is the supreme self, the goal of the whole series, अतः सूत्रकारानुमतत्वात् आनन्दमयः परमात्मैव (Kūranārāyaṇa). Madhva too sees Nārāyaṇa standing in it. The question turns on the suffix -maya and on ‘brahma puccham’ (see morphology and the next ambiguity).
‘Brahman is the tail, the support’
For Śaṅkara this names Brahman apart from the bliss-self, as the support in which all duality ends: अद्वैतं ब्रह्म प्रतिष्ठा. For the Viśiṣṭādvaita commentators the bliss-self is Brahman, and the same Brahman is pictured as trunk (as bliss) and as tail (as Brahman): आनन्दत्वरूपेण मध्यकायत्वारोपः ब्रह्मत्वेन च रूपेण पुच्छत्वारोपः (Rāmānanda).
brahma jyeṣṭham: ‘Brahman, the eldest’, or ‘superior to Brahman’?
Śaṅkara: the gods worship understanding as Brahman, the eldest, because it is first-born (प्रथमजत्वात्). Kūranārāyaṇa and Gopālānanda take brahma here as primal matter (pradhāna) and jyeṣṭham as ‘superior to it’: the gods worship the knowing self as higher than matter (ब्रह्मणः प्रधानात् प्रकृष्टम्, Gopālānanda). Gopālānanda writes the two words as one, brahmajyeṣṭham.
vijñāna: intellect or knower?
Śaṅkara: the one who has understanding performs the rite, so understanding is called the agent. Bhāradvāja rejects the reading ‘mere intellect’ — अत्रत्य विज्ञानशब्दस्य बुद्धिमात्रपरत्वं कैश्चित् अङ्गीकृतम् — because the verse makes vijñāna the doer of sacrifice; vijñāna is the knowing self.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Emotional attachments

The bliss-self is drawn from ordinary joys: seeing someone we love, having them near, enjoying their company. The passage does not despise these; it uses them as the head and wings of the innermost self. But it places something steadier beneath them as the foundation. A life built only on the pleasures of what is dear rises and falls with them; the text points to a support that does not. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Think of a happy moment. Was the happiness in the thing, or in you?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Śaṅkara says even the bliss-self must be seen through; the Viśiṣṭādvaita commentators say it is the goal itself. What would follow, for a life, from each answer?

A contemplation exercise Notice, once today, the moment when seeing something you like becomes wanting it, and the moment wanting becomes having. Watch the three, without acting on them.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.

  • Bhāradvāja’s comment on this anuvāka is followed in the six-commentary edition by a passage marked ‘तै. भा.’; nothing from that passage is quoted here.

Uncertainties recorded

  • interpretation Whether the ānandamaya is a product (Śaṅkara’s Taittirīya commentary) or the supreme self (Kūranārāyaṇa, Rāmānanda, Gopālānanda, Bhāradvāja; Madhva differently) is the central interpretive dispute of this vallī. The translation ‘made of bliss’ follows the word’s form and leaves the dispute open.
  • uncertain Madhva’s comment on this anuvāka is abridged in the digital text; his reading is given from the lines printed with anuvāka 6.
