Taittirīya Upaniṣad · Brahmānandavallī · Anuvāka 8 of 9

The measure of bliss: from the happiest of men to Brahman

Everything works from awe of Brahman; then an inquiry measures bliss in steps of a hundred, from the happiest human life to Brahman, says at each step that the learned one free of desire already has it, and ends with the knower passing through the five selves.

स एको मनुष्यगन्धर्वाणामानन्दः ।श्रोत्रियस्य चाकामहतस्य ।ते ये शतं मनुष्यगन्धर्वाणामानन्दाः ।स एको देवगन्धर्वाणामानन्दः ।श्रोत्रियस्य चाकामहतस्य ।ते ये शतं देवगन्धर्वाणामानन्दाः ।स एकः पितॄणां चिरलोकलोकानामानन्दः ।श्रोत्रियस्य चाकामहतस्य ।ते ये शतं पितॄणां चिरलोकलोकानामानन्दाः ।स एक आजानजानां देवानामानन्दः ॥ २ ॥

श्रोत्रियस्य चाकामहतस्य ।ते ये शतमाजानजानां देवानामानन्दाः ।स एकः कर्मदेवानां देवानामानन्दः ।ये कर्मणा देवानपियन्ति ।श्रोत्रियस्य चाकामहतस्य ।ते ये शतं कर्मदेवानां देवानामानन्दाः ।स एको देवानामानन्दः ।श्रोत्रियस्य चाकामहतस्य ।ते ये शतं देवानामानन्दाः ।स एक इन्द्रस्यानन्दः ॥ ३ ॥

श्रोत्रियस्य चाकामहतस्य ।ते ये शतमिन्द्रस्यानन्दाः ।स एको बृहस्पतेरानन्दः ।श्रोत्रियस्य चाकामहतस्य ।ते ये शतं बृहस्पतेरानन्दाः ।स एकः प्रजापतेरानन्दः ।श्रोत्रियस्य चाकामहतस्य ।ते ये शतं प्रजापतेरानन्दाः ।स एको ब्रह्मण आनन्दः ।श्रोत्रियस्य चाकामहतस्य ॥ ४ ॥

स यश्चायं पुरुषे ।यश्चासावादित्ये ।स एकः ।स य एवंवित् ।अस्माल्लोकात्प्रेत्य ।एतमन्नमयमात्मानमुपसङ्क्रामति ।एतं प्राणमयमात्मानमुपसङ्क्रामति ।एतं मनोमयमात्मानमुपसङ्क्रामति ।एतं विज्ञानमयमात्मानमुपसङ्क्रामति ।एतमानन्दमयमात्मानमुपसङ्क्रामति ।तदप्येष श्लोको भवति ॥ ५ ॥

7 Explain it to a beginner

This passage tries to measure happiness. It starts with the happiest person you can imagine: young, good, educated, quick, strong, healthy, and owner of the whole world and all its riches. His joy counts as one unit. A hundred of those units make the joy of a heavenly singer; a hundred of those make the joy of another, higher kind of being; and so on, ten times, each step a hundred times greater, up to the joy of Brahman. At every step after the first the text adds a surprising line: a learned person who is not driven by wanting things already has that joy. The point is not the numbers. It is that joy grows as wanting falls away. Then the text says the one in us and the one in the sun are the same, and that whoever knows this passes, layer by layer, into the self made of bliss.

An analogy Like an old surveyor who measures a mountain by stacking a familiar staff again and again: the staff is ordinary, the count becomes absurd, and the point is that the mountain is beyond any staff.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the passage say?

Out of fear of ‘this’ the wind blows, the sun rises, Agni, Indra and Death do their work. Then ‘the inquiry into bliss’: the bliss of a young, good, learned, able, firm, strong man who owns the whole wealthy earth is one human bliss; a hundred of those are one bliss of the human gandharvas; and so on, each a hundredfold — divine gandharvas, ancestors, gods born in the ājāna, gods by rite, gods, Indra, Bṛhaspati, Prajāpati, Brahman. From the second step on, each bliss is said to be also ‘the śrotriya’s, not struck by desire’. Then: the one in the person and the one in the sun are one; the one who knows thus, going forth from this world, passes into the selves made of food, breath, mind, understanding and bliss. A verse follows (2.9).

Traditional Interpretation

Why do the powers of nature act ‘out of fear’?

Śaṅkara reads the lines as proof that there is a ruler: great and free beings keep to toilsome tasks only because someone governs them (तस्मादस्ति भयकारणं तेषां प्रशास्तृ ब्रह्म), like servants under a king (यतस्ते भृत्या इव राज्ञः अस्मात् ब्रह्मणः भयेन प्रवर्तन्ते). Kūranārāyaṇa cites the Brahmasūtra (कम्पनात्) and the Kaṭha verse, and draws a lesson for worship: even the gods one might worship live in fear, so no lasting joy is to be had from them. Gopālānanda: not only the breathing of creatures but every working of the elements, the planets and death depends on the Self of bliss (सर्वम् आनन्दमयायत्तम्). In the digital text of Madhva these lines are commented on at the end of his section on 2.7: the gods, though knowers, do their work in fear, to please Viṣṇu (भीताः स्वकर्म कुर्वन्ति विष्णोः प्रीत्यर्थमञ्जसा).

Traditional Interpretation

What is the ‘inquiry into bliss’ inquiring?

Śaṅkara: whether the bliss of Brahman is like worldly joy, produced by contact of subject and object, or natural to it (किमानन्दो विषयविषयिसम्बन्धजनितः लौकिकानन्दवत् , आहोस्वित् स्वाभाविकः, इत्येवमेषा आनन्दस्य मीमांसा). Worldly joy is itself a fragment of Brahman’s bliss (लौकिकोऽप्यानन्दः ब्रह्मानन्दस्यैव मात्रा), as another Upaniṣad says (एतस्यैवानन्दस्यान्यानि भूतानि मात्रामुपजीवन्ति), and the scale is a way to lead the mind from the known joy to the unknown. Rāmānanda puts it as a measuring rod: the emperor’s joy is fixed as the unit by which the others are measured (अयमेव आनन्दो मानदण्डस्थानीयः स्थिरीकृतः).

Traditional Interpretation

Why is the learned one ‘not struck by desire’ named at every step but the first?

Śaṅkara finds three means named in the passage — learning in the Veda, freedom from fault, and freedom from desire — and only the third varies (तस्मादेतानि त्रीणि साधनानीत्यवगम्यते): as desire for each lower level falls away, the bliss of the higher level is directly known, so freedom from desire is taught as the means to the highest bliss (अकामहतत्वस्य परमानन्दप्राप्तिसाधनत्वविधानार्थम्). The phrase first appears at the second step to say that one not struck by desire for human pleasures has a joy equal to a human gandharva’s (प्रथमं तु अकामहताग्रहणं मनुष्यविषयभोगकामानभिहतस्य श्रोत्रियस्य मनुष्यानन्दात् शतगुणेन आनन्दोत्कर्षः मनुष्यगन्धर्वेण तुल्यो वक्तव्य इत्येवमर्थम्). Rāmānanda gives another reason: the learned one does not seek the human joy at all, being without passion for it (अयं च आनन्दः श्रोत्रियेण विदुषा न अनुभूयते).

Comparative Vedānta

Where does the scale end?

For Śaṅkara the scale ends with Hiraṇyagarbha, in whom these degrees of bliss become one; his bliss is still a measured fragment of the supreme bliss, of which the others are drops of spray from the ocean (यस्य मात्रा समुद्राम्भस इव विप्रुषः प्रविभक्ताः); the supreme bliss is natural and non-dual (स एष परमानन्दः स्वाभाविकः, अद्वैतात्). Madhva’s verses read the whole scale as a scale of the released: even in liberation bliss differs (स्यादेव मोक्षस्तत्रापि ह्यानन्दस्य विचित्रता), each step a hundredfold, up to Brahmā. Gopālānanda and Rāmānanda make the last step the supreme Brahman, and explain that the number only says it surpasses Prajāpati’s bliss, as ‘the sun moves like an arrow’ says only that it is not slow (इषुवत् सविता गच्छति).

Comparative Vedānta

What does ‘the one in the person and the one in the sun are one’ mean?

Śaṅkara: the supreme Self, entered into the body and also the bliss shown in the sun, is one, as space in different pots is one space (भिन्नप्रदेशघटाकाशाकाशैकत्ववत्); the sun is named to show that no height or lowness remains for the knower. Kūranārāyaṇa identifies the person in the sun with the golden person of the Chāndogya, the lotus-eyed Lord (पुरुषः पुण्डरीकाक्षः पीताम्बररूपी उपास्यत्वेन उच्यते). Gopālānanda: the one in the heart and the one in the sun are one deity, to be meditated on in the heart with that divine form (हृदयगुहावर्तितया तादृशदिव्यविग्रहविशिष्टत्वेन च ध्यानं कर्तव्यम्).

Comparative Vedānta

Does the knower really ‘pass into’ the five selves?

Śaṅkara argues at length that no movement is meant: the all-pervading Self cannot travel, and a self cannot step into itself; ‘passing into’ is a figure for knowledge that destroys the mistaken identification with the sheaths (विज्ञानमात्रं सङ्क्रमणश्रुतेरर्थः, न जलूकादिवत् सङ्क्रमणमिहोपदिश्यते). Gopālānanda: the knower departs after death by the path of light and reaches the Self of bliss, so the text marks a difference between the one who attains and what is attained, and a journey to a place (प्राप्तृप्राप्ययोः भेदः देशविशेषगमनं चेति मुक्तौ विशेषः निरूपितः भवति). Kūranārāyaṇa likewise refers it to the path of light (अर्चिरादिमार्गः).

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Why the superlatives, and what kind of word is ‘bhīṣā’?

bhīṣā is an old instrumental of bhī, ‘fear’, kept in the Veda; the same three lines appear in the Kaṭha with bhayāt. The young man is described by three superlatives in -iṣṭha (āśiṣṭha, dṛḍhiṣṭha, baliṣṭha); the first has no agreed base, and the commentators give four different senses (ambiguities). The repeated pattern ‘te ye śataṃ … sa ekaḥ …’ is a relative–correlative pair: ‘those hundred … that is one …’.

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

The steps of the scale are steps among the released: liberation is certain for the knowers, yet bliss in it differs (स्यादेव मोक्षस्तत्रापि ह्यानन्दस्य विचित्रता), each class a hundredfold above the one before, up to Brahmā; the same holds for knowledge, devotion to Viṣṇu and strength. Madhva’s comment is reported from the verses he cites; the digital text marks it as abridged.

Where they disagree

All three read the scale as leading the mind beyond every measured joy. They differ on what lies at the end and what the knower gains. For Śaṅkara the degrees belong to ignorance and dissolve into one bliss that the knower is. For the Viśiṣṭādvaita commentators the released reaches the Lord, distinct from him, and enjoys his bliss as his body. For Madhva the degrees continue in liberation itself.

    The visarga becomes y, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), and is dropped, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य).

  • 2.8.5 स यश्चायम् sa yaścāyam → सः + यः + च + अयम्

    saḥ before a consonant; visarga before c; vowel sandhi

  • 2.8.5 यश्चासावादित्ये yaścāsāvāditye → यः + च + असौ + आदित्ये

    asau + āditye: au before a vowel becomes āv, P. 6.1.78 (एचोऽयवायावः).

  • 2.8.5 अस्माल्लोकात्प्रेत्य asmāllokātpretya → अस्मात् + लोकात् + प्रेत्य

    t before l

    A final t before l becomes l, P. 8.4.60 (तोर्लि): asmāl lokāt.

  • 2.8.5 एतमन्नमयमात्मानमुपसङ्क्रामति etamannamayamātmānamupasaṅkrāmati → एतम् + अन्नमयम् + आत्मानम् + उपसङ्क्रामति

    final m before a vowel

    A final m before a vowel is written together with it.

  • 2.8.5 तदप्येष श्लोको भवति tadapyeṣa śloko bhavati → तत् + अपि + एषः + श्लोकः + भवति

    consonant, vowel and visarga sandhi

    tat before a vowel → tad, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते); api + eṣaḥ → apy eṣaḥ, P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि); eṣaḥ drops its ending before a consonant, P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि); ślokaḥ before bh → śloko, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

Morphology

2.8.1 भीषा
A Vedic instrumental of the root noun bhī, ‘fear’ (classical bhiyā): ‘through fear’. The same three lines, with bhayāt, are Kaṭha 2.3.3.
2.8.1 स्यात्
Optative of as, ‘to be’, used for a supposition: ‘let there be a young man …’ — the imagined case from which the measure of bliss starts.
2.8.1 दृढिष्ठः
Superlative in -iṣṭha. Classical grammar replaces the ṛ of dṛḍha by ra before this suffix (draḍhiṣṭha), P. 6.4.161 (र ऋतो हलादेर्लघोः); Rāmānanda and Gopālānanda write so (द्रढिष्ठः अतिशयेन दृढः). The Upaniṣad keeps ṛ.
2.8.1 आशिष्ठः
A superlative in -iṣṭha whose base is not certain. Śaṅkara takes it from ā-śās, ‘to direct’ (आशास्तृतमः), Rāmānanda from āśu, ‘quick’ (अनलसतया क्षिप्रकारी), Gopālānanda from aś, ‘to eat’ (अशनसमर्थः).
2.8.2 पितॄणाम्
Genitive plural of pitṛ with long ṝ before -ṇām. The Sāyaṇa-pāṭha keys पितृणां with short ṛ (corrections.json: spelling only).
2.8.2 श्रोत्रियस्य
The genitive stands without a verb: ‘[this bliss is] of the śrotriya, too’. The line follows each step from here to the end, but not the first (human) step; see the commentary.
2.8.3 अपियन्ति
Present 3rd plural of i, ‘to go’, with api, ‘into’: ‘go into, attain’. Śaṅkara: they reach the gods by Vedic rites alone.
2.8.4 ब्रह्मणः
Genitive singular of brahman, which is the same in the masculine (Brahmā, the creator) and the neuter (Brahman). The grammar cannot decide which is meant; the commentators divide (see ambiguities).
2.8.5 आनन्दमयम्
The suffix -maya (mayaṭ) can mean ‘made of’ or ‘abounding in’; P. 5.4.21 (तत्प्रकृतवचने मयट्) gives the second sense. Gopālānanda takes ānandamaya as ‘abundance of bliss’ (आनन्दमयशब्दाभिप्रेतम् आनन्दप्राचुर्यं); whether the ānandamaya is Brahman itself is argued in the Brahmasūtra (BS 1.1.12).
2.8.5 उपसङ्क्रामति
Present of kram, ‘to step’, with upa and sam: ‘steps up to, passes into’. Whether the step is literal is the main question of the unit (see commentary).
2.8.5 प्रेत्य
Absolutive of i with pra, ‘having gone forth’; in many texts ‘having died’. Śaṅkara takes it here as turning away from the world of objects.

Anvaya

Syntax

Four short clauses of cause (bhīṣā … pavate, udeti; Agni, Indra and Death sharing dhāvati) introduce a nominal announcement (‘sā eṣā ānandasya mīmāṃsā bhavati’). The measure begins with optatives of supposition (yuvā syāt …, pṛthivī … tasya syāt) and a nominal sentence (sa eko mānuṣa ānandaḥ). Then one sentence-pattern is repeated ten times: a relative clause ‘te ye śataṃ X-ānām ānandāḥ’ (those which are a hundred blisses of X) taken up by ‘sa ekaḥ Y-ānām ānandaḥ’ (that is one bliss of Y), followed by the verbless genitive ‘śrotriyasya cākāmahatasya’ (and [it is] the śrotriya’s). One step has an explanatory relative clause (ye karmaṇā devān apiyanti). 2.8.5 is a relative–correlative identity (sa yaś cāyaṃ … yaś cāsāv … sa ekaḥ), then five parallel main clauses with the same verb, and a formula introducing a verse.

Ambiguities

Whose bliss is at the top: Brahmā’s or Brahman’s?
brahmaṇaḥ is the genitive of both masculine brahman (Brahmā) and neuter brahman. Śaṅkara takes it as Hiraṇyagarbha, the cosmic Brahmā (स एष हिरण्यगर्भो ब्रह्मा), whose bliss is still a fragment of the supreme. Kūranārāyaṇa takes it as the lotus-born lord of one cosmic egg (स्वनाभिपद्मजन्य-एकाण्डाधिपतेः), as does Madhva (तस्माद् ब्रह्मा शतगुणो मुक्त इत्येष निर्णयः). Gopālānanda takes it as the supreme Brahman named at the beginning of the vallī (ब्रह्मविदाप्नोति परम् इति प्रस्तुतस्य परस्य ब्रह्मणः आनन्दः), and adds that ‘one’ does not limit it (न च एक इति निर्देशात् ब्रह्मानन्दस्य परिच्छिन्नता); Rāmānanda says the same (प्रजापतेः आनन्दतः शतगुणोत्कर्षवान् आनन्दः प्रकृतस्य आनन्दमयस्य इत्यर्थः).
Who is Prajāpati?
Śaṅkara: Virāj, the cosmic person whose body is the three worlds (प्रजापतिः विराट् त्रैलोक्यशरीरो). Rāmānanda: the four-faced Brahmā (प्रजापतिश्च अत्र चतुर्मुखो ब्रह्मा). Madhva (cited verses): Rudra, lord of procreation (तस्मात् प्रजापतिर्मुक्तो रुद्रः प्रजननेशिता).
adhyāyaka: learned or teaching?
Śaṅkara: one who has studied the Veda (अध्यायकः अधीतवेदः). Gopālānanda and Rāmānanda gloss it as a teacher of his own age-group (यूनां समवयसाम् अध्यापकः). The text’s word is adhyāyaka; the second reading treats it as adhyāpaka.
āśiṣṭha
The base of this superlative is uncertain (morphology). Śaṅkara: most able to command (आशास्तृतमः); Rāmānanda: quickest, not lazy (अनलसतया क्षिप्रकारी); Gopālānanda: best able to eat, i.e. healthy (अशनसमर्थः); Kūranārāyaṇa gives several, ‘of wide fame’ among them (आशिष्ठः पृथुकीर्तिः).
pretya: dying, or turning away?
pretya often means ‘having died’. Śaṅkara takes it as withdrawing from the world of objects while living (अस्माल्लोकात्प्रेत्य प्रत्यावृत्य निरपेक्षो भूत्वा). Gopālānanda, Kūranārāyaṇa and Rāmānanda take it as departure after death by the path of light (अर्चिरादिना मार्गेण निर्गतः).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Wealth and consumption

The scale begins where most ambition ends: youth, health, education, ability and the whole world’s wealth. The Upaniṣad treats that as the smallest unit and then says, at every step, that someone free of desire already has more. Those who study happiness often say that more income adds less and less once needs are met, and that wanting more can spoil what one has. The passage makes a sharper claim: the measure of joy is not what one owns but how little one is driven. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

Further notes

  • Śaṅkara’s comment on 2.8.5 contains a long debate on how the knower can ‘pass into’ the self of bliss if he is not other than it; it is summarised above.
  • Madhva’s bhāṣya on this anuvāka is marked as abridged in the digital text (it begins with ‘…..’), and his comment on the opening lines about fear stands at the end of his section on 2.7; his views are reported from the verses that survive.
  • Rāmānanda’s notes here name the goal as the abode called Sāketa and the Lord as Śrī Rāma (साकेताख्यं देशविशेषं, श्रीरामपदप्रसिद्धम्), as a Rāmānandī author would.
