Taittirīya Upaniṣad · Bhṛguvallī · Anuvāka 2 of 10

The first answer: food is Brahman

Bhṛgu tests the first candidate against his father’s definition — food: beings are born of it, live by it and return to it — finds that it fits, and still goes back; his father sends him away with the same word: austerity.

7 Explain it to a beginner

After his effort, Bhṛgu reaches a first answer: food is Brahman. It seems to fit his father’s test. Every living being is born from food, lives on food, and when it dies goes back into the earth that becomes food again. Yet Bhṛgu is not satisfied, and he goes back to his father with the same request. The commentators guess why: food itself is produced and destroyed, so it cannot be the final source. His father does not correct him or give him a better answer. He only says: “Seek to know Brahman by austerity — austerity is Brahman.” In other words, keep working. Bhṛgu goes away and practises austerity again.

An analogy A student who finds an answer that passes the first check but still feels incomplete is on the right track; the teacher’s reply is not a correction but the instruction to keep going.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the passage say?

Bhṛgu concludes that food is Brahman, because beings are born of food, live by it and return to it. He goes to his father again and asks to be taught Brahman; Varuṇa answers only: seek to know Brahman by austerity; austerity is Brahman. Bhṛgu practises austerity again.

Traditional Interpretation

Did Bhṛgu make a mistake?

Not exactly: food does satisfy the definition as far as it goes — Śaṅkara says it has the stated marks (तद्धि यथोक्तलक्षणोपेतम्). But Bhṛgu fell into doubt because food is itself seen to come into being (अन्नस्योत्पत्तिदर्शनात्). Rāmānanda: food here means the body (शरीरम् इह उच्यते), already called ‘made of food’ in the second vallī, and it too is born and perishes.

Traditional Interpretation

Why only ‘austerity’ in reply?

Śaṅkara: the repetition teaches that austerity is the best means, to be kept up as long as the mark of Brahman is not found to be unsurpassed and the wish to know does not cease (यावद्ब्रह्मणो लक्षणं निरतिशयं न भवति, यावच्च जिज्ञासा न निवर्तते, तावत्तप एव ते साधनम्). Kūranārāyaṇa: everything that could be taught in words had been taught; the direct seeing cannot come from words, only from austerity (साक्षात्कारस्तु न वाक्येन शक्यते । किन्तु तपसा एव).

Traditional Interpretation

How does Madhva read the steps?

In his single comment on 3.2–3.7, Madhva reads the steps as Bhṛgu coming to know, in order, the one Viṣṇu in many forms beginning with food (क्रमाद् भृगुर्व्यजानात् तमन्नादिबहुरूपिणम्); ‘food’ is a name of the Lord (अन्ननामा तु भगवान्).

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What do the forms tell us?

The three clauses of the definition in 3.1 (yataḥ, yena, yat) are answered here, case for case, by the ablative, instrumental and accusative of anna. The verb vyajānāt supplies the main verb that the absolutive taptvā of 3.1 was waiting for, and the anuvāka ends on taptvā again.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Taittirīyopaniṣad-bhāṣya, Bhṛguvallī 2

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

Food meets the definition only in part; because it is produced, it cannot be the last word. The steps are a gradual inward movement, completed when Bhṛgu reaches bliss: शनैः शनैः अन्तरनुप्रविश्य अन्तरतममानन्दं ब्रह्म विज्ञातवान्. Austerity is repeated because it is the means until the search ends.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Kūranārāyaṇa; Rāmānanda; Gopālānanda (modern), commentaries on Bhṛguvallī 2 (six-commentary edition)

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

Food is a part of the world Brahman pervades, and seeing Brahman in it is a real but partial insight; what Bhṛgu lacks is not information but direct vision, which comes from austerity, not from the spoken word (ब्रह्मसाक्षात्कारसाधनं तपः एव, न वाक्यमित्यर्थः). Rāmānanda: he was not content because food too is born and perishes.

Dvaita · Madhva, Taittirīyopaniṣad-bhāṣya, one comment on Bhṛguvallī 2–7

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

Food is a name of Viṣṇu, the first of his forms that Bhṛgu came to know in order (क्रमाद् भृगुर्व्यजानात् तमन्नादिबहुरूपिणम्); the steps are not errors corrected but forms of the one Lord known one after another.

Where they disagree

For Śaṅkara and the Viśiṣṭādvaita commentators the first answer is partial and is left behind as Bhṛgu goes inward; for Madhva it is a true knowing of Viṣṇu under one of his names, completed by the others.

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • अन्नं ब्रह्मेति annaṃ brahmeti → अन्नम् + ब्रह्म + इति

    m before a consonant; guṇa

    A final m becomes anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः); brahma + iti → brahmeti, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • अन्नाद्ध्येव annāddhyeva → अन्नात् + हि + एव

    t before h; yaṇ

    The final t becomes d before a voiced sound, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते), and the following h may become the voiced aspirate dh, P. 8.4.62 (झयो होऽन्यतरस्याम्); hi + eva → hyeva, P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि).

  • खल्विमानि khalvimāni → खलु + इमानि

    yaṇ

    u before a dissimilar vowel becomes v: P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि).

  • प्रयन्त्यभिसंविशन्तीति prayantyabhisaṃviśantīti → प्रयन्ति + अभिसंविशन्ति + इति

    yaṇ; savarṇa-dīrgha

    prayanti + abhi-: P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि). abhisaṃviśanti + iti → -īti: P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • तद्विज्ञाय tadvijñāya → तत् + विज्ञाय

    consonant sandhi

    A final t becomes d before a voiced consonant: P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

  • पुनरेव punareva → पुनः + एव

    r-final visarga

    punaḥ ends in an original r, which reappears before a vowel: punar eva.

  • भगवो ब्रह्मेति bhagavo brahmeti → भगवः + ब्रह्म + इति

    visarga before a voiced consonant; guṇa

    bhagavaḥ before the voiced b: the visarga becomes u, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च), and a + u gives o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः). brahma + iti → brahmeti, also by P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • होवाच hovāca → ह + उवाच

    vowel sandhi (guṇa)

    a + u → o: P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • तपो ब्रह्मेति tapo brahmeti → तपः + ब्रह्म + इति

    visarga before a voiced consonant

    As bhagavo brahmeti: P. 6.1.114 (हशि च) and P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • स तपोऽतप्यत sa tapo'tapyata → सः + तपः + अतप्यत

    saḥ before a consonant; aḥ before a

    saḥ loses its ending before a consonant, P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि). tapaḥ before a: the visarga becomes u, P. 6.1.113 (अतो रोरप्लुतादप्लुते), a + u → o, and the following a is absorbed, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति), written with avagraha.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this anuvāka.

Morphology

व्यजानात्
Imperfect of vi-√jñā (ninth class, stem jānā-), with the augment a after the preverb: vi + a-jānāt → vyajānāt. It supplies the main verb for the absolutive taptvā that ended the previous anuvāka.
विजिज्ञासस्व
Desiderative imperative of vi-√jñā, ātmanepada by P. 1.3.57 (ज्ञाश्रुस्मृदृशां सनः); here with tapasā, ‘by austerity’, which 3.1 had left unsaid.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

This anuvāka sets the pattern for the next three. A main verb, vyajānāt, completes the absolutive left open at the end of 3.1, with a quoted equation (annaṃ brahma iti) as its content. Three clauses follow, explaining the conclusion with hi and repeating the three parts of the definition with the case forms of anna (ablative, instrumental, accusative), closed by iti. The narrative then repeats the approach of 3.1 (punar eva … upasasāra, adhīhi bhagavo brahmeti), Varuṇa’s new answer (tapasā brahma vijijñāsasva; tapo brahmeti), and the open ending (sa tapas taptvā).

Ambiguities

Why go back?
The text does not say why Bhṛgu was not satisfied. Śaṅkara: he doubted because food is seen to be produced (अन्नस्योत्पत्तिदर्शनात्). Rāmānanda gives the same reason (अन्नस्यापि उत्पादविनाशवत्वदर्शनात्). Kūranārāyaṇa has Bhṛgu tell his father that food is not infinite, not the cause of all principles and not unsurpassed bliss (त्वया उक्तमन्नं नानन्तं, न च सर्वतत्त्वकारणं, न चानवधिकातिशयानन्दम् इति).
tapo brahma
‘Austerity is Brahman’ is not read as identity. Śaṅkara takes it as stressing that austerity is the supreme means (तपसः पुनः पुनरुपदेशः साधनातिशयत्वावधारणार्थः); Kūranārāyaṇa: austerity, not the spoken word, is the means of seeing Brahman (ब्रह्मसाक्षात्कारसाधनं तपः एव, न वाक्यमित्यर्थः); Gopālānanda: तपो ब्रह्म ब्रह्मप्रतिपत्तौ साधनम्.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Wealth and consumption

The first thing we take to be fundamental is usually the material: what feeds and houses us. The passage does not call that wrong; it calls it incomplete, and it sends the seeker back to work rather than to a better slogan. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What is the first answer you give when someone asks what your life depends on?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry If food really meets the test — beings are born of it, live by it, return to it — what is missing? Why might an answer that fits still not satisfy?

A contemplation exercise At your next meal, notice for a moment that the body eating it is made of what it has eaten.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.

Further notes

  • Śaṅkara’s comment on this anuvāka also explains the pattern of the next three; he adds nothing on 3.3–3.5 beyond the text (ऋज्वन्यत्, ‘the rest is plain’).
  • The Kūranārāyaṇa file for this anuvāka opens with a passage quoting the Mahānārāyaṇa on austerity, apparently an editor’s note; it is not quoted here as his.
