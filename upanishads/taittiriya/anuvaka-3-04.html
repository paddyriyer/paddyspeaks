Taittirīya Upaniṣad · Bhṛguvallī · Anuvāka 4 of 10

The third answer: mind is Brahman

The formula repeats with mind: beings are born of mind, live by it and go into it. Bhṛgu returns once more, and is sent back to austerity.

7 Explain it to a beginner

The third time round, Bhṛgu decides that mind is Brahman. This goes deeper than food and breath: everything we know and do passes through the mind, and when the mind goes, our whole world goes with it. It looks like the source, support and end of everything we experience. Yet once again Bhṛgu returns to his father unsatisfied, and once again the answer is the same: practise austerity. One commentator explains why the mind still falls short: another Upaniṣad says the mind is made from food, so it too depends on something else. The search continues inward.

An analogy A screen shows everything we watch, but the screen is not where the pictures come from.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the passage say?

Bhṛgu concludes that mind is Brahman, because beings are born of mind, live by it and go into it. He returns, asks again, and is again told to seek Brahman by austerity.

Traditional Interpretation

In what sense do beings come from mind?

Kūranārāyaṇa: mind governs all knowing, acting and ceasing (मन आयत्तत्वाच्च सर्वबोद्धप्रवृत्तिनिवृत्तीनाम्), and when it departs everything perishes for the person (मनोविगमे सर्वस्य विनाशाच्च); he cites the Gītā, which counts mind among the eight parts of nature, to show that mind is a principle of the world.

Traditional Interpretation

Why is mind not enough?

Rāmānanda: another scripture says mind is made of food (अन्नमयं हि सोम्य मनः), so mind is a product, subject to coming and going, and cannot be Brahman (मनसः अन्नविकारत्वावगमात्).

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What do the forms tell us?

manas is an s-stem: ablative manasaḥ, instrumental manasā, accusative manaḥ. Before hi the ablative becomes manaso, so this anuvāka has manaso hy eva where the others have -ād dhy eva. 9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Taittirīyopaniṣad-bhāṣya, Bhṛguvallī 2 and 6

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

No comment of Śaṅkara’s own on this anuvāka (the rest is plain, ऋज्वन्यत्). Mind, like food and breath, shows only part of the mark; the series moves inward to bliss (शनैः शनैः अन्तरनुप्रविश्य अन्तरतममानन्दं ब्रह्म विज्ञातवान्).

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Kūranārāyaṇa; Rāmānanda, commentaries on Bhṛguvallī 4 (six-commentary edition)

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

Mind truly governs the whole of a creature’s activity (मनोविगमे सर्वस्य विनाशाच्च), but it is a product of food, as the Chāndogya says (अन्नमयं हि सोम्य मनः), and so it is not the final cause.

Dvaita · Madhva, Taittirīyopaniṣad-bhāṣya, one comment on Bhṛguvallī 2–7

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

Viṣṇu is called ‘mind’ because he is the thinker (मनो मन्तृत्वतस्तथा); this is the next of his forms that Bhṛgu came to know.

Where they disagree

As in 3.2–3.3. Madhva’s reading differs in kind: ‘mind’ names the Lord as thinker, not the human faculty.

  • मनो ब्रह्मेति mano brahmeti → मनः + ब्रह्म + इति

    visarga before a voiced consonant; guṇa

  • मनसो ह्येव manaso hyeva → मनसः + हि + एव

    visarga before a voiced consonant; yaṇ

    manasaḥ before h: P. 6.1.114 (हशि च) and P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः). hi + eva → hyeva, P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि).

  • खल्विमानि khalvimāni → खलु + इमानि

    yaṇ

    u before a dissimilar vowel becomes v: P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि).

  • प्रयन्त्यभिसंविशन्तीति prayantyabhisaṃviśantīti → प्रयन्ति + अभिसंविशन्ति + इति

    yaṇ; savarṇa-dīrgha

    prayanti + abhi-: P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि). abhisaṃviśanti + iti → -īti: P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • तद्विज्ञाय tadvijñāya → तत् + विज्ञाय

    consonant sandhi

    A final t becomes d before a voiced consonant: P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

  • पुनरेव punareva → पुनः + एव

    r-final visarga

    punaḥ ends in an original r, which reappears before a vowel: punar eva.

  • भगवो ब्रह्मेति bhagavo brahmeti → भगवः + ब्रह्म + इति

    visarga before a voiced consonant; guṇa

    bhagavaḥ before the voiced b: the visarga becomes u, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च), and a + u gives o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः). brahma + iti → brahmeti, also by P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • होवाच hovāca → ह + उवाच

    vowel sandhi (guṇa)

    a + u → o: P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • तपो ब्रह्मेति tapo brahmeti → तपः + ब्रह्म + इति

    visarga before a voiced consonant

    As bhagavo brahmeti: P. 6.1.114 (हशि च) and P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • स तपोऽतप्यत sa tapo'tapyata → सः + तपः + अतप्यत

    saḥ before a consonant; aḥ before a

    saḥ loses its ending before a consonant, P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि). tapaḥ before a: the visarga becomes u, P. 6.1.113 (अतो रोरप्लुतादप्लुते), a + u → o, and the following a is absorbed, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति), written with avagraha.

  • स तपस्तप्त्वा sa tapastaptvā → सः + तपः + तप्त्वा

    visarga before t

    The visarga becomes s before t: P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

Morphology

व्यजानात्
Imperfect of vi-√jñā (ninth class, stem jānā-), with the augment a after the preverb: vi + a-jānāt → vyajānāt. It supplies the main verb for the absolutive taptvā that ended the previous anuvāka.
मनसः
Ablative of manas (s-stem); before h its visarga becomes o (manaso hy eva). Here hi stands without the preceding t-stop that produced ddh in the other anuvākas.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

The same frame as 3.2 and 3.3, with the four case forms of manas. The base file drops a letter in upasasāra here (corrected).

Ambiguities

How are beings born of mind?
Kūranārāyaṇa: all knowing, acting and ceasing depend on mind, and when mind departs all is lost (मन आयत्तत्वाच्च सर्वबोद्धप्रवृत्तिनिवृत्तीनाम्; मनोविगमे सर्वस्य विनाशाच्च); he also cites the Gītā, where mind is one of the eight parts of nature. Rāmānanda, by contrast, explains Bhṛgu’s dissatisfaction: the Chāndogya says mind is made of food (अन्नमयं हि सोम्य मनः), so mind too is produced.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Technology and digital distractions

Much of modern life treats attention as the most valuable thing there is, and it is easy to conclude that whatever holds the mind is what matters most. The passage grants the mind its power and still asks what lies beneath it. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin How much of your day is shaped by what your mind is busy with?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry If the mind depends on the body, as the Chāndogya says, can the mind be the source of everything?

A contemplation exercise For a minute, watch thoughts arise and pass without following them, and notice that you can notice them.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
