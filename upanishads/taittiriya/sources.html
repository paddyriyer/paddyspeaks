Rāmānuja wrote no commentary on the Taittirīya. The Viśiṣṭādvaita commentaries are those of the six-commentary edition (Melkote): Kūranārāyaṇa, Raṅgarāmānuja (Prakāśikā), Bhāradvāja-Rāmānuja (Pratipadārthadīpikā), Rāmānanda (Ānandabhāṣya), Gopālānanda (Subodhinī, modern) and Kauśika Govindarāja. The edition prints several of them as running notes inside another commentator’s section, marked ‘आ.भा.’ (Rāmānanda), ‘तै. भा.’ (Govindarāja) and ‘प्र.’ (Raṅgarāmānuja), as its own colophons show; the importer files each passage under its commentator and cuts at the colophons. The source is OCR text and should be read against the printed book. Purāṇic and other verses that Madhva cites are reported as cited by him.

Open questions of the studies

  • Śīkṣā: the science of sound and the saṃhitā meditations: Do the six items of 1.2 summarise a particular śikṣā text, or the discipline in general? The commentators cite the Pāṇinīya Śikṣā and the Taittirīya Prātiśākhya; the anuvāka names neither. Kūranārāyaṇa’s quotations from those works are reported as cited by him and have not been checked against their editions.
  • Śīkṣā: the science of sound and the saṃhitā meditations: suvargeṇa or suvargyeṇa in the last line of 1.3? Śaṅkara’s text and gloss support the first; the Sāyaṇa-pāṭha and Madhva’s text read the second. A printed Taittirīya Āraṇyaka with Sāyaṇa’s commentary should be consulted.
  • Śīkṣā: the science of sound and the saṃhitā meditations: Madhva’s comment on 1.2–1.3 in the digital text is very brief. Whether a fuller construction exists in his bhāṣya or its sub-commentaries is not known from the materials of this edition.
  • Bhūr bhuvaḥ suvaḥ, the fivefold and Om: Who was Māhācamasya? The anuvāka names him only as the one who made the fourth vyāhṛti known; Śaṅkara explains the name as ‘son of Mahācamasa’, and Madhva’s cited verses identify him with Brahmā. Nothing else in the materials of this edition identifies him.
  • Bhūr bhuvaḥ suvaḥ, the fivefold and Om: Madhva cites verses from works he calls the Vyāhṛtitattva, Vyāhṛtisāra, Brahmasāra and Tattvasaṃhitā. They are reported as cited by him; the edition has no independent text of these works.
  • Bhūr bhuvaḥ suvaḥ, the fivefold and Om: The line ‘om śom iti śastrāṇi śaṃsanti’ (1.8) is printed alike in every witness. How it is to be understood in recitation would need a printed Āraṇyaka with Sāyaṇa’s commentary.
  • The teacher’s last instruction: satyaṃ vada, dharmaṃ cara: What does rerivā in 1.10 mean? The commentators give ‘mover’ (Śaṅkara), ‘one who wears away’ (Kūranārāyaṇa, Rāmānanda) and ‘cutter’ (Madhva’s cited verse); the word is rare and the materials of this edition do not decide it. Whether ahaṃ vṛkṣasya is two words or one compound is part of the same question.
  • The teacher’s last instruction: satyaṃ vada, dharmaṃ cara: Who are Satyavacas Rāthītara, Taponitya Pauruśiṣṭi and Nāka Maudgalya, and Triśaṅku? Śaṅkara takes Satyavacas and Taponitya as either epithets or names; Madhva’s cited verse calls Triśaṅku a king. Nothing else in the sources of this edition identifies them.
  • The teacher’s last instruction: satyaṃ vada, dharmaṃ cara: Madhva’s comment on 1.11 is missing from the digital text used here. A printed edition of his bhāṣya is needed before his reading of the address can be described.
  • Satyaṃ jñānam anantaṃ brahma: defining what cannot be defined: Madhva’s comment on 2.1 is missing from the digital text except for its final verse; his reading of satyam, jñānam and anantam needs a printed edition of his bhāṣya.
  • Satyaṃ jñānam anantaṃ brahma: defining what cannot be defined: Whether parama vyoman, ‘the highest space’, is the unmanifest (Śaṅkara’s avyākṛta), the space in the heart, or the Lord’s supreme abode — the commentators’ readings differ and the verse allows each.
  • Satyaṃ jñānam anantaṃ brahma: defining what cannot be defined: Whether ‘with Brahman, the wise one’ (brahmaṇā vipaścitā) qualifies the attaining (all desires at once, as Brahman) or the company of the attainer (together with Brahman); the readings follow from the school, not from the grammar.
  • The five selves: from food to bliss: Madhva’s comment on the self made of breath (2.2 in the text, filed under 2.3 in the digital source) is missing; which form of the Lord he places there needs a printed edition.
  • The five selves: from food to bliss: Whether ‘uttara pakṣa’ should be rendered ‘left’ or ‘northern’ wing: Śaṅkara’s gloss (the left arm, for one facing east) explains the word but does not show whether a ritual altar is in view.
  • The five selves: from food to bliss: Whether the five ‘selves’ were already understood as ‘sheaths’ (kośa) when the text was composed; the word is the commentators’, and the Upaniṣad’s own word is ātman.
  • Bliss, its measure and its limit: Madhva’s comment on 2.9 is absent from the digital text, and his comment on 2.7 ends in a gap (‘तमआख्यमनुत्थानम् ....’); his reading of the closing verse needs a printed edition.
  • Bliss, its measure and its limit: How to construe abhayaṃ pratiṣṭhāṃ vindate (2.7): Śaṅkara allows abhayam both as an adverb and as an adjective changed in gender; the choice affects whether ‘fearless’ describes the finding or the foundation.
  • Bliss, its measure and its limit: Whether brahmaṇa ānandaḥ at the top of the ladder (2.8) is the bliss of Brahmā (Hiraṇyagarbha), as Śaṅkara and Madhva take it, or points to Brahman itself, as the verse that follows might suggest.
  • Bliss, its measure and its limit: The base text prints ‘साधु ना करवम्’ with a space in 2.9 where the commentators read नाकरवम्; the letters are the same.
