The ‘आ.भा.’ passages — run on inside the six-commentary edition; attribution uncertain (most likely Rāmānanda’s Ānandabhāṣya)
The passages marked ‘आ.भा.’ (attribution uncertain; most likely Rāmānanda’s Ānandabhāṣya), commentary on the Taittirīyopaniṣad, in “Taittirīyopaniṣat with six commentaries” (Melkote; archive.org item 178-taittiriyopanisad-with-six-commentaries), via vishvAsa/vedAH_yajuH. Everything printed after an ‘आ.भा.’ mark inside another commentator’s section, by anuvāka. The edition runs this commentary on page by page without an attribution line and never marks where the first commentator resumes, so these passages may include some of his text. Rāmānanda’s own file lacks exactly these anuvākas, and his commentary is called the Ānandabhāṣya; the attribution is still unconfirmed.

Rāmānuja wrote no commentary on the Taittirīya; the Viśiṣṭādvaita commentaries used are those printed together in the six-commentary edition (Kūranārāyaṇa, Raṅgarāmānuja, Bhāradvāja-Rāmānuja, Rāmānanda, Gopālānanda, Kauśika Govindarāja), split by their own attribution lines. Gopālānanda’s is modern and is used only as a witness to the tradition. Purāṇic and other verses that Madhva cites are reported as cited by him; they were not checked against editions of those works.

Corrections to the base witness

  • Anuvāka 1.3: अथातः सं हिताया → अथातः संहिताया (orthographic) — A space inside the word saṃhitā in the base file. The letters are unchanged.
  • Anuvāka 1.3: ता महासं हिता इत्याचक्षते → ता महासंहिता इत्याचक्षते (orthographic) — A space inside mahāsaṃhitā in the base file (also in the two places below).
  • Anuvāka 1.3: इतीमा महासं हिताः → इतीमा महासंहिताः (orthographic) — As above.
  • Anuvāka 1.3: एवमेता महासं हिता → एवमेता महासंहिता (orthographic) — As above.
  • Anuvāka 1.3: अथाधििलोकम् → अथाधिलोकम् (keying) — The base file doubles the vowel sign (अथाधििलोकम्); S and Madhva’s text read अथाधिलोकम्, as the parallel अथाधिज्यौतिषम् etc. require.
  • Anuvāka 1.4: प्रमा पाहि → प्र मा भाहि (reading) — The base file reads पाहि, ‘protect’, but Śaṅkara’s own gloss explains bhāhi, ‘shine’: मा मां प्रति प्रभाहि प्रकाशयात्मानम्. S and Madhva’s text read भाहि. The base file’s पाहि is a slip of its mūla; corrected on the evidence of the commentary it accompanies.
  • Anuvāka 1.9: अग्नयश्य → अग्नयश्च (keying) — य for च in the base file; every other line of the anuvāka has ‘… ca svādhyāyapravacane ca’, and S and Madhva’s text read अग्नयश्च.
  • Anuvāka 2.6: सन्तमेन न्ततो → सन्तमेनं ततो (orthographic) — The base file breaks the word space inside the anusvāra’s class nasal (सन्तमेन न्ततो for santam enaṃ tato). The letters are unchanged; S and Madhva’s text read सन्तमेनं ततो.
  • Anuvāka 2.8: मीमां सा → मीमांसा (orthographic) — A space inside the word mīmāṃsā in the base file. The letters are unchanged; S reads मीमाँसा.
  • Anuvāka 2.8: चाकामहातस्य → चाकामहतस्य (keying) — The last of the ten occurrences of ‘śrotriyasya cākāmahatasya’ is keyed चाकामहातस्य in the base file; the other nine, S and Madhva’s text read चाकामहतस्य.
  • Anuvāka 3.3: अधीही भगवो → अधीहि भगवो (keying) — A long ī for short in the base file; the same request reads अधीहि in every other anuvāka and in S and Madhva’s text.
  • Anuvāka 3.4: पितरमुससार → पितरमुपससार (keying) — प dropped in the base file; every other anuvāka, S and Madhva’s text read पितरमुपससार.

The corrections restore readings that the base file has mistyped (spacing, a dropped or doubled letter); each is confirmed by the Sāyaṇa-pāṭha and by Madhva’s text, and none adopts a variant reading.

Unresolved issues

Textual verification required

  • Anuvāka 2.8, Madhva’s mūla: Madhva’s text as keyed lacks sarvā (‘all this earth full of wealth’); verify against a printed Madhva bhāṣya.
  • Anuvāka 2.8, Madhva’s mūla: Madhva’s text as keyed lacks one ‘devānām’ in the ānanda-mīmāṃsā (2.8); verify against a printed Madhva bhāṣya.

Uncertainties recorded in the analysis

  • Anuvāka 1.1 variant The Sāyaṇa-pāṭha reads ‘tvam eva pratyakṣaṃ brahma vadiṣyāmi’ in the eighth line; the base and Madhva’s text read tvām (corrections.json).
  • Anuvāka 1.2 variant The Sāyaṇa-pāṭha prefixes ॐ to the anuvāka, and Madhva’s text reads śikṣām for śīkṣām (corrections.json).
  • Anuvāka 1.2 interpretation The Dvaita reading (the six terms as qualities of the Lord) is inferred from the masculine forms of Madhva’s short gloss and his opening verse; the gloss itself does not name its subject.
  • Anuvāka 1.3 variant suvargeṇa (base, Śaṅkara) against suvargyeṇa (Sāyaṇa-pāṭha, Madhva’s text); four spacing corrections and one keying correction (अथाधिलोकम्) to the base (corrections.json).
  • Anuvāka 1.3 interpretation Whether the opening wish is the student’s or the teacher’s is disputed among the commentators; the text does not say.
  • Anuvāka 1.4 variant pāhi / bhāhi in the last line: corrected to bhāhi on the evidence of Śaṅkara’s gloss, the Sāyaṇa-pāṭha and Madhva’s text, but Gopālānanda and the ‘आ.भा.’ comment read pāhi. The correction should be reviewed against a printed Taittirīya Āraṇyaka.
  • Anuvāka 1.4 variant Madhva’s text reads dhātar ā yantu brahmacāriṇaḥ for dhātar āyantu sarvataḥ; the Sāyaṇa-pāṭha writes tasmint sahasraśākhe (corrections.json).
  • Anuvāka 1.4 uncertain The division of damāyantu / śamāyantu as dam āyantu / śam āyantu follows a single, imperfectly attributed comment.
  • Anuvāka 1.4 review The second comment on this anuvāka inside the Kūranārāyaṇa witness file (marked ‘आ.भा.’) may belong to another commentator in the six-commentary edition; its attribution needs checking against the printed book.
  • Anuvāka 1.5 variant Madhva’s text writes svar for the Taittirīya suvar (corrections.json, orthographic); the Sāyaṇa-pāṭha writes a visarga before a sibilant as that sibilant. Neither changes the meaning.
  • Anuvāka 1.5 interpretation The identification of the Brahman meditated on here (Hiraṇyagarbha, the supreme, or Viṣṇu in four forms) is a matter of school; the text names it only as ‘mahaḥ … Brahman … the self’.
  • Anuvāka 1.6 variant Madhva’s text writes upāsva for the base’s upāssva (orthographic, corrections.json).
  • Anuvāka 1.6 interpretation The identity of the person made of mind and of ‘Indra’ in indrayoni differs between and within the schools (see ambiguities).
  • Anuvāka 1.6 review The anatomy (the ‘teat’ as the uvula, the parting of the hair as the crown) follows Śaṅkara’s gloss; it is given as his reading, not as a physiological claim.
  • Anuvāka 1.7 variant The Sāyaṇa-pāṭha and Madhva’s text read avāntaradiśāḥ for the base’s avāntaradiśaḥ (corrections.json); both are forms of ‘intermediate quarters’.
  • Anuvāka 1.7 uncertain The digital text of Kūranārāyaṇa and Gopālānanda (OCR-derived) spells pāṅkta as पाङ्क throughout; quotations keep the printed spelling.
  • Anuvāka 1.8 variant The Sāyaṇa-pāṭha and Madhva’s text read anukṛti ha for the base’s anukṛtir ha (corrections.json).
  • Anuvāka 1.8 uncertain The ritual roles of śom and of the adhvaryu’s pratigara are given from the commentators’ glosses; this edition has no Śrauta source to verify them.
  • Anuvāka 1.9 variant The base text (Advaita Sharada) reads अग्नयश्य in the sixth line, a keying error for अग्नयश्च; corrected (corrections.json). Śaṅkara’s gloss अग्नयश्च आधातव्याः and the Sāyaṇa-pāṭha read च.
  • Anuvāka 1.9 variant Madhva’s text doubles च after tapas, damas and śamas (‘tapaś ca ca svādhyāyapravacane ca’); the base and the Sāyaṇa-pāṭha have one (corrections.json, near 1.9).
  • Anuvāka 1.9 review In the imported Kūranārāyaṇa file for this anuvāka, the text after the marker ‘आ.भा.’ appears to be a second commentary (probably Rāmānanda’s ‘Ānandabhāṣya’) run on in the source. It is not quoted here as Kūranārāyaṇa’s; the import should be checked.
  • Anuvāka 1.10 uncertain The derivation of rerivā is uncertain; the commentators derive it differently (Śaṅkara ‘mover’, Rāmānanda an intensive of rī ‘destroyer’).
  • Anuvāka 1.10 uncertain The metre: Kūranārāyaṇa’s ‘six-pāda jagatī’ is not reconciled with the printed syllable count (45).
  • Anuvāka 1.10 interpretation Madhva’s cited verse implies the division vājinīvasu for the letters read by the others as vājini iva su; the edition prints the base text’s spacing.
  • Anuvāka 1.11 variant Madhva’s text reads ayuktāḥ for āyuktāḥ (twice); the Sāyaṇa-pāṭha differs only in spelling (ऱ, anusvāra, visarga written as s before a sibilant). See corrections.json.
  • Anuvāka 1.11 interpretation Two word divisions are possible in tvayāsanena and aśraddhayādeyam; the padaccheda follows Śaṅkara (his first reading of tvayāsanena; his only reading of aśraddhayādeyam). Madhva’s printed spacing implies the other division of both.
  • Anuvāka 1.12 variant The Sāyaṇa-pāṭha and Madhva’s text close the Śīkṣāvallī with ‘om śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ’; the base does not print it. Madhva’s text reads avīt for āvīt (four times). See corrections.json.

Open questions of the studies

  • Śīkṣā: the science of sound and the saṃhitā meditations: Do the six items of 1.2 summarise a particular śikṣā text, or the discipline in general? The commentators cite the Pāṇinīya Śikṣā and the Taittirīya Prātiśākhya; the anuvāka names neither. Kūranārāyaṇa’s quotations from those works are reported as cited by him and have not been checked against their editions.
  • Śīkṣā: the science of sound and the saṃhitā meditations: suvargeṇa or suvargyeṇa in the last line of 1.3? Śaṅkara’s text and gloss support the first; the Sāyaṇa-pāṭha and Madhva’s text read the second. A printed Taittirīya Āraṇyaka with Sāyaṇa’s commentary should be consulted.
  • Śīkṣā: the science of sound and the saṃhitā meditations: Madhva’s comment on 1.2–1.3 in the digital text is very brief. Whether a fuller construction exists in his bhāṣya or its sub-commentaries is not known from the materials of this edition.
  • Bhūr bhuvaḥ suvaḥ, the fivefold and Om: Who was Māhācamasya? The anuvāka names him only as the one who made the fourth vyāhṛti known; Śaṅkara explains the name as ‘son of Mahācamasa’, and Madhva’s cited verses identify him with Brahmā. Nothing else in the materials of this edition identifies him.
  • Bhūr bhuvaḥ suvaḥ, the fivefold and Om: Madhva cites verses from works he calls the Vyāhṛtitattva, Vyāhṛtisāra, Brahmasāra and Tattvasaṃhitā. They are reported as cited by him; the edition has no independent text of these works.
  • Bhūr bhuvaḥ suvaḥ, the fivefold and Om: The line ‘om śom iti śastrāṇi śaṃsanti’ (1.8) is printed alike in every witness. How it is to be understood in recitation would need a printed Āraṇyaka with Sāyaṇa’s commentary.
  • The teacher’s last instruction: satyaṃ vada, dharmaṃ cara: What does rerivā in 1.10 mean? The commentators give ‘mover’ (Śaṅkara), ‘one who wears away’ (Kūranārāyaṇa, Rāmānanda) and ‘cutter’ (Madhva’s cited verse); the word is rare and the materials of this edition do not decide it. Whether ahaṃ vṛkṣasya is two words or one compound is part of the same question.
  • The teacher’s last instruction: satyaṃ vada, dharmaṃ cara: Who are Satyavacas Rāthītara, Taponitya Pauruśiṣṭi and Nāka Maudgalya, and Triśaṅku? Śaṅkara takes Satyavacas and Taponitya as either epithets or names; Madhva’s cited verse calls Triśaṅku a king. Nothing else in the sources of this edition identifies them.
  • The teacher’s last instruction: satyaṃ vada, dharmaṃ cara: Madhva’s comment on 1.11 is missing from the digital text used here. A printed edition of his bhāṣya is needed before his reading of the address can be described.
