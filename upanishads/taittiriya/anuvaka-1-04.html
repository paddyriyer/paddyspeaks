Taittirīya Upaniṣad · Śīkṣāvallī · Anuvāka 4 of 12

The teacher’s prayer: for understanding, for means, and for students

A teacher asks Om — ‘the bull of the Vedas’ — first for intelligence and a body fit to learn, then for prosperity, then for students who will come as water runs downhill.

यश्छन्दसामृषभो विश्वरूपः ।छन्दोभ्योऽध्यमृतात्सम्बभूव ।स मेन्द्रो मेधया स्पृणोतु ।अमृतस्य देव धारणो भूयासम् ।शरीरं मे विचर्षणम् ।जिह्वा मे मधुमत्तमा ।कर्णाभ्यां भूरि विश्रुवम् ।ब्रह्मणः कोशोऽसि मेधया पिहितः ।श्रुतं मे गोपाय ।आवहन्ती वितन्वाना ॥ १ ॥

कुर्वाणाचीरमात्मनः ।वासां सि मम गावश्च ।अन्नपाने च सर्वदा ।ततो मे श्रियमावह ।लोमशां पशुभिः सह स्वाहा ।आमायन्तु ब्रह्मचारिणः स्वाहा ।विमायन्तु ब्रह्मचारिणः स्वाहा ।प्रमायन्तु ब्रह्मचारिणः स्वाहा ।दमायन्तु ब्रह्मचारिणः स्वाहा ।शमायन्तु ब्रह्मचारिणः स्वाहा ॥ २ ॥

यशो जनेऽसानि स्वाहा ।श्रेयान् वस्यसोऽसानि स्वाहा ।तं त्वा भग प्रविशानि स्वाहा ।स मा भग प्रविश स्वाहा ।तस्मिन्सहस्रशाखे ।निभगाहं त्वयि मृजे स्वाहा ।यथापः प्रवता यन्ति ।यथा मासा अहर्जरम् ।एवं मां ब्रह्मचारिणः ।धातरायन्तु सर्वतः स्वाहा ।प्रतिवेशोऽसि प्र मा भाहि प्र मा पद्यस्व ॥ ३ ॥

यश्छन्द॑सामृष॒भो वि॒श्वरू॑पः । छन्दो॒भ्योऽध्य॒मृता॑त्सं ब॒भूव॑ । स मेन्द्रो॑ मे॒धया॑ स्पृणोतु । अ॒मृत॑स्य देव॒ धार॑णो भूयासम् । शरी॑रं मे॒ विच॑र्षणम् । जि॒ह्वा मे॒ मधु॑मत्तमा । कर्णा॑भ्यां॒ भूरि॒ विश्रु॑वम् । ब्रह्म॑णः को॒शो॑ऽसि मे॒धयाऽपि॑हितः । श्रु॒तं मे॑ गोपाय । आ॒वह॑न्ती वितन्वा॒ना ॥

7 Explain it to a beginner

This is a teacher’s prayer, and it asks for things in a careful order. First: a mind that understands and remembers, a healthy body, a kind tongue, ears that hear a great deal, and protection for what has already been learned. Only then does it ask for wealth — clothes, cattle, food — because, as a commentator says, money in the hands of someone without judgement only does harm. Then it asks for students: that they come, self-controlled and calm, from every direction, as naturally as water runs downhill. It also asks to be good, to be respected, and to be washed clean. The one addressed is, for the commentators, the sacred syllable Om, through which the highest reality is approached.

An analogy Like someone setting up a school who first asks for a clear head, then for the money to keep the doors open, and then for pupils — in that order, because the building is worthless without the teacher’s understanding, and the understanding is lonely without the pupils.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the passage actually say?

It addresses one who is ‘bull of the hymns, having every form’, arisen from the immortal hymns, and asks him to strengthen the speaker with medhā; asks to be a bearer of the immortal, with an able body, a sweet tongue and ears that hear much; calls him ‘the sheath of Brahman, covered by medhā’ and asks him to guard what has been learned. Then it asks for prosperity that provides clothes, cattle, food and drink, and for students, in formulas ending in svāhā; for renown, for excellence, for mutual ‘entering’ with Bhaga, for cleansing in the ‘thousand-branched’; and that students come like water down a slope and months into the year. It ends: ‘you are a resting-place; shine forth to me, come to me’. The name Om does not occur.

Traditional Interpretation

Why is a prayer for wealth inside an Upaniṣad?

Śaṅkara classifies the anuvāka as mantras of recitation and oblation for one who desires intelligence and one who desires prosperity: मेधाकामस्य श्रीकामस्य च तत्प्राप्तिसाधनं जपहोमावुच्येते. He justifies the prayer for wealth in a teaching about knowledge by a chain of means: श्रीकामोऽस्मिन्विद्याप्रकरणे अभिधीयमानो धनार्थः, wealth is for ritual action, कर्म च उपात्तदुरितक्षयार्थम्, and तत्क्षये हि विद्या प्रकाशते — when past wrongdoing is exhausted, knowledge shines. He explains why wealth comes after intelligence (tataḥ, ‘then’): अमेधसो हि श्रीरनर्थायैवेति — for one without intelligence, prosperity only does harm. Gopālānanda (modern) spells out the same chain: without wealth no rites, without rites no purification, without a clear mind no knowledge.

Traditional Interpretation

Who is ‘the bull of the hymns’?

Śaṅkara: Om, chief of the Vedas and ‘having every form’ because it pervades all speech, as the Chāndogya says (parallels): ओङ्कारो ह्यत्रोपास्य इति ऋषभादिशब्दैः स्तुतिर्न्याय्यैव ओङ्कारस्य. ‘Arose from the immortal hymns’ cannot mean that the eternal Om was produced; it means Om shone forth to Prajāpati as their essence — न हि नित्यस्य ओङ्कारस्य अञ्जसैवोत्पत्तिरवकल्पते. Kūranārāyaṇa also takes it as the praṇava, and sees in the praise a likeness between the praṇava, Brahman as word, and Nārāyaṇa, Brahman as reality: साधर्म्यं प्रतिपाद्यते.

Traditional Interpretation

What is asked for first, and why the body?

Śaṅkara takes medhā as strength of understanding (प्रज्ञाबलं हि प्रार्थ्यते) and ‘bearer of the immortal’ as holding the knowledge of Brahman. The requests for an able body, a sweet tongue and ears that hear much amount to one wish: आत्मज्ञानयोग्यः कार्यकरणसङ्घातोऽस्त्विति वाक्यार्थः — may this complex of body and senses be fit for knowledge of the Self.

Comparative Vedānta

‘May I enter you; enter me’ — union or service?

Śaṅkara reads the exchange as identity: having entered you, may I become you and nothing else, प्रविश्य चानन्यस्त्वदात्मैव भवानीत्यर्थः, आवयोरेकात्मत्वमेवास्तु; and ‘come to me’ as ‘make me one with you, like iron steeped in mercury’, रसविद्धमिव लोहं त्वन्मयं त्वदात्मानं कुर्वित्यर्थः. Kūranārāyaṇa addresses Bhaga as the Lord of Śrī and reads ‘entering’ as reaching him as one who enjoys him, receiving the whole of service to him — भोक्तृत्वेन प्राप्नुवानि, अशेषं कैङ्कर्यं लभेय — and ‘enter me’ as a plea to be accepted, as the sages of the Daṇḍaka forest were: दण्डकारण्यवासिनः शरभङ्गादिमुनिवर्यानिव त्वमेव मां स्वीकुरु. The same words carry the two schools’ different ends: becoming one, or being taken into service.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

pāhi or bhāhi in the last line?

The base file (Śaṅkara’s mūla in Advaita Sharada) prints ‘pra mā pāhi’, ‘protect me’; the edition reads bhāhi, ‘shine’, because Śaṅkara’s own gloss explains bhāhi (मा मां प्रति प्रभाहि प्रकाशयात्मानम्) and the Sāyaṇa-pāṭha and Madhva’s text agree (corrections.json). Kūranārāyaṇa also reads bhāhi (प्रभाहीति अन्तर्भावितण्यर्थः). But pāhi has commentarial support too: Gopālānanda writes मां प्र पाहि, and Rāmānanda’s Ānandabhāṣya (printed in the six-commentary edition as notes marked ‘आ.भा.’) explains प्रपाहि मां प्ररक्ष. The reading is not settled by the commentaries alone.

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

For Śaṅkara the addressee is Om as the symbol in which Brahman is perceived (त्वं हि ब्रह्मणः प्रतीकम् , त्वयि ब्रह्मोपलभ्यते). The prayers for intelligence serve knowledge of the Self; the prayers for wealth serve it indirectly, through rites that remove the wrongdoing that obscures knowledge, and he cites a smṛti to that effect. Viśiṣṭādvaita · Kūranārāyaṇa, commentary on Śīkṣāvallī 4 (six-commentary edition)

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

Kūranārāyaṇa reads the praṇava as the word that expresses the supreme Brahman, praised as a limb of the knowledge (अत्र परब्रह्मवाचकः प्रणवः विद्यागन्त्वेन स्तूयते), and the later formulas as addressed to the Lord with Śrī. ‘Entering’ is attainment as one who enjoys the Lord, completed in service (kaiṅkarya), and ‘enter me’ asks the Lord to accept the devotee. The ‘sheath of Brahman’ is a jewel-casket in which the Vedas and the Lord with his qualities are kept (रत्नमञ्जूशिकायां निधिरिव निहितमिति).

Dvaita · Madhva, Taittirīyopaniṣad-bhāṣya, Śīkṣāvallī 4

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

Madhva’s comment consists of verses he cites (without naming their source beyond ‘इति प्रक्लृप्ते’ and ‘इति च’). They make Hari the subject throughout: the bull of the hymns is यो विश्वरूपो भगवांश्च्छन्दसामधिपो हरिः; he is ‘the sheath of Brahman’ because the Vedas dwell in him, वेदावासत्वतो विष्णुर्ब्रह्मकोश इति स्मृतः; Bhaga because he is full of the six qualities, भगः षड्गुणपूर्णत्वाद्; and, pervading all, ‘thousand-branched’ because he has many forms (बहुरूपत्वतो विभुः). These verses are reported as cited by Madhva, not as verified quotations of any work.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara and Kūranārāyaṇa both make Om the addressee of the opening and read the praise as of what Om expresses; Madhva makes Hari the addressee directly. On ‘may I enter you, enter me’ the schools divide sharply: identity (Śaṅkara), attainment and service (Kūranārāyaṇa). All agree that the prayers for wealth and students serve the life of learning.

Morphology

1.4.1 स्पृणोतु
Imperative of spṛ (5th class), ‘to win, rescue, gladden’. Śaṅkara offers two senses, ‘gladden’ or ‘strengthen’, and prefers the second: प्रज्ञाबलं हि प्रार्थ्यते — what is asked for is strength of understanding.
1.4.1 भूयासम्
Benedictive (precative) of bhū, first person: ‘may I become’. The wish-mood is kept through the following lines.
1.4.1 विचर्षणम्
A Vedic adjective which Śaṅkara equates with vicakṣaṇa, ‘clear-sighted, able’. Since the subject is ‘my body’, the verb of the previous line must be supplied in the third person: भूयादिति प्रथमपुरुषविपरिणामः.
1.4.1 मधुमत्तमा
madhumat, ‘having honey’, with the superlative suffix tama.
1.4.1 विश्रुवम्
A Vedic form of śru with vi. Śaṅkara glosses it as a past form, ‘vyaśravam’, used for a wish (विश्रुवं व्यश्रवम्); Kūranārāyaṇa calls it a benedictive (विश्रुवमिति लिङ् आशिषि). Either way the sense is ‘may I hear much’.
1.4.1 पिहितः
Past participle of dhā with api, the a of the preverb lost (api-hita → pihita).
1.4.2 गावः
Nominative plural used where the construction wants an accusative (‘making clothes and cows’). Kūranārāyaṇa calls it a Vedic exchange of cases: विभक्तिव्यत्ययश्छान्दसः.
1.4.2 वासांसि
The base file writes वासां सि with a space inside the word, as it did with सं हिता; the letters are those of vāsāṃsi.
1.4.2 आवह
Imperative of vah with ā, ‘bring’. Śaṅkara and Gopālānanda take the one addressed to be the same as in the first unit: Om (अधिकारादोङ्कार एवाभिसम्बध्यते).
1.4.2 यन्तु
Imperative of i, ‘go’; with ā, ‘come’; with vi and pra, ‘come from different places’, ‘come forth’.
1.4.2 दम्
The division of damāyantu and śamāyantu is not explained by Śaṅkara or Kūranārāyaṇa. The comment printed after Kūranārāyaṇa’s in the six-commentary file takes dam and śam as words qualifying the students; the division followed here is its reading, and is uncertain.
1.4.3 असानि
Imperative first person singular of as, ‘to be’: ‘let me be’. The first-person imperative ends in āni (P. 3.4.92 (आडुत्तमस्य पिच्च) adds the augment ā).
1.4.3 वस्यसः
A Vedic comparative of vasu, ‘good, rich’, equivalent to vasīyas (Kūranārāyaṇa: छन्दसः ईकारलोपः), here ablative of comparison: ‘better than the richer’.
1.4.3 तस्मिन्
The Sāyaṇa-pāṭha inserts t before s (तस्मिन्त्सहस्रशाखे), an optional junction of recitation (corrections.json).
1.4.3 मृजे
Present middle of mṛj, ‘wipe, cleanse’, with the preverb ni standing apart: ni … mṛje, ‘I wipe myself clean’.
1.4.3 धातः
Vocative of dhātṛ, ‘arranger, ordainer’ (pausal dhātaḥ). Its final visarga goes back to r, which reappears before the vowel of āyantu. Madhva’s text reads dhātar ā yantu brahmacāriṇaḥ (corrections.json).
1.4.3 भाहि
Imperative of bhā, ‘shine’, with pra separated: pra … bhāhi. The base file printed pāhi, ‘protect’; the edition reads bhāhi with Śaṅkara’s gloss, the Sāyaṇa-pāṭha and Madhva’s text (corrections.json). Kūranārāyaṇa also reads bhāhi and takes it causatively, ‘make me shine’: प्रभाहीति अन्तर्भावितण्यर्थः.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

Unit 1 opens with a relative clause (‘who … arose’) resumed by ‘he’ (sa) and a third-person imperative (spṛṇotu). Then come first-person wishes in the benedictive and verbless wishes (‘my body [be] able’), a second-person statement (‘you are the sheath of Brahman’) and an imperative (‘guard’). From āvahantī a long feminine participial phrase describes an unnamed ‘she’ who brings and increases, resolved only at ‘bring me prosperity (śriyam)’. Units 2–3 are short formulas each closed by svāhā, mostly imperatives in the first and third person; the preverbs ā, vi, pra, ni and pra are several times separated from their verbs. Two comparisons with yathā … evam close the prayer for students.

Ambiguities

Whom does the prayer address?
The text names ‘the bull of the hymns, having every form’, ‘Indra’, ‘god’, ‘Bhaga’, ‘Dhātṛ’, but never says Om. Śaṅkara: Om, the object of meditation here (ओङ्कारो ह्यत्रोपास्य इति), praised with the names of the lord. Kūranārāyaṇa: the praṇava as that which expresses the supreme Brahman (अत्र परब्रह्मवाचकः प्रणवः विद्यागन्त्वेन स्तूयते), and in the later formulas the Lord of Śrī himself. Madhva: Hari (see the Dvaita position).
Who is covered by what?
‘You are the sheath of Brahman, covered by medhā’. Śaṅkara: Om is where Brahman is perceived, as a sword in its scabbard, but it is hidden by worldly understanding. Kūranārāyaṇa: all the Vedas and Brahman lie in it like treasure in a jewel-casket, and it is ‘covered by medhā’ in the sense of divine knowledge, so that those without it cannot grasp it (ज्ञानविशेषदर्शनीयस्वरूपत्वात् तज्ज्ञानरहितानां दुर्ग्रहोऽसीति). The same word medhā is the gift asked for at the start of the anuvāka.
acīram or cīram
kurvāṇācīram can be divided kurvāṇā acīram, ‘making quickly’, or kurvāṇā cīram, ‘making for long’. Śaṅkara gives both, preferring the first; Kūranārāyaṇa also gives both.
yaśo jane
Kūranārāyaṇa first takes yaśaḥ as ‘renowned’ (the sense of a possessive suffix understood) and jane as ‘among people’; he and Śaṅkara also read yaśojane as a compound, ‘among the renowned’ (यशोजने यशस्विजनेषु). The padaccheda follows the first reading.
dam āyantu, śam āyantu
The last two student formulas are not explained by Śaṅkara (‘and so on’). The division dam / śam + āyantu follows the comment printed after Kūranārāyaṇa’s in the six-commentary file; it is uncertain.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Wealth and consumption

The prayer does not despise money: it asks for clothes, food and cattle without embarrassment. But it asks for them second, after a clear mind and a body fit to learn, and Śaṅkara gives the reason bluntly — prosperity in the hands of someone without judgement only does harm. Many people today seek resources first and hope wisdom will follow. The order here suggests the reverse, and treats wealth as something that serves learning and teaching rather than the measure of a life. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

  • variant pāhi / bhāhi in the last line: corrected to bhāhi on the evidence of Śaṅkara’s gloss, the Sāyaṇa-pāṭha and Madhva’s text, but Gopālānanda and Rāmānanda read pāhi. The correction should be reviewed against a printed Taittirīya Āraṇyaka.
  • variant Madhva’s text reads dhātar ā yantu brahmacāriṇaḥ for dhātar āyantu sarvataḥ; the Sāyaṇa-pāṭha writes tasmint sahasraśākhe (corrections.json).
  • uncertain The division of damāyantu / śamāyantu as dam āyantu / śam āyantu follows a single, imperfectly attributed comment.
