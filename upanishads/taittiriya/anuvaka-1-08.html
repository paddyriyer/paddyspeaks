Taittirīya Upaniṣad · Śīkṣāvallī · Anuvāka 8 of 12

Om is Brahman, and Om is the word of yes

Om is named Brahman and the whole, and is shown to be the word that begins or permits every act of the ritual and of recitation.

5 Faithful English translation

‘Om’ [is] Brahman. ‘Om’ [is] all this. ‘Om’ — this, indeed, as is known, [is] assent. Moreover, [with] ‘O śrāvaya’ they make the call. [With] ‘Om’ they sing the chants. [With] ‘Om śom’ they recite the hymns. [With] ‘Om’ the adhvaryu gives the response. [With] ‘Om’ the brahman [priest] gives leave. [With] ‘Om’ he permits the fire-offering. [With] ‘Om’ a brāhmaṇa about to recite says: ‘May I obtain brahman.’ He obtains brahman indeed.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Om is Brahman. Om is all this. Om is the word of assent, as everyone knows. With ‘O, make heard’ the call is made; with Om the chants are sung; with ‘Om śom’ the hymns are recited; with Om the adhvaryu responds; with Om the brahman priest gives leave; with Om the fire-offering is permitted. With Om a brāhmaṇa about to recite says, ‘May I reach brahman’ — and he does reach it.

Key concepts ओम् Om ब्रह्मन् Brahman उपासना worship, meditation वेद Veda मन्त्र mantra

7 Explain it to a beginner

This short passage is about one syllable, Om. It begins with two large claims: Om is the highest reality, and Om is everything. Then it explains them through ordinary use. In the old Vedic rituals Om was the word of yes. When one priest asked, ‘Shall I go ahead?’, another answered Om. Om opens the call to the gods, the singing of chants, the recitation of hymns and the answers between priests. The priest in charge says Om to give permission, and Om is said before the daily fire-offering. Finally, a student about to recite the Veda says Om and wishes, ‘May I take in what is sacred’ — and, the passage says, he does. Because every sacred action starts with this one word, the text treats the word as worth meditating on.

An analogy Like the word ‘go’ at the start of every race on a track: no race starts without it, so that the one short word comes to stand for the whole day of running.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the passage actually say?

It says that Om is Brahman and that Om is all this; that Om is the word of assent; and that the ritual call, the chants, the hymns, the adhvaryu’s response, the brahman priest’s leave and the permission for the fire-offering are all given with Om. It says that a brāhmaṇa about to recite says Om, wishing to obtain brahman, and that he obtains it. It does not explain how Om is ‘all this’; that is left to the commentators.

Traditional Interpretation

Why is Om ‘all this’?

Śaṅkara: whatever has the form of sound is pervaded by Om, as the Chāndogya says, all speech is held together by Om as leaves by a stalk (तद्यथा शङ्कुना); and since a thing is known through its name, ‘all this’ can be called Om: ‘अभिधानतन्त्रं ह्यभिधेयम्’ इत्यतः इदं सर्वमोङ्कार इत्युच्यते. The rest of the anuvāka, he says, praises Om, because it is to be meditated on: ओङ्कारस्तुत्यर्थ उत्तरो ग्रन्थः, उपास्यत्वात्तस्य.

Traditional Interpretation

Why is Om ‘assent’?

Śaṅkara explains anukṛti as ‘repeating after’: when one person says ‘I shall do it’, the other answers Om, and so Om is the answering word. Gopālānanda calls it the syllable of permission (अनुज्ञाक्षरम्) and adds that whoever permits any act says Om: कार्यमात्रमनुजानन्नोमिति हि उच्चारयति. The ritual examples that follow show the same use between the priests.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Who are the priests named?

The passage names the four kinds of Vedic priest by their acts: the singers of Sāman chants (udgātṛs), the reciters of hymns (hotṛs), the adhvaryu, who performs the rite and responds to the recitation, and the brahman, who oversees and gives leave. Śaṅkara glosses the brahman’s act as permission (प्रसौति अनुजानाति), and the fire-offering clause as the answer Om given when someone says ‘I offer’ (जुहोमीत्युक्ते ॐ इत्येव अनुज्ञां प्रयच्छति). Gopālānanda defines śastras as verses recited without song.

Traditional Interpretation

What is the point of the last sentence?

Because every act that begins with Om bears fruit, Om should be meditated on as Brahman: ओङ्कारपूर्वं प्रवृत्तानां क्रियाणां फलवत्त्वं यस्मात् , तस्मादोङ्कारं ब्रह्मेत्युपासीतेति वाक्यार्थः. The brāhmaṇa who begins with Om wishing for brahman obtains it — the Veda in study, or, on Śaṅkara’s second reading, the supreme self.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Taittirīyopaniṣad-bhāṣya, Śīkṣāvallī 8

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

Om is the limb of every meditation: सर्वोपासनाङ्गभूतस्य ओङ्कारस्योपासनं विधित्स्यते. Meditated on as the higher or the lower Brahman, Om, though only a sound, becomes the means of reaching either: परापरब्रह्मदृष्ट्या हि उपास्यमान ओङ्कारः शब्दमात्रोऽपि परापरब्रह्मप्राप्तिसाधनं भवति. Śaṅkara supports this from the Praśna Upaniṣad.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Gopālānanda (modern), commentary on Śīkṣāvallī 8 (six-commentary edition)

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

No comment of Kūranārāyaṇa on this anuvāka is in the digital text used here. The modern commentator Gopālānanda reads the anuvāka as praise of Om, the support of the meditation on the supreme Brahman: परब्रह्मोपासनालम्बनभूतं प्रणवं स्तौति. Om is the word that denotes Brahman, and is praised as Brahman by superimposing on it the non-difference of the word and its meaning.

Dvaita · Madhva, Taittirīyopaniṣad-bhāṣya, on the eighth anuvāka

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

Om is a name of Viṣṇu, who has qualities beyond all others: ॐनामा भगवान् विष्णुरधिकोच्चगुणत्वतः. The priests perform their acts with Om because they direct them to him: ओमित्येव स्वकर्माणि कुर्वन्त्युद्दिश्य केशवम्. Madhva gives these lines as verses (closing इति च), without naming their source here.

Where they disagree

All take Om as the support or name of Brahman. For Śaṅkara it is a support for meditation on the higher or the lower Brahman; for Gopālānanda the word that denotes the supreme, praised as one with it; for Madhva a name of Viṣṇu, to whom every ritual act is directed.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • ओमितीदं omitīdaṃ → ओम् + इति + इदम्

  • अनुकृतिर्ह anukṛtirha → अनुकृतिः + ह

    visarga sandhi (r-visarga)

    A visarga after i becomes r before the voiced h.

  • वा अप्यो श्रावयेत्याश्रावयन्ति vā apyo śrāvayetyāśrāvayanti → वै + अपि + ओ + श्रावय + इति + आश्रावयन्ति

    A final n after a short vowel is doubled before a vowel: P. 8.3.32 (ङमो ह्रस्वादचि ङमुण्नित्यम्).

Morphology

अनुकृतिः
Action noun from kṛ with anu, ‘doing after, imitation’: the word with which one answers another. Śaṅkara’s example: when someone says ‘I shall do it’, ‘I shall go’, the other repeats it with Om — करोमि यास्यामि चेति कृतमुक्त ओमित्यनुकरोत्यन्यः.
ओ
The syllable o (Om) in the adhvaryu’s ritual call o śrāvaya, ‘make [the gods] hear’. Śaṅkara writes the call as one word, ओश्रावय.
प्रसौति
Present of sū, ‘to impel, to give leave’ (second class, with vṛddhi sau-), with pra.
प्रवक्ष्यन्
Future participle of vac with pra (pra-vak-ṣya-nt), ‘about to recite, about to teach’.
उपाप्नवानि
First person singular of āp with upa in the form of the imperative (originally the subjunctive): ‘may I obtain’. The next sentence answers it with the indicative upāpnoti, ‘obtains’.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

Ten clauses, each beginning ‘om iti’. The first two are nominal identifications (‘Om is Brahman’, ‘Om is all this’); the third gives a reason, with the particles ha sma vai, ‘as is well known’. The remaining clauses are verbal: in each, ‘om iti’ functions adverbially, ‘with the word Om’, and a priest or reciter is the subject of a verb of speaking, singing, responding or permitting. The last clause quotes a wish with iti (upāpnavāni, ‘may I obtain’) and answers it with an indicative (upāpnoti, ‘he obtains’).

Ambiguities

What is ‘brahman’ in the last two clauses?
Śaṅkara gives both readings: the reciter wishes to obtain the Veda, that is, to learn it (ब्रह्म वेदम् उपाप्नवानि इति प्राप्नुयां ग्रहीष्यामीति), or to reach the supreme self (अथवा, ब्रह्म परमात्मानम्). The Upaniṣad uses brahman in both senses in this vallī.
Is Om Brahman, or a means to it?
The first sentence identifies them. Śaṅkara reads it as an instruction to meditate on Om as Brahman, and explains that Om is a support for Brahman, as an image is for Viṣṇu: स ह्यालम्बनं ब्रह्मणः परस्यापरस्य च, प्रतिमेव विष्णोः. Gopālānanda calls the identification praise, by superimposing on the word the non-difference of word and meaning (वाचको वाच्याभेदारोपेण स्तूयते).
anukṛtiḥ or anukṛti
The base reads anukṛtir ha (anukṛtiḥ, feminine nominative), which Śaṅkara glosses अनुकृतिः अनुकरणम्; the Sāyaṇa-pāṭha and Madhva’s text read anukṛti ha, without the visarga (corrections.json, variant). The meaning, ‘imitation, answering word’, is the same.

Metre

11 Connections with other scriptures

  • Chāndogya Upaniṣad 1.1.8 Doctrinal comparison

    तद्वा एतदनुज्ञाक्षरं यद्धि किञ्चानुजानात्योमित्येव तदाह

    tadvā etadanujñākṣaraṃ yaddhi kiñcānujānātyomityeva tadāha

    The Chāndogya also calls Om the syllable of assent: whatever one permits, one says Om.

  • Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad 1 Doctrinal comparison

    ओमित्येतदक्षरमिदं सर्वं

    omityetadakṣaramidaṃ sarvaṃ

    The Māṇḍūkya opens with the same equation: the syllable Om is all this.

  • Chāndogya Upaniṣad 2.23.3 Doctrinal comparison

    ओङ्कार एवेदं सर्वम्

    oṅkāra evedaṃ sarvam

    Cited by Śaṅkara: all speech is held together by Om, as leaves by a stalk; ‘Om indeed is all this’.

  • Praśna Upaniṣad 5.2 Doctrinal comparison

    एतद्वै सत्यकाम परं चापरं च ब्रह्म यदोङ्कारः

    etadvai satyakāma paraṃ cāparaṃ ca brahma yadoṅkāraḥ

    Cited by Śaṅkara for Om as the support of both the higher and the lower Brahman.

  • Kaṭha Upaniṣad 1.2.15 Modern thematic association

    ओमित्येतत्

    omityetat

    In the Kaṭha, Yama gives Om as the word which all the Vedas declare.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Corporate life

In the rituals described here, nothing proceeds without someone’s explicit yes: one priest calls, another answers Om, and only then does the act go on. Many failures in organisations begin with an assumed yes — work that proceeds because no one objected. The passage makes assent a spoken, deliberate act, and then honours that act as sacred. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What is a word you say many times a day without noticing? What does it begin or allow?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry How can one syllable be both ‘all this’ and the everyday word for ‘yes’? What does the second claim do for the first?

A contemplation exercise Before beginning a piece of work today, pause and say to yourself a deliberate ‘yes’ to it. Notice whether you begin differently.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
