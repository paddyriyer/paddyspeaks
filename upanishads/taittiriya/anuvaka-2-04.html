Taittirīya Upaniṣad · Brahmānandavallī · Anuvāka 4 of 9

The self made of understanding

A verse on the bliss that words and mind turn back from, and a fourth self within the mind — the self made of understanding, with faith for its head and concentration for its body.

5 Faithful English translation

‘From which words turn back, together with the mind, not having reached [it] — knowing the bliss of Brahman, he does not fear at any time.’ Of that [previous one] this very [self] is the embodied self, [the self] which [is the self] of the previous [one]. From that, from this [self] made of mind, [there is] another, inner self made of understanding. By it this [one] is filled. That indeed is person-shaped. Following the person-shape of that [one], this [one] is person-shaped. Of it faith indeed [is] the head; the right [is] the right wing; truth [is] the left wing; yoga [is] the trunk; mahas [is] the tail, the support. On that also there is this verse.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘Words turn back from it, with the mind, without reaching it. One who knows the bliss of Brahman is never afraid.’ This is the embodied self of the one before. Within the self made of mind there is another self, made of understanding, and it fills the mind-self. It too has the shape of a person, following the shape of the one before: faith is its head, the right is its right wing, truth its left wing, concentration its body, and the great its tail and foundation. There is a verse about this too.

Key concepts आत्मन् self, Self विद्या vidyā श्रद्धा faith, trust ऋत the right; cosmic and moral order सत्य truth, the real पुरुष person ब्रह्मन् Brahman जीव individual self मनस् mind कोश sheath आनन्द bliss विज्ञान understanding, the knowing self

7 Explain it to a beginner

The Taittirīya describes a person as five selves, one inside another, like layers. We have met the body made of food, the self made of breath and the self made of mind. Here the fourth appears: the self made of understanding — the part of us that weighs, decides and is sure. The text pictures it, like the others, as a bird. Its head is faith, because one must trust something before one acts; its two wings are what is right and what is true; its body is focused attention; its tail is ‘the great’, which holds it steady. Before that, a short verse looks ahead to the goal: there is a joy that words cannot reach and the mind cannot grasp, and one who knows it is never afraid. The text is moving inward, step by step, towards that joy.

An analogy Like peeling an onion with a guide beside you naming each layer as it comes off — skin, flesh, heart — while promising there is something at the centre that no layer contains.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the passage say?

It quotes a verse: words turn back, with the mind, from the bliss of Brahman, and one who knows that bliss fears nothing at any time. It then says that the self just described (made of mind) has the same embodied self as the one before it, and names a further self within it, made of understanding, which fills the mind-self as each inner self filled the outer. This self is pictured in the same person-like (bird-like) shape: faith the head, ṛta and satya the wings, yoga the trunk, mahas the tail.

Traditional Interpretation

Why does the understanding-self lie within the mind?

Śaṅkara: the mind-self is the Veda as held in the mind; within it lies the self made of settled, valid knowledge, because every rite is undertaken on the basis of such knowledge: प्रमाणविज्ञानपूर्वको हि यज्ञादिः तायते. Faith is its head because faith comes first in all that is to be done: सा सर्वकर्तव्यानां प्राथम्यात् शिर इव शिरः. Yoga, ‘collectedness’, is its trunk, because the limbs only serve one who is collected: योगः युक्तिः समाधानम्.

Traditional Interpretation

Who is the self made of understanding?

For the Viśiṣṭādvaita commentators it is the individual self, the knower, not an organ: Kūranārāyaṇa argues from the suffix (‘made of’ marks a self distinct from the mere intellect) and from the next verse, which makes vijñāna the agent of sacrifice. Gopālānanda (modern) and Rāmānanda (Ānandabhāṣya) agree: विज्ञानमयपदेन तु मनोव्यापारजन्य विज्ञानप्रचुरो जीवः इति विवक्ष्यते. Faith, right and truth, yoga and mahas are then the self’s own qualities pictured as limbs; Kūranārāyaṇa describes the faith as महाविश्वासपूर्विका भक्तिगर्भा भगवदुपासनोद्योगत्वलक्षणा श्रद्धैव शिर.

Traditional Interpretation

Why is the verse about bliss placed here?

Because it praises the mind, the self of anuvāka 3. Śaṅkara, commenting on the verse when it returns in 2.9, says it was cited for the mind-self because the mind is the instrument of knowing Brahman: मनोमये च उदाहृतः मन्त्रः, मनसो ब्रह्मविज्ञानसाधनत्वात्. Kūranārāyaṇa calls it a hymn to the mind’s power: only a purified mind sees the ocean of Brahman’s bliss, केवलं मन एव भक्तिसिद्धाञ्जनं दिव्यचक्षुरिति मनोवैभवस्तोत्रं भवति. Gopālānanda: इयञ्च मनसः स्तुतिः.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Does the verse say Brahman cannot be known?

It says words turn back ‘not having reached’ — and in the same breath speaks of one who knows the bliss of Brahman. The Viśiṣṭādvaita commentators read the not-reaching as the failure to find the bliss’s limit: Rāmānanda glosses aprāpya as not reaching its measure, ब्रह्मानन्दस्य इयत्तालक्षणं पारम् अलब्ध्वा, and Kūranārāyaṇa has the Vedas, counting Brahman’s bliss in anuvāka 8, turn back because they find no limit. Śaṅkara’s fuller reading, in 2.9, is that words and concepts fall away before what is without distinctions. The text itself holds both ‘not reaching’ and ‘knowing’ together.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Taittirīyopaniṣad-bhāṣya, Brahmānandavallī 4

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

The understanding-self is the self formed of the intellect’s settled knowledge, निश्चयविज्ञानैः प्रमाणस्वरूपैर्निर्वर्तितः आत्मा विज्ञानमयः. Like the sheaths before it, it is a product and a step: the text leads inward through it towards Brahman, which is the true self of all of them. Mahas, its tail, is the great principle, the first-born, which supports all knowing as its cause: कारणं हि कार्याणां प्रतिष्ठा, यथा वृक्षवीरुधां पृथिवी.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Kūranārāyaṇa; Gopālānanda (modern), commentaries on Brahmānandavallī 4 (six-commentary edition)

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

The understanding-self is the individual self (jīva), the knower and agent, distinct from the inner organ (विज्ञानमयो जीवः, न बुद्धिमात्रं). Its limbs are the self’s own qualities — faith, right knowledge, truth, meditation on the Lord, and the power that removes obstacles to yoga. Gopālānanda: विज्ञानमयः विज्ञानप्रचुरो विज्ञाता जीवः. The next anuvāka will name, within it, the supreme self.

Dvaita · Madhva, Taittirīyopaniṣad-bhāṣya, on these anuvākas (abridged in the digital text)

From the school’s commentary on this anuvāka

Madhva’s comment as imported is abridged (‘....’). In what survives, a form of Hari stands in each of these selves — तस्मिन् सङ्कर्षणः स्थितः is said of the mind-self, and वासुदेवोऽन्तरस्ततः opens the account of the next — and the limbs are named for what the Lord does: the head is called faith because he holds the Veda, श्रद्धाख्यं श्रुतिधर्तृत्वाच्छ्रद्धायां तच्छिरः स्मृतम्. The digital text prints these lines with the next anuvāka.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara’s understanding-self is the intellect’s knowledge identified with as a self — a sheath to be passed through. For Kūranārāyaṇa and Gopālānanda it is the real individual self, the knower, and its limbs are its real qualities. Madhva sees a form of the Lord abiding in each sheath, and reads the limbs as his names.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • यतो वाचो yato vāco → यतः + वाचः

    visarga sandhi (aḥ before a voiced consonant)

    aḥ before a voiced consonant becomes o: the visarga (ru) becomes u, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च), and a + u gives o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • कदाचनेति kadācaneti → कदाचन + इति

    vowel sandhi (guṇa)

    a + i gives e: P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • तस्यैष tasyaiṣa → तस्य + एषः

    vowel sandhi (vṛddhi)

    a or ā + e gives ai: P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि).

  • शारीर आत्मा śārīra ātmā → शारीरः + आत्मा

    visarga sandhi (aḥ before a vowel)

    aḥ before a vowel other than a loses its visarga and the hiatus stays: P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि) makes it y, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य) drops the y.

  • अन्योऽन्तर आत्मा anyo'ntara ātmā → अन्यः + अन्तरः + आत्मा

    visarga sandhi (aḥ before a; aḥ before ā)

    aḥ before a gives o, and the following a is elided (written with avagraha): P. 6.1.113 (अतो रोरप्लुतादप्लुते), then P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति). Then antaraḥ before ā: aḥ before a vowel other than a loses its visarga and the hiatus stays: P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि) makes it y, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य) drops the y.

  • तेनैष tenaiṣa → तेन + एषः

    vowel sandhi (vṛddhi)

    a or ā + e gives ai: P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि). The visarga of eṣaḥ before the consonant of pūrṇaḥ is dropped, saḥ and eṣaḥ lose their visarga before a consonant: P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि).

  • स वा एष sa vā eṣa → सः + वै + एषः

    visarga loss; ai before a vowel

    saḥ and eṣaḥ lose their visarga before a consonant: P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि). ai before a vowel becomes āy, P. 6.1.78 (एचोऽयवायावः), and the y is dropped, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य); the hiatus stays.

  • अन्वयम् anvayam → अनु + अयम्

    vowel sandhi (yaṇ)

    i or u before an unlike vowel becomes y or v: P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि).

  • श्रद्धैव śraddhaiva → श्रद्धा + एव

    vowel sandhi (vṛddhi)

    a or ā + e gives ai: P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि).

  • योग आत्मा yoga ātmā → योगः + आत्मा

    visarga sandhi (aḥ before a vowel)

    aḥ before a vowel other than a loses its visarga and the hiatus stays: P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि) makes it y, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य) drops the y.

  • तदप्येष tadapyeṣa → तत् + अपि + एषः

    consonant and vowel sandhi

    Final t before a vowel or a voiced consonant becomes d: P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते). Then i or u before an unlike vowel becomes y or v: P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि).

  • श्लोको भवति śloko bhavati → श्लोकः + भवति

    visarga sandhi (aḥ before a voiced consonant)

    aḥ before a voiced consonant becomes o: the visarga (ru) becomes u, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च), and a + u gives o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • पुरुषविधः puruṣavidhaḥ

    bahuvrīhi · पुरुष + विधा

    Vigraha पुरुषस्य विधा यस्य सः

    one whose form is that of a person: shaped like a man, with head, two wings, trunk and tail

  • पुरुषविधताम् puruṣavidhatām

    bahuvrīhi with the abstract suffix -tā · पुरुष + विधा + ता

    Vigraha पुरुषविधस्य भावः

    the state of being person-shaped

Morphology

अप्राप्य
Absolutive of āp with the preverb pra; because the verb is compounded, the suffix is -ya, P. 7.1.37 (समासेऽनञ्पूर्वे क्त्वो ल्यप्‌). The negative a- comes before it: ‘without reaching’.
विद्वान्
Nominative of vidvas, the participle of vid ‘know’, P. 7.1.36 (विदेः शतुर्वसुः): ‘one who knows’.
बिभेति
Present of bhī ‘fear’, a reduplicating (third-class) verb: bi-bhe-ti.
मनोमयात्
manas + the suffix -maya, ‘consisting of, made of’. Whether -maya here means ‘made of’ (a product) or ‘abounding in’ becomes the commentators’ question in the next anuvāka.
विज्ञानमयः
vijñāna + -maya. Śaṅkara takes vijñāna as the intellect’s settled knowledge; Kūranārāyaṇa insists the suffix marks something distinct from mere intellect, the knowing self (विज्ञानमयो जीवः, न बुद्धिमात्रं, मयट् प्रत्ययेन व्यतिरेकप्रतीतेः).
पुरुषविधताम्
The abstract suffix -tā forms ‘the state of’, P. 5.1.119 (तस्य भावस्त्वतलौ).
महः
mahas, ‘greatness’: the fourth vyāhṛti of Śīkṣāvallī 5. Here it is the tail of the understanding-self.
एषः
The pronoun eṣaḥ (pausal form). After tena it fuses (tena + eṣaḥ → tenaiṣa) and loses its visarga before the consonant of pūrṇaḥ, P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि).

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

The anuvāka opens with a four-line verse (the ‘verse’ promised at the end of anuvāka 3). Its first two lines are a relative clause (yataḥ … nivartante, ‘from which words turn back’) whose antecedent is ānandam brahmaṇaḥ, ‘the bliss of Brahman’, object of the participle vidvān; the main verb is na bibheti. Then the prose returns to the formula of the previous anuvākas: an identifying sentence (tasyaiṣa eva śārīra ātmā, yaḥ pūrvasya), the introduction of the next, inner self with ablatives of comparison (tasmād vā etasmān manomayāt anyo ’ntara ātmā vijñānamayaḥ), and five nominal sentences that assign the parts of a bird-shaped figure: head, two wings, trunk, tail.

Ambiguities

Whose verse is ‘yato vāco nivartante’?
The verse stands at the head of this anuvāka but illustrates the self made of mind, the subject of anuvāka 3, which ends ‘there is this verse on it’. Śaṅkara says so when the verse returns in 2.9: मनोमये च उदाहृतः मन्त्रः, मनसो ब्रह्मविज्ञानसाधनत्वात्. Rāmānanda (Ānandabhāṣya) raises the same puzzle — a verse meant to show the mind-self speaks of Brahman’s bliss — and answers that the mind is praised as the means by which that bliss is known.
vijñāna: intellect or knower?
For Śaṅkara the self made of understanding is formed of the intellect’s settled knowledge, वेदार्थविषया बुद्धिर्निश्चयात्मिका विज्ञानम्. Kūranārāyaṇa and Gopālānanda take it as the individual self, the knower, विज्ञानमयः विज्ञानप्रचुरो विज्ञाता जीवः (Gopālānanda). The grammar allows both; the question decides how the next anuvāka’s ānandamaya is read.
What is mahas?
Śaṅkara: the great principle, the first-born (मह इति महत्तत्त्वं प्रथमजम्), citing the Bṛhadāraṇyaka; it is the tail because a cause supports its effects. Kūranārāyaṇa offers two readings: Brahman, self-luminous and the support of all qualities, or योगविरोधिनिरसनसामर्थ्यं वा महः, the power that removes what obstructs yoga.

Metre

mixed · syllables counted as written: 8 + 8 + 8 + 9 + 10 + 4 + 10 + 11 + 5 + 11 + 8 + 8 + 7 + 7 + 7 + 4 + 7 + 9

Lines 1–4 are an anuṣṭubh verse of 8 + 8 + 8 + 9 syllables (the last pāda is hypermetric by the closing iti, kadācana iti); the rest is prose.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Technology and digital distractions

The figure gives the understanding-self a body of yoga — collectedness, attention held together — and a head of faith, meaning the trust that comes before acting. Much of what we call thinking today is scattered: a dozen half-read things, no settled view. The passage suggests that understanding is not more information but a gathered state, and that it needs a firm footing to stand on. This is a modern reflection on the passage, not part of its traditional meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Is there a kind of joy you have felt that you could not put into words?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry The verse says words turn back without reaching, yet someone ‘knows’ the bliss of Brahman. What kind of knowing could that be, if not a description?

A contemplation exercise Before your next decision, notice what you are trusting without checking. Name it once to yourself, without judging it.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
