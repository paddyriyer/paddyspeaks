Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 17 of 29

The triple Nāciketa: crossing birth and death

One who has piled the Nāciketa fire three times, joined with the three, doing the three works, crosses birth and death; knowing the god, he reaches lasting peace.

5 Faithful English translation

‘One who has thrice [piled] the Nāciketa [fire], having come to union with the three, a doer of the three works, crosses birth and death. Having known the knower born of Brahman, the god to be praised, having perceived [him], he goes to this peace completely.’

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘Whoever has built the Nāciketa fire three times, joined with the three and doing the three works, crosses beyond birth and death. Knowing the all-knowing god born of Brahman, the one to be praised, and seeing him, he reaches this peace for ever.’

Key concepts अग्नि Agni, fire कर्मन् action, ritual work ज्ञान knowledge शान्ति peace हिरण्यगर्भ Hiraṇyagarbha, ‘golden embryo’

7 Explain it to a beginner

Death now praises the fire he has taught. A person who builds this fire three times, who lives in harmony with the three — whom the commentators identify differently as parents and teacher, the Vedas, or the sacred fires — and who performs the three duties of sacrifice, giving and either study or austerity, goes beyond birth and death. Then, knowing the shining one born of Brahman and seeing him, he reaches a lasting peace. The verse links ritual with knowledge: the fire alone is not the end, it is joined to understanding. The commentators argue at length about who the three are and who the god is.

An analogy Like a long climb in three stages, where the last stage is not a further step but a view that opens once you arrive.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse promise?

One who piles the fire three times, joined with the three and doing the three works, crosses birth and death; knowing and seeing the god born of Brahman, he reaches lasting peace.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Śaṅkara read it?

Still as praise of action: पुनरपि कर्मस्तुतिमेवाह. The fruit is reaching the state of Virāj by combining knowledge and ritual: वैराजं पदं ज्ञानकर्मसमुच्चयानुष्ठानेन प्राप्नोतीत्यर्थः. The ‘peace’ is the cessation known within: इमां स्वबुद्धिप्रत्यक्षां शान्तिम् उपरतिम्.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Raṅgarāmānuja read it?

The triṇāciketa is one who studies the three anuvākas of the Nāciketa (Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa 3.11.7): इत्याद्यनुवाकत्रयाध्यायी. The union is meditation on the Supreme; perceiving means seeing oneself as having Brahman for one’s self: ब्रह्मात्मकं स्वात्मानं साक्षात्कृत्य. He cites the Śrībhāṣya’s gloss on brahmajajña: जीवात्मानम् उपासकं ब्रह्मात्मकत्वेनावगम्य इत्यर्थः.

Traditional Interpretation

And Madhva?

Madhva’s own lines: ‘with the three’ means not contrary to the Vedas, वेदैरविरुद्धः, knowing the Lord as the Veda teaches, वेदोक्तप्रकारेण भगवत्तत्वादिकं जानन्नित्यर्थः; the three works are sacrifice, giving and austerity, त्रिकर्मकृत् यज्ञदानतपःकर्ता, as the Gītā says: यज्ञदानतपःकर्म न त्याज्यं कार्यमेव तत्. In his comment filed at 1.1.18 he cites the Nāmanirukta for brahmajajña: the Veda is ‘brahman’, Hari is manifest from it, and he is the knower: ब्रह्मजस्तेन कथितस्स एव ज्ञोऽखिलज्ञतः.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What is the textual point?

The base file read निचाय्य मां, ‘having perceived me’; the other three witnesses and Śaṅkara’s gloss (निचाय्य दृष्ट्वा चात्मभावेन इमां स्वबुद्धिप्रत्यक्षां शान्तिम्) have निचाय्येमां, ‘having perceived, this [peace]’, and the edition corrects to it (corrections.json).

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.17

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Praise of ritual combined with knowledge, whose fruit is the state of Virāj, not release: वैराजं पदं ज्ञानकर्मसमुच्चयानुष्ठानेन प्राप्नोतीत्यर्थः. brahmajajña is Hiraṇyagarbha’s offspring, the all-knowing.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, citing the Śrībhāṣya, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.1.17

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The verse teaches release through ritual joined to meditation on the Supreme; brahmajajña is the individual self, to be known as having Brahman for its self: जीवात्मानम् उपासकं ब्रह्मात्मकत्वेनावगम्य इत्यर्थः.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.17 and on 1.1.12–18

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Ritual done in accord with the Vedas aids knowledge of the Lord; brahmajajña is Hari, ब्रह्मजस्तेन कथितस्स एव ज्ञोऽखिलज्ञतः, and the fruit is not reached by knowing the fire alone: न चाग्निपरिज्ञानमात्रेणानन्तलोकाप्तिर्भगवज्ज्ञानं विना.

Where they disagree

The verse is a test case for the relation of ritual and knowledge. For Śaṅkara its fruit (crossing birth and death) is figurative — a long-lasting state, Virāj — because ritual cannot release. For Raṅgarāmānuja and Madhva it belongs to release, reached by ritual joined to knowledge of the Lord. They also identify brahmajajña three different ways: Hiraṇyagarbha’s offspring, the individual self, Hari.

  • ब्रह्मजज्ञम् brahmajajñam

    karmadhāraya · ब्रह्मज + ज्ञ

    the knower born of Brahman (Śaṅkara: of Hiraṇyagarbha)

Morphology

त्रिणाचिकेतः
The ṇ of -ṇāciketa is the cerebralisation of n after r in tri (as in a compound, P. 8.4.3 (पूर्वपदात्‌ संज्ञायामगः) and following); Madhva’s digital text writes the dental n (a witness error, corrections.json).
निचाय्य
Absolutive of ni-cāy ‘observe, perceive’; Śaṅkara: ‘having seen as oneself’, Madhva’s commentators: ‘having considered’.
ईड्यम्
Gerundive of īḍ ‘praise’: ‘to be praised’.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

Two sentences with the same subject. In the first, triṇāciketaḥ and trikarmakṛt describe the subject and tribhir etya sandhim the condition; the verb is tarati janmamṛtyū. In the second, two absolutives (viditvā, nicāyya) lead to the verb eti, with śāntim atyantam as its goal.

Ambiguities

Who are ‘the three’?
Śaṅkara: mother, father and teacher, whose teaching one has rightly received: त्रिभिः मातृपित्राचार्यैः एत्य प्राप्य सन्धिं सन्धानं सम्बन्धम्; or the Veda, smṛti and the learned; or perception, inference and scripture: वेदस्मृतिशिष्टैर्वा प्रत्यक्षानुमानागमैर्वा. Madhva: the Vedas — त्रिभिरेत्य सन्धिं वेदैरविरुद्धः, not contrary to the Vedas. Raṅgarāmānuja: the three fires, through meditation on the Supreme.
What are the three works?
Śaṅkara: sacrifice, study and giving, त्रिकर्मकृत् इज्याध्ययनदानानां कर्ता. Madhva: sacrifice, giving and austerity, citing the Gītā: त्रिकर्मकृत् यज्ञदानतपःकर्ता. Raṅgarāmānuja gives both Śaṅkara’s list and a second: the cooked offerings, oblations and soma rites.
Who is brahmajajña?
Śaṅkara: born of Brahman (Hiraṇyagarbha) and all-knowing: ब्रह्मणो हिरण्यगर्भाज्जातो ब्रह्मजः ब्रह्मजश्चासौ ज्ञश्चेति ब्रह्मजज्ञः. Raṅgarāmānuja, following the Śrībhāṣya: the individual self, born of Brahman and a knower, ब्रह्मणो जातत्वात् ज्ञत्वाच्च. Madhva, citing the Nāmanirukta: Hari, manifest from the Veda and all-knowing (see the commentary).

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 22

Two lines of 22 syllables, regular (with the corrected निचाय्येमां).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Practice and understanding

The verse joins repeated practice (the fire built three times, the three duties) with understanding (knowing and seeing). Many disciplines say the same: practice prepares, but the change comes when one understands what the practice was for. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What do you do regularly that you have never stopped to understand?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Can doing the right things, by themselves, bring peace? What does the verse add to them?

A contemplation exercise Choose one routine act today and, before doing it, say to yourself what it is for.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
