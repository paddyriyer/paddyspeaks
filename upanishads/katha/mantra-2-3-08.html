Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 8 of 18

The all-pervading person without a mark

Beyond the unmanifest is the person, all-pervading and without a mark; knowing him a creature is freed and goes to immortality.

5 Faithful English translation

But beyond the unmanifest [is] the person, all-pervading and indeed without a mark, knowing whom a creature is freed and goes to immortality.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Beyond the unmanifest is the Person, who pervades all and has no distinguishing mark. Knowing him, a living being is set free and reaches immortality.

Key concepts अलिङ्ग aliṅga, without a mark पुरुष person अव्यक्त avyakta, the unmanifest मोक्ष liberation अमृत immortality, the deathless

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse completes the ladder of the last one. Beyond even the hidden, unmanifest source stands the Person. He pervades everything and has no mark or feature by which he could be picked out, as we pick out things by their shape or colour. The verse then says what knowing him brings: a living being is set free and goes to immortality. Śaṅkara notices that two things are said. Knowing him, one is freed here and now from the knots of ignorance in the heart; and when the body falls, one goes to immortality. He adds that this knowledge comes from a teacher and from scripture. Raṅgarāmānuja explains ‘without a mark’ as ‘not to be known by any mark’, and reads the two verses together as teaching surrender to the Lord.

An analogy Space holds every shape yet has no shape of its own to point to.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

Beyond the unmanifest is the person, all-pervading and without a mark; knowing him a creature is freed and goes to immortality.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: freed while living

He is all-pervading because he is the cause of all, even of pervasive space, व्यापकस्याप्याकाशादेः सर्वस्य कारणत्वात्, and without a mark because he has none of the intellect and the rest by which things are known: सर्वसंसारधर्मवर्जित इत्येतत्. Knowing him from teacher and scripture a creature is freed while still living from the knots of the heart, ignorance and the rest, and when the body has fallen goes to immortality: यं ज्ञात्वा आचार्यतः शास्त्रतश्च मुच्यते जन्तुः अविद्यादिहृदयग्रन्थिभिर्जीवन्नेव.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: control of the highest by surrender

Commenting on 2.3.7–2.3.8 together, he reads the two mantras as restating surrender: being ‘higher’ means being harder to bring under control, परत्वं च वशीकार्यतायां विवक्षितम्, and the highest is brought under control only by refuge, परस्य च वशीकरणं शरणागतिरेव. The rest, he says, is plain.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: the gradation and Hari

His comment on the series, given again in 2.3.7–2.3.8, cites a verse (‘iti ca’, no source named) that the great purport of all statements is Hari’s supremacy, known through the gradation of beings: तारतम्यपरिज्ञानपूर्वकं सर्वतो हरेः । आधिक्ये सर्ववाक्यानां तात्पर्यं महदिष्यते.

Comparative Vedānta

The puruṣa of 1.3.11

The first adhyāya ended its series at the same place: महतः परमव्यक्तमव्यक्तात्पुरुषः परः, adding that beyond the puruṣa there is nothing, पुरुषान्न परं किञ्चित्सा काष्ठा सा परा गतिः.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.3.8

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The puruṣa is the cause of all and without any mark of saṃsāra; knowing him one is freed while living and goes to immortality when the body falls: पतितेऽपि शरीरे अमृतत्वं च गच्छति.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.3.7–8

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The highest, the puruṣa, is brought under control only by surrender: परस्य च वशीकरणं शरणागतिरेव.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.3.7–8

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The ascending series teaches the gradation of beings, and through it Hari’s supremacy over all, which is the great purport of the Veda: तारतम्यपरिज्ञानपूर्वकं सर्वतो हरेः.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara reads the verse as teaching release while living and immortality after the body falls; Raṅgarāmānuja reads it, with the last verse, as the teaching of surrender to the highest; Madhva as the gradation that shows Hari above all.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • अव्यक्तात्तु avyaktāttu → अव्यक्तात् + तु

    t before t

    A final t stays before t.

  • पुरुषो व्यापकोऽलिङ्ग एव puruṣo vyāpako'liṅga eva → पुरुषः + व्यापकः + अलिङ्गः + एव

    aḥ before a voiced consonant; aḥ before a; aḥ before a vowel other than a

    -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च); before a it becomes o and the a merges into it, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति); before e the visarga becomes y and is dropped, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि) with P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य), the hiatus left unjoined.

  • यं ज्ञात्वा yaṃ jñātvā → यम् + ज्ञात्वा

    m before a consonant

    Final m → anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • जन्तुरमृतत्वं janturamṛtatvaṃ → जन्तुः + अमृतत्वम्

    uḥ before a vowel; m before a consonant

    After u the visarga becomes r before a voiced sound, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः); final m before a consonant becomes anusvāra, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • अलिङ्गः aliṅgaḥ

    nañ-bahuvrīhi · अ + लिङ्ग

    Vigraha न विद्यते लिङ्गं यस्य सः

    one who has no mark

Morphology

अलिङ्गः
liṅga, ‘mark, sign by which a thing is known’, with the negative a-. Śaṅkara: the mark is the intellect and the rest, by which something is known, लिङ्ग्यते गम्यते येन तल्लिङ्गं बुद्ध्यादि.
अमृतत्वम्
The abstract noun in -tva from amṛta: ‘the state of being immortal’.
मुच्यते
√muc, present passive: ‘is released’.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

The first line completes the series of 2.3.7: avyaktāt (ablative) tu paraḥ puruṣaḥ, with two qualifiers. The second line is a relative clause, yaṃ jñātvā, with two main verbs joined by ca: mucyate jantuḥ and amṛtatvaṃ gacchati.

Ambiguities

Freed when?
Śaṅkara distinguishes the two verbs: knowing him one is freed from the knots of the heart while still living, and when the body has fallen one goes to immortality: मुच्यते जन्तुः अविद्यादिहृदयग्रन्थिभिर्जीवन्नेव ; पतितेऽपि शरीरे अमृतत्वं च गच्छति. Raṅgarāmānuja treats this verse with the last as one teaching of surrender and explains only aliṅga.
yam or tam
Madhva’s text reads taṃ jñātvā, ‘knowing him’, for yaṃ jñātvā, ‘knowing whom’ (corrections.json); the sense is the same.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 17 + 16

Counted as written, 17 and 16 syllables. The first pāda, avyaktāt tu paraḥ puruṣo, has nine.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: व्यापकोऽलिङ्ग एव च — Madhva’s text reads taṃ jñātvā, ‘knowing him’, for yaṃ jñātvā, ‘knowing whom’.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Science and wonder

Some of the deepest things we study, space, time, law, have no shape of their own to point to. The verse names such a reality ‘without a mark’. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin How would you recognise something that has no features at all?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Śaṅkara separates being freed from going to immortality. What is the difference?

A contemplation exercise Spend a minute attending not to any object in the room but to the space that holds them.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
