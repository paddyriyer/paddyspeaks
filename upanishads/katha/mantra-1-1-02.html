Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 2 of 29

Faith enters the boy

Watching the gifts being led away, the young Naciketas is seized by śraddhā and begins to think.

5 Faithful English translation

Faith entered him, being [yet] a boy, while the gifts were being led away; he thought:

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Though he was only a boy, as the cows were led away to the priests, faith took hold of him, and he thought:

Key concepts श्रद्धा faith, trust कर्मन् action, ritual work दक्षिणा dakṣiṇā, sacrificial fee

7 Explain it to a beginner

Naciketas is a boy, too young to have a part in the ritual. But as he watches the cows being led away as gifts to the priests, something happens to him: faith takes hold of him. In the Upaniṣads this word means more than belief. The commentators explain it as a settled conviction that what the scripture teaches is true, a conviction that makes one care how things are done. The boy sees the gifts, and he begins to think. The next verse tells us what he thought. The quiet point of this verse is that seriousness about truth can arise in anyone, even a child, and that it begins with simply looking at what is in front of you.

An analogy Like a child at a family celebration who notices, before any adult does, that something being done is not quite right.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the mantra say?

As the gifts were being led to the priests, śraddhā entered Naciketas, though he was only a boy, and he reflected.

Traditional Interpretation

What is the śraddhā that entered him?

Śaṅkara: श्रद्धा आस्तिक्यबुद्धिः पितुर्हितकामप्रयुक्ता आविवेश प्रविष्टवती — faith, the conviction that [the scripture] is true, prompted by the wish for his father’s good, entered him. Raṅgarāmānuja repeats the definition almost word for word: आस्तिक्यबुद्धि पितुः हितकामप्रयुक्ता.

Traditional Interpretation

Why stress that he was a boy?

Śaṅkara glosses kumāram santam as a child not yet able to beget — अप्राप्तप्रजननशक्तिं बालमेव — that is, one who had no claim of his own in the rite. His concern is entirely for the father.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Why are the gifts plural?

Raṅgarāmānuja, as a ritualist, asks why dakṣiṇāsu is plural when a rite has one fee, and answers that the fee is reckoned per priest, so there are many: ऋत्विग्बहुत्वापेक्षया दक्षिणाबहुत्वसम्भवात् दक्षिणासु इति बहुवचनम्.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

नीयमानासु
Present passive participle of nī, ‘lead’, locative plural agreeing with dakṣiṇāsu: a locative absolute, ‘while the gifts were being led [to the priests]’.
दक्षिणासु
Plural. Raṅgarāmānuja discusses at length why dakṣiṇā, the sacrificial fee, is plural here: one fee per rite, but several priests receive their shares.
अमन्यत
Imperfect middle of man, ‘think’. What he thought is given in the next mantra.

Anvaya

दक्षिणासु नीयमानासु कुमारम् सन्तम् तम् ह श्रद्धा आविवेश । सः अमन्यत ॥

dakṣiṇāsu nīyamānāsu kumāram santam tam ha śraddhā āviveśa | saḥ amanyata ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

One sentence with a locative absolute (‘as the gifts were being led away’), a main clause (‘faith entered him, though a boy’) and a short second clause (‘he thought’), which leads into the next mantra, the content of his thought.

Ambiguities

What kind of faith?
Śaṅkara and Raṅgarāmānuja both define śraddhā as āstikya-buddhi, conviction that the scripture’s teaching is true, and both say it was prompted by concern for his father’s good. The text itself only says that śraddhā ‘entered’ him.
The end of the verse in Madhva’s text
Madhva’s text ends the verse at āviveśa and lacks ‘so ’manyata’, ‘he thought’ (corrections.json); it counts mantras 1–2 as one verse.

Metre

11 Connections with other scriptures

  • Bhagavad Gītā 17.3 Modern thematic association

    श्रद्धामयोऽयं पुरुषो यो यच्छ्रद्धः स एव सः

    śraddhāmayo'yaṃ puruṣo yo yacchraddhaḥ sa eva saḥ

    The Gītā’s claim that a person is made of śraddhā, and is what his śraddhā is, gives the word the weight it carries here: the boy’s faith decides what he will become.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Ethical leadership

The person who notices that something is wrong is not always the one in charge; here it is the youngest present. Conscience does not wait for seniority. This is a modern reflection, not the text’s claim.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Have you ever noticed something wrong that the adults or seniors around you did not?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Is śraddhā, as the commentators define it, something one decides to have, or something that ‘enters’?

A contemplation exercise Today, look carefully at one routine you take part in and ask whether it is done as it should be.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
