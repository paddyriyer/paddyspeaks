Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 18 of 29

Knowing the three, building the fire

One who knows the three and so builds the Nāciketa fire casts off the nooses of death beforehand and, beyond grief, rejoices in heaven.

5 Faithful English translation

‘One who has thrice [piled] the Nāciketa, having known this triad, and who, knowing thus, builds the Nāciketa — he, having thrust away the nooses of death beforehand, gone beyond grief, rejoices in the heaven-world.’

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘Whoever has built the Nāciketa fire three times, knowing these three things, and builds it in that knowledge, throws off the nooses of death while still alive and, past all sorrow, rejoices in heaven.’

Key concepts अग्नि Agni, fire मृत्यु death शोक grief, sorrow लोक world, realm

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse closes the teaching of the fire. Whoever builds the Nāciketa fire, knowing the three things about it, and builds it with that understanding, throws off the nooses of death even before the body dies, and rejoices in heaven beyond all sorrow. The nooses of death are what bind us — wrong deeds, ignorance, craving and hatred, in the commentators’ lists. The point is that knowing what one is doing changes the doing. A ritual performed with understanding frees in a way that a mechanical one does not. The same act, done knowingly, becomes a different act.

An analogy Like two people planting the same tree: one only follows the instructions, the other understands soil, sun and season, and so tends it differently.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

One who knows the triad and builds the fire with that knowledge casts off death’s nooses beforehand and rejoices in heaven, beyond grief. It closes the teaching of the fire.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Śaṅkara conclude?

He sums up the fruit of knowing and building the fire: इदानीमग्निविज्ञानचयनफलमुपसंहरति. The nooses are cast off before the body falls: पूर्वमेव शरीरपातादित्यर्थः; the heaven is Virāj’s, reached by realising oneself as Virāj: मोदते स्वर्गलोके वैराजे विराडात्मस्वरूपप्रतिपत्त्या.

Traditional Interpretation

And Raṅgarāmānuja?

The triad is Brahman, oneself as having Brahman for one’s self, and the fire. Building the fire with these in mind, one becomes free of attraction and aversion while still living: जीवद्दशायामेव रागादिरहितस्सन्नित्यर्थः.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What follows in Raṅgarāmānuja’s text?

His text has a further verse here, absent from the witnesses of this edition, which he defends: अयं च मन्त्रः केषुचित्कोशेषु न दृष्टः; कैश्चित् अव्याकृतश्च — ‘this mantra is not seen in some manuscripts and not commented on by some’ — but since Vyāsārya and others explain it, ‘no suspicion of interpolation should be entertained’.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.18

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The close of the ritual section: knowing the fire as oneself (आत्मस्वरूपेण अग्निं विद्वान्) one reaches Virāj’s heaven. The third boon will ask for what this does not give.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.1.18

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The triad is Brahman, the self and the fire, ब्रह्मस्वरूपं, तदात्मकस्वात्मस्वरूपं and अग्निस्वरूपं च; the heaven reached is release.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.18

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The triad is what was taught of the bricks, त्रयमेतत् या इष्टका इत्यादि; and what the fire-section praises — first of the worlds, support, the knower born of brahman, the endless world — is the Lord himself: लोकादिः प्रतिष्ठा ब्रह्मजज्ञो अनन्तलोकाप्तिरित्यादिविशेषणैश्च भगवानेव.

Where they disagree

As in 1.1.17: for Śaṅkara the fruit is a high but bound state (Virāj’s heaven); for Raṅgarāmānuja and Madhva it belongs to the way to release. Śaṅkara and Madhva take the ‘triad’ as the bricks, their number and method; Raṅgarāmānuja as Brahman, the self and the fire.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • त्रिणाचिकेतस्त्रयमेतद्विदित्वा triṇāciketastrayametadviditvā → त्रिणाचिकेतः + त्रयम् + एतत् + विदित्वा

    visarga before t; t before v

    Visarga before t becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः); t before a voiced consonant becomes d, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

  • य एवम् ya evam → यः + एवम्

    aḥ before a vowel

    The visarga of -aḥ before a vowel other than a is lost and the hiatus stays: P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य).

  • विद्वांश्चिनुते vidvāṃścinute → विद्वान् + चिनुते

    n before c

    Final n before c is replaced by ru (anusvāra + ś): P. 8.3.7 (नश्छव्यप्रशान्), then P. 8.4.40 (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः).

  • मृत्युपाशान्पुरतः mṛtyupāśānpurataḥ → मृत्युपाशान् + पुरतः

    no change

    Final n before p stays.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • मृत्युपाशान् mṛtyupāśān

    ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa · मृत्यु + पाश

    Vigraha मृत्योः पाशाः

    the nooses of death

Morphology

नाचिकेतम्
vṛddhi derivative of naciketas: ‘belonging to Naciketas’, the fire named for him (1.1.16).
चिनुते
Present middle of ci ‘pile, build’ (fifth class): the fire-altar is built of bricks.
विद्वान्
Perfect participle of vid ‘know’ (vidvas), nominative singular.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

A relative sentence (ya evaṃ vidvāṃś cinute nāciketam) with a preceding description of the subject (triṇāciketas trayam etad viditvā), and the main clause sa … modate svargaloke with the absolutive praṇodya.

Ambiguities

What is the ‘triad’?
Śaṅkara and Madhva: the three things taught in 1.1.15 — which bricks, how many, how. Raṅgarāmānuja: Brahman, the self that has Brahman for its self, and the fire: ब्रह्मस्वरूपं, तदात्मकस्वात्मस्वरूपं, ‘त्रिभिरेत्य सन्धिम्’ इति निर्दिष्टमग्निस्वरूपं च.
What are the nooses of death?
Śaṅkara: wrong-doing, ignorance, attraction and aversion, अधर्माज्ञानरागद्वेषादिलक्षणान्; Raṅgarāmānuja: attraction, aversion and the like.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 24 + 22

Counted as written, 24 and 22 syllables: the first line (triṇāciketas trayam etad viditvā ya evaṃ vidvāṃś cinute nāciketam) has two syllables more than the norm; ya evaṃ may have been read with the hiatus contracted.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Witness error Madhva’s mūla: निचाय्येमां शान्तिमत्यन्तमेति — Madhva’s digital text writes tri-nāciketa with the dental n; the base, GRETIL and the vishvAsa mūla have the retroflex ṇ (triṇāciketa), as the n after ri requires.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Mindful action

The verse distinguishes doing a thing from doing it knowingly. People who understand why they work often find that the same work weighs less on them. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What would change if you understood why you do your daily tasks?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Which ‘nooses’ — habits, fears, cravings — tighten without our noticing?

A contemplation exercise Name one habit that binds you; for a day, notice each time it pulls.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
