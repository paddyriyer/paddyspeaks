Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 9 of 18

Not in the field of sight

His form does not stand within sight; no one sees him with the eye. He is grasped by the heart, by thought, by the mind; those who know this become immortal.

5 Faithful English translation

His form does not stand within sight; no one sees him with the eye. By the heart, by the ruler of the mind, by the mind [he is] grasped; those who know this become immortal.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

His form is not there to be seen; no one sees him with the eye. He is grasped by the heart, by insight, by the mind. Those who know this become immortal.

Key concepts आत्मन् self, Self मनस् mind इन्द्रिय indriya, a sense अमृत immortality, the deathless

7 Explain it to a beginner

Can we see the highest reality? This verse says not with the eyes. Its form is not something that stands in front of us to be looked at, and no one sees it with the eye, or with any of the senses. How, then, is it known? By the heart, by insight and by the mind. Śaṅkara explains the three as the intellect in the heart, a clear judgment that steadies the mind, and deep reflection. Raṅgarāmānuja quotes an older teacher who read ‘heart’ as devotion and the second word as steadfastness, and connects this with the Gītā’s teaching that the Lord is seen by single-minded devotion. Madhva says the Lord is seen not by the senses but by the eye of knowledge. The verse ends with a promise heard before: those who know this become immortal.

An analogy No microscope will show you a friend’s kindness; you know it another way.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

His form does not stand within sight; no one sees him with the eye. He is grasped by the heart, by thought, by the mind; those who know this become immortal.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: known by the purified intellect

The form of this inner self does not stand in the field of sight, न सन्दृशे सन्दर्शनविषये न तिष्ठति प्रत्यगात्मनः अस्य रूपम्; ‘eye’ stands for every sense. It is known by the intellect in the heart, by manīṣā, ‘the ruler of the mind’, an intellect free of doubt, and by the mind as right seeing: मनसा मननरूपेण सम्यग्दर्शनेन अभिक्लृप्तः अभिसमर्थितः अभिप्रकाशित इत्येतत्. The words ‘the self can be known’ are to be supplied, आत्मा ज्ञातुं शक्य इति वाक्यशेषः.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: seen by devotion and steadfastness

His form, his own nature or his body, does not stand before the eye because he pervades all, स्वरूपं विग्रहो वा, व्यापकत्वाद् एव, अभिमुखतया न तिष्ठति इत्यर्थः. He reports Vyāsārya’s gloss, हृदा इति भक्तिर् उच्यते, मनीषा इति धृतिः, and the Mahābhārata verse he cites, and draws the sense: with the self composed by steadfastness, one sees the Supreme Person by devotion, धृत्या समाहितात्मा, भक्तया पुरुषोत्तमं पश्यति, साक्षात्करोति प्राप्नोति इत्यर्थः, as the Gītā says and as the Vedārthasaṅgraha explains, both as cited by him.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: not by the senses

In verses he cites (‘iti ca’, no source named), Viṣṇu is not seen by the senses except in his manifestations, प्रादुर्भावानृते विष्णुमिन्द्रियैर्नैव पश्यति, and even those are truly seen by the eye of knowledge; then the yogin is freed, never by the faulty senses: तदैव मुच्यते योगी न दुष्टैरिन्द्रियैः क्वचित्.

Comparative Vedānta

Not grasped by the eye

The Muṇḍaka says the same, न चक्षुषा गृह्यते नापि वाचा. Raṅgarāmānuja’s reading leans on the Gītā, भक्त्या त्वनन्यया शक्य अहमेवंविधोऽर्जुन. The close recalls the Bṛhadāraṇyaka, ये तद्विदुरमृतास्ते भवन्त्यथेतरे दुःखमेवापियन्ति.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.3.9

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The self is not an object of any sense; it is known by the purified intellect as right seeing, and those who know it as Brahman become immortal: तमात्मानं ब्रह्म एतत् ये विदुः अमृताः ते भवन्ति.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.3.9

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The Supreme Person is seen, directly attained, by devotion with a mind made steady: धृत्या समाहितात्मा, भक्तया पुरुषोत्तमं पश्यति, साक्षात्करोति प्राप्नोति इत्यर्थः.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.3.9

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Viṣṇu is seen not by the faulty senses but by the eye of knowledge, and then the yogin is freed: तदैव मुच्यते योगी न दुष्टैरिन्द्रियैः क्वचित्.

Where they disagree

All three agree that the senses cannot see it. For Śaṅkara the means is the purified intellect, right seeing of the self; for Raṅgarāmānuja, following Vyāsārya, it is devotion and steadfastness, by which the Supreme Person is seen; for Madhva, the eye of knowledge by which Viṣṇu is seen.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • रूपमस्य rūpamasya → रूपम् + अस्य

    m before a vowel

    Final m stays before a vowel and is joined to it.

  • कश्चनैनम् kaścanainam → कश्चन + एनम्

    a + e

    a + e → ai, P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि).

  • मनसाभिक्लृप्तो य एतद्विदुरमृतास्ते manasābhiklṛpto ya etadviduramṛtāste → मनसा + अभिक्लृप्तः + ये + एतत् + विदुः + अमृताः + ते

    ā + a; aḥ before y; e before a vowel; t before a voiced consonant; uḥ before a vowel; āḥ before t

    ā + a give ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः); -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced sound, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च); ye before e becomes ay with the y dropped, P. 6.1.78 (एचोऽयवायावः), P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य); t becomes d, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते); after u the visarga becomes r, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः); before t it becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

सन्दृशे
saṃ + √dṛś, a root noun in the dative, used as a Vedic infinitive: ‘for seeing’; Śaṅkara renders it ‘in the field of sight’.
मनीषा
Śaṅkara derives it from manīṭ, ‘ruler of the mind’ (manas + √īś), instrumental manīṣā. It may also be the feminine noun manīṣā, ‘thought’, used with instrumental sense; Vyāsārya, quoted by Raṅgarāmānuja, glosses it as steadfastness.
अभिक्लृप्तः
abhi + √kḷp, past participle: ‘fitted, made ready, grasped’. GRETIL writes the vowel ḷ with its own sign (अभिकॢप्तो).

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

The first line has two negated clauses: na saṃdṛśe tiṣṭhati rūpam asya, and na cakṣuṣā paśyati kaścanainam. The second line has three instrumentals with the participle abhiklṛptaḥ, the self being the understood subject, and then the relative clause ye etad viduḥ answered by amṛtās te bhavanti.

Ambiguities

What are heart, manīṣā and mind?
Śaṅkara: the heart is the intellect in the heart, manīṣā the intellect free of doubt that rules the mind, manas the right seeing that is reflection; by these the self can be known, आत्मा ज्ञातुं शक्य इति वाक्यशेषः. Raṅgarāmānuja quotes Vyāsārya: the heart is devotion, manīṣā steadfastness, हृदा इति भक्तिर् उच्यते, मनीषा इति धृतिः, and reads the line with the Gītā: by devotion and steadfastness one sees the Supreme Person.
rūpam: what form?
Raṅgarāmānuja allows two senses, his own nature or his divine form, स्वरूपं विग्रहो वा, व्यापकत्वाद् एव, neither standing before the eye because he pervades all. Śaṅkara: the form of the inner self.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 23

Counted as written, 22 and 23 syllables. The last pāda, ya etad vidur amṛtās te bhavanti, has twelve; the same words close 2.3.2.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: पश्यति कश्चिदेनम् — Madhva’s text reads kaścid enam for kaścanainam, ‘anyone [sees] him’; the same sense.
  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: मनीषा मनसाऽभिक्लृप्तो य एनं विदुरमृतास्ते — Madhva’s text reads ya enaṃ vidur, ‘who know him’, for ya etad vidur, ‘who know this’.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Science and wonder

Much of what science knows, it knows without seeing it directly, by inference and model. The verse names another kind of knowing that is not by the eye. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What do you know well that you have never seen?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Heart, steady judgment, mind: are these three ways of knowing, or one?

A contemplation exercise Close your eyes for a minute and notice what you still know without them.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
