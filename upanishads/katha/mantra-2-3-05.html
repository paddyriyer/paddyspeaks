Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 5 of 18

As in a mirror, so in the self

As in a mirror, so in the self; as in a dream, so in the world of the fathers; as seen in water, so in the world of the gandharvas; as shade and sunlight, so in Brahmā’s world.

5 Faithful English translation

As in a mirror, so in the self; as in a dream, so in the world of the fathers; as in water [one] is seen as it were all round, so in the world of the gandharvas; as in shade and sunlight, [so] in Brahmā’s world.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

As in a mirror, so it is seen in oneself; as in a dream, so in the world of the ancestors; as a reflection wavers in water, so in the world of the gandharvas; as clearly as shade from sunlight, so in Brahmā’s world.

Key concepts आत्मन् self, Self लोक world, realm गन्धर्व gandharva, celestial musician ब्रह्मन् Brahman

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse compares four kinds of seeing with four worlds. In this life, in oneself, the self can be seen as clearly as a face in a mirror. In the world of the ancestors it is seen only as in a dream. In the world of the heavenly musicians it wavers, like a reflection in water. In the highest world, Brahmā’s, it is as distinct as shade is from sunlight. Śaṅkara draws the lesson that the best chance is here, in a mind made clean like a mirror, since Brahmā’s world is very hard to reach. Madhva reads it in a similar way. Raṅgarāmānuja reads the same images as showing how hard the self is to know anywhere. The commentators agree that the effort belongs to this life.

An analogy A face is clearest in a polished mirror, blurred in a dream, trembling in a pond.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

As in a mirror, so in the self; as in a dream, in the world of the fathers; as seen in water, in the world of the gandharvas; as shade and sunlight, in Brahmā’s world.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: seen clearly here

In one’s own intellect made clear like a mirror, the self is seen distinctly, तथा इह आत्मनि स्वबुद्धावादर्शवन्निर्मलीभूतायां विविक्तमात्मनो दर्शनं भवतीत्यर्थः. In the world of the fathers it is seen indistinctly, as in a dream, because of attachment to the fruits of action, and so in the gandharva world, as in water. In Brahmā’s world alone it is as distinct as shade and sunlight, but that world is hard to reach. So the effort must be made here: तस्मादात्मदर्शनाय इहैव यत्नः कर्तव्य इत्यभिप्रायः.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: the self is hard to know

The verse states how hard the self is to know, आत्मनो दुर्बोधत्वमेवाह. In the gandharva world there is only a fleeting appearance, गन्धर्वलोकेऽपि आपाततः प्रतीतिमात्रम् इत्यर्थः, and in Brahmā’s world, with shade and sunlight mixed, no full perception, so the truth of the self is hard to reach, अतो दुरधिगमम् आत्मतत्त्वमिति भावः. Or, in Brahmā’s world self and non-self may be told apart like shade and sunlight, yet that is not easy for those here, तथापि न अत्रत्यानामात्मतत्त्वं सुलभमिति भावः.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: Hari seen within the jīva

In verses he cites (‘iti ca’, no source named), the Lord standing in the jīva is seen by those with the eye of knowledge, clearly, like a face in a mirror, not so in the world of the fathers: जीवे स्थितस्तु भगवान् दृश्यते ज्ञानदृष्टिभिः । आदर्शे मुखवत् सम्यङ् न तथा पितृलोकगः. In Brahmā’s world the Supreme Person is seen clearly: स्पष्टं तथा ब्रह्मलोके दृश्यते पुरुषोत्तमः.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.3.5

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The self is seen distinctly here, in a purified intellect, and in Brahmā’s world only, which is hard to reach; so the effort belongs to this life: तस्मादात्मदर्शनाय इहैव यत्नः कर्तव्य इत्यभिप्रायः.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.3.5

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The four images show that the self is hard to know in every world: अतो दुरधिगमम् आत्मतत्त्वमिति भावः.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.3.5

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

What is seen is the Lord standing in the jīva, clearly to the eye of knowledge, as a face in a mirror: जीवे स्थितस्तु भगवान् दृश्यते ज्ञानदृष्टिभिः.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara and Madhva take the mirror as an image of clear seeing here, though they differ on what is seen (the self; the Lord within the jīva). Raṅgarāmānuja reads the four images as degrees of unclear seeing, to show that the self is hard to know.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • यथादर्शे yathādarśe → यथा + आदर्शे

    ā + ā

    Like vowels merge into the long vowel, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • तथात्मनि tathātmani → तथा + आत्मनि

    ā + ā

    Like vowels merge into the long vowel, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • यथाप्सु परीव yathāpsu parīva → यथा + अप्सु + परि + इव

    ā + a; i + i

    Like vowels merge into the long vowel, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः): yathā + apsu → yathāpsu, pari + iva → parīva.

  • गन्धर्वलोके च्छायातपयोरिव gandharvaloke cchāyātapayoriva → गन्धर्वलोके + छायातपयोः + इव

    ch after a long vowel; oḥ before a vowel

    After a vowel ch may be doubled; after a long vowel at the end of a word it is optional, P. 6.1.76 (पदान्ताद्वा), and the base writes च्छ. After o the visarga becomes r before a vowel, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः).

Morphology

ददृशे
Perfect middle 3rd singular of √dṛś, used with passive sense: ‘is seen’. Madhva’s text has the present passive dṛśyate.
अप्सु
Locative plural of ap-, ‘water’, used only in the plural.
छायातपयोः
Locative dual of the dvandva chāyātapa, ‘shade and sunlight’: with iva, ‘as in shade and sunlight’.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

Four comparisons built with yathā … tathā (the last with iva), each setting a way of seeing beside a world. The verb ‘is seen’ is expressed once, dadṛśe in the third comparison, and understood in the others.

Ambiguities

Is the mirror an image of clear or unclear seeing?
Śaṅkara: clear, यथा आदर्शे प्रतिबिम्बभूतमात्मानं पश्यति लोकः अत्यन्तविविक्तम्, in an intellect made pure like a mirror; Madhva, in a verse he cites, also clear, आदर्शे मुखवत् सम्यङ्. Raṅgarāmānuja reads the whole verse as showing how hard the self is to know, आत्मनो दुर्बोधत्वमेवाह, and the mirror as a seeing that is not direct.
Shade and sunlight
Śaṅkara: in Brahmā’s world the self is seen as distinct as shade from sunlight, छायातपयोरिव अत्यन्तविविक्तं ब्रह्मलोक एवैकस्मिन्. Raṅgarāmānuja gives two readings: shade and sunlight mixed, so not clear even there; or distinct, yet hard for those here to attain, तथापि न अत्रत्यानामात्मतत्त्वं सुलभमिति भावः.

Metre

irregular · syllables counted as written: 18 + 27

Counted as written, 18 and 27 syllables: four prose-like comparisons, not a regular metre. yathādarśe tathātmani is an anuṣṭubh pāda of eight; the rest runs on.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Science and wonder

Optics distinguishes a sharp mirror image from a refracted one in water. The verse uses the same difference to grade ways of knowing. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Why would a mirror show you more clearly than a pond?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Is the clarity here in the mirror or in the one who looks?

A contemplation exercise Look at your reflection in still water and then in a mirror; notice what each shows and hides.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
