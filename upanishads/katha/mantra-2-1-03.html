Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 3 of 15

That by which one knows

That by which one knows form, taste, smell, sounds, touches and the pleasures of union — by this alone one knows; what is left here? This is that.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘It is by this that one knows colour and taste and smell, sounds and touch and the joys of love. What is there that it does not know? This is what you asked about.’

Key concepts आत्मन् self, Self इन्द्रिय indriya, a sense विज्ञान understanding, the knowing self ज्ञान knowledge

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse asks a quiet question. Think of everything you know through the senses: colours, tastes, smells, sounds, the feel of things, the pleasures of love. Each is known by something — not by the eye or the tongue on its own, but by the awareness that uses them. That awareness is what the verse points to. Śaṅkara gives an argument: if the body, which is itself made of colours, textures and the rest, could know them, then a stone or a flower should be able to know its own colour too, and it cannot. So the knower must be something other than what it knows. Then the verse asks: what is left that this does not know? Nothing. And it ends with a refrain that will return several times: this, truly, is that — the very thing Naciketas asked about.

An analogy A lamp that shows every object in a room; one does not need another lamp to see the lamp.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

That by which one knows every object of the senses — this alone is the knower; nothing is left that it does not know. This is that.

Traditional Interpretation

Why the body is not the knower

Śaṅkara grants that people think ‘I, the body, know’, and answers: if what is itself form and the rest could know form, then outer things should know one another and themselves, तर्हि बाह्या अपि रूपादयोऽन्योन्यं स्वं स्वं रूपं च विजानीयुः; they do not, so it is the self, other than the body, that knows. Nothing is left unknown to it, so it is all-knowing, स आत्मा सर्वज्ञः.

Comparative Vedānta

Raṅgarāmānuja: the senses act by its favour

The senses that show form and the rest begin their work only as favoured by it, रूपादिप्रकाशकानाम् इन्द्रियाणां तदनुगृहीतानामेव कार्यारम्भकत्वात् इति भावः; he cites a line of the Bṛhadāraṇyaka in support (‘the light of lights’). ‘This is that’: this self taught in the verse is the highest place named before, एतन्मन्त्रप्रतिपाद्यात्मस्वरूपमेव इत्यर्थः.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

The refrain

etad vai tat is printed as a short line of its own. It closes this verse and the five that follow, and ties each description back to the question Naciketas asked.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.1.3

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The knower is the self whose nature is consciousness, other than the body, देहादिव्यतिरिक्तेनैव विज्ञानस्वभावेनात्मना; ‘this is that’: this self is the highest place of Viṣṇu that Naciketas asked about.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.1.3

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The senses work only as favoured by the self (with the Supreme as the light of lights); ‘this is that’ identifies the self taught here with the goal named before, पूर्वं प्राप्यतया निर्दिष्टं तत्परमं पदं.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Both read ‘that’ as the goal Naciketas asked about; for Śaṅkara the knower of all objects is itself that goal, while Raṅgarāmānuja grounds the senses’ work in a light beyond them. In our source Madhva’s bhāṣya has no comment on this verse (see flags).

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • रूपं रसं गन्धं rūpaṃ rasaṃ gandhaṃ → रूपम् + रसम् + गन्धम्

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

मैथुनान्
A derivative of mithuna ‘pair’: what belongs to union. The masculine plural agrees in form with śabdān and sparśān; the commentators gloss it as the pleasures that arise from union.
परिशिष्यते
Present passive of śiṣ ‘leave’ with pari: ‘is left over, remains’.
एतत्
With vai tat, the refrain that closes this verse and the following ones: ‘this, truly, is that’ — this self is what was asked about.

Anvaya

येन [आत्मना] रूपम् रसम् गन्धम् शब्दान् स्पर्शान् मैथुनान् च [लोकः विजानाति], एतेन एव विजानाति । अत्र किम् परिशिष्यते ? एतत् वै तत् ॥

yena ātmanā rūpam rasam gandham śabdān sparśān maithunān ca lokaḥ vijānāti, etena eva vijānāti | atra kim pariśiṣyate ? etat vai tat ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A relative clause (yena …) whose verb is supplied from the main clause, picked up by etenaiva vijānāti; then a rhetorical question (kim atra pariśiṣyate) and the refrain etad vai tat, a nominal sentence.

Ambiguities

What does ‘what is left here?’ ask?
Śaṅkara: nothing is left that this self does not know, so it is all-knowing, स आत्मा सर्वज्ञः. Raṅgarāmānuja: what is there that it does not illumine, किं तत् अप्रकाश्यम् ? इति भावः.
What is ‘that’?
Śaṅkara: what Naciketas asked about, which even the gods doubted, यन्नचिकेतसा पृष्टं देवादिभिरपि विचिकित्सितं, the highest place of Viṣṇu. Raṅgarāmānuja: the highest place named earlier as the goal, पूर्वं प्राप्यतया निर्दिष्टं तत्परमं पदं.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16 + 4

Two half-lines of 16 syllables (four pādas of eight), followed by the refrain etad vai tat, four syllables, which also closes 2.1.5–2.1.8.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Witness error GRETIL (after Olivelle 1998): शब्दान् स्पर्शींश् च — GRETIL keys स्पर्शींश्; the accusative plural is sparśān, as the others read and Śaṅkara glosses.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Awareness

Everything we experience comes with someone experiencing it, and that one is the easiest thing to overlook. The verse simply asks us to notice it. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Name five things you are aware of right now. What is aware of all five?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why does Śaṅkara think that what is known cannot itself be the knower?

A contemplation exercise While eating something, notice the taste, and then notice the noticing.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
