Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 7 of 18

Beyond the senses, the mind

Beyond the senses is the mind, higher than the mind the sattva; above the sattva the great self, higher than the great the unmanifest.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Higher than the senses is the mind; higher than the mind the intellect; above the intellect the great self; and higher than the great self the unmanifest.

Key concepts इन्द्रिय indriya, a sense मनस् mind बुद्धि buddhi, the intellect महत् mahat, the great (self) अव्यक्त avyakta, the unmanifest

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse returns to a ladder we met at the end of the first chapter. Higher than the senses is the mind. Higher than the mind is the ‘sattva’, which the commentators say means the intellect, the power to decide and understand. Above the intellect is the ‘great self’, and higher still the ‘unmanifest’, the hidden source from which things unfold. The next verse completes the ladder. Śaṅkara says the steps lead inward, towards the inner self of all. Raṅgarāmānuja says the higher a thing is, the more it must be brought under control, and that the highest is reached by taking refuge in the Lord. Madhva says the ladder is repeated to teach that the Lord is above all. Why repeat it at all? Because the Kaṭha is building to its final teaching on yoga.

An analogy A ladder seen a second time from a different floor shows the same rungs in a new light.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

Higher than the senses is the mind, higher than the mind the sattva; above the sattva the great self, higher than the great the unmanifest.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the inner self of all

The self, of which the senses were said to be separate, is not to be sought outside, because it is the inner self of all, यस्मात्प्रत्यगात्मा स सर्वस्य. The objects are included here under the senses, being of the same kind, अर्थानामिहेन्द्रियसमानजातीयत्वादिन्द्रियग्रहणेनैव ग्रहणम्; the rest is as before, and sattva means the intellect, सत्त्वशब्दाद्बुद्धिरिहोच्यते.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: the series restates refuge

Commenting on 2.3.7–2.3.8 together, he says the two mantras restate the earlier teaching of surrender: even with true knowledge of the self as distinct from the body, refuge in the Lord is the means, देहविविक्तप्रत्यगात्मयाथात्म्यज्ञानेऽपि भगवच्छरणागतिरेव उपायः इति पूर्वोक्तं शरणवरणमेव प्रतिपादयति. ‘Senses’ includes the objects, as in 1.3.10, and sattva means the intellect, सत्त्वशब्दो बुद्धिपरः. Being ‘higher’ means being harder to bring under control, परत्वं च वशीकार्यतायां विवक्षितम्.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: the gradation that shows Hari supreme

The series beginning ‘beyond the senses the mind’ is given again to make known that the great purport of all statements is the knowledge of the Lord as supreme, reached through knowing the gradation of the deities: पुनः इन्द्रियेभ्यः परं मनः इत्यादि देवतानां तारतम्यज्ञानपूर्वकं भगवतः सर्वोत्तमत्वज्ञान एव सर्ववाक्यानां महातात्पर्यमिति ज्ञापयितुम्.

Comparative Vedānta

The ladder of 1.3.10–11 and the Gītā

The first adhyāya gave the same series with the objects and buddhi, इन्द्रियेभ्यः परा ह्यर्था अर्थेभ्यश्च परं मनः. The Gītā has a shorter form, इन्द्रियाणि पराण्याहुरिन्द्रियेभ्यः परं मनः.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.3.7

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The series leads inward to the self that is the inner self of all, not to be sought outside: यस्मात्प्रत्यगात्मा स सर्वस्य.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.3.7–8

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Each stage is higher in being harder to control; the highest is brought under control only by refuge in the Lord: परस्य च वशीकरणं शरणागतिरेव.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.3.7–8

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The repeated series teaches the gradation of the deities, and through it the supremacy of the Lord, which is the great purport of all statements: आधिक्ये सर्ववाक्यानां तात्पर्यं महदिष्यते.

Where they disagree

The three read the same ladder to different ends: for Śaṅkara it leads inward to the self of all; for Raṅgarāmānuja each rung is a thing to be mastered, the last by surrender; for Madhva it is a gradation of presiding deities that shows Hari above all.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • परं मनो मनसः paraṃ mano manasaḥ → परम् + मनः + मनसः

    m before a consonant; aḥ before a voiced consonant

    Final m becomes anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः); -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

  • सत्त्वमुत्तमम् sattvamuttamam → सत्त्वम् + उत्तमम्

    m before a vowel

    Final m stays before a vowel and is joined to it.

  • सत्त्वादधि sattvādadhi → सत्त्वात् + अधि

    t before a vowel

    A final t becomes d before a voiced sound, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

  • महानात्मा mahānātmā → महान् + आत्मा

    n before a vowel

    A final n after a long vowel stays before a vowel and is joined to it.

  • महतोऽव्यक्तमुत्तमम् mahato'vyaktamuttamam → महतः + अव्यक्तम् + उत्तमम्

    aḥ before a; m before a vowel

    The visarga of -aḥ before a becomes u, giving o, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च), and the a merges into it, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति); final m stays before a vowel and is joined to it.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

उत्तमम्
uttama, a superlative in form (‘highest’), used here as a comparative with the ablative: ‘higher than’.
अधि
adhi with the ablative, ‘above’. Madhva’s text reads sattvād api, ‘even above the sattva’.
सत्त्वम्
sattva stands here for the intellect (buddhi), as both Śaṅkara and Raṅgarāmānuja say, taking the place of buddhi in 1.3.10.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

Four nominal clauses, each naming what is higher (param, uttamam, adhi with the ablative) than the one before; the series continues into 2.3.8.

Ambiguities

Why the series again?
The series repeats 1.3.10–11 with sattva for buddhi and without the objects (arthāḥ). Śaṅkara: the objects are included under the senses here, and the rest is as before, पूर्ववदन्यत्. Raṅgarāmānuja: it restates the earlier teaching that, even with knowledge of the self as distinct from the body, refuge in the Lord is the means. Madhva: it is repeated to show that the great purport of all the Vedic statements is the Lord’s supremacy, known through the gradation of the deities.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16

Regular: 16 and 16 syllables.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: सत्त्वादपि महानात्मा — Madhva’s text reads sattvād api, ‘even above sattva’, for sattvād adhi, ‘above sattva’.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Science and wonder

Accounts of the mind often stack levels, from sensation to judgment to something deeper. The verse is an old version of that stacking. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.
