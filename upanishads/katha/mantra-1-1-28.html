Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 28 of 29

Having met the unageing, who would delight in long life?

A mortal who has come near the undying, and knows the difference, will not cling to the pleasures of beauty and love, nor rejoice in a very long life.

5 Faithful English translation

Having approached the unageing immortals, [what] ageing mortal standing on the earth below, knowing, reflecting on [the passing nature of] beauty, love and delights, would delight in a very long life?

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Who, having come near the undying who never grow old, would — himself a mortal who grows old, here on the earth below, and knowing better — still dwell on the pleasures of beauty and love, and delight in a very long life?

Key concepts मृत्यु death अमृत immortality, the deathless भोग enjoyment, use

7 Explain it to a beginner

This is Naciketas’ last argument against the offers. He has come face to face with the gods who never grow old and never die. He himself is a mortal, ageing, living down here on the earth, and he knows the difference. Having seen that, he asks, who could still dwell on the pleasures of beauty and love, and be glad of a very long life? The answer the question expects is: no one with understanding. Śaṅkara adds a simple observation about people: everyone wants to rise higher, so once someone has glimpsed what is higher, the lesser offers lose their pull.

An analogy Like someone who has heard a great orchestra live, and no longer wants to spend the evening with a tinny radio, however long it can play.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What is the argument?

A mortal who has approached the unageing immortals, and knows the difference, will not delight in a very long life spent dwelling on beauty, love and pleasure.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

How does Śaṅkara explain kvadhaḥsthaḥ?

As ‘standing on the earth, which is below’: कुः पृथिवी अधश्चासावन्तरिक्षादिलोकापेक्षया तस्यां तिष्ठतीति क्वधःस्थः. He records another reading, क्व तदास्थः, ‘how could he be attached to those things?’.

Traditional Interpretation

Why would no one delight?

Śaṅkara: everyone wishes to rise higher — सर्वो ह्युपर्युपर्येव बुभूषति लोकः — so one who has seen the higher will not be tempted by the lower; reflecting on pleasures as unstable, अतिदीर्घे जीविते कः विवेकी रमेत.

Comparative Vedānta

The Viśiṣṭādvaita reading

Raṅgarāmānuja reads kva tadāsthaḥ, and takes the unageing immortals as the freed: जरामरणशून्यानां मुक्तानां स्वरूपं ज्ञात्वा. A long life here is very little beside their joy: अत्यल्पे ऐहिके चिरजीविते कः प्रीतिमान् स्यात् इत्यर्थः.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara’s immortals are the gods whom the boy has approached, and the delights are those Death offered; Raṅgarāmānuja’s are the freed, and the delights theirs. They also read the text differently (kvadhaḥsthaḥ / kva tadāsthaḥ). Madhva’s bhāṣya on the passage 1.1.26–29 comments only on ‘the great passing-on’ of 1.1.29.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • अजीर्यताममृतानामुपेत्य ajīryatāmamṛtānāmupetya → अजीर्यताम् + अमृतानाम् + उपेत्य

    m before a vowel

    Final m before a vowel is kept: ajīryatām amṛtānām upetya.

  • जीर्यन्मर्त्यः jīryanmartyaḥ → जीर्यन् + मर्त्यः

    none

    n before m is kept; written together.

  • को रमेत ko rameta → कः + रमेत

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    kaḥ before r becomes ko, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च) and P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • क्वधःस्थः kvadhaḥsthaḥ

    upapada-tatpuruṣa (with a karmadhāraya) · कु + अधस् + स्थ

    Vigraha कुः पृथिवी अधश्चासावन्तरिक्षादिलोकापेक्षया तस्यां तिष्ठतीति

    standing on the earth, which is below; the vigraha is Śaṅkara’s

  • वर्णरतिप्रमोदान् varṇaratipramodān

    itaretara-dvandva · वर्ण + रति + प्रमोद

    Vigraha वर्णान् च रतीः च प्रमोदान् च

    beauty (colour, form), love-play and delights

  • अतिदीर्घे atidīrghe

    prādi-tatpuruṣa · अति + दीर्घ

    Vigraha अतिशयेन दीर्घे

    very long

Morphology

उपेत्य
Absolutive of upa-i, ‘having approached’; with the genitive amṛtānām (‘to the presence of the immortals’, Śaṅkara: सकाशम् उपेत्य).
रमेत
Optative middle of ram, in a rhetorical question: ‘who would delight?’ — no one.

Anvaya

अजीर्यताम् अमृतानाम् उपेत्य जीर्यन् क्वधःस्थः प्रजानन् मर्त्यः वर्णरतिप्रमोदान् अभिध्यायन् अतिदीर्घे जीविते कः रमेत ।

ajīryatām amṛtānām upetya jīryan kvadhaḥsthaḥ prajānan martyaḥ varṇaratipramodān abhidhyāyan atidīrghe jīvite kaḥ rameta |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

One long rhetorical question. The subject (martyaḥ, picked up by kaḥ) is qualified by participles and an adjective (jīryan, kvadhaḥsthaḥ, prajānan, abhidhyāyan); upetya with its genitive gives the prior action; the main verb is the optative rameta with a locative of the thing delighted in.

Ambiguities

kvadhaḥsthaḥ or kva tadāsthaḥ
Śaṅkara divides kvadhaḥsthaḥ as ku (earth) + adhaḥ-stha, ‘dwelling on the earth below’, and records another reading, क्व तदास्थः, ‘how could he be attached to that?’. Raṅgarāmānuja reads that variant in his text and explains it: जरामरणशून्यानां मुक्तानां स्वरूपं ज्ञात्वा, knowing the nature of the freed, how would one fix his attachment there? Madhva’s text reads kvādhasthaḥ (corrections.json).
Whose delights?
Śaṅkara: the pleasures Death offered, the apsarases and the rest, seen as unstable. Raṅgarāmānuja takes varṇa-rati-pramoda as the delights of the freed in the higher world, which make long earthly life seem short; he glosses the last words as अत्यल्पे ऐहिके चिरजीविते कः प्रीतिमान् स्यात् इत्यर्थः.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 21 + 22

Triṣṭubh. The half-lines count 21 and 22: the second pāda (jīryan martyaḥ kvadhaḥsthaḥ prajānan) has 10 syllables as written, one short of the norm.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Aging and mortality

A longer life is a good, but the verse asks what the length is for. Someone who has glimpsed what does not age measures years differently. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin If you could live twice as long, what would you do with the extra years?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Does knowing something better change how we value what we already have?

A contemplation exercise Recall a moment that felt timeless. Ask what it had that ordinary pleasures lack.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
