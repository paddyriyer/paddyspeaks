Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 8 of 17

The one who arrives

One with understanding, with a held mind, always pure, reaches that place from which one is not born again.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘One who has understanding, whose mind is held and who is always pure, reaches that goal, from which there is no being born again.’

Key concepts संसार saṃsāra, the round of births मोक्ष liberation मनस् mind पद the goal; the word

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse completes the pair. Where the previous verse described a journey that fails, this one describes a journey that arrives. A person who has understanding, who keeps the mind steady and whose life is clean reaches 'that goal' — and it is a goal from which there is no being born again. Śaṅkara explains that this is the rider who has a wise driver: in other words, a person who knows. Having reached that place, he says, one does not fall from it and is not born again into saṃsāra. The two verses together turn the chariot picture into a choice between two outcomes, and they set up the question the next verse answers: what is this goal, the end of the road?

An analogy Like a traveller who finally reaches home after a long journey: the point of the road was never to keep travelling.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

That one with understanding, a held mind and always pure reaches that place from which one is not born again.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara

The second kind of rider, with a discerning charioteer, the one who knows, विज्ञानवत्सारथ्युपेतो रथी, विद्वानित्येतत्, reaches that place; having reached it he does not fall from it and is not born again in saṃsāra, यस्मादाप्तात्पदादप्रच्युतः सन् भूयः पुनः न जायते संसारे.

Comparative Vedānta

Raṅgarāmānuja

His comment on 1.3.7–8 is given under 1.3.7: these two verses show the outcome of controlling the senses or not, हयत्वेन रूपितानाम् इन्द्रियाणां वशीकरणतदभावयोः प्रयोजनं दर्शयति मन्त्रद्वयेन.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Madhva

In our source Madhva prints 1.3.3–1.3.11 without comment and then comments once, marking it ॥ १०-१२ ॥, on the deities who preside over the senses, their objects, the mind and the intellect; he does not gloss this verse.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.3.8

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The one who knows reaches the place from which there is no falling back and no rebirth, यस्मादाप्तात्पदादप्रच्युतः सन्.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.3.7–8

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

With 1.3.7 the verse states the outcome of mastering the senses or not, वशीकरणतदभावयोः प्रयोजनं दर्शयति मन्त्रद्वयेन: reaching the goal, as against falling into saṃsāra.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Both read the verse as the reward of control; what the goal is they say elsewhere (1.3.9, Viṣṇu’s highest place, which Śaṅkara also names). In our source Madhva prints 1.3.3–1.3.11 without comment and then comments once, marking it ॥ १०-१२ ॥, on the deities who preside over the senses, their objects, the mind and the intellect; he does not gloss this verse.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • यस्तु yastu → यः + तु

    visarga sandhi

    Visarga before t becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः).

  • स तु sa tu → सः + तु

    saḥ before a consonant

    saḥ loses its ending before a consonant, P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि).

  • तत्पदमाप्नोति tatpadamāpnoti → तत् + पदम् + आप्नोति

    consonant; m before a vowel

    t stays before p; final m stays before a vowel.

  • यस्माद्भूयो न yasmādbhūyo na → यस्मात् + भूयः + न

    voicing; visarga sandhi

    Final t becomes d before a voiced sound, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते); aḥ → o before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • समनस्कः samanaskaḥ

    bahuvrīhi · स + मनस्

    Vigraha मनसा सह वर्तते इति

    one with (a held) mind; with the suffix -ka

Morphology

समनस्कः
The counterpart of amanaskaḥ in 1.3.7: sa- ‘with’ + manas + -ka; Śaṅkara: युक्तमनाः.
जायते
Present middle of jan ‘be born’ (stem jāya-).

Anvaya

यः तु विज्ञानवान् समनस्कः सदा शुचिः भवति, सः तु तत् पदम् आप्नोति, यस्मात् भूयः न जायते ॥

yaḥ tu vijñānavān samanaskaḥ sadā śuciḥ bhavati, saḥ tu tat padam āpnoti, yasmāt bhūyaḥ na jāyate ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

The counterpart of 1.3.7. A relative clause with three predicates (vijñānavān, samanaskaḥ, śuciḥ); the main clause sa tu tat padam āpnoti; a second relative clause, yasmād bhūyo na jāyate, describes the place.

Ambiguities

Who arrives?
Śaṅkara: the rider who has a discerning charioteer, that is, one who knows, विज्ञानवत्सारथ्युपेतो रथी, विद्वानित्येतत्. Raṅgarāmānuja comments on 1.3.7–8 together as the outcome of controlling the senses.
sadā śuciḥ
Madhva’s mūla writes the words together, सदाशुचिः, which could be read ‘always impure’; the contrast with 1.3.7 shows sadā śuciḥ, as the other witnesses space it.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 17 + 16

17 and 16 syllables: the first pāda, yas tu vijñānavān bhavati, has nine, as in 1.3.6.

Textual variations between witnesses

The witnesses differ here only in spelling (listed in section 14).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Purpose

A journey is defined by where it ends. The verse names no shortcut: understanding, a steady mind and a clean life are the whole of the method. (A modern reflection.)
