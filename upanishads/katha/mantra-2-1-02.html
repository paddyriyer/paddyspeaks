Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 2 of 15

The net of death

The childish follow outward desires into the wide-spread net of death; the wise, knowing the immortal that is firm, ask for nothing among the things that are not.

5 Faithful English translation

‘The childish follow outward desires; they go into the net of wide-spread death. But the wise, having known immortality [that is] firm, do not ask here for [anything] among the things that are not firm.’

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘The immature chase what lies outside and walk into the wide net of death. The wise, who know the immortal that does not change, ask for nothing among the things here that do.’

Key concepts प्रत्यगात्मन् pratyagātman, the inward self मृत्यु death अमृत immortality, the deathless धीर the wise अविद्या avidyā एषणा desire, ‘the three desires’ भोग enjoyment, use

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse follows on from the last. Because our senses face outward, most people simply follow whatever pulls them from outside — the next pleasure, the next thing to own. The verse calls such people childish, not because they are bad but because they have not yet grown up in this one respect. It says they walk into a net that death has spread wide: whatever is gained this way is lost again, and the chase goes on. The wise are different. They have understood that there is something in them that does not die and does not change, and so they stop asking the changing things of the world to give them what those things cannot give. Śaṅkara takes this as the reason the wise give up the three great longings — for children, for wealth and for a better world after death.

An analogy Chasing the reflection of the moon from one puddle to the next, when the moon itself is overhead and never moves.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

The immature follow outward desires and go into the wide net of death; the wise, knowing the immortal that is firm, ask for nothing among the impermanent things here.

Traditional Interpretation

Ignorance and craving

Śaṅkara: seeing only what is outward is the ignorance that blocks seeing the self, and the craving for the pleasures it shows is the second obstacle; those blocked by the two, ताभ्यामविद्यातृष्णाभ्यां प्रतिबद्धात्मदर्शनाः, follow outward desires. The result is the endless round of birth, death, old age and illness, अनवरतं जन्ममरणजरारोगाद्यनेकानर्थव्रातं प्रतिपद्यन्त इत्यर्थः. The wise turn away from the three longings, पुत्रवित्तलोकैषणाभ्यो व्युत्तिष्ठन्त्येवेत्यभिप्रायः.

Traditional Interpretation

The firm immortality

Śaṅkara supports ‘firm’ with a scriptural line, न कर्मणा वर्धते नो कनीयान्: the self’s greatness neither grows nor shrinks by action.

Comparative Vedānta

Raṅgarāmānuja: the Supreme is inward too

He glosses the verse much as Śaṅkara does, and adds that the Supreme Self is also ‘inward’, being the chief referent of ‘I’ in every self, परमात्मनः सर्वजीवगताहन्तास्पदत्वेन मुख्याहमर्थत्वात्, प्रत्यक्त्वमस्तीति द्रष्टव्यम्.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Madhva

Madhva’s text reads pāśān, ‘nets’. In our source his comment printed after 2.1.2–3 does not gloss these verses: it begins with words of 2.2.6 (‘I shall tell you this secret Brahman … how [the self fares] after death’), and is not used here.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.1.2

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Ignorance and craving keep people from seeing the self; the firm immortality is resting in the inward self, प्रत्यगात्मस्वरूपावस्थानलक्षणम् अमृतत्वं, which the wise know and so seek nothing else.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.1.2

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The wise, knowing the firm immortality in the inward self, प्रत्यगात्मन्येव ध्रुवम् - अमृतत्वं विदित्वा, seek nothing in the round of saṃsāra; the Supreme too is inward, as the chief referent of ‘I’.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

The two readings agree on the verse’s plain sense; Raṅgarāmānuja’s addition that the Supreme is the chief referent of ‘I’ in every self keeps the self and the Supreme distinct where Śaṅkara’s ‘inward self’ is one. Madhva’s bhāṣya, in our source, has no comment on this verse.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • कामाननुयन्ति kāmānanuyanti → कामान् + अनुयन्ति

    n before a vowel

    Final n stays n before a vowel (it is not doubled after a long vowel).

  • बालास्ते bālāste → बालाः + ते

    visarga → s

    Visarga becomes s before t, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः).

  • मृत्योर्यन्ति mṛtyoryanti → मृत्योः + यन्ति

    visarga → r

    The final s of mṛtyoḥ becomes ru, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः), heard as r before a voiced sound after a vowel other than a/ā.

  • धीरा अमृतत्वं dhīrā amṛtatvaṃ → धीराः + अमृतत्वम्

    visarga lost after ā

    After ā, ru becomes y before a vowel, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), and the y is dropped, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य); the hiatus that results is not joined again.

  • ध्रुवमध्रुवेष्विह dhruvamadhruveṣviha → ध्रुवम् + अध्रुवेषु + इह

    m before a vowel; u → v

    Final m stays before a vowel; u becomes v before a dissimilar vowel, P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

पराचः
Accusative plural masculine of parāñc ‘turned away’; the weak stem parāc- (without the nasal) appears in the weak cases.
विततस्य
Past passive participle of tan ‘stretch’ with vi (vitata, ‘stretched out’); Śaṅkara takes it with mṛtyoḥ, ‘death that is spread everywhere’.
प्रार्थयन्ते
From the denominative root arth ‘seek’ (tenth class, middle), with pra: ‘ask for’.
पाशम्
Madhva’s text reads pāśān, plural, ‘nets’ (corrections.json).

Anvaya

बालाः पराचः कामान् अनुयन्ति । ते विततस्य मृत्योः पाशम् यन्ति । अथ धीराः ध्रुवम् अमृतत्वम् विदित्वा इह अध्रुवेषु [किञ्चित्] न प्रार्थयन्ते ॥

bālāḥ parācaḥ kāmān anuyanti | te vitatasya mṛtyoḥ pāśam yanti | atha dhīrāḥ dhruvam amṛtatvam viditvā iha adhruveṣu kiñcit na prārthayante ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two contrasting sentences joined by atha. In the first, bālāḥ is the subject of anuyanti and is picked up by te, subject of yanti; mṛtyor vitatasya depends on pāśam. In the second, dhīrāḥ governs the absolutive viditvā (object amṛtatvam, with dhruvam as its predicate or qualifier) and the main verb na prārthayante; adhruveṣv iha says where they ask for nothing.

Ambiguities

Whose net?
Śaṅkara: death is the whole of ignorance, desire and action, अविद्याकामकर्मसमुदायस्य, and its net is the binding of body and senses, देहेन्द्रियादिसंयोगवियोगलक्षणम्. Raṅgarāmānuja adds a second reading in which Death speaks of himself: सर्वत्र अप्रतिहताज्ञस्य मृत्योः मम पाशं यान्ति.
What is firm?
Śaṅkara: the immortality of the gods is not firm, देवाद्यमृतत्वं ह्यध्रुवम्; firm is resting in the inward self, प्रत्यगात्मस्वरूपावस्थानलक्षणं ध्रुवम्.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 23

22 and 23 syllables: the last pāda, dhruvam adhruveṣv iha na prārthayante, has twelve; the others have eleven.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: विततस्य पाशान् — Madhva’s text reads pāśān, plural, ‘nets’, for pāśam, singular.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Desire

The verse does not condemn wanting things; it observes that what changes cannot give what does not change, and that asking it to keeps the chase going. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What did you want badly a year ago that you no longer think about?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry What would it mean to know something as ‘firm’ in a way that changes what one asks of the world?

A contemplation exercise The next time you reach for something to make you feel settled, pause and ask what you are really asking it for.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
