Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 15 of 29

The fire taught and repeated

Death teaches the fire, the first of the worlds — which bricks, how many, how laid — and Naciketas repeats it back; Death, pleased, speaks again.

5 Faithful English translation

‘He told him that fire, the beginning of the worlds — which bricks, how many, or how [laid]. And he too repeated it back as it was told. Then Death, pleased at him, spoke again.’

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘So Death taught him the fire that is the origin of the worlds — what bricks it takes, how many, and how they are laid. Naciketas repeated it back exactly. Pleased, Death spoke again.’

Key concepts अग्नि Agni, fire लोक world, realm

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse is told by the Upaniṣad itself, not by Yama or Naciketas. Death teaches the boy the fire ritual in detail: which bricks are used, how many, and how they are laid. The Upaniṣad does not repeat the details; it only says they were taught. Then Naciketas repeats everything back exactly, as a good student in the Vedic schools would. Death is pleased with him. The verse shows the old way of learning by careful listening and exact repetition, and it shows a teacher’s delight in a student who has truly taken in the lesson.

An analogy Like a music teacher who plays a passage once, hears the student play it back without a single slip, and smiles.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What happens?

Death teaches the fire — bricks, number, method; Naciketas repeats it exactly; Death, pleased, speaks again (the next verses).

Traditional Interpretation

Why are the details not given?

The Upaniṣad states that the bricks, their number and their method were taught, without giving them. Śaṅkara: याः इष्टकाः चेतव्याः स्वरूपेण यावतीर्वा सङ्ख्यया यथा वा चीयतेऽग्निर्येन प्रकारेण सर्वमेतदुक्तवानित्यर्थः. Raṅgarāmānuja says the same: यल्लक्षणाः इष्टकाश्चेतव्याः, यत्संख्याकाः, येन प्रकारेण चेतव्याः.

Traditional Interpretation

Why is Death pleased?

Śaṅkara: pleased by the student’s exact repetition, Death wants to give a further boon besides the three: अथ अस्य प्रत्युच्चारणेन तुष्टः सन् मृत्युः पुनरेवाह वरत्रयव्यतिरेकेणान्यं वरं दित्सुः. Raṅgarāmānuja: शिष्यस्य ग्रहणसामर्थ्यदर्शनेन सन्तुष्टस्सन्.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.15

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The fire is ‘first of the worlds’ as Virāj, the first embodied being: लोकानामादिं प्रथमशरीरित्वात्. The verse is narrative; its teaching is the student’s exact reception.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.1.15

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The fire is the cause of reaching that world: लोकस्य आदिम् – हेतुम्; स्वर्ग्यमिति यावत्. The student’s capacity to grasp is what pleases the teacher.

Dvaita · Madhva (citing a text he does not name), Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.15

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The bricks have deities, and their deity is Viṣṇu; one who knows this rightly is freed from the bondage of action: इष्टकादेवतां विष्णुं षष्ठ्युत्तरशतत्रिकम् । यथावदेव विज्ञाय मुच्यते कर्मबन्धनात्. The number in the verse (three hundred and sixty) is the bricks’ count as Madhva’s cited verse gives it.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara’s ‘first embodied being’ against Raṅgarāmānuja’s ‘cause of [reaching] the world’ for lokādi. Madhva reads the bricks themselves as pointing to Viṣṇu, their deity.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • लोकादिमग्निम् lokādimagnim → लोकादिम् + अग्निम्

    final m before a vowel

    Final m stays before a vowel.

  • या इष्टका yā iṣṭakā → याः + इष्टकाः

    āḥ before a vowel

    The visarga of -āḥ is lost before a vowel: P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि) with P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य).

  • इष्टका यावतीर्वा iṣṭakā yāvatīrvā → इष्टकाः + यावतीः + वा

    āḥ before a voiced consonant; īḥ before v

    āḥ loses its visarga before a voiced consonant, P. 8.3.22 (हलि सर्वेषाम्); after ī the visarga becomes r.

  • चापि cāpi → च + अपि

    vowel sandhi (savarṇa-dīrgha)

    a + a → ā: P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • प्रत्यवदद्यथोक्तमथास्य pratyavadadyathoktamathāsya → प्रत्यवदत् + यथोक्तम् + अथ + अस्य

    consonant and vowel sandhi

    Final t before y becomes d, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते); m before a vowel stays; a + a → ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • पुनरेवाह punarevāha → पुनः + एव + आह

    r-final word; vowel sandhi

    punar keeps its r before a vowel; a + ā → ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • लोकादिम् lokādim

    ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa · लोक + आदि

    Vigraha लोकानाम् आदिः

    the first of the worlds

  • यथोक्तम् yathoktam

    avyayībhāva · यथा + उक्त

    Vigraha उक्तम् अनतिक्रम्य

    exactly as told

Morphology

यावतीः
Nominative plural feminine of yāvat; classical yāvatyaḥ. Raṅgarāmānuja: a Vedic pūrvasavarṇa form, पूर्वसवर्णः छान्दसः.
आह
The perfect of ah ‘say’, used with present or past sense.
प्रत्यवदत्
Imperfect of prati-vad ‘say back, repeat’.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

The first half is narrative: Death told him the fire, with an indirect list of what was taught (yā iṣṭakā yāvatīr vā yathā vā — which bricks, how many, how). The second: Naciketas repeated it; Death, pleased, spoke again. Śaṅkara notes that the voice is now the text’s own, not Yama’s: इदं श्रुतेर्वचनम्.

Ambiguities

lokādi: ‘first of the worlds’ in what sense?
Śaṅkara: the fire is the first of the worlds because it was the first embodied being (Virāj): लोकानामादिं प्रथमशरीरित्वात्. Raṅgarāmānuja: the cause of the world reached, i.e. leading to heaven: लोकस्य आदिम् – हेतुम्; स्वर्ग्यमिति यावत्.
punar evāha or punar āha?
GRETIL and Madhva’s text read punar āha without eva; the base, the vishvAsa mūla and Śaṅkara read punar evāha (corrections.json).

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 23

Counted as written, 22 and 23 syllables; the second line is one syllable long (sa cāpi tat pratyavadad yathoktam athāsya mṛtyuḥ punar evāha tuṣṭaḥ). Without eva, as GRETIL and Madhva read, it would have 22 — a metrical argument some editors use for that reading; the edition keeps the base text.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading GRETIL (after Olivelle 1998): अथास्य मृत्युः पुनर् — GRETIL reads punar āha, ‘spoke again’, without eva; AS and the vishvAsa mūla read punar evāha, which Śaṅkara comments on. Madhva’s text agrees with GRETIL.
  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: अथास्य मृत्युः पुनराह — Madhva’s text reads punar āha, ‘spoke again’, without eva, as GRETIL does; AS and the vishvAsa mūla read punar evāha.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Learning and apprenticeship

Repeating a lesson back exactly is an old method, and still a good test of listening: it shows what was actually heard. The teacher’s pleasure here is the pleasure of being understood. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin When did someone last understand you exactly as you meant?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why might exact repetition be the first step to understanding, not the last?

A contemplation exercise In your next conversation, repeat back the other person’s main point in their words before answering.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
