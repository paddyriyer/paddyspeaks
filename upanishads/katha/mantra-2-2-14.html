Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 14 of 15

How shall I know it?

‘This is that,’ they think: the highest happiness, beyond description. How then shall I know it? Does it shine, or does it shine forth?

5 Faithful English translation

‘That [is] this,’ they think — the highest happiness, not to be described. How then could I know that? Does it shine, or does it shine forth?

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘This is it,’ the wise say of that highest happiness, which no words can point to. But how am I to know it? Does it shine, does it shine out clearly?

Key concepts आनन्द bliss ब्रह्मन् Brahman ज्ञान knowledge आचार्य ācārya, teacher

7 Explain it to a beginner

After all this teaching about the self within, the listener asks a very honest question. The wise say, ‘This is it!’ about a happiness so deep that no words can describe it. But how am I supposed to know it? Does it shine like a light I could see? Does it shine clearly? Śaṅkara takes this as the seeker wondering how the joy the sages speak of could become his own experience. Raṅgarāmānuja hears the student himself speaking: ‘You masters see it as clearly as a fruit in your hand, but my mind only grasps things with shape and colour. How can I know it?’ Madhva says this highest happiness is God’s own nature, and even the joy of the wise is only a drop of it. The next verse gives the answer.

An analogy Like asking someone who has tasted honey what it tastes like: they can only say ‘like this’ and point.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

The knowers hold that the highest, indescribable happiness is present, ‘this’; the speaker asks how he could know it, and whether it shines, or shines forth.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the seeker’s question

The bliss of knowing the self cannot be described and is beyond the speech and mind of ordinary people, yet those who have given up all desire take it as directly present, प्राकृतपुरुषवाङ्मनसयोरगोचरमपि सन्निवृत्तैषणा ये ब्राह्मणास्ते तदेतत्प्रत्यक्षमेवेति मन्यन्ते. How, by what means, shall I know that happiness, कथं नु केन प्रकारेण तत्सुखमहं विजानीयाम्? Does it shine, being of the nature of light, and is it seen clearly by our minds, or not?

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: the student speaks

So instructed, the student says, एवमुक्तः शिष्य आह: people like you, perfected in yoga, hold that transcendent bliss to be present, like a fruit on the palm, करतलामलकवत् अपरोक्षं. How shall I know it, whose mind cannot grasp Brahman without form, कथं रूपादिहीनब्रह्मग्रहणासमर्थमानसः अहं विजानीयाम्? Does it shine with brilliance, clearly, or not clearly, being mixed with other lights?

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: a drop of the Lord’s bliss

This highest happiness is the Lord’s own form, एतदेव भगवद्रूपं परमं सुखम्; the happiness of the knower is a mere drop of it, ज्ञानिसुखं तु तद्विप्लुण्मात्रम्. He cites verses he attributes to the Mahāvarāha: how am I to know whether it shines fully or not, सम्यग् भाति न भातीति जानीयां तत्कथं न्वहम्, that divine, indescribable highest happiness, without his grace, तत्प्रसादमृते दिव्यमनिर्देश्यं परं सुखम्.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.2.14

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The highest happiness is the bliss of self-knowledge, present to those free of desire; the question is how it can become the seeker’s own, कथं नु केन प्रकारेण तत्सुखमहं विजानीयाम्.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.2.14

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The student asks how one whose mind grasps only what has form can know formless Brahman, कथं रूपादिहीनब्रह्मग्रहणासमर्थमानसः अहं विजानीयाम्.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.2.14

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The highest happiness is the Lord’s own form, the knower’s happiness a drop of it, and it is known only by his grace, तत्प्रसादमृते दिव्यमनिर्देश्यं परं सुखम् (a verse cited by him).

Where they disagree

All three take the verse as a question about knowing the highest. For Śaṅkara the happiness is the self’s own bliss; for Raṅgarāmānuja the difficulty is a mind bound to form; for Madhva the happiness is the Lord’s, of which the knower’s is a drop, reached only by his grace.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • तदेतदिति tadetaditi → तत् + एतत् + इति

    t before a vowel

    A final t becomes d before a voiced sound, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

  • मन्यन्तेऽनिर्देश्यं manyante'nirdeśyaṃ → मन्यन्ते + अनिर्देश्यम्

    e + a

    After e at the end of a word an initial a is merged into it, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति); the avagraha marks it.

  • तद्विजानीयां tadvijānīyāṃ → तत् + विजानीयाम्

    t before a voiced consonant

    A final t becomes d before a voiced sound, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

  • किमु kimu → किम् + उ

    m before a vowel

    Final m stays before a vowel and is joined to it.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • अनिर्देश्यम् anirdeśyam

    nañ-tatpuruṣa · अ + निर्देश्य

    Vigraha न निर्देश्यम् अनिर्देश्यम्

    not to be pointed out, beyond description

Morphology

अनिर्देश्यम्
A gerundive (-ya) of nis + √diś, ‘point out’, negated: ‘what cannot be pointed out’. Śaṅkara: निर्देष्टुमशक्यं.
विजानीयाम्
Optative of vi + √jñā (class 9, jānāti): ‘how could I come to know’.
विभाति
vi + √bhā, ‘shine forth, shine clearly’, set against the simple bhāti. Madhva’s text reads bhāti na bhāti vā, ‘does it shine or not shine’ (corrections.json).

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

The first line reports a thought: ‘tad etat’ closed by iti, with manyante as the verb and anirdeśyaṃ paramaṃ sukham in apposition to tad. The second line asks two questions: kathaṃ nu … vijānīyām (optative), and kim u bhāti vibhāti vā, an alternative question.

Ambiguities

Who speaks the questions?
Raṅgarāmānuja: the student, so instructed, speaks, एवमुक्तः शिष्य आह: people like you, perfected in yoga, see it directly; how can I, whose mind cannot grasp what is without form? Śaṅkara has the seeker ask how he may know it as the renunciants do, and whether it shines, किमु तत् भाति दीप्यते प्रकाशात्मकं तत्.
‘Shines or shines forth’
Śaṅkara: does it shine, and is it seen clearly by our minds, or not, विभाति विस्पष्टं दृश्यते किं वा नेति. Raṅgarāmānuja: does it shine with brilliance, clearly, or not clearly, being mixed with other lights, उत तेजोऽन्तरसंवलनान्न विस्पष्टं प्रकाशते इति प्रश्नः. Madhva reads ‘does it shine or not shine’.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16

Two lines of 16 syllables (8 + 8).

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: तद्विजानीयां किमु भाति न — Madhva’s text reads bhāti na bhāti vā, ‘does it shine or not shine’, for bhāti vibhāti vā, ‘does it shine, or shine forth’.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Philosophy of mind

How can one know what cannot be described or shown? The question returns in modern discussions of experience that resists report. The verse lets the questioner speak in the first person. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Is there anything you know that you cannot put into words?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Is ‘does it shine?’ a question about the thing, or about whether it can be seen?

A contemplation exercise Write down one honest question you have about what you are studying, as plainly as this verse does.
