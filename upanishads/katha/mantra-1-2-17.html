The best support

This support is the best, this support is the highest; whoever knows this support is honoured in the world of Brahman.

7 Explain it to a beginner

The praise of Om continues. Now it is called a support — something to lean on, as a climbing plant leans on a stake. In meditation the mind needs something to rest on, and Death says this syllable is the best and the highest such support. Whoever knows it is honoured in the world of Brahman. Śaṅkara explains the last phrase in two ways, matching the two forms of Brahman in the previous verse: one who seeks the highest becomes Brahman itself; one who seeks the lower is honoured like the Creator. Raṅgarāmānuja says that meditation resting on this syllable is the best of all meditation, and finds the rest of the verse plain. The verse Madhva cites makes the support the Lord himself, on whom all things rest. Om here is not the goal itself but what one holds on the way.

An analogy Like a handrail on a steep stair: it is not the top of the stair, but whoever holds it does not fall.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does Death say?

That this is the best and the highest support, and that one who knows it is honoured in the world of Brahman.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the best support

Because of what was just said, this is the best of the supports for attaining Brahman — ब्रह्मप्राप्त्यालम्बनानां श्रेष्ठं प्रशस्यतमम् — and it is ‘highest’ in that it supports both the higher and the lower Brahman. The knower is honoured in either: परस्मिन्ब्रह्मण्यपरस्मिंश्च ब्रह्मभूतो ब्रह्मवदुपास्यो भवतीत्यर्थः.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: the support of meditation

Om is the support, ओंकाररूपमालम्बनम्, best for meditation and the like, श्रेष्ठम् ध्यानादेरिति शेषः; and meditation resting on it is the highest of all: एतदालम्बनकं ध्यानादि सर्वोत्कृष्टम् इत्यर्थः. The second half needs no comment.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: the support of all

In the verse Madhva cites on this passage, the support is the Lord, the support of all: सर्वस्यालम्बनं ज्ञात्वा मुच्यते नात्र संशयः — ‘knowing the support of all, one is freed; there is no doubt of it’.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.2.14–17

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

On the verse Madhva cites, the support is the Lord, the support of all: सर्वस्यालम्बनं ज्ञात्वा मुच्यते नात्र संशयः.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara and Raṅgarāmānuja take the support as the syllable Om held in meditation; the verse Madhva cites makes the support Viṣṇu himself. Only Śaṅkara reads brahmaloka two ways, for the two Brahmans.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • एतदालम्बनं श्रेष्ठमेतदालम्बनं परम् etadālambanaṃ śreṣṭhametadālambanaṃ param → एतत् + आलम्बनम् + श्रेष्ठम् + एतत् + आलम्बनम् + परम्

    t voiced before a vowel; m before a consonant

    etat before ā becomes etad, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते); ālambanam before ś and p is written with anusvāra, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः); śreṣṭham is written joined to the vowel that follows.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • ब्रह्मलोके brahmaloke

    ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa · ब्रह्मन् + लोक

    Vigraha ब्रह्मणः लोकः

    the world of Brahman (for Śaṅkara, of the higher or the lower Brahman; see ambiguities)

Morphology

आलम्बनम्
From ā-√lamb ‘hang on, lean on’: what one rests on; in meditation, what holds the mind.
महीयते
Passive of √mah ‘be great’: ‘is magnified, is honoured’.

Anvaya

एतत् आलम्बनम् श्रेष्ठम्, एतत् आलम्बनम् परम् । एतत् आलम्बनम् ज्ञात्वा [सः] ब्रह्मलोके महीयते ।

etat ālambanam śreṣṭham, etat ālambanam param | etat ālambanam jñātvā saḥ brahmaloke mahīyate |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two nominal sentences and a third with an absolutive, built like 1.2.16; the subject of mahīyate, ‘the one who knows’, is understood.

Ambiguities

brahmaloka
Śaṅkara: the knower is honoured in the higher Brahman, having become Brahman, or in the lower, as one worshipped like Brahmā — परस्मिन्ब्रह्मण्यपरस्मिंश्च ब्रह्मभूतो ब्रह्मवदुपास्यो भवतीत्यर्थः. Raṅgarāmānuja calls the second half plain and does not gloss it: उत्तरार्धस्य स्पष्टोऽर्थः.
Support of what?
Śaṅkara: the best of the supports for reaching Brahman, ब्रह्मप्राप्त्यालम्बनानां श्रेष्ठं प्रशस्यतमम्. Raṅgarāmānuja: Om as the support of meditation, ओंकाररूपमालम्बनम्, best for meditation and the like — श्रेष्ठम् ध्यानादेरिति शेषः — and meditation resting on it is the highest. In the verse Madhva cites, the support is the support of all: सर्वस्यालम्बनं ज्ञात्वा मुच्यते नात्र संशयः.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Emotional attachments

Everyone leans on something: a routine, a person, a phrase remembered at a hard moment. The verse does not say one should lean on nothing; it says some supports are better than others, and names the best. A modern reader might ask, honestly, what they actually lean on, and whether it can bear the weight. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What do you lean on when things are hard?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry What is the difference between a support and a goal? Why does the verse call Om a support?

A contemplation exercise Notice one thing you reach for when you are anxious. Ask whether it steadies you or only distracts you.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
