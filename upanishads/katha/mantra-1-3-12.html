Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 12 of 17

Hidden in all beings

This self, hidden in all beings, does not shine forth; it is seen by those who see the subtle, with a sharpened, subtle intellect.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Hidden in every being, this self does not show itself; but those who see what is subtle see it, with an intellect made sharp and fine.

Key concepts आत्मन् self, Self अविद्या avidyā विज्ञान understanding, the knowing self गुहा the cave

7 Explain it to a beginner

The ladder of the previous verses ended in the Person, the inmost reality. Now Death says why most people never notice it. It is hidden inside every living thing, so close that it does not stand out as something to look at. It does not shine forth by itself. Yet it can be seen, by people who have trained themselves to notice what is fine and subtle, using an intellect that has become sharp and steady, like the point of a needle. Śaṅkara says that what hides it is ignorance: people take the body and the senses for themselves, and do not take the self for themselves even when told. Raṅgarāmānuja says it does not show itself to those who have not mastered their senses and mind. The next verse says how that mastery is practised.

An analogy Like the thread inside a string of beads: it runs through every bead and holds them together, but one sees the beads, not the thread.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

The self is hidden in all beings and does not shine forth, but it is seen with a sharp and subtle intellect by those who see the subtle.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: hidden by ignorance, seen by the trained

The Person is hidden in all beings, covered by ignorance and māyā, अविद्यामायाच्छन्नः, and so does not appear to anyone as his self. Śaṅkara marvels at this: अहो अतिगम्भीरा दुरवगाह्या विचित्रा चेयं माया. It is seen by an intellect that is trained and one-pointed, एकाग्रतयोपेतयेत्येतत्, and subtle, सूक्ष्मवस्तुनिरूपणपरया, by those who have learned to see the subtle through the steps of 1.3.10–11.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: present as the self of all

The self of this verse is the one present in all beings as their self, सर्वेषु भूतेषु आत्मतया वर्तमानः; it does not appear as it is to those who have not mastered their faculties, अजितबाह्यान्तःकरणानां. It is seen with an intellect free of outer and inner activity, by the power that discerns subtle things: सूक्ष्मार्थविवेचनशक्त्या.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara quotes the Gītā

For the world's not seeing what is its own self, Śaṅkara cites the Gītā (7.25): नाहं प्रकाशः सर्वस्य योगमायासमावृतः — ‘I am not manifest to all, covered by yogamāyā’.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

The text of agryayā

The base file reads अग्न्यया, and its copy of Śaṅkara's bhāṣya repeats the slip. GRETIL, the vishvAsa mūla and Madhva's text read अग्र्यया, and Śaṅkara's own gloss, agram iva, ‘like a point’, points to agra, ‘point’; the edition corrects the text (corrections.json). The sandhi gūḍho 'tmā, with the long ā elided, is in every witness.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.3.12

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The self of all is hidden by ignorance and māyā — अविद्यामायाच्छन्नः — so that no one takes it for his own self; a one-pointed, subtle intellect sees it.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.3.12

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The supreme Self dwells in all beings as their self, सर्वेषु भूतेषु आत्मतया वर्तमानः, and is seen only by those whose outer and inner faculties are mastered.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

What hides the self — ignorance and māyā, so that one's own identity with it is missed (Śaṅkara), or unmastered faculties, which keep the indwelling Lord from being perceived as he is (Raṅgarāmānuja). Madhva's bhāṣya, in our source, has no comment on this verse; his comment after 1.3.13 concerns 1.3.13.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • एष सर्वेषु eṣa sarveṣu → एषः + सर्वेषु

    eṣaḥ before a consonant

    The pronoun saḥ / eṣaḥ drops its visarga before any consonant.

  • गूढोऽत्मा gūḍho'tmā → गूढः + आत्मा

    aḥ before ā, with the ā elided (Vedic)

    As before a short a, the visarga becomes u and a + u give o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः); here the following long ā is also elided, as the avagraha shows. The ordinary rule would give गूढ आत्मा; every witness of this edition has the elided form, which keeps the pāda at eight syllables.

  • त्वग्र्यया tvagryayā → तु + अग्र्यया

    u before a vowel

    u becomes v before a dissimilar vowel, P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • सूक्ष्मदर्शिभिः sūkṣmadarśibhiḥ

    upapada-tatpuruṣa · सूक्ष्म + दर्शिन्

    Vigraha सूक्ष्मं द्रष्टुं शीलं येषां ते

    those whose habit is to see the subtle (Śaṅkara's analysis)

Morphology

अग्र्यया
agrya, ‘at the point, foremost’, from agra with the suffix -ya. The base file of the Advaita Sharada text reads अग्न्यया here and in Śaṅkara's quotation; this edition corrects it to अग्र्यया with the other witnesses (corrections.json).
दृश्यते
Present passive of √dṛś, ‘see’, built on the weak root form dṛś-; the agent stands in the instrumental (sūkṣmadarśibhiḥ).

Anvaya

एषः आत्मा सर्वेषु भूतेषु गूढः [सन्] न प्रकाशते; सूक्ष्मदर्शिभिः तु अग्र्यया सूक्ष्मया बुद्ध्या दृश्यते ।

eṣaḥ ātmā sarveṣu bhūteṣu gūḍhaḥ san na prakāśate; sūkṣmadarśibhiḥ tu agryayā sūkṣmayā buddhyā dṛśyate |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two clauses set against each other by tu: an active clause with a negative (na prakāśate) and a passive clause (dṛśyate) whose agent is the instrumental plural sūkṣmadarśibhiḥ and whose means is the instrumental buddhyā with two adjectives.

Ambiguities

How can it be ‘seen’ if it ‘does not shine’?
Śaṅkara raises the contradiction with 1.2.22 (‘having known, the wise one does not grieve’) and answers that it does not shine for an untrained intellect: असंस्कृतबुद्धेरविज्ञेयत्वान्न प्रकाशत इत्युक्तम्; it is seen by a trained one.
Hidden by what?
Śaṅkara: by ignorance and māyā, so that no one takes it as his self — अविद्यामायाच्छन्नः; Raṅgarāmānuja: it does not shine for those who have not mastered their outer and inner faculties, अजितबाह्यान्तःकरणानां, न यथावत् प्रकाशते.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16

Anuṣṭubh, 2 × 16 (8 + 8), regular as written: gūḍho 'tmā has three syllables because the ā of ātmā is elided.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Witness error GRETIL (after Olivelle 1998): दृष्यते त्व् — GRETIL keys दृष्यते with ṣ for ś.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Psychology and inner life

What is always present is the hardest thing to notice: one hears the clock only when it stops. The verse says the self is hidden not because it is far but because it is everywhere, and that seeing it is a matter of a finer attention rather than a new object. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What is always with you that you almost never notice?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry If the self is the one who sees, in what sense can it be ‘seen’? How do Śaṅkara and Raṅgarāmānuja each answer?

A contemplation exercise For a few minutes, notice not what you are seeing but that seeing is going on.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
