Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 10 of 25

‘I know that treasure does not last’

Death says he knows that what is called treasure does not last and that the lasting is not reached by things that pass; yet he built the Nāciketa fire of perishable things and obtained ‘the lasting’ — a puzzle each commentator resolves in his own way.

5 Faithful English translation

I know [that what is called] treasure [is] impermanent; for that permanent [one] is not obtained by unstable [things]. Therefore by me the Nāciketa fire was built with impermanent materials; [by it] I have obtained the permanent.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

I know that what people call treasure does not last, and that what is lasting is not reached through things that pass. That is why I built the Nāciketa fire out of perishable things — and through it I have reached what lasts.

Key concepts कर्मन् action, ritual work अग्नि Agni, fire भोग enjoyment, use

7 Explain it to a beginner

Death speaks again, pleased with the boy, and admits something about himself. He knows that what people call treasure — the rewards that rituals and good works bring — does not last. He knows too that something lasting cannot be bought with things that pass. Yet he once built the fire altar named after Naciketas out of perishable things, and he says that through it he reached what lasts. How can both be true? The commentators answer differently. Śaṅkara says Death’s position lasts only by comparison with ours: it is long, not eternal. Raṅgarāmānuja says Death built the fire for the sake of knowledge, and it is knowledge, not the bricks, that leads to the eternal. Madhva says what was reached is the Lord, approached with a mind turned toward him. All three keep the first line: the permanent cannot be purchased.

An analogy Like paying for a ladder with coins: the ladder can lift you to a high shelf, but no number of coins becomes the sky.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does Death say he knows?

That treasure is impermanent, that the permanent is not reached by unstable things, and that he nevertheless built the Nāciketa fire with impermanent materials and obtained ‘the permanent’.

Traditional Interpretation

How can the impermanent obtain the permanent?

Śaṅkara: the treasure is the fruit of action, निधिः कर्मफललक्षणः. The truly permanent treasure, the supreme Self, is not obtained by impermanent means — तत् प्राप्यते परमात्माख्यः शेवधिः is denied of them. What Death won by the fire is his own post, heaven, permanent only by comparison: तेनाहमधिकारापन्नो नित्यं याम्यं स्थानं स्वर्गाख्यं नित्यमापेक्षिकं प्राप्तवानस्मि.

Traditional Interpretation

What did the fire win, on Raṅgarāmānuja’s reading?

The fire was built with bricks and the like for the sake of the knowledge that is the means to Brahman — ब्रह्मप्राप्तिसाधनज्ञानोद्देशेन अनित्यैः इष्टकादिद्रव्यैः नाचिकेतोऽग्निश्चितः — and so what Death obtained is knowledge, the means to the eternal fruit: नित्यफलसाधनं ज्ञानं प्राप्तवानस्मि इत्यर्थः. Brahman remains reached by knowledge alone.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: the permanent is the Lord

Madhva reads nityam as the Lord: नित्यं भगवन्तं प्राप्तवानस्मि । ध्रुवो भगवान्. The unstable means are those without devotion to him — अध्रुवैस्तद्भक्तिवर्जितैर्न प्राप्यते — and the materials are the mind and the like: द्रव्यैर्मन आदिभिः.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Madhva’s text and the vishvAsa mūla

Madhva’s mūla reads naciketaḥ (vocative) for nāciketaḥ, and the compound anityadravyaiḥ for anityair dravyaiḥ. The vishvAsa mūla writes agniḥ anityaiḥ with the visarga kept, an orthographic difference (corrections.json).

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.2.10

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The fire wins only a relative permanence, Death’s own station — नित्यमापेक्षिकं. The eternal treasure, the supreme Self, is not reached by such means.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.2.10

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The fire was built for the sake of knowledge, and knowledge is what was gained: नित्यफलसाधनं ज्ञानं प्राप्तवानस्मि इत्यर्थः. Brahman is still reached by knowledge alone.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.2.10

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The permanent obtained is the Lord — ध्रुवो भगवान् — who is not reached by means without devotion.

Where they disagree

All three keep the first line’s principle. They differ on what ‘the permanent’ of the second line is: Death’s relatively lasting station (Śaṅkara), the knowledge that leads to Brahman (Raṅgarāmānuja), or the Lord (Madhva).

Morphology

अनित्यम्
A neuter predicate with the masculine śevadhiḥ: ‘[is] an impermanent thing’. Śaṅkara restates it in the masculine: असौ अनित्यम् अनित्य इति जानामि.
नाचिकेतः
Taddhita (vṛddhi of the first syllable) from Naciketas: the fire named after the boy in 1.1.16 (‘by your name shall this fire be known’). Madhva’s text reads the vocative naciketaḥ (see ambiguities).
चितः
Past passive participle of √ci ‘pile, build up’: the fire altar is piled of bricks.
प्राप्तवान्
Past active participle in -vat of pra-√āp; with asmi it gives a past sense, ‘I have obtained’.

Anvaya

अहम् शेवधिः इति [यत् उच्यते तत्] अनित्यम् [इति] जानामि । हि अध्रुवैः तत् ध्रुवम् न हि प्राप्यते । ततः मया अनित्यैः द्रव्यैः नाचिकेतः अग्निः चितः, [तेन] नित्यम् प्राप्तवान् अस्मि ।

aham śevadhiḥ iti yat ucyate tat anityam iti jānāmi | hi adhruvaiḥ tat dhruvam na hi prāpyate | tataḥ mayā anityaiḥ dravyaiḥ nāciketaḥ agniḥ citaḥ, tena nityam prāptavān asmi |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

The first line holds a statement of knowledge (jānāmy ahaṃ … iti) and its reason (na hy … tat). The second draws a consequence (tataḥ) in two clauses: a passive one (mayā … citaḥ agniḥ, ‘the fire was built by me’) and an active periphrasis (prāptavān asmi, ‘I have obtained’). The speaker is not named in the verse.

Ambiguities

The puzzle of ‘therefore’
The first line says the permanent is not reached by impermanent means; the second says that Death, building a fire of impermanent things, obtained the permanent. Śaṅkara: the ‘permanent’ is relative, Death’s own station, which lasts long compared with human pleasures — नित्यं याम्यं स्थानं स्वर्गाख्यं नित्यमापेक्षिकं प्राप्तवानस्मि. Raṅgarāmānuja: the fire was built for the sake of the knowledge that leads to Brahman, so what was obtained is that knowledge, the means to the eternal fruit — नित्यफलसाधनं ज्ञानं प्राप्तवानस्मि इत्यर्थः. Madhva: the permanent is the Lord, and the ‘materials’ are the mind and the like directed to him — नित्यं भगवन्तं प्राप्तवानस्मि.
Who speaks, and to whom
Śaṅkara and Raṅgarāmānuja introduce the verse as Death speaking again, pleased (Śaṅkara: पुनरपि तुष्ट आह). Madhva’s text reads naciketaḥ, a vocative, for nāciketaḥ: ‘therefore, Naciketas, the fire was built by me …’ (corrections.json).

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 23

Triṣṭubh, 22 + 23 syllables: pādas of 11, 11, 11 and 12. The last pāda (anityair dravyaiḥ prāptavān asmi nityam) has twelve syllables in every witness; Madhva’s compound anityadravyaiḥ does not change the count.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: ततो मया नचिकेतश्चितोऽग्नि — Madhva’s text reads naciketaḥ, the vocative, for nāciketaḥ, ‘the Nāciketa [fire]’.
  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: नचिकेतश्चितोऽग्नि रनित्यद्रव्यैः प्राप्तवानस्मि — Madhva’s text reads anityadravyaiḥ, a compound (‘with non-eternal materials’), for anityair dravyaiḥ; the same sense.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Wealth and consumption

Some goals are reached by effort and money, and some are not. Death’s admission — I know treasure does not last — is followed by an honest account of what his own effort bought. A modern reader might take from it the habit of asking, of any large investment of time or money, what kind of thing it can buy and what kind it cannot. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What have you worked hard for that turned out to last less long than you expected?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Can something lasting be reached by means that do not last? Which of the three readings of the second line seems most honest to you, and why?

A contemplation exercise Before your next large purchase or commitment, write down what you hope it will give you, and whether it is the kind of thing that can give it.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
