Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 18 of 18

Naciketas reached Brahman

Having received from Death this knowledge and the whole rule of yoga, Naciketas reached Brahman, free of passion and of death; so does any other who knows thus the inner self.

5 Faithful English translation

Having then received this knowledge taught by Death, and the whole rule of yoga, Naciketas became one who has reached Brahman, free of passion, free of death; another too who [is] thus a knower of the inner self itself.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Naciketas received from Death this knowledge and the whole discipline of yoga, and reached Brahman, free of all stain and of death. So will any other who knows the inner self in this way.

Key concepts विद्या vidyā मृत्यु death ब्रह्मन् Brahman अध्यात्मयोग inward yoga अमृत immortality, the deathless

7 Explain it to a beginner

The Upaniṣad ends by returning to its story. Naciketas, the boy who went to Death and refused every distraction, received this knowledge and the whole discipline of yoga, and reached Brahman, free of stain and free of death. Then the promise widens: anyone else who knows the inner self in this way will reach the same. Śaṅkara says the story is told to praise the knowledge: Naciketas became free, rid of merit and demerit, desire and ignorance, and so can anyone. Raṅgarāmānuja says Naciketas reached Brahman and his true nature shone out with its eight qualities, as the Chāndogya describes, and that ‘the whole rule of yoga’ is the discipline taught from ‘when the five senses rest’. Madhva’s bhāṣya ends here with its own closing words: that Viṣṇu is supreme.

An analogy A teacher closes the lesson by pointing to the student who first learned it, and says: you can stand where he stands.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

Having received from Death this knowledge and the whole rule of yoga, Naciketas reached Brahman, free of stain and of death; so too any other who thus knows the inner self.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the story told in praise of the knowledge

The story’s conclusion is told to praise the knowledge, विद्यास्तुत्यर्थोऽयमाख्यायिकार्थोपसंहारः. Naciketas received the knowledge and the whole yoga, with its aids and fruit, समस्तं सोपकरणं सफलमित्येतत्, and reached Brahman, that is, became free, मुक्तोऽभवदित्यर्थः, free of merit and demerit, विगतधर्माधर्मः, and of desire and ignorance. Not he alone: anyone who knows the inner self as it truly is, अध्यात्ममेव निरुपचरितं प्रत्यक्स्वरूपं, becomes free of death.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: reaching Brahman, the true nature shining forth

He concludes the story: Naciketas received the knowledge of the self and the rule of yoga taught from ‘yadā pañca’, इत्यादिना उक्तं योगविधिं च, and reached Brahman as the Chāndogya describes, परं ज्योतिरुपसम्पद्य स्वेन रूपेणाभिनिष्पद्यते, becoming one in whom the eight qualities are manifest, आविर्भूतगुणाष्टकोऽभूत् इत्यर्थः. Anyone else who knows the knowledge of the inner self becomes like Naciketas.

Traditional Interpretation

How the bhāṣyas close

Madhva’s bhāṣya has no comment of its own on this mantra. After the colophon of the Upaniṣad it closes with its own conclusion, अतः सर्वोत्तमो विष्णुरिति सिद्धम्, and a verse of homage. Śaṅkara’s and Raṅgarāmānuja’s entries end with the closing śānti, which this edition keeps apart from the numbered text.

Comparative Vedānta

Free of death

The Chāndogya describes the self in the same words: य आत्मापहतपाप्मा विजरो विमृत्युर्विशोको.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.3.18

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

To reach Brahman is to be released, मुक्तोऽभवदित्यर्थः; the same awaits anyone who knows the inner self as it truly is.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.3.18

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Reaching Brahman, the self’s own nature with its eight qualities becomes manifest, आविर्भूतगुणाष्टकोऽभूत् इत्यर्थः.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Both read the verse as the fruit of the knowledge. For Śaṅkara, reaching Brahman is release, freedom from merit, demerit, desire and ignorance; for Raṅgarāmānuja it is the soul reaching Brahman and its own nature, with its eight qualities, becoming manifest. Madhva’s bhāṣya has no comment of its own on this mantra; it closes by stating that Viṣṇu is supreme.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • मृत्युप्रोक्तां नचिकेतोऽथ mṛtyuproktāṃ naciketo'tha → मृत्युप्रोक्ताम् + नचिकेतः + अथ

    m before a consonant; aḥ before a

    Final m → anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः). The visarga of -aḥ becomes u before a, giving o, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च) with P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः), and the following a is merged, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति); the avagraha marks it.

  • विद्यामेतां योगविधिं च vidyāmetāṃ yogavidhiṃ ca → विद्याम् + एताम् + योगविधिम् + च

    m before a vowel; m before a consonant

    Final m stays before a vowel and is joined to it. Final m → anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • प्राप्तो विरजोऽभूद्विमृत्युरन्योऽप्येवं prāpto virajo'bhūdvimṛtyuranyo'pyevaṃ → प्राप्तः + विरजः + अभूत् + विमृत्युः + अन्यः + अपि + एवम्

    aḥ before a voiced consonant; aḥ before a; t before a voiced sound; uḥ before a vowel; i before an unlike vowel

    -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च). The visarga of -aḥ becomes u before a, giving o, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च) with P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः), and the following a is merged, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति); the avagraha marks it. A final t becomes d before a voiced sound, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते). After i or u the visarga becomes r before a voiced sound: the s becomes ru, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः). i before an unlike vowel becomes y, P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि).

  • यो विदध्यात्ममेव yo vidadhyātmameva → यः + वित् + अध्यात्मम् + एव

    aḥ before a voiced consonant; t before a vowel; m before a vowel

    -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च). A final t becomes d before a voiced sound, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते). Final m stays before a vowel and is joined to it.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • मृत्युप्रोक्ताम् mṛtyuproktām

    tṛtīyā-tatpuruṣa · मृत्यु + प्रोक्त

    Vigraha मृत्युना प्रोक्ता

    taught by Death

  • योगविधिम् yogavidhim

    ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa · योग + विधि

    Vigraha योगस्य विधिः

    the rule, the method of yoga

Morphology

नचिकेतः
The name is an s-stem, Naciketas, whose nominative is naciketāḥ (1.1.1). Here the text has naciketo ’tha, which presupposes naciketaḥ, a nominative inflected as if from an a-stem. Śaṅkara glosses with the regular form, नचिकेताः; Madhva’s text reads nāciketaḥ (corrections.json).
वित्
A root noun from √vid, ‘knower’, in the nominative; with yaḥ: ‘who [is] a knower’. Śaṅkara takes evaṃ with it, ‘one who knows thus’, एवंवित्; Raṅgarāmānuja paraphrases ‘who knows the knowledge of the inner self’.
विरजः
Written as an a-stem nominative (viraja-, ‘free of rajas’); Śaṅkara glosses with the s-stem form virajāḥ, विगतरजाः.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

The absolutive clause mṛtyuproktāṃ … labdhvā (with two objects, vidyām etām and yogavidhiṃ kṛtsnam) leads to the main clause brahma prāpto virajo ’bhūd vimṛtyuḥ, with Naciketas as subject. The closing clause anyo ’py evaṃ yo vid adhyātmam eva has no verb of its own: ‘[becomes so]’ is supplied from the main clause, as Śaṅkara supplies it.

Ambiguities

What did Naciketas ‘become’?
Śaṅkara: he became free, released, मुक्तोऽभवदित्यर्थः, free of merit and demerit and of desire and ignorance. Raṅgarāmānuja: reaching Brahman as the Chāndogya describes (quoted by him), परं ज्योतिरुपसम्पद्य स्वेन रूपेणाभिनिष्पद्यते, he became one in whom the eight qualities are manifest, आविर्भूतगुणाष्टकोऽभूत् इत्यर्थः.
‘The inner self itself’
Śaṅkara: the inner self as it truly is, not by courtesy, अध्यात्ममेव निरुपचरितं प्रत्यक्स्वरूपं. Raṅgarāmānuja: the knowledge of the inner self, अध्यात्ममेव अध्यात्मविद्यां य: अन्योऽपि वेत्ति.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 22

Regular: 22 and 22 syllables (11 + 11 in each line).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Learning and apprenticeship

The Kaṭha began with a boy who would not be put off, and ends by saying that what he learned is open to anyone who learns it the same way. Few teachings end by handing the student’s place to the reader. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What did Naciketas do that made him able to receive this teaching?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Is ‘reaching Brahman’ becoming free, or becoming fully oneself? What does each reading make of the last line’s promise?

A contemplation exercise Reread the first verse of the Kaṭha and this last one together.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
