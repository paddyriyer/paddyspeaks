Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 3 of 18

From fear of him fire burns

From fear of him fire burns, from fear the sun gives heat; from fear Indra and Vāyu, and Death the fifth, run.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

For fear of him the fire burns and the sun shines; for fear of him Indra and the wind, and Death as the fifth, go about their work.

Key concepts अग्नि Agni, fire सूर्य the Sun वायु breath, air मृत्यु death ईश्वर the Lord

7 Explain it to a beginner

This short verse explains the last one. Why does the world keep its order? Because even the greatest powers act ‘out of fear’ of the highest reality. Fire burns, the sun gives heat, the god Indra and the wind go about their work, and even Death, the fifth, runs to do its job, all because of him. Śaṅkara says that powerful rulers of the world would not keep so strictly to their tasks unless someone stood over them, like servants who are careful when their master is watching. Raṅgarāmānuja says that ‘running’ simply means getting on with one’s own work. The same list, with fire, sun, wind and Death, also appears in the Taittirīya Upaniṣad. The point is not terror for its own sake, but that nothing in the world, however great, acts on its own authority.

An analogy Planets keep their orbits without ever being told twice.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

From fear of him fire burns and the sun gives heat; from fear Indra, Vāyu and Death, the fifth, run.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the lords of the worlds under a ruler

The verse shows how the world keeps to its order from fear of him: fire, sun, Indra, Vāyu and Death are mighty lords of the worlds, and their fixed activity would not be possible unless there were a ruler over them like one with a thunderbolt in his raised hand: न हि, ईश्वराणां लोकपालानां समर्थानां सतां नियन्ता चेद्वज्रोद्यतकरवन्न स्यात्.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: each to its own work

He explains only the word dhāvati: it means that Indra and the others engage in their own functions, धावति शब्दः इन्द्रादीनां स्वव्यापारप्रवृत्तिपरः; the rest is plain.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva

Madhva’s bhāṣya has no comment of its own on this mantra; the comment filed under 2.3.3–2.3.5 concerns the images of 2.3.5. His comment on 2.3.2 already speaks of the fear Hari gives when one oversteps one’s dharma.

Comparative Vedānta

The Taittirīya’s fear

The Taittirīya has almost the same list, opening the inquiry into bliss: भीषास्माद्वातः पवते । भीषोदेति सूर्यः, with Death running as the fifth.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.3.3

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The mighty lords of the world act in fixed order only because there is a ruler over them, the supreme Lord: भयात् भीत्या अस्य परमेश्वरस्य अग्निः तपति.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.3.3

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The gods ‘run’ in that they carry out their own functions, from fear of him: धावति शब्दः इन्द्रादीनां स्वव्यापारप्रवृत्तिपरः.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara and Raṅgarāmānuja agree that the verse describes the gods doing their work under the Lord’s rule; Śaṅkara argues from it that there must be a ruler, Raṅgarāmānuja explains only the verb. Madhva’s bhāṣya has no comment on this mantra.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • भयादस्याग्निस्तपति bhayādasyāgnistapati → भयात् + अस्य + अग्निः + तपति

    t before a vowel; a + a; iḥ before t

    The final t becomes d before a vowel, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते); a + a give ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः); before t the visarga becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः).

  • भयात्तपति bhayāttapati → भयात् + तपति

    t before t

    A final t stays before t.

  • भयादिन्द्रश्च bhayādindraśca → भयात् + इन्द्रः + च

    t before a vowel; aḥ before c

    The final t becomes d before a vowel, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते); before c the visarga becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः), and s becomes ś before c, P. 8.4.40 (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः).

  • वायुश्च vāyuśca → वायुः + च

    uḥ before c

    Before c the visarga becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः), and then ś, P. 8.4.40 (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः).

  • मृत्युर्धावति mṛtyurdhāvati → मृत्युः + धावति

    uḥ before a voiced consonant

    After i or u the visarga becomes r before a voiced sound: the s becomes ru, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

भयात्
Ablative of cause, ‘out of fear’, with the genitive asya of the one feared.
पञ्चमः
The ordinal of pañcan: Death is the fifth after fire, sun, Indra and Vāyu.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

Three clauses each opening with bhayāt. The genitive asya, ‘of him’, stated once, belongs to all three. In the last clause four subjects, Indra, Vāyu and Death (with ca … ca), take the singular verb dhāvati, agreeing with the nearest, mṛtyuḥ pañcamaḥ.

Ambiguities

What does ‘runs’ mean?
Raṅgarāmānuja: ‘runs’ means that Indra and the others engage in their own functions, धावति शब्दः इन्द्रादीनां स्वव्यापारप्रवृत्तिपरः. Śaṅkara reads the verse as the lords of the worlds keeping to their fixed work, as servants afraid of their master would: स्वामिभयभीतानामिव भृत्यानां नियता प्रवृत्तिरुपपद्यते.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 15 + 16

Counted as written, 15 and 16 syllables. The second pāda, bhayāt tapati sūryaḥ, has seven; read sūriyaḥ, with the old trisyllabic value of sūrya, it has eight. No witness writes it so.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Science and wonder

Fire, sun and wind are, for us, forces that follow laws. The verse imagines those laws as obedience. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Who or what keeps the sun to its schedule?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Śaṅkara reasons from the world’s order to a ruler over it. What do you make of that kind of argument?

A contemplation exercise Notice one thing today that does its work steadily without being thanked.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
