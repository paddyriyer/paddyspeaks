Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 21 of 25

Sitting, he travels far

Seated, he goes far; lying down, he goes everywhere. Who but me can know that god, who rejoices and does not rejoice?

5 Faithful English translation

‘Sitting, [he] goes far; lying, [he] goes everywhere. Who other than me is able to know that god, joyful and joyless?’

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘Though seated, he travels far; though lying still, he goes everywhere. Who but I can know this god, in whom joy and its absence meet?’

Key concepts आत्मन् self, Self ईश्वर the Lord देवता deity

7 Explain it to a beginner

The verse uses riddles to show how hard the self is to grasp. It sits still, yet travels far; it lies down, yet goes everywhere. It is joyful and joyless at once. Then Yama asks, who but me can know such a being? Śaṅkara explains the riddles this way: the self never moves, but it seems to move along with the mind and the senses. When the senses rest, as in sleep, it is as if it were everywhere. Raṅgarāmānuja explains that the supreme Lord is the inner self of all beings, so what they do can be said of him. Madhva says the Lord simply holds opposite qualities by his power. All agree that ordinary thinking cannot hold this together without help.

An analogy Like the moon on a cloudy night: it seems to race through the sky, while it is the clouds that move.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

That the self, though seated, goes far and, though lying, goes everywhere; it is a god of joy and of no joy; and Yama asks who other than he can know it.

Traditional Interpretation

Why the paradoxes?

Śaṅkara: to show that the self is hard to know for those who desire, since it bears contradictory properties through its adjuncts, स्थितिगतिनित्यानित्यादिविरुद्धानेकधर्मोपाधिकत्वाद्विरुद्धधर्मवान्, appearing in many forms ‘like a wish-gem’, विश्वरूप इव चिन्तामणिवत्कस्यचिदवभासते.

Comparative Vedānta

How does Raṅgarāmānuja join this to 1.2.20?

He opens by saying that without the grace named dhātuprasāda the Supreme is hard to reach, being beyond the world, धातुप्रसादशब्दितभगवदनुग्रहशून्यस्य परमात्मतत्त्वम् अत्यन्तालौकिकत्वात् दुरधिगममिति. The contradictory properties are the Supreme’s because he is the self of all.

Traditional Interpretation

And Madhva?

In one line, that the Lord does these things by his lordship, ऐश्वर्यादेव आसीनो दूरं व्रजति इत्यादि; a verse he cites says Viṣṇu by his power joins what is contradictory, ऐश्वर्याद् भगवान् विष्णुर्विरुद्धं घटयत्यसौ.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.2.21

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The motionless self seems to move and rest with its adjuncts, the mind and senses; the contradictions belong to the adjuncts: स्थितिगतिनित्यानित्यादिविरुद्धानेकधर्मोपाधिकत्वाद्विरुद्धधर्मवान्.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.2.21

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

As the self of all, the Supreme has the contradictory properties through the individual selves, जीवद्वारा तत्र सन्ति; he is known only by those his grace has favoured.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.2.21

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The Lord holds opposite properties by his own power: ऐश्वर्यादेव आसीनो दूरं व्रजति इत्यादि.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara attributes the contradictions to adjuncts of a self that is itself beyond them; Raṅgarāmānuja to the Supreme as the self of all beings; Madhva to the Lord’s inconceivable power.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • आसीनो दूरं व्रजति āsīno dūraṃ vrajati → आसीनः + दूरम् + व्रजति

    visarga; anusvāra

    aḥ becomes o before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च); final m → anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • शयानो याति śayāno yāti → शयानः + याति

    visarga sandhi

    As above: aḥ → o before y, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

  • कस्तं मदामदं देवं kastaṃ madāmadaṃ devaṃ → कः + तम् + मदामदम् + देवम्

    visarga before t; anusvāra

    Visarga before t becomes s: P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः); each final m → anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • मदन्यो ज्ञातुमर्हति madanyo jñātumarhati → मदन्यः + ज्ञातुम् + अर्हति

    visarga; m before a vowel

    aḥ → o before j, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च); final m stays before a vowel.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • मदामदम् madāmadam

    dvandva used as a bahuvrīhi · मद + अमद

    Vigraha मदः च अमदः च यस्य सः

    one who has joy and no joy

  • मदन्यः madanyaḥ

    pañcamī-tatpuruṣa · मद् + अन्य

    Vigraha मत् अन्यः

    other than me

Morphology

आसीनः
Present middle participle of ās ‘sit’, with the irregular -īna for -āna: ‘sitting’. शयानः is the present middle participle of śī ‘lie’.
मदामदम्
mada ‘joy, exhilaration’ and amada ‘its absence’ joined: a being of whom opposites are said. Śaṅkara glosses both ways, with joy and without.
मदन्यः
mad-, the stem of the first person pronoun in compounds, + anya ‘other’: ‘other than I’. The speaker is Yama.

Anvaya

[आत्मा] आसीनः दूरम् व्रजति । शयानः सर्वतः याति । तम् मदामदम् देवम् मदन्यः कः ज्ञातुम् अर्हति ॥

ātmā āsīnaḥ dūram vrajati | śayānaḥ sarvataḥ yāti | tam madāmadam devam madanyaḥ kaḥ jñātum arhati ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two paradoxes in parallel, each a participle with a verb of going (āsīno … vrajati; śayāno yāti), with the subject understood from 1.2.20. A rhetorical question follows, kas … arhati, with tam … devam as the object of jñātum.

Ambiguities

How can the one who sits travel?
Śaṅkara: the self stays as it is, but takes on the movements of the mind and senses, its adjuncts, and so seems to travel; when the senses rest (‘lying down’) only general awareness remains, and it is as though it went everywhere: करणानामुपशमः शयनं. Raṅgarāmānuja: properties that would conflict elsewhere belong to the Supreme through the individual selves of which he is the self: इतरत्र विरुद्धतया प्रतीयमानाऽपि आसीनत्वदूरगन्तृत्वादिधर्माः जीवद्वारा तत्र सन्ति. Madhva: by his lordship alone, ऐश्वर्यादेव आसीनो दूरं व्रजति इत्यादि.
‘Who but me?’
Śaṅkara: only a subtle-minded learned one like ourselves can know him, अस्मदादेरेव सूक्ष्मबुद्धेः पण्डितस्य विज्ञेयोऽयमात्मा. Raṅgarāmānuja: who but people like me, favoured by the Supreme’s grace, परमात्म प्रसादानुगृहीतमादृशजनादन्यः को वा ज्ञातेत्यर्थः — the grace he found in dhātuprasāda in 1.2.20.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Holding contradictions

People often have to hold two true things that do not fit: grief and gratitude, stillness and change. The verse treats such contradiction not as an error but as a sign that a category has been outgrown. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Can you think of something that seems to move only because you are moving?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Śaṅkara puts the contradictions in the adjuncts, Raṅgarāmānuja in the Supreme’s relation to all beings, Madhva in his power. Which account explains ‘who but me’ best?

A contemplation exercise Sit still for a few minutes and notice how far the mind travels while the body does not move.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
