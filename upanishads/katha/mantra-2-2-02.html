Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 2 of 15

The swan in the bright

One being is named in every seat and birth — sky, air, altar, house, men, gods, waters, earth — and is the true, the great.

The swan seated in the bright, the Vasu seated in the mid-air, the invoker seated on the altar, the guest seated in the house; seated in men, seated among the excellent, seated in the true, seated in the sky; born of the waters, born of the earth, born of the rite, born of the mountains; the true, the great.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

He is the swan in the shining sky, the giver of dwelling in the air between, the priest at the altar, the guest in the house; he dwells in men, in the gods, in what is true, in the sky; he is born from the waters, the earth, the rite and the mountains: the true, the great.

Key concepts हंस haṃsa, the swan आत्मन् self, Self ऋत the right; cosmic and moral order ब्रह्मन् Brahman सूर्य the Sun अग्नि Agni, fire अतिथि guest

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse is a list, and the list is the point. One being is named again and again in different places: the bright sky, the air between earth and heaven, the altar, the house where a guest is welcomed, human beings, the gods, the sky. Then it is named as born from water, earth, the rite and the mountains. The verse ends by calling all of it true and great. The commentators read it as showing that the self that lives in this body is not confined to it: Śaṅkara says it lives in every city, every body, and that there is finally one self of the world. Raṅgarāmānuja says everything has Brahman as its self; Madhva hears each name as a name of the Lord.

An analogy Like one sunlight called by many names — dawn on the river, warmth on a wall, a glint on glass — though it is the same light.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

One being is named as the swan in the bright, the Vasu in the air, the priest at the altar, the guest in the house, and as dwelling in men, gods, truth and sky; he is born of waters, earth, rite and mountains, and is the true, the great.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: one self of the world

The self is not in one city only but in all, स तु नैकपुरवर्त्येवात्मा किं तर्हि सर्वपुरवर्ती. He reads haṃsa as the one who goes, हंसः हन्ति गच्छतीति, and glosses each seat in turn. His conclusion: in every way there is one self of the world and no plurality of selves, सर्वथाप्येक एवात्मा जगतः, नात्मभेद इति मन्त्रार्थः.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: all has Brahman as its self

The verse confirms the Supreme as the self of all, पुनरपि अस्य सर्वात्मतामेव द्रढयति. The sun, the wind, the priest, the guest, the inner self in the highest place and all born things are, together, the true and the great, अपरिच्छिन्नसत्यरूपब्रह्मात्मकमित्यर्थः, having Brahman as their self.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: every name names Hari

In verses of his own he derives each epithet as a name of Hari: haṃsa because he is free of faults, हंस इत्युच्यते तस्माद्वायुस्थश्शुचिषत्स्मृतः; vyoman is Śrī in whom the world is woven; ṛtam because the Vedas speak of him chiefly; bṛhat because his qualities are full, बृहत्पूर्णगुणत्वाच्च स एव पुरुषोत्तमः.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

A Vedic verse in an Upaniṣad

The verse is in the old Vedic style, with root-noun compounds in -sad (‘seated in’) and -jā (‘born of’), and it is also found in the Ṛgveda (4.40.5), where it is spoken of a bird of the sky. The Kaṭha sets it among its own verses about the self, and Śaṅkara says expressly that reading it of the sun does not conflict with reading it of Brahman.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.2.2

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Each name in the verse is a name of Hari, the highest person, स एव पुरुषोत्तमः.

Where they disagree

All three read the verse as naming one being present everywhere. For Śaṅkara this shows there is no plurality of selves; for Raṅgarāmānuja that all things have Brahman as their self; for Madhva that every name belongs to Hari, who remains distinct from what he indwells.

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • शुचिषद्वसु śuciṣadvasu → शुचिषत् + वसुः

    t before a voiced consonant

  • वसुरन्तरिक्षसद् vasurantarikṣasad → वसुः + अन्तरिक्षसत्

    uḥ before a vowel

    The final s of vasus becomes r, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः), and the r stays before a vowel.

  • अन्तरिक्षसद्धोता antarikṣasaddhotā → अन्तरिक्षसत् + होता

    The y from the visarga, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), is dropped before a vowel, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य), and the vowels stay apart; so also ऋतजा अद्रिजा and अद्रिजा ऋतं.

Morphology

शुचिषत्
The root √sad, ‘sit’, as a root noun at the end of a compound, nominative -sat. Its s is retroflex after i, ṛ and u (śuciṣat, vediṣat, nṛṣat), dental after a (antarikṣasat, varasat, ṛtasat, vyomasat, duroṇasat).
अब्जाः
The root √jan, ‘be born’, as a root noun -jā at the end of a compound, nominative singular -jāḥ; so also gojāḥ, ṛtajāḥ, adrijāḥ. ab- is the stem ap, ‘water’, before j.
होता
Nominative singular of hotṛ, the priest who invokes the gods (√hu or √hve).

Syntax

A string of nominatives with no finite verb: each names one subject in a different seat or birth. The last two, ṛtam and bṛhat, are neuter and stand as the predicate: all of this is the true, the great. The commentators supply the subject, the self or the Lord.

Ambiguities

Whom does the verse describe?
Śaṅkara: the one self that dwells in every city, not only in one body, स तु नैकपुरवर्त्येवात्मा किं तर्हि सर्वपुरवर्ती; even if the verse is about the sun, the sun is the self, so there is no conflict, यदाप्यादित्य एव मन्त्रेणोच्यते तदाप्यात्मस्वरूपत्वमादित्यस्याङ्गीकृतमिति ब्रह्मणि व्याख्यानेऽप्यविरोधः. Raṅgarāmānuja: it confirms that the Supreme is the self of all, पुनरपि अस्य सर्वात्मतामेव द्रढयति. Madhva: every name is a name of Hari.
Seats and births
Śaṅkara reads each epithet in turn: the sun in heaven, the wind in the air, the fire on the altar, Soma in the jar; then waters, earth, rite and mountains as the forms in which he is born. Raṅgarāmānuja reads śuci as the hot season and vyoman as the highest place; Madhva reads vyoman as Śrī, व्योतं जगदिदं यस्यां सा व्योम श्रीरुदाहृता.

Metre

irregular · syllables counted as written: 24 + 25

Counted as written, 24 and 25 syllables: two long lines of the old Vedic kind, not the śloka; the verse is also a Ṛgveda verse (RV 4.40.5).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Purpose and meaning

A list of places — sky, altar, guest-room, mountain — invites us to notice the same presence in very different settings, the solemn and the ordinary alike. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Which of the places in the verse surprised you most?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Śaṅkara says the verse can be read of the sun without conflict. Why does he need to say so?

Further notes

Uncertainties recorded

  • variant The vishvAsa mūla keys वसुरान्तरिक्षसद्, a witness error, and writes हँसः and antarikṣasad hotā unjoined, spellings only (corrections.json).
  • interpretation Whom the verse names: the one self of the world (Śaṅkara), Brahman as the self of all (Raṅgarāmānuja) or Hari (Madhva); several epithets are read differently (śuci, atithi, vyoman).
