Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 7 of 15

Aditi, who arises with breath

Aditi, made of the deities, who comes to be with breath and, having entered the cave, dwells there and was born with the elements — this is that.

5 Faithful English translation

‘[He who sees] Aditi, who comes to be with breath, made of the deities, dwelling, having entered the cave, who was born with the elements — this, truly, is that.’

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘Aditi, the eater, made of all the gods, who comes into being as breath, who has entered the cave of the heart and dwells there, and who was born together with the elements: this is that.’

Key concepts अदिति Aditi, ‘the eater’ प्राण breath, life-force देवता deity गुहा the cave हिरण्यगर्भ Hiraṇyagarbha, ‘golden embryo’ जीव individual self

7 Explain it to a beginner

Aditi is an old name in the Vedas, the boundless mother of the gods. Here she is described as one who comes to be through breath, who is made of all the gods, who has entered the cave of the heart and lives there, and who was born together with the elements. The commentators read the name through a play on words: a-diti can be heard as linked to 'eating' (ad), so Aditi is 'the eater'. For Śaṅkara she is the first cosmic being, who 'eats' — that is, experiences — all the objects of the senses. For Raṅgarāmānuja, following the great teacher he calls the Bhāṣyakāra, she is the individual soul, who 'eats' the results of its actions. For Madhva she is Viṣṇu himself, born again and again in many forms. And again the refrain: this is that.

An analogy One tenant in many houses, called by a different name on every door.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

Aditi, made of the deities, who arises with breath and, having entered the cave, dwells there, born with the elements — this is that.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: Aditi as the first-born

Aditi is the one made of all the deities, सर्वदेवतामयी सर्वदेवतात्मिका, who arises from the supreme Brahman as breath, in the form of Hiraṇyagarbha, प्राणेन हिरण्यगर्भरूपेण परस्माद्ब्रह्मणः सम्भवति; she dwells in the cave like the first-born of 2.1.6.

Comparative Vedānta

Raṅgarāmānuja and the Bhāṣya

He notes that the Bhāṣyakāra explained this verse under the sūtra on ‘the two in the cave’, भगवता भाष्यकृता व्याख्यातः, and quotes him: Aditi is the individual self, who eats the fruits of action, lives with the breath, प्राणेन सह वर्तते, and is born in many forms, as god and the rest, देवादिरूपेण विविधा जायते. These are words of the Bhāṣya as quoted by Raṅgarāmānuja; Rāmānuja wrote no commentary on the Kaṭha.

Comparative Vedānta

Madhva: Viṣṇu in his forms

Madhva cites a verse (with इति च, no source named) in which Aditi is Viṣṇu, highest of the gods, who brought himself forth in the cave in many forms, मत्स्यकूर्मादिरूपेण, age after age; devatāmayī means ‘highest of the deities’, देवतामयी देवतोत्तमा.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Hiatus and readings

The base leaves sambhavati aditiḥ unjoined, keeping each pāda at eight; other witnesses join it. The accusative tiṣṭhantīm has no verb of its own here.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.1.7

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Aditi is Hiraṇyagarbha, the eater of all objects, शब्दादीनामदनात् अदितिः, arising from Brahman; seeing her in the heart is seeing Brahman.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.1.7

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Following the Bhāṣya he quotes, Aditi is the individual self, eater of the fruits of action, कर्मफलानि अत्तीति; ‘this is that’ means it has Brahman as its self, तदात्मकम् इत्यर्थः.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.1.7

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Aditi is Viṣṇu, called so from eating, अदनादितिर्विष्णुर्यः प्राणसहितः स्थितः (in a verse he cites), who takes many forms among beings.

Where they disagree

One name, three referents: the cosmic first-born (Śaṅkara), the individual self (Raṅgarāmānuja, after the Bhāṣya on BS 1.2.11), and Viṣṇu himself (Madhva). All three hear ‘Aditi’ as ‘eater’.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • अदितिर्देवतामयी aditirdevatāmayī → अदितिः + देवतामयी

    visarga → r

    The s of aditiḥ becomes ru, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः), heard as r before a voiced sound after i.

  • तिष्ठन्तीं या tiṣṭhantīṃ yā → तिष्ठन्तीम् + या

    anusvāra

    Final m → anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • भूतेभिर्व्यजायत bhūtebhirvyajāyata → भूतेभिः + व्यजायत

    visarga → r

    As above, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः).

Junctions outside the general rules

  • सम्भवति · अदितिः — hiatus at the pāda boundary: sambhavati aditiḥ: i before a is not turned into y. The words end the first pāda and begin the second, and both are counted (8 + 8). The base and Śaṅkara’s quotation of the verse print the vowels apart; the vishvAsa mūla and Madhva’s text write sambhavaty aditiḥ (corrections.json), which would leave the half-line with 15.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

देवतामयी
Feminine of devatāmaya, ‘consisting of the deities’, with the suffix -maya.
तिष्ठन्तीम्
Accusative, with no verb in the verse to govern it: Śaṅkara supplies the seeing of 2.1.6. The mūla printed with Raṅgarāmānuja’s commentary has the nominative tiṣṭhantī, and the Bhāṣya he quotes explains it so.
व्यजायत
Imperfect middle of jan with vi: ‘was born variously, in many forms’.

Anvaya

या देवतामयी अदितिः प्राणेन सम्भवति, या भूतेभिः [सह] व्यजायत, [ताम्] गुहाम् प्रविश्य तिष्ठन्तीम् [यः पश्यति सः एतत् एव पश्यति] । एतत् वै तत् ॥

yā devatāmayī aditiḥ prāṇena sambhavati, yā bhūtebhiḥ saha vyajāyata, tām guhām praviśya tiṣṭhantīm yaḥ paśyati saḥ etat eva paśyati | etat vai tat ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two relative clauses (yā … sambhavati; yā … vyajāyata) about Aditi, with the accusative guhāṃ praviśya tiṣṭhantīm depending on the ‘seeing’ of 2.1.6, which Śaṅkara supplies. The refrain closes the verse.

Ambiguities

Who is Aditi?
Śaṅkara: Hiraṇyagarbha as breath, made of all the gods, called Aditi from ‘eating’ sound and the other objects, शब्दादीनामदनात् अदितिः. Raṅgarāmānuja, quoting the Bhāṣyakāra on BS 1.2.11: the individual self, which ‘eats’ the fruits of action, कर्मफलानि अत्तीति. Madhva, in a verse he cites: Viṣṇu, called Aditi from eating, अदनादितिर्विष्णुर्यः प्राणसहितः स्थितः.
‘This is that’
Raṅgarāmānuja: ‘that’ means ‘having that as its self’, तदात्मकम् इत्यर्थः — the individual self is ‘that’ because Brahman is its self, as the Gītā’s ‘know me as the field-knower’ is explained to mean ‘having me as its self’. Śaṅkara: this Aditi is the Brahman under discussion.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16 + 4

Two half-lines of 16 syllables (four pādas of eight, counting sambhavati aditiḥ unjoined), then the refrain etad vai tat, four syllables.

The witnesses differ here only in spelling (listed in section 14).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Interpretation

Three careful readers take one name three ways, each with reasons. The verse is a reminder that a text can hold more than one reading without any of them being careless. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What in you ‘eats’ — takes in and lives on — the world?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why might all three commentators hear ‘Aditi’ as ‘the eater’, and still disagree about who she is?

A contemplation exercise At your next meal, notice how much of the day you spend ‘eating’ in the wider sense: taking in sights, words and news.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
