Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 16 of 29

A boon beyond the three: the fire named for Naciketas

Pleased, Death gives an extra boon: the fire will bear Naciketas’s name, and he is given a many-formed chain.

5 Faithful English translation

‘The great-souled one, being pleased, said to him: “Here, now, I give you a boon again: this fire will be [known] by your name indeed. And take this chain of many forms.”’

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘Delighted, the great-hearted Death said to him: “I give you one more gift here and now. This fire will be called by your name. And take this chain of many colours.”’

Key concepts अग्नि Agni, fire मृत्यु death वर boon

7 Explain it to a beginner

Death is so pleased with his student that he adds a gift that was not asked for. From now on, he says, this fire ritual will be known by your name — the Nāciketa fire. And he gives the boy a chain or necklace of many forms. The gift of a name means that whenever people perform the ritual they will remember the boy who learned it from Death. The chain is a prize, the kind a teacher gives a brilliant pupil. Some commentators also read it as a sign of the many paths of action now open to him.

An analogy Like a professor who names a theorem after the student who first understood it, and hands over a medal as well.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What is the extra boon?

Two things: the fire will be called by Naciketas’s name, and he is given a many-formed chain.

Traditional Interpretation

Why a fourth boon?

Śaṅkara: Death, seeing the pupil’s fitness, gives a fourth boon out of pleasure: शिष्यस्य योग्यतां पश्यन्प्रीयमाणः प्रीतिमनुभवन् … वरं तव चतुर्थम्. Raṅgarāmānuja agrees: पुनः चतुर्थं वरं ददानि.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Madhva read it?

His comment on 1.1.12–18 is one passage, filed at 1.1.18. He glosses ‘of many forms’ as golden, अनेकरूपां सुवर्णमयीम्, and cites a verse he attributes to the Pādma: Yama, pleased by the repetition, gave the fire its name and a golden neck-chain: यमोऽनुवादसन्तुष्टो वह्नेस्तन्नामतामपि । शृङ्कां स्वर्णमयीं चैव कण्ठमालामदाद् विभुः.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.16

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

A fourth boon given from pleasure; Śaṅkara’s second sense of the chain — the way of action with its many fruits — keeps the verse within the realm of ritual: अन्यदपि कर्मविज्ञानमनेकफलहेतुत्वात्स्वीकुर्वित्यर्थः.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.1.16

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The plain sense: the fire will be known as the Nāciketa, नाचिकेतः इति प्रसिद्धो भविता, and the chain is a gift.

Dvaita · Madhva (citing the Pādma), Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya on 1.1.12–18

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The same plain sense; the chain is a golden necklace, शृङ्कां स्वर्णमयीं चैव कण्ठमालामदाद् विभुः.

Where they disagree

No doctrinal disagreement; only Śaṅkara offers the second, figurative sense of the chain.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • तमब्रवीत्प्रीयमाणो tamabravītprīyamāṇo → तम् + अब्रवीत् + प्रीयमाणः

    m before a vowel; t before p

    Final m stays before a vowel; final t before p stays.

  • प्रीयमाणो महात्मा prīyamāṇo mahātmā → प्रीयमाणः + महात्मा

    visarga before a voiced consonant

    aḥ before a voiced consonant becomes o: P. 6.1.114 (हशि च) with P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • तवेहाद्य tavehādya → तव + इह + अद्य

    vowel sandhi

    a + i → e, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः); a + a → ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • तवैव tavaiva → तव + एव

    vowel sandhi (vṛddhi)

    a + e → ai: P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि).

  • भवितायमग्निः bhavitāyamagniḥ → भविता + अयम् + अग्निः

    vowel sandhi; m before a vowel

    ā + a → ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः); m stays before a vowel.

  • चेमामनेकरूपाम् cemāmanekarūpām → च + इमाम् + अनेकरूपाम्

    vowel sandhi (guṇa)

    a + i → e: P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • महात्मा mahātmā

    bahuvrīhi · महत् + आत्मन्

    Vigraha महान् आत्मा यस्य सः

    one whose self is great, magnanimous

  • अनेकरूपाम् anekarūpām

    bahuvrīhi · अनेक + रूप

    Vigraha अनेकानि रूपाणि यस्याः सा

    having many forms, many-coloured

Morphology

प्रीयमाणः
Present participle of the passive stem of prī ‘be pleased’ (prīyate): ‘being pleased’.
ददामि
Present of dā. Madhva’s text and Raṅgarāmānuja read ददानि (imperative-subjunctive 1 sg., ‘let me give’).
सृङ्काम्
A rare word. Śaṅkara gives two senses: a jewelled chain, and, alternatively, a good path, the way of action: यद्वा, सृङ्काम् अकुत्सितां गतिं कर्ममयीं गृहाण.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

A narrative frame (tam abravīt prīyamāṇo mahātmā) and Death’s speech: the extra boon (varaṃ tavehādya dadāmi bhūyaḥ) and its content in two parts, the naming of the fire and the gift of the chain, with the imperative gṛhāṇa.

Ambiguities

What is the sṛṅkā?
Śaṅkara: a tinkling jewelled chain, or alternatively the good way of action: यद्वा, सृङ्काम् अकुत्सितां गतिं कर्ममयीं गृहाण. Raṅgarāmānuja: a tinkling string of jewels, शब्दवती रत्नमालां स्वीकुरु. Madhva: golden, अनेकरूपां सुवर्णमयीम् (see the commentary).
dadāmi or dadāni?
Madhva’s text and Raṅgarāmānuja’s read dadāni, ‘let me give’; the base and the other witnesses dadāmi, ‘I give’ (corrections.json).

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 23

Counted as written, 22 and 23 syllables: the second line (tavaiva nāmnā bhavitāyam agniḥ sṛṅkāṃ cemām anekarūpāṃ gṛhāṇa) has one syllable more than the norm.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: वरं तवेहाद्य ददानि — Madhva’s text reads dadāni, ‘let me give’, for dadāmi, ‘I give’.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Recognition and generosity

Death gives more than was asked, because the student learned well. Generous teachers and leaders still do this: they name the work after the one who made it possible. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Has anyone ever given you more than you asked for, because of how you worked?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry What is the difference between a reward and a recognition?

A contemplation exercise Today, credit someone by name for something they made possible.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
