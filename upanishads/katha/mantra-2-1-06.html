Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 6 of 15

The first-born before the waters

The one who sees the first-born — born of tapas, born before the waters — dwelling in the cave with the elements, sees this. This is that.

5 Faithful English translation

‘[He] who [sees] the one born first from tapas, who was born before the waters, dwelling, having entered the cave, with the elements — who saw [him sees this]. This, truly, is that.’

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘Whoever sees the first-born — born of tapas before the waters came to be, who has entered the cave of the heart and dwells there with the elements — sees this. This is that.’

Key concepts हिरण्यगर्भ Hiraṇyagarbha, ‘golden embryo’ तपस् austerity; disciplined effort गुहा the cave ईश्वर the Lord ब्रह्मन् Brahman

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse is one of the hardest in the Kaṭha, and the old commentators read its grammar differently. All agree it speaks of the 'first-born': the first being to appear at the start of creation, before even the waters, born from the creative power the Upaniṣads call tapas — literally 'heat', the concentrated effort of creation. This first-born is said to have entered the 'cave', the inner space of the heart, and to dwell there along with the elements that make up a living body. For Śaṅkara the point is that whoever sees this cosmic first-being dwelling in the heart sees the very Brahman the teaching is about. For Raṅgarāmānuja and Madhva the first-born is the creator god Brahmā, and the one who 'saw' or brought him forth is the Supreme Lord. The refrain closes it again: this is that.

An analogy A family tree traced back to its first ancestor, only to find that same ancestor alive in each descendant.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

Whoever sees the first-born, born of tapas before the waters, dwelling in the cave with the elements, sees this; this is that.

Traditional Interpretation

The first-born in the heart

Śaṅkara: the verse shows that the one described as the inward self and the Lord is the self of all, स सर्वात्मेत्येतद्दर्शयति. The first-born is Hiraṇyagarbha, who after producing the bodies of gods and others entered the cave of all beings, the space of the heart, देवादिशरीराण्युत्पाद्य सर्वप्राणिगुहां हृदयाकाशं प्रविश्य.

Comparative Vedānta

Raṅgarāmānuja: Brahmā and the Supreme

He reads the first-born as Brahmā, born from the waters in the account of the Manusmṛti he quotes, तस्मिन् जज्ञे स्वयं ब्रह्मा सर्वलोकपितामहः, and the one who saw him as the Supreme, who looked on him to make him creator.

Comparative Vedānta

Madhva: Viṣṇu who produced Brahmā

Madhva cites a verse (with इति च, no source named) in which Viṣṇu, before the waters, produced the four-faced first-born and sees himself in the heart with all beings, यः पश्यति सदा विष्णुस्स एष हृदि संस्थितः. Unlike Indra and the other gods, born again from Kaśyapa, Brahmā is not born of the Lord in that way, न तथा भगवतो ब्रह्मा; ajāyata means ‘caused to be born’.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Whoever sees Hiraṇyagarbha, the first-born, dwelling in the heart sees the very Brahman under discussion, यत्तत्प्रकृतं ब्रह्म: the self of all.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.1.6

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The first-born is Brahmā; the Supreme saw him in the heart, endowed with body and senses, and made him creator, अयं सकलजगत्स्रष्टा स्यात् इति कटाक्षेण ऐक्षत इत्यर्थः.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.1.6

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Viṣṇu produced the four-faced first-born before the waters, पूर्वं यो जनयामास पूर्वजातं चतुर्मुखम् (a verse he cites), and dwells in the heart; ajāyata is causative.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara makes the seer a seeker and the seen the cosmic first-born, finally Brahman; Raṅgarāmānuja and Madhva make the first-born Brahmā and the subject the Lord who looked on him or produced him. The verse’s two relatives allow both.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • तपसो जातमद्भ्यः tapaso jātamadbhyaḥ → तपसः + जातम् + अद्भ्यः

    aḥ → o; m before a vowel

    The ru of -aḥ becomes u before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च), and a + u → o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः); final m stays before a vowel.

  • पूर्वमजायत pūrvamajāyata → पूर्वम् + अजायत

    m before a vowel

    Final m stays m before a vowel; before a consonant it becomes anusvāra, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • यो भूतेभिर्व्यपश्यत yo bhūtebhirvyapaśyata → यः + भूतेभिः + व्यपश्यत

    aḥ → o; visarga → r

    yaḥ → yo before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च); the s of bhūtebhiḥ becomes ru, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः), heard as r before a voiced sound.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

भूतेभिः
Vedic instrumental plural in -ebhiḥ for classical bhūtaiḥ (so Śaṅkara glosses it).
अजायत
Imperfect middle of jan ‘be born’. Madhva takes it in a causative sense, ‘caused to be born’, as with an unexpressed causative suffix, अन्तर्णीतणिच्: the Lord who produced the first-born.
व्यपश्यत
Imperfect middle of paś (dṛś) with vi; Śaṅkara takes the past as present, ‘who sees’.
अद्भ्यः
Ablative plural of ap ‘water’ (stem ad- before bh), the standard of comparison with pūrvam, ‘before the waters’.

Anvaya

यः तपसः पूर्वम् जातम्, [यः] अद्भ्यः पूर्वम् अजायत, [तम्] गुहाम् प्रविश्य भूतेभिः [सह] तिष्ठन्तम् यः व्यपश्यत, [सः एतत् एव पश्यति] । एतत् वै तत् ॥

yaḥ tapasaḥ pūrvam jātam, yaḥ adbhyaḥ pūrvam ajāyata, tam guhām praviśya bhūtebhiḥ saha tiṣṭhantam yaḥ vyapaśyata, saḥ etat eva paśyati | etat vai tat ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two relative clauses, each opening with yaḥ, and a refrain; the construction is loose and the commentators distribute the relatives differently (see ambiguities). jātam and tiṣṭhantam are the accusatives of what is seen; bhūtebhiḥ marks accompaniment.

Ambiguities

Who is born, and who sees?
Śaṅkara: the seeker, यः कश्चिन्मुमुक्षुः, who sees the first-born Hiraṇyagarbha, उत्पन्नं हिरण्यगर्भम्, born of Brahman before the five elements, dwelling in the heart; whoever sees so sees the Brahman under discussion, स एतदेव पश्यति — यत्तत्प्रकृतं ब्रह्म. Raṅgarāmānuja: the four-faced Brahmā is the first-born, and the one who ‘saw’ him is the Supreme, who looked on him meaning him to be creator of the world, अयं सकलजगत्स्रष्टा स्यात् इति कटाक्षेण ऐक्षत इत्यर्थः. Madhva: Viṣṇu produced Brahmā before the waters and before the Śiva called tapas, in a verse he cites, पूर्वं यो जनयामास पूर्वजातं चतुर्मुखम्.
What is ‘tapas’ here?
Śaṅkara: Brahman, whose tapas is knowledge, ज्ञानादिलक्षणाद्ब्रह्मण इत्येतत्. Raṅgarāmānuja: by mere resolve, संकल्पमात्रादेव. In Madhva’s cited verse tapas is a name of Śiva, तपोनाम्नः शिवादपि.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16 + 4

Two half-lines of 16 syllables, four pādas of eight, then the refrain etad vai tat, four syllables.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Origins

The verse ties the beginning of everything to what dwells in each heart now; the question of origins becomes a question about what is present. (A modern reflection.)
