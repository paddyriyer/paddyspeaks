Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 20 of 29

The third boon: does a man exist after death?

Naciketas asks his third boon: there is doubt about a man who has died — some say he is, some say he is not; taught by you, I would know this.

5 Faithful English translation

‘This doubt which [exists] concerning a man who has departed — “He is,” some [say], and “He is not,” some — this may I know, taught by you. Of the boons this is the third boon.’

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘When a man dies there is this doubt: some say he still exists, others say he does not. Teach me, and let me know the truth of it. That is my third wish.’

Key concepts आत्मन् self, Self मृत्यु death विद्या vidyā मोक्ष liberation वर boon

7 Explain it to a beginner

This is the question the whole Upaniṣad answers. When a person dies, Naciketas says, people disagree: some say he still exists, some say he does not. Teach me the truth, he asks; that is my third wish. It is the oldest question there is, and the boy asks it of Death himself. The commentators agree that this is the turning point: the first two wishes were about family and ritual, this one is about what we really are. Śaṅkara notes that neither perception nor reasoning can settle it, so it must be taught.

An analogy Like asking the one person who has seen the other side of a locked door to say what is there.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does Naciketas ask?

Whether a man who has died still exists: some say he does, some that he does not. He asks Death to teach him the truth, as the third boon.

Traditional Interpretation

Why is this the turning point?

Śaṅkara: what the first two boons point to can be known from the ritual portion of the Veda, but not the true nature of the self: नात्मतत्त्वविषययाथात्म्यविज्ञानम्. So the rest of the Upaniṣad teaches the knowledge of the one Brahman-self that ends ignorance. Neither perception nor inference can decide the question: अत्र चास्माकं न प्रत्यक्षेण नाप्यनुमानेन निर्णयविज्ञानम्.

Comparative Vedānta

What is the doubt about, for Raṅgarāmānuja?

He reports the Śrībhāṣya (on Brahmasūtra 1.2.9): the question concerns release, न शरीरवियोगमात्राभिप्रायम् अपि तु सर्वबन्धविनिर्मोक्षाभिप्रायम्, and lists rival views of release — annihilation, the end of ignorance, a stone-like isolation, the self’s merging into the Supreme — against the Vedānta of the Lord’s qualified unity.

Traditional Interpretation

And for Madhva?

‘The departed man’ is the released soul: the wise say the Lord is there as its controller, the ignorant that he is not: प्रेते मुक्ते मनुष्ये नियामकत्वेन भगवानस्तीति ज्ञानिनो वदन्ति । नास्तीत्यज्ञाः. Madhva argues from the answer the Upaniṣad later gives, एतद्वै तत्, that the question is about the Lord in the released: मुक्ते स्थितो भगवान् पृच्छ्यते इति सिद्धम्.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.20

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The question is whether a self exists beyond body and mind: अस्ति शरीरेन्द्रियमनोबुद्धिव्यतिरिक्तो देहान्तरसम्बन्ध्यात्मा. The answer, unfolded in the rest of the Upaniṣad, is the self’s identity with Brahman, which only teaching can give.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, reporting the Śrībhāṣya, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.1.20

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The doubt concerns the nature of the released self, not mere survival: न शरीरवियोगमात्राभिप्रायम् अपि तु सर्वबन्धविनिर्मोक्षाभिप्रायम्. Release is the self’s natural experience of the Supreme, of whom it is the body.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.20

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The third boon is knowledge of the Lord who abides as controller in the released soul: तस्य नियामकस्य स्वरूपं यथावदहं विद्याम्.

Where they disagree

What the doubt is about is itself disputed. Śaṅkara: the existence of a self beyond the body. The Śrībhāṣya (via Raṅgarāmānuja): the nature of the released self. Madhva: the Lord in the released self. All agree that the answer must be taught, not inferred.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • येयम् yeyam → या + इयम्

    vowel sandhi (guṇa)

    ā + i → e: P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • मनुष्ये अस्तीत्येके manuṣye astītyeke → मनुष्ये + अस्ति + इति + एके

    hiatus; i before a vowel

    e before a is left unjoined (see the junction below); i before a dissimilar vowel becomes y, P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि).

  • नायमस्तीति चैके nāyamastīti caike → न + अयम् + अस्ति + इति + च + एके

    vowel sandhi

    a + a → ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः); i + i → ī, the same rule; a + e → ai, P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि).

  • एतद्विद्यामनुशिष्टस्त्वयाहम् etadvidyāmanuśiṣṭastvayāham → एतत् + विद्याम् + अनुशिष्टः + त्वया + अहम्

    consonant and visarga sandhi

    t before v becomes d, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते); visarga before t becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः); ā + a → ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • वराणामेष वरस्तृतीयः varāṇāmeṣa varastṛtīyaḥ → वराणाम् + एषः + वरः + तृतीयः

    eṣaḥ before a consonant; visarga before t

    eṣaḥ loses its visarga before a consonant, P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि); visarga before t becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः).

Junctions outside the general rules

  • मनुष्ये · अस्ति — vedic hiatus: manuṣye asti: final e before a is not joined (no avagraha); the text keeps the two words apart, as the witnesses do.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

विचिकित्सा
A desiderative noun from cit ‘perceive’: ‘wishing to discern’, hence doubt.
विद्याम्
Optative 1 sg. of vid ‘know’ (vidyām), ‘may I know’ — not the noun vidyā.
प्रेते
Past participle of pra-i ‘go forth, depart’: the dead. The Śrībhāṣya, as Raṅgarāmānuja reports, takes it as released from all bonds.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

A relative clause (yeyaṃ prete vicikitsā manuṣye) defines the subject, quoted with the two views (astīty eke, nāyam astīti caike). The main clause etad vidyām anuśiṣṭas tvayāham states the wish, and varāṇām eṣa varas tṛtīyaḥ closes it formally.

Ambiguities

What is the doubt about — survival after death, or release?
Śaṅkara: whether there is a self, distinct from body, senses, mind and intellect, that is connected with another body: अस्ति शरीरेन्द्रियमनोबुद्धिव्यतिरिक्तो देहान्तरसम्बन्ध्यात्मा इत्येके मन्यन्ते. Raṅgarāmānuja, reporting the Śrībhāṣya: the question is not about bodily death but about the released state, न शरीरवियोगमात्राभिप्रायम् अपि तु सर्वबन्धविनिर्मोक्षाभिप्रायम्. Madhva: about the Lord present as controller in the released, प्रेते मुक्ते मनुष्ये नियामकत्वेन भगवानस्तीति ज्ञानिनो वदन्ति.
varaḥ or vṛtaḥ?
Madhva’s text reads vṛtaḥ, ‘chosen’, for varaḥ, ‘boon’ (corrections.json).

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 21

Counted as written, 22 and 21 syllables: the second line (etad vidyām anuśiṣṭas tvayāhaṃ varāṇām eṣa varas tṛtīyaḥ) is one syllable short; Vedic metre often allows a short pāda.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: एतद्विद्यामनुशिष्टस्त्वयाहं वराणामेष वृतस्तृतीयः — Madhva’s text reads vṛtaḥ, ‘chosen’, for varaḥ, ‘boon’ (‘this is the third [boon] chosen’).
11 Connections with other scriptures

  • Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 2.4.12 Modern thematic association

    न प्रेत्य सञ्ज्ञास्तीत्यरे

    na pretya sañjñāstītyare

    Yājñavalkya’s ‘after death there is no awareness’ is the kind of statement that makes people say ‘he is not’; the Śrībhāṣya, as Raṅgarāmānuja reports it, cites the same words in explaining this question.

  • Bhagavad Gītā 2.20 Doctrinal comparison

    न हन्यते हन्यमाने शरीरे

    na hanyate hanyamāne śarīre

    The Gītā’s answer to the same doubt — the self is not slain when the body is slain — is close in words to the Kaṭha’s own answer at 1.2.18.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Death and meaning

Whether anything of a person continues after death is still the question people bring to every tradition, and to science. Naciketas asks it plainly and refuses easier gifts. The Upaniṣad treats the asking as the beginning of wisdom. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What do you think happens to a person after death — and why do you think so?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why would perception and reasoning be unable to settle this question?

A contemplation exercise Sit quietly for five minutes and ask yourself, without answering: ‘What in me would remain?’

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
