Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 6 of 15

The secret promised

Death promises to declare the secret, eternal Brahman, and how the self fares when it meets death.

5 Faithful English translation

Come now, I shall declare to you this secret, eternal Brahman, and how the self fares, having reached death, O Gautama.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Now I will tell you this hidden, ancient Brahman, Gautama, and what becomes of the self when it meets death.

Key concepts ब्रह्मन् Brahman आत्मन् self, Self मृत्यु death संसार saṃsāra, the round of births मोक्ष liberation

7 Explain it to a beginner

Death now promises Naciketas two things. He will explain the hidden, ancient Brahman, and he will explain what happens to a person, the self, when it meets death. This returns to the boy’s third question from the beginning of the story, about a man who has died. The commentators hear the promise differently. Śaṅkara says the second part concerns the person who has not understood: such a one goes on into new lives, as the next verse says. Raṅgarāmānuja reads ‘death’ here as release, so Death is promising to say what the freed self is like. Madhva says the point of the promise is to show that the individual self is different from the Lord.

An analogy Like a teacher who, after many lessons, says: now I will tell you the thing all of this was for.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

Death promises to declare the secret, eternal Brahman, and how the self fares after reaching death.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the knowing and the not knowing

He will tell the secret Brahman by whose knowledge all saṃsāra ceases, यद्विज्ञानात्सर्वसंसारोपरमो भवति, and how the self that does not know fares after death, going on in saṃsāra, यथा आत्मा संसरति तथा शृणु हे गौतम.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: death as release

For him maraṇa here is release, आत्मा मरणम् - मोक्षं प्राप्य, यथा यत्प्रकारविशिष्टो भवति: Death will tell the qualified pupil, free of passion, how the released self is, मुमुक्षवे रागाद्यनुपहताय उपदेशयोग्याय तुभ्यं वक्ष्यामि इत्यर्थः.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: to show the difference

His comment on these words is filed in madhva.json under 2.1.2–3 (see the flags): he will tell the secret Brahman, and how the individual self fares after death, to make known the difference between the individual and the Lord, इदं गुह्यं ब्रह्म प्रवक्ष्यामि । यथा मरणं प्राप्य जीवो भवति तच्च प्रवक्ष्यामि जीवेश्वरभेदज्ञापनाय.

Comparative Vedānta

Two promises, three readings

All agree that Death promises two things: Brahman, and the self’s state after death. They disagree on which self and which ‘death’: the self that does not know and so is reborn (Śaṅkara), the released self (Raṅgarāmānuja), or the individual self as distinct from the Lord (Madhva).

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.2.6

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Knowledge of Brahman ends saṃsāra; without it the self goes on after death, यद्विज्ञानात्सर्वसंसारोपरमो भवति.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.2.6

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

maraṇa is release, and Death promises to teach the form of the released self, आत्मा मरणम् - मोक्षं प्राप्य.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya (filed under 2.1.2–3)

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The teaching of the self’s state after death is given to show the difference of the individual from the Lord, जीवेश्वरभेदज्ञापनाय.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara and Madhva take maraṇa as death; Raṅgarāmānuja as release. Śaṅkara reads the second promise of the ignorant self’s rebirth; Madhva of the individual self as distinct from the Lord.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • त इदं ta idaṃ → ते + इदम्

    e before a vowel

    e becomes ay before a vowel, P. 6.1.78 (एचोऽयवायावः), and the y is dropped, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य), leaving the vowels apart.

  • इदं प्रवक्ष्यामि idaṃ pravakṣyāmi → इदम् + प्रवक्ष्यामि

    m before a consonant

    A final m becomes anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

Junctions outside the general rules

  • प्राप्य · आत्मा — pāda-boundary hiatus: prāpya ātmā stands unjoined at the pāda boundary in AS and the other witnesses; Madhva’s text joins it (prāpyātmā) and lacks ca (corrections.json). Unjoined, the third pāda keeps eight syllables.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

प्रवक्ष्यामि
The simple future of pra-√vac, ‘declare’: Death promises the teaching.
गौतम
A patronymic, ‘descendant of Gotama’; Naciketas’ father is called Gautama at 1.1.10, and Death so addresses the son.

Anvaya

हन्त गौतम ते इदम् गुह्यम् सनातनम् ब्रह्म प्रवक्ष्यामि । आत्मा मरणम् प्राप्य यथा च भवति [तत् च प्रवक्ष्यामि] ।

hanta gautama te idam guhyam sanātanam brahma pravakṣyāmi | ātmā maraṇam prāpya yathā ca bhavati tat ca pravakṣyāmi |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

One verb, pravakṣyāmi, with two objects: the noun phrase idaṃ guhyaṃ brahma sanātanam and the clause introduced by yathā ca, ‘and how …’. The vocative gautama closes the verse.

Ambiguities

What does maraṇa mean here?
Śaṅkara: death, and what becomes of the self that does not know, which goes on in saṃsāra, अविज्ञानाच्च यस्य मरणं प्राप्य यथा च आत्मा भवति यथा आत्मा संसरति तथा शृणु. Raṅgarāmānuja: release, आत्मा मरणम् - मोक्षं प्राप्य, and what form the released self has. Madhva: death, and how the individual self fares, taught so as to show its difference from the Lord.
hanta
An encouraging ‘come now’ addressed to Naciketas (Śaṅkara), or Death’s own exclamation of wonder (Raṅgarāmānuja).

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 17 + 16

Counted as written, 17 and 16 syllables: the first pāda has nine because te idam stands in hiatus (ta idaṃ); the second half-line has sixteen only with prāpya ātmā unjoined.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: यथा मरणं — Madhva’s text lacks ca (yathā maraṇaṃ prāpya).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Death and meaning

The teacher’s promise to speak plainly about death comes only after long preparation. Questions about death are often met either with silence or with quick answers; the dialogue suggests a third way, earned and careful. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.
