Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 1 of 17

Two drinking in the cave

Two dwell in the cave of the heart, drinking the truth of deeds; the knowers of Brahman call them shade and sunlight.

5 Faithful English translation

‘The two who drink the truth (ṛta) of the well-done [deed] in the world, having entered the cave, in the highest region — knowers of Brahman call [them] shade and sunlight, and [so do] those who keep the five fires and who have [piled] the Nāciketa [fire] three times.’

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘There are two who drink the truth of what has been done, hidden in the cave of the heart, in its highest place. Those who know Brahman call them shade and sunlight — and so do householders who tend the five fires and those who have built the Nāciketa fire three times.’

Key concepts गुहा the cave आत्मन् self, Self जीव individual self ऋत the right; cosmic and moral order अग्नि Agni, fire

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse opens the third part of the Kaṭha with a picture. Inside each person, in the 'cave' of the heart, two beings live together. Both are said to 'drink' — to taste the results of what has been done. The wise compare them to shade and sunlight: close together, yet completely different. Śaṅkara says the two are the self that travels from life to life and the highest self, and that only the first really tastes results; the second is named with it the way a whole group can be called 'the umbrella-carriers' when only some carry umbrellas. Raṅgarāmānuja reads them as the individual soul and the Lord who dwells with it. The verse adds that not only renunciants say this: so do householders who tend the sacred fires and those who have built Naciketas' fire three times.

An analogy Like a tree that casts shade under a bright sun: the shade exists only because of the light, and stands right beside it, yet the two are opposites.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

That two who ‘drink’ the truth of deeds dwell in the cave, in the highest place, and that knowers of Brahman, householders and those who have piled the Nāciketa fire three times call them shade and sunlight.

Traditional Interpretation

Why does the vallī begin here?

Śaṅkara: the second vallī set knowledge and ignorance apart as having opposite fruits, but did not settle what the fruits are; the image of the chariot is introduced to settle this and make it easy to grasp, तन्निर्णयार्था रथरूपककल्पना, तथा च प्रतिपत्तिसौकर्यम्. To separate the one who goes from the goal, two selves are set out, प्राप्तृप्राप्यगन्तृगन्तव्यविवेकार्थं रथरूपकद्वारा द्वावात्मानावुपन्यस्येते.

Traditional Interpretation

Shade and sunlight

Śaṅkara: the two differ like shade and sunlight, as the one that transmigrates and the one that does not, विलक्षणौ संसारित्वासंसारित्वेन. Raṅgarāmānuja: the two words point to the one who knows little and the one who knows all, किञ्चिज्ज्ञसर्वज्ञयोः.

Comparative Vedānta

How does Raṅgarāmānuja defend the reading?

He answers the objection that the Supreme does not enjoy the fruits of action and cannot be confined to a cave. The Brahmasūtra itself, he says, reads the verse as speaking of the self and the Supreme, 'गुहां प्रविष्टौ आत्मानौ' (ब्र.सू.१-२-११); when ‘two’ is said and one is a conscious self, the second is taken to be of the same kind, the Supreme, who ‘drinks’ as the one who causes the other to drink. He also notes that this very section has already placed the Supreme in the cave, 'गुहाहितं गह्वरेष्ठम्' (क.उ.२-१२).

Comparative Vedānta

How does Madhva read it?

Madhva: the all-pervading Lord stands in two ways, as self and inner self, आत्मान्तरात्मेति विभुरेक एव द्विधा स्थितः; to the wise he is like shade, छायेव विदुषां प्रभुः, and to the wicked always heat, आतपः पापिनां नित्यं. The rest of his line is missing in our source.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Who are the speakers?

Three groups: knowers of Brahman, those with five fires (Śaṅkara: householders, पञ्चाग्नयो गृहस्थाः) and those who have piled the Nāciketa fire three times. Śaṅkara: not only those who have left ritual say this, न केवलमकर्मिण एव वदन्ति. Raṅgarāmānuja takes the last two as qualifying the knowers of Brahman, since ritualists alone could not teach such a Supreme.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.3.1

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Two selves are set out so that the goer and the goal can be told apart: the one who transmigrates and experiences the fruit, and the one who does not, differing as shade and sunlight; only one of them ‘drinks’, एकस्तत्र कर्मफलं पिबति भुङ्क्ते नेतरः.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.3.1

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The two are the individual self and the Supreme in the one cave of the heart, जीवपरमात्मपर एवायं मन्त्रः; because the worshipped and the worshipper share the cave, the Supreme is easy to worship.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.3.1

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The one Lord stands as two, self and inner self, आत्मान्तरात्मेति विभुरेक एव द्विधा स्थितः: shade to the wise and heat to the wicked.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara’s two are the conditioned self and the highest self, which are finally not two; Raṅgarāmānuja’s are two really distinct selves, the soul and the Lord; for Madhva the verse speaks of two forms of the one Lord.

Morphology

पिबन्तौ
Dual present participle of pā ‘drink’ (stem piba-). The dual names two drinkers though, for Śaṅkara, only one drinks: the other is called so by association, as a group is called ‘the umbrella-bearers’ when only some carry umbrellas (छत्रिन्यायेन).
गुहाम्
Accusative with pra-√viś ‘enter into’; Śaṅkara glosses it with the locative (गुहायां).
त्रिणाचिकेताः
The n of nāciketa becomes ṇ after the r of tri- in the compound; Madhva’s digital text writes the dental n (corrections.json, witness error).

Anvaya

ब्रह्मविदः [ये च] पञ्चाग्नयः ये च त्रिणाचिकेताः [ते] सुकृतस्य ऋतम् पिबन्तौ लोके परमे परार्धे गुहाम् प्रविष्टौ [द्वौ] छायातपौ [इति] वदन्ति ॥

brahmavidaḥ ye ca pañcāgnayaḥ ye ca triṇāciketāḥ te sukṛtasya ṛtam pibantau loke parame parārdhe guhām praviṣṭau dvau chāyātapau iti vadanti ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

One sentence. The first line describes two beings by duals: pibantau (drinking) and praviṣṭau (having entered). The second line gives the main clause: brahmavido vadanti, with chāyātapau as what they call the two; pañcāgnayo ye ca triṇāciketāḥ adds two further groups of speakers. sukṛtasya may be read with ṛtam (Śaṅkara) or with loke (Raṅgarāmānuja).

Ambiguities

Who are the two?
Śaṅkara: two selves, set out to distinguish the one who reaches from what is reached, द्वावात्मानावुपन्यस्येते — the transmigrant and the highest, differing as shade and sunlight. Raṅgarāmānuja: the individual self and the Supreme, जीवपरमात्मपर एवायं मन्त्रः, as the Brahmasūtra (1.2.11) teaches; he rejects the reading ‘the intellect and the self’. Madhva: one Lord standing in two ways, आत्मान्तरात्मेति विभुरेक एव द्विधा स्थितः.
How can the Supreme ‘drink’?
Only one of the two experiences the fruit of action, Śaṅkara says; the dual names both by association. Raṅgarāmānuja: the Supreme is the one who makes the other drink, परमात्मनः प्रयोजककर्तृतया, 'पिबन्तौ' इति निर्देशस्यापि सम्भवात्.
sukṛtasya
Śaṅkara joins it to ṛtam: the truth of one’s own deed (सुकृतस्य स्वयङ्कृतस्य कर्मणः ऋतमिति पूर्वेण सम्बन्धः). Raṅgarāmānuja joins it to the world, सुकृतसाध्ये, लोके — the world reached by good deeds.
parārdhe
Śaṅkara: the place of the highest Brahman, the space in the heart. Raṅgarāmānuja comments on परार्ध्ये, ‘excellent’ (the furthest limit of number): परार्ध्ये संख्यायाः उत्तरावधिः — a reading of his text, not among our witnesses.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Self-knowledge

Two in one cave: the part of us that is swept along by what we do and what happens to us, and something that only witnesses. The image asks whether we ever notice the second. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin When you look back on a hard day, who is it that looks back?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why does Śaṅkara need two selves here, if in the end he holds the self is one?

A contemplation exercise Notice once today the difference between being caught up in something and simply noticing it.
