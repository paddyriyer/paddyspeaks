Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 7 of 17

The one who does not arrive

One without understanding, without a held mind, always impure, does not reach that place, but comes back into saṃsāra.

5 Faithful English translation

‘But he who is without understanding, without [a held] mind, always impure, does not reach that place, and enters saṃsāra.’

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘One who lacks understanding, whose mind is never held and who is never pure, does not reach that goal, but comes back again into the round of births.’

Key concepts संसार saṃsāra, the round of births मोक्ष liberation मनस् mind पद the goal; the word

7 Explain it to a beginner

The chariot was going somewhere. This verse says what happens when the journey fails. A person who lacks understanding, who never steadies the mind and whose life is not clean does not arrive at the goal. Worse, says the verse, such a person goes round again into saṃsāra, the cycle of birth and death. Śaṅkara explains that 'that goal' is the imperishable, highest place spoken of earlier, and saṃsāra is marked by birth and death. Raṅgarāmānuja adds that such a person does not merely fail to arrive: the uncontrolled horses actually drag them into the thick forest of saṃsāra. The word 'impure' is explained by him as a mind always leaning towards wrong thoughts. The verse is less a threat than a statement of where an unguided journey ends: back where it started.

An analogy Like a carriage whose horses have the bit between their teeth: not only does it miss the town it set out for, it ends up lost in the woods.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

That one without understanding, without a held mind and always impure does not reach that place, and enters saṃsāra.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara

This is the result for the rider whose charioteer lacks understanding, पूर्वोक्तस्याविज्ञानवतो बुद्धिसारथेरिदं फलमाह: ‘that place’ is the imperishable, highest place spoken of before, तत् पूर्वोक्तमक्षरं यत्परं पदम्; not only does he not reach it, he enters saṃsāra, marked by birth and death.

Comparative Vedānta

Raṅgarāmānuja

Commenting on 1.3.7–8 together: ‘impure’ because always inclined to contrary thoughts, सर्वदा विपरीतचिन्ताप्रवणत्वात्; not merely failing to reach the goal, the senses lead into the dense wilderness of saṃsāra, प्रत्युत गहनं संसारकान्तारमेव प्रापयति.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Madhva

In our source Madhva prints 1.3.3–1.3.11 without comment and then comments once, marking it ॥ १०-१२ ॥, on the deities who preside over the senses, their objects, the mind and the intellect; he does not gloss this verse. His digital text keys यस्तवविज्ञानवान् for yas tv avijñānavān, a witness error (corrections.json).

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.3.7–8

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara and Raṅgarāmānuja agree on the sense; they differ on what ‘that place’ finally is (the imperishable Brahman; Viṣṇu’s highest place, as Raṅgarāmānuja says on 1.3.3). In our source Madhva prints 1.3.3–1.3.11 without comment and then comments once, marking it ॥ १०-१२ ॥, on the deities who preside over the senses, their objects, the mind and the intellect; he does not gloss this verse.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • यस्त्वविज्ञानवान्भवत्यमनस्कः yastvavijñānavānbhavatyamanaskaḥ → यः + तु + अविज्ञानवान् + भवति + अमनस्कः

    visarga; vowel sandhi

    Visarga before t becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः); u and i before a dissimilar vowel become v and y, P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि); n of -vān stays before bh.

  • सदाशुचिः sadāśuciḥ → सदा + अशुचिः

    vowel sandhi

    ā + a → ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • न स तत्पदमाप्नोति na sa tatpadamāpnoti → न + सः + तत् + पदम् + आप्नोति

    saḥ before a consonant; m before a vowel

    saḥ loses its ending before a consonant, P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि). Final m stays before a vowel.

  • संसारं चाधिगच्छति saṃsāraṃ cādhigacchati → संसारम् + च + अधिगच्छति

    anusvāra; vowel sandhi

    Final m → anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः); a + a → ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • अविज्ञानवान् avijñānavān

    nañ-tatpuruṣa · अ + विज्ञानवत्

    Vigraha न विज्ञानवान्

    not possessed of understanding

  • अमनस्कः amanaskaḥ

    nañ-bahuvrīhi · अ + मनस्

    Vigraha अविद्यमानं मनः यस्य सः

    one who has no (held) mind; with the suffix -ka

  • अशुचिः aśuciḥ

    nañ-tatpuruṣa · अ + शुचि

    Vigraha न शुचिः

    impure

Morphology

अमनस्कः
A bahuvrīhi of a- and manas with the suffix -ka; the commentators take it not as ‘mindless’ but as one whose mind is not held.
अधिगच्छति
Present of gam with adhi, ‘go towards, attain’; the same verb that names attaining the goal is here used of falling back into saṃsāra.

Anvaya

यः तु अविज्ञानवान् अमनस्कः सदा अशुचिः भवति, सः तत् पदम् न आप्नोति, संसारम् च अधिगच्छति ॥

yaḥ tu avijñānavān amanaskaḥ sadā aśuciḥ bhavati, saḥ tat padam na āpnoti, saṃsāram ca adhigacchati ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A relative clause (yas tu … bhavati) with three predicates (avijñānavān, amanaskaḥ, aśuciḥ); the main clause has two verbs, the negated āpnoti and adhigacchati, joined by ca.

Ambiguities

sadāśuciḥ
The letters सदाशुचिः can be read sadā śuciḥ or sadā aśuciḥ; the sense here, and the contrast with 1.3.8, require sadā aśuciḥ. The vishvAsa mūla and Raṅgarāmānuja’s text mark the elided a with avagraha (सदाऽशुचिः).
Who is ‘he’ now?
Śaṅkara: in 1.3.5–6 the charioteer; here the rider who has such a charioteer, न सः रथी तत् पूर्वोक्तमक्षरं यत्परं पदम् आप्नोति तेन सारथिना. Raṅgarāmānuja: these two verses give the outcome of controlling the senses or not, वशीकरणतदभावयोः प्रयोजनं दर्शयति मन्त्रद्वयेन.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16

Four pādas of eight syllables.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Habit and direction

‘Comes back into the round’ describes something familiar on a small scale too: a direction never held tends to return to where it began. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What journeys of yours have ended back at the start, and why?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why does the verse add ‘impure’ to ‘without understanding’ and ‘without a held mind’?

A contemplation exercise Name one aim you keep returning to without reaching, and one thing that pulls you off it.
