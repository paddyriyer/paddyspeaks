Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 7 of 15

Wombs and the stationary

Some embodied beings go to a womb, others to the stationary state, according to their deeds and their learning.

5 Faithful English translation

Some embodied ones enter a womb for embodiment; others follow into the stationary — according to their deeds, according to what they have learned.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Some embodied beings go to a womb to take a body again; others pass into things that do not move, such as trees — each according to what they have done and what they have come to know.

Key concepts संसार saṃsāra, the round of births कर्मन् action, ritual work जीव individual self अविद्या avidyā

7 Explain it to a beginner

Death begins to answer the question about what happens after death, and he speaks of those who have not understood the teaching. Some take a new body through a womb; others, he says, go into the state of things that do not move, like trees. Which way each goes depends on two things: what they have done and what they have learned. The verse describes what the text teaches, and the commentators read it within their own frameworks. Śaṅkara says these are people without knowledge. Raṅgarāmānuja says Death mentions them to show how unlike them Naciketas is, since he wants to hear about the highest. Madhva says such going into wombs belongs to the individual self, never to Brahman.

An analogy Like travellers who each set out by the road their own steps have worn, one to a near town, one to a far one.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

Some embodied beings enter a womb to be embodied again; others go to the stationary state; each according to deeds and learning.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: according to deed and understanding

Those without knowledge enter a womb to take a body, अन्ये केचिदविद्यावन्तो मूढाः प्रपद्यन्ते शरीरत्वाय शरीरग्रहणार्थं; the very lowest go to the state of trees and the like, स्थाणुं वृक्षादिस्थावरभावम्. Each takes a body according to the deeds done and knowledge gained; he cites ‘यथाप्रज्ञं हि सम्भवाः’, giving it as from the Aitareya Āraṇyaka.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: unlike you

Death speaks of those turned away from hearing of the Supreme, unlike Naciketas, परमात्मतत्त्वश्रवणविमुखाः त्वद्विसदृशाः. They go to a womb or to the stationary state by the rites and meditations they have done, citing the Chāndogya and the Bṛhadāraṇyaka, 'तं विद्याकर्मणी समन्वारभेते'.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: this is the individual, not Brahman

In his comment filed under 2.1.2–3 (see the flags) he notes that going into wombs belongs to the individual self, not to Brahman, न हि जीवस्य योन्यादिगमनं ब्रह्म.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Two adverbs of manner

yathākarma and yathāśrutam are avyayībhāva compounds, adverbs formed with yathā, ‘according to’. Both qualify the two clauses together: the destination is fixed by deeds and by learning.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.2.7

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The verse describes those turned away from the Supreme, to set off the pupil’s fitness, परमात्मतत्त्वश्रवणविमुखाः त्वद्विसदृशाः.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya (filed under 2.1.2–3)

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Going into wombs belongs to the individual self, which is other than Brahman, न हि जीवस्य योन्यादिगमनं ब्रह्म.

Where they disagree

All read the verse as describing the fate of those who do not know. Śaṅkara stresses deed and understanding; Raṅgarāmānuja the contrast with the fit pupil; Madhva the difference between the individual who is reborn and Brahman who is not.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • योनिमन्ये yonimanye → योनिम् + अन्ये

    m before a vowel

    A final m remains before a vowel and is written joined to it.

  • अन्येऽनुसंयन्ति anye'nusaṃyanti → अन्ये + अनुसंयन्ति

    e + a

    After a word-final e an initial a is absorbed, marked by the avagraha, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • यथाकर्म yathākarma

    avyayībhāva · यथा + कर्मन्

    Vigraha कर्म अनतिक्रम्य

    in accordance with deeds

  • यथाश्रुतम् yathāśrutam

    avyayībhāva · यथा + श्रुत

    Vigraha श्रुतम् अनतिक्रम्य

    in accordance with what has been learned

Morphology

शरीरत्वाय
The abstract noun in -tva from śarīra, in the dative of purpose: ‘for becoming a body’, ‘in order to be embodied’.
अनुसंयन्ति
anu-sam-√i, present plural (yanti): ‘go along after, follow into’.

Anvaya

अन्ये देहिनः शरीरत्वाय योनिम् प्रपद्यन्ते । अन्ये यथाकर्म यथाश्रुतम् स्थाणुम् अनुसंयन्ति ।

anye dehinaḥ śarīratvāya yonim prapadyante | anye yathākarma yathāśrutam sthāṇum anusaṃyanti |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two parallel clauses with anye … anye, ‘some … others’. The two adverbial compounds yathākarma yathāśrutam close the verse and qualify both clauses.

Ambiguities

Who are the ‘others’?
Śaṅkara: those without knowledge, अविद्यावन्तो मूढाः, some of whom pass into wombs and the very lowest into plants. Raṅgarāmānuja: those turned away from hearing of the Supreme, unlike Naciketas, so that the verse sets off his fitness, अधिकारिविशेषनिर्देशपरेण.
yathāśrutam
Śaṅkara: according to the knowledge acquired, citing a text as saying ‘births are according to understanding’, ‘यथाप्रज्ञं हि सम्भवाः’. Raṅgarāmānuja: according to the rites and meditations performed, citing the Chāndogya 'रमणीयचरणाः' and the Bṛhadāraṇyaka 'तं विद्याकर्मणी समन्वारभेते'.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Learning and apprenticeship

The verse joins what we do and what we learn as the two things that shape where we go. Whatever one makes of its account of rebirth, it treats learning as no less weighty than action. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Do you think what you learn shapes who you become as much as what you do?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why might Raṅgarāmānuja read this verse as being about the pupil’s fitness rather than about others’ fate?

A contemplation exercise Today, notice one thing you learned and one thing you did, and ask how each might shape tomorrow.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
