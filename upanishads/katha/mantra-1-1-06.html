Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 6 of 29

Like grain, mortals ripen and come again

The boy urges his father to keep his word: those before and those now were true to theirs, and life is too short for a lie.

5 Faithful English translation

Consider how [were] those before; observe how [are] the others. Like grain a mortal ripens; like grain he is born again.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘Think how our forebears acted, and see how good people act now. A mortal ripens like grain, and like grain he springs up again.’

Key concepts सत्य truth, the real मृत्यु death धर्म dharma

7 Explain it to a beginner

The father seems to regret his angry words. The boy answers him: think how our ancestors lived, and look at how good people live now. None of them made their word false. And why would anyone break their word? Life is short. A human being ripens and falls like grain, and like grain springs up again in another life. Nothing lasting is won by a lie in so brief a span. So keep your word, father: send me to Death. It is a remarkable speech for a child. He is not holding his father to a technicality; he is reminding him that truthfulness is what holds a life together when everything else passes.

An analogy Like a harvest field: the grain ripens, is cut, and the field grows again next season — no single stalk lasts long enough to be worth a lie.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the mantra say?

Consider those before and observe those now; a mortal ripens like grain and comes up again like grain.

Traditional Interpretation

What is the boy asking of his father?

Śaṅkara: to keep his word. The ancestors and the good never made their word false — and no one becomes free of age and death by a lie: न च मृषाभूतं कृत्वा कश्चिदजरामरो भवति. So: पालयात्मनः सत्यम्, keep your own truth, and send me to Yama. Raṅgarāmānuja: एवमनित्ये जीवलोके किं मृषाकरणेन, in so impermanent a world, what is gained by falsehood? — प्रेषय मां मृत्यवे.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Who are ‘the others’?

Śaṅkara reads apare, the good of the present: वर्तमानाश्च अपरे साधवो यथा वर्तन्ते. The printed spacing (तथा परे) would allow pare, ‘the later ones’; the commentators’ reading needs the a.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • तथा परे tathā pare → तथा + अपरे

    savarṇa-dīrgha

    tathā + apare → tathāpare, ā + a → ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः); the base prints a space, so the letters can also be read tathā pare.

  • सस्यमिवाजायते sasyamivājāyate → सस्यम् + इव + आजायते

    final m before a vowel; savarṇa-dīrgha

    m stays before a vowel; iva + ājāyate → ivājāyate, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

पच्यते
Passive of pac, ‘cook, ripen’: a mortal ‘is ripened’ — grows old and dies — like grain.
आजायते
ā + jan, ‘comes to be again’. Madhva’s text reads sasyam iva jāyate punaḥ, without ā (corrections.json).

Anvaya

पूर्वे यथा [वृत्ताः], [तत्] अनुपश्य । अपरे तथा [वर्तन्ते], [तत्] प्रतिपश्य । मर्त्यः सस्यम् इव पच्यते, सस्यम् इव पुनः आजायते ॥

pūrve yathā vṛttāḥ, tat anupaśya | apare tathā vartante, tat pratipaśya | martyaḥ sasyam iva pacyate, sasyam iva punaḥ ājāyate ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two imperatives (‘consider’, ‘observe’) with clauses of manner whose verbs are understood, then two comparisons with sasyam iva, ‘like grain’. The verse is the boy’s argument to his father.

Ambiguities

tathā pare or tathāpare?
The base prints तथा परे; the letters can be tathā pare (‘so the later ones’) or tathā apare (‘so the others’). Śaṅkara and Raṅgarāmānuja both comment apare, ‘the good who live now’.
Who speaks, and to what end?
Śaṅkara and Raṅgarāmānuja: the boy speaks to his repentant father, urging him to keep his word and send him to Death. The point of the grain image is that life is brief, so a lie gains nothing.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 18

Anuṣṭubh with a hypermetric second line: 16 + 18 syllables as written (each pāda of the second line has nine).

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: पच्यते सस्यमिव जायते पुनः — Madhva’s text reads sasyam iva jāyate punaḥ, ‘is born again like grain’, for sasyam ivājāyate (ā-jāyate, ‘comes to be again’), which Śaṅkara glosses (आजायते आविर्भवति).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Ethical leadership

A promise made rashly is still a promise. The boy asks his father to act as people of integrity have always acted, even when it costs him dearly. This is a modern reflection, not the text’s claim.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Is it ever right to keep a promise you regret making?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry How does the shortness of life bear on whether a lie is worth telling?

A contemplation exercise Keep one small promise today that you would rather not keep, and notice what it costs and what it gives.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
