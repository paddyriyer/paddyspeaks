Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 8 of 15

Awake among the sleeping

The person who stays awake among the sleeping, shaping desire after desire, is the bright, Brahman, the immortal, in which all worlds rest.

5 Faithful English translation

This person who is awake among the sleeping, fashioning desire after desire — that alone is the bright, that is Brahman, that alone is called the immortal. In it all the worlds rest; no one indeed goes beyond it. This truly is that.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

The one who stays awake while all else sleeps, shaping one wish after another — that is the bright, that is Brahman, that is called the immortal. All the worlds rest in it, and no one goes beyond it. This is that.

Key concepts ब्रह्मन् Brahman पुरुष person अमृत immortality, the deathless लोक world, realm आत्मन् self, Self

7 Explain it to a beginner

Here Death begins to tell the secret he promised. When we sleep, our senses go quiet, yet something goes on: in dreams scene after scene is made, one wish after another taking shape. Who makes them? The one who stays awake when everything else sleeps. That, says the verse, is the bright, the immortal, Brahman itself; all the worlds rest in it and nothing goes beyond it. Śaṅkara reads the sleepers as the senses and the wakeful one as the self that fashions dream objects. Raṅgarāmānuja reads it as the Lord, awake while all individual souls sleep, creating by his will. Madhva too reads it as Viṣṇu, awake when beings sleep in dissolution.

An analogy Like the one person in a dark house who stays awake through the night and sees the dreams of every sleeper.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

The person awake among the sleeping, fashioning desire after desire, is the bright, is Brahman, is called the immortal; all worlds rest in it and none goes beyond it. This is that.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the secret promised

This is the secret Brahman promised in 2.2.6, यत्प्रतिज्ञातं गुह्यं ब्रह्म प्रवक्ष्यामीति तदाह. While the breaths and senses sleep, he stays awake, fashioning each desired object through ignorance, कामं कामं तं तमभिप्रेतं स्त्र्याद्यर्थमविद्यया निर्मिमाणः. That alone is the bright and pure, and there is no other secret Brahman, तदेव शुक्रं शुभ्रं शुद्धं तद्ब्रह्म नान्यद्गुह्यं ब्रह्मास्ति. The last line is as at 2.1.9, तदु नात्येति कश्चनेत्यादि पूर्ववदेव.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: the Lord who wills

While all selves sleep, सर्वेषु सुप्तेषु जीवेषु, the Supreme creates by willing again and again, संकल्प्य संकल्प्य स्वच्छन्दानुरोधेन; he refers for this sense to the Bhāṣya on the Sandhya-adhikaraṇa and the Śrutaprakāśikā, अयं चार्थः सन्ध्याधिकरणभाष्यश्रुतप्रकाशिकयोः स्पष्टः. He adds that the restriction ‘that alone is the immortal’ excludes the hope that the released and the Supreme are identical, एतेन अमृतान्तरनिषेधात् मुक्तपरमात्मनोः अभेदप्रत्याशा प्रत्युक्ता.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: the one awake is Viṣṇu

In his comment filed under 2.1.2–3 (see the flags): the individual, who enjoys the fruit of action and sleeps, is the jīva, यः कर्मफलभोक्ता सुप्त्यादिमान् स जीवः; he who is awake when beings sleep in dissolution is Viṣṇu, the supreme Brahman, यः प्रलयादिषु जीवेषु सुप्तेषु जागर्ति स विष्णुः परं ब्रह्मेत्यर्थः.

Comparative Vedānta

Dream, will, dissolution

Śaṅkara places the scene in dream: the self fashions objects while the senses sleep. Raṅgarāmānuja and Madhva place it at the level of the Lord, awake while all souls sleep, creating by his will (Raṅgarāmānuja) or awake through dissolution (Madhva). For Raṅgarāmānuja the verse rules out identity of the released self with the Supreme; for Śaṅkara it names the one Brahman beside which there is no other.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya (filed under 2.1.2–3)

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The one awake while beings sleep is Viṣṇu, distinct from the sleeping jīva, यः प्रलयादिषु जीवेषु सुप्तेषु जागर्ति स विष्णुः परं ब्रह्मेत्यर्थः.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara reads the wakeful one as the self in dream, one with Brahman; Raṅgarāmānuja and Madhva as the Lord awake among sleeping souls. Raṅgarāmānuja draws from the verse an argument against the identity of the released self and the Supreme; Madhva the difference of jīva and Viṣṇu.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • य एष ya eṣa → यः + एषः

    aḥ before a vowel other than a

    After a the visarga becomes y, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), which is dropped, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य), leaving the vowels apart.

  • एष सुप्तेषु eṣa supteṣu → एषः + सुप्तेषु

    eṣaḥ before a consonant

    The nominative ending of eṣaḥ is dropped before a consonant, P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि).

  • पुरुषो निर्मिमाणः puruṣo nirmimāṇaḥ → पुरुषः + निर्मिमाणः

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

  • तद्ब्रह्म tadbrahma → तत् + ब्रह्म

    t before a voiced consonant

    A final t becomes d before a voiced sound, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

  • तदेवामृतम् tadevāmṛtam → तत् + एव + अमृतम्

    t before a vowel; a + a

    The t becomes d, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते); eva and amṛtam merge into the long vowel, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • तस्मिंल्लोकाः tasmiṃllokāḥ → तस्मिन् + लोकाः

    n before l

    A final n before l becomes a nasalised l, P. 8.4.60 (तोर्लि), written with anusvāra (or candrabindu) and ll.

  • नात्येति nātyeti → न + अत्येति

    a + a

    Two like vowels merge into the long vowel, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

कामम्
The doubled kāmaṃ kāmam expresses ‘desire after desire’. Śaṅkara reads kāma as a noun, each desired thing, the object of nirmimāṇaḥ. Raṅgarāmānuja reads it as an absolutive in -am (ṇamul), ‘having willed again and again’, the form of repeated action, P. 3.4.22 (आभीक्ष्ण्ये णमुल् च), and rejects taking kāma as the sons and other objects of desire offered at 1.1.25.
निर्मिमाणः
nir-√mā, ‘measure out, fashion’, with the reduplicated present (mimīte) and the middle participle -āna; the n is retroflex after the r of nir.
तस्मिन्
The locative of tad; before lokāḥ its n becomes a nasalised l (तस्मिंल्लोकाः).

Anvaya

यः एषः पुरुषः सुप्तेषु कामम् कामम् निर्मिमाणः जागर्ति तत् एव शुक्रम् तत् ब्रह्म तत् एव अमृतम् उच्यते । तस्मिन् सर्वे लोकाः श्रिताः । तत् उ कश्चन न अत्येति । एतत् वै तत् ।

yaḥ eṣaḥ puruṣaḥ supteṣu kāmam kāmam nirmimāṇaḥ jāgarti tat eva śukram tat brahma tat eva amṛtam ucyate | tasmin sarve lokāḥ śritāḥ | tat u kaścana na atyeti | etat vai tat |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A relative clause (yaḥ eṣa … puruṣo nirmimāṇaḥ) is taken up by the neuter tat of the main clauses: tad eva śukraṃ, tad brahma, tad evāmṛtam ucyate. The neuter tat refers to the masculine puruṣa as the reality named. Two further clauses (tasmiṃl lokāḥ śritāḥ; tad u nātyeti kaścana) repeat 2.1.9 and look ahead to 2.3.1.

Ambiguities

Who are the sleeping?
Śaṅkara: the breaths and senses, which sleep while the self keeps fashioning objects in dream, सुप्तेषु प्राणादिषु जागर्ति न स्वपिति. Raṅgarāmānuja: all individual selves, सर्वेषु सुप्तेषु जीवेषु. Madhva: beings asleep in dissolution, while Viṣṇu is awake.
kāmaṃ kāmam
Desired objects, fashioned by ignorance (Śaṅkara, स्त्र्याद्यर्थमविद्यया), or the absolutive ‘having willed again and again’ of the Lord who creates by his will (Raṅgarāmānuja). See the morphology note.
eṣa or eṣu
The base reads ya eṣa supteṣu, ‘he who among the sleeping’, on which Śaṅkara comments. Madhva’s text reads ya eṣu supteṣu, ‘who among these sleeping ones’ (corrections.json), as does the mūla printed with Raṅgarāmānuja.

Metre

irregular · syllables counted as written: 20 + 16 + 16 + 4

Counted as written, 20, 16, 16 and 4 syllables. The first line is a long line of 9 + 11 (ya eṣa supteṣu jāgarti, then the triṣṭubh-length kāmaṃ kāmaṃ puruṣo nirmimāṇaḥ); two regular anuṣṭubh half-lines follow, and the refrain (4) stands outside the metre.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: य एषु सुप्तेषु — Madhva’s text reads ya eṣu supteṣu, ‘who among these sleeping ones’, for ya eṣa supteṣu, ‘he who [is awake] among the sleeping’, on which Śaṅkara comments (य एष सुप्तेषु).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Psychology and inner life

Every night the mind goes on making scenes and wishes while the senses are shut. The verse asks us to notice the one who is present through that making. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin When you dream, who is watching the dream?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Is kāmaṃ kāmam better read as ‘desire after desire’ or as ‘willing again and again’? What changes with each?

A contemplation exercise On waking tomorrow, before moving, recall one dream image and the sense of having been there to see it.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
