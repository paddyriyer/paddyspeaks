Cows that have drunk their last

The boy’s thought: gifts that are spent and useless lead the giver to joyless worlds.

5 Faithful English translation

[Cows] that have drunk [their] water, eaten [their] grass, been milked of [their] milk, without strength — joyless, indeed, are those worlds; to them he goes, giving them.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘Cows that have drunk their last water, eaten their last grass, given their last milk, and can no longer calve — whoever gives such gifts goes to worlds without joy.’

Key concepts लोक world, realm कर्मन् action, ritual work श्रद्धा faith, trust दक्षिणा dakṣiṇā, sacrificial fee

7 Explain it to a beginner

Here we hear what the boy thinks as he watches. The cows being given away are old: they have drunk their last, eaten their last, given their last milk, and can no longer bear calves. A gift like that costs the giver nothing. The boy concludes that such giving does not lead to happiness; it leads to joyless worlds. This is the logic of the ritual his father believes in, taken seriously: if the gift is worthless, the reward will be worthless too. The boy is not rebelling against his father’s religion; he is holding it to its own standard. The verse also shows how the Upaniṣad works: from an ordinary scene it draws a question about what our actions really bring us.

An analogy Like donating only the clothes you would have thrown away anyway, and expecting gratitude for your generosity.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the mantra say?

It describes worn-out cows and states that one who gives such gifts goes to ‘joyless worlds’. It is the content of the boy’s reflection.

Traditional Interpretation

Why are these gifts faulty?

Śaṅkara: they are spent, useless cows — निष्फला गाव इत्यर्थः — and the giver goes to the worlds called joyless: अनन्दाः अनानन्दाः असुखा नामेत्येतत्. Raṅgarāmānuja reads the verse as showing the shape of the boy’s faith: श्रद्धाप्रकारमेव दर्शयति.

Traditional Interpretation

How does the Dvaita tradition read the scene?

Madhva’s cited verse at the opening already names the fault: the father gave ददौ गाश्च निरिन्द्रियाः, cows past use. Later Dvaita commentators on Madhva (Vyāsatīrtha, Vedeśatīrtha) press the past participles — the cows have drunk and will drink no more — and connect the ‘joyless worlds’ with the realm of darkness; they are not quoted here.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • लोकास्तान्स lokāstānsa → लोकाः + तान् + सः

    visarga before t; n before s

    Visarga becomes s before t, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः); the n of tān stands before s.

  • ता ददत् tā dadat → ताः + ददत्

    āḥ before a voiced consonant

    After ā the visarga is dropped before a voiced sound: P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि) and P. 8.3.22 (हलि सर्वेषाम्).

  • स गच्छति sa gacchati → सः + गच्छति

    saḥ before a consonant

    The visarga of saḥ is dropped before a consonant, P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि).

Morphology

ददत्
Present active participle of dā (reduplicating class), nominative masculine, ‘giving’: the one who gives such cows goes to those worlds.
अनन्दाः
a- + nanda, ‘without joy’; Śaṅkara glosses anānandāḥ.

Anvaya

[याः] पीतोदकाः जग्धतृणाः दुग्धदोहाः निरिन्द्रियाः [गावः], ताः ददत् सः अनन्दाः नाम ते लोकाः [सन्ति] तान् गच्छति ॥

yāḥ pītodakāḥ jagdhatṛṇāḥ dugdhadohāḥ nirindriyāḥ gāvaḥ, tāḥ dadat saḥ anandāḥ nāma te lokāḥ santi tān gacchati ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Four bahuvrīhi compounds describe the cows (the noun is understood); the second line is a nominal sentence (‘joyless are those worlds’) followed by ‘to them he goes, giving them’. The participle dadat (‘giving’) carries the condition.

Ambiguities

Whose thought is it?
The verse gives the content of the boy’s reflection (1.1.2 ends ‘he thought’). Raṅgarāmānuja closes it with एवम् अमन्यत इत्यर्थः, ‘this is what he thought’.
pītodakāḥ: past use or past need?
Śaṅkara takes the compounds as describing worn-out cows. Later Dvaita commentators on Madhva press the past participles: the cows have drunk and will drink no more — old cows near death.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Wealth and consumption

Charity that disposes of what we no longer need can feel generous while costing nothing. The boy’s question is whether such giving changes anything in the giver. This is a modern reflection, not the text’s claim.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Is a gift that costs you nothing really a gift?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why would the worth of what is given determine the worth of what is gained?

A contemplation exercise Before your next gift or donation, ask what it costs you and whether that matters.
