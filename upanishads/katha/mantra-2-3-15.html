Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 15 of 18

When the knots of the heart are cut

When all the knots of the heart are cut here, then the mortal becomes immortal. This much is the teaching.

5 Faithful English translation

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

When all the knots of the heart are cut here and now, then the mortal becomes immortal. That is the whole of the teaching.

Key concepts ग्रन्थि knot (of the heart) अमृत immortality, the deathless अविद्या avidyā मोक्ष liberation उपनिषद् Upaniṣad

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse repeats the promise of the last one with a new image: knots in the heart. When all of them are cut, here in this life, the mortal becomes immortal, and that, it says, is the whole teaching. Śaṅkara says the knots are the mistaken thoughts that tie us, such as ‘I am this body, this money is mine, I am happy, I am miserable’. When one knows oneself as Brahman, these knots are cut and the desires that grew from them die at the root. He takes ‘this much is the teaching’ as the summary of all the Upaniṣads. Raṅgarāmānuja says the knots are attachment and aversion, and that ‘this much’ is all the seeker has to do: what comes after, the journey at death described in the next verse, is the Lord’s work, not ours. A Muṇḍaka verse uses the same image.

An analogy A parcel tied with many knots cannot be opened by pulling harder; it opens when each knot is undone.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

When all the knots of the heart are cut here, the mortal becomes immortal; this much is the teaching.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the knots of ignorance

The verse tells when desires are destroyed at the root: when the knots of the heart, that is the intellect, are split, here while one lives, हृदयस्य बुद्धेरिह जीवत एव ग्रन्थयो. The knots are the cognitions of ignorance, अविद्याप्रत्यया इत्यर्थः, such as ‘I am this body’; when the knowledge ‘I am Brahman’ arises they perish, and the desires born of them perish at the root. ‘This much’ means this much and no more, the teaching of all the Vedāntas, अनुशासनम् अनुशिष्टः उपदेशः सर्ववेदान्तानामिति वाक्यशेषः.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: the seeker’s part and the Lord’s

Repeating the point for emphasis, the verse concludes: the knots are attachment, aversion and the like, ग्रन्थिवत् दुर्मोचा: रागद्वेषादयो. This much is what the seeker is to be taught to do; the going out by the crown channel and the path of light described next are not the seeker’s act but the Lord’s, pleased by meditation, वक्ष्यमाणमूर्धन्यनाडीनिष्क्रमणार्चिरादिगमनादिकं न साधकस्य कृत्यम्, किन्तु उपासनप्रीतभगवत्कृत्यम्.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: the knots of false knowledge

In a verse in his comment: when one is wholly freed from the knots of false knowledge, one reaches immortality, and the teaching is for that, मिथ्याज्ञानग्रन्थिभिस्तु नितरां मुच्यते यदा । तदाऽमृतत्वमेवैति तदर्थं चानुशासनम्.

Comparative Vedānta

The Muṇḍaka’s knot of the heart

The Muṇḍaka has the same image: भिद्यते हृदयग्रन्थिश्छिद्यन्ते सर्वसंशयाः.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.3.15

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The knots are cognitions of ignorance, अविद्याप्रत्यया इत्यर्थः; when they are cut, desire perishes at the root, and this is the whole teaching of the Vedānta.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.3.15

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Cutting the knots of attachment and aversion is the seeker’s part; the journey that follows is the Lord’s doing, उपासनप्रीतभगवत्कृत्यम्.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.3.15

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The knots are those of false knowledge, मिथ्याज्ञानग्रन्थिभिस्तु नितरां मुच्यते यदा.

Where they disagree

The knots are cognitions of ignorance (Śaṅkara), attachment and aversion (Raṅgarāmānuja), or false knowledge (Madhva). ‘This much is the teaching’ is for Śaṅkara the sum of the Vedānta; for Raṅgarāmānuja the limit of the seeker’s own task.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • हृदयस्येह hṛdayasyeha → हृदयस्य + इह

    a + i

    a + i give e, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • मर्त्योऽमृतो भवत्येतावद्ध्यनुशासनम् martyo'mṛto bhavatyetāvaddhyanuśāsanam → मर्त्यः + अमृतः + भवति + एतावत् + हि + अनुशासनम्

    aḥ before a; aḥ before a voiced consonant; i before an unlike vowel; t before h

    The visarga of -aḥ becomes u before a, giving o, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च) with P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः), and the following a is merged, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति); the avagraha marks it. -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च). i before an unlike vowel becomes y, P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि). t before h becomes d, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते), and the h after it becomes dh, P. 8.4.62 (झयो होऽन्यतरस्याम्).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

प्रभिद्यन्ते
Passive present of pra + √bhid, ‘are split apart’: the knots are cut, the image being of a tight bond loosened.
एतावत्
etāvat, ‘this much, so much’, with the suffix -vat after the pronoun etad. Śaṅkara stresses ‘only this much’; Madhva’s text lacks hi (corrections.json).

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

A temporal clause yadā … prabhidyante with its subject granthayaḥ, answered by atha martyo ’mṛto bhavati as in 2.3.14; then a closing verbless clause, etāvad dhy anuśāsanam.

Ambiguities

What are the knots?
Śaṅkara: the cognitions of ignorance, tight as knots, such as ‘I am this body, this wealth is mine, I am happy or unhappy’, अहमिदं शरीरं ममेदं धनं सुखी दुःखी चाहमित्येवमादिलक्षणाः. Raṅgarāmānuja: attachment, aversion and the like, hard to loosen as knots, ग्रन्थिवत् दुर्मोचा: रागद्वेषादयो. Madhva, in a verse in his comment: the knots of false knowledge, मिथ्याज्ञानग्रन्थिभिस्तु.
‘This much is the teaching’
Śaṅkara: this much and no more is the instruction of all the Vedāntas; one should not suspect anything further. Raṅgarāmānuja: this much is the seeker’s own task; what the next verse describes, the going out by the crown channel and the path of light, is not the seeker’s doing but the Lord’s, न साधकस्य कृत्यम्, किन्तु उपासनप्रीतभगवत्कृत्यम्.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16

Regular: 16 and 16 syllables. Madhva’s text, without hi, would have fifteen in the second line.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: मर्त्योऽमृतो भवत्येतावदनुशासनम् — Madhva’s text lacks hi in etāvad dhy anuśāsanam.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Ego and self-image

Śaṅkara’s examples of the knots are plain: ‘I am this body, this is mine, I am happy, I am miserable’. Much ordinary distress is tied to such knots. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What thought about yourself feels most like a knot?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Are the knots mistaken beliefs, or attachments? Does cutting one cut the other?

A contemplation exercise When you next say ‘I am …’ or ‘this is mine’, notice what it ties you to.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
