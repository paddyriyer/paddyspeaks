Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 1 of 25

The good and the pleasant

Death’s teaching begins: the good and the pleasant are different, both lay hold of a person, and the one who chooses the pleasant misses the goal.

5 Faithful English translation

‘Other [is] the better, and quite other the dearer; these two, with different aims, bind a person. Of these two, it becomes good for the one taking the better; he falls away from the goal who just chooses the dearer.’

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘The good is one thing, the pleasant quite another. Both, with their different ends, take hold of a person. It goes well with the one who takes the good; whoever chooses the pleasant misses the goal.’

Key concepts श्रेयस् śreyas, the good प्रेयस् preyas, the pleasant मृत्यु death विद्या vidyā अविद्या avidyā पुरुष person मोक्ष liberation

7 Explain it to a beginner

Death has tested Naciketas with every pleasure he could offer, and the boy has refused them all. Now Death begins to teach, and he starts with a choice everyone faces. There is what is good for us, and there is what is pleasant to us. They are not the same thing, and they pull in different directions; both have a hold on every person. Whoever takes the good does well. Whoever chooses the merely pleasant misses the real aim of life. The verse does not say that pleasure is wicked. It says that the pleasant and the good are two different things, and that a life is shaped by which of the two we allow to decide. Everything Death teaches afterwards rests on this first distinction.

An analogy Like two roads leaving a town by the same gate: one is pleasant to walk at first, the other goes where you actually need to arrive.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does Death say first?

That the good (śreyas) and the pleasant (preyas) are different, that both lay hold of a person, and that taking the good goes well while choosing the pleasant loses the goal. It is the first sentence of Death’s teaching, after the tests of 1.1.21–29.

Traditional Interpretation

Why does the teaching begin here?

Śaṅkara: Death speaks having tested the pupil and found him fit for knowledge — परीक्ष्य शिष्यं विद्यायोग्यतां चावगम्याह. The good is the highest good, श्रेयः निःश्रेयसं; the pleasant is ‘the dearer’, प्रेयः प्रियतरमपि. The two are knowledge and ignorance, ताभ्यां विद्याविद्याभ्याम्, and one person cannot pursue both together without giving one up: अन्यतरापरित्यागेनैकेन पुरुषेण सहानुष्ठातुमशक्यत्वात्. The one who chooses the pleasant falls from the lasting aim: प्रयोजनान्नित्यात् प्रच्यवत इत्यर्थः.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Raṅgarāmānuja read it?

As Death’s praise of the desire for release, once he has tested the pupil and found that desire steady: एवं शिष्यं परीक्ष्य, तस्य मुमुक्षा स्थैर्यं निश्चित्य. The good is the path of release, अतिप्रशस्तं मोक्षवर्त्माऽपि अन्यत्; the pleasant is the path of enjoyment, प्रियत्वास्पदं भोगवर्त्मापि अन्यत्. Whoever chooses the pleasant falls from the human aim: स पुरुषार्थाद्भ्रष्टो भवति.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What exactly are śreyas and preyas?

Two comparatives in -īyas: śreyas of praśasya ‘praiseworthy’ (P. 5.3.60 (प्रशस्यस्य श्रः)), preyas of priya ‘dear’. Literally ‘the better’ and ‘the dearer’. Śaṅkara keeps the comparative in his gloss प्रियतरमपि: the pleasant is not bad, only dearer — preferred now.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.2.1

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The good is knowledge and the highest good; the pleasant is ignorance and worldly prosperity. Every person seeking prosperity or immortality acts for one of them: प्रेयःश्रेयसोर्हि अभ्युदयामृतत्वार्थी पुरुषः प्रवर्तते. Since they are knowledge and ignorance, they exclude each other.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.2.1

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The good is the path of release, the pleasant the path of enjoyment: अतिप्रशस्तं मोक्षवर्त्माऽपि अन्यत्. The verse praises the pupil’s desire for release.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Both commentaries read the pair as the way to release against the way of enjoyment; Śaṅkara also identifies them with knowledge and ignorance, which cannot be combined. Madhva’s comment on the passage 1.2.1–3 is a single gloss on a word of 1.2.3; his text reads uteva for utaiva here.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • अन्यच्छ्रेयोऽन्यदुतैव anyacchreyo'nyadutaiva → अन्यत् + श्रेयः + अन्यत् + उत + एव

    t before ś; aḥ before a; t before a vowel; vṛddhi

    anyat + śreyaḥ: t becomes c before ś, P. 8.4.40 (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः), and the ś becomes ch, P. 8.4.63 (शश्छोऽटि). śreyaḥ before a: the visarga becomes u, P. 6.1.113 (अतो रोरप्लुतादप्लुते), a + u give o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः), and the a that follows is taken into the o, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति) — the avagraha marks it. anyat before u: t becomes d, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते). uta + eva: a + e give ai, P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि).

  • प्रेयस्ते उभे preyaste ubhe → प्रेयः + ते + उभे

    visarga before t; pragṛhya dual

    preyaḥ before t: the visarga becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः). te is a dual ending in e and so pragṛhya, P. 1.1.11 (ईदूदेद्द्विवचनं प्रगृह्यम्): it does not combine with the u of ubhe, P. 6.1.125 (प्लुतप्रगृह्या अचि नित्यम्).

  • पुरुषं सिनीतः puruṣaṃ sinītaḥ → पुरुषम् + सिनीतः

    final m before a consonant

    Final m before a consonant becomes anusvāra: P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • श्रेय आददानस्य śreya ādadānasya → श्रेयः + आददानस्य

    aḥ before a vowel other than a

    The visarga after a becomes y before a vowel, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), and the y is dropped, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य). The hiatus that results is not joined again.

  • हीयतेऽर्थाद्य उ hīyate'rthādya u → हीयते + अर्थात् + यः + उ

    e before a; t before a voiced sound; aḥ before u

    hīyate + arthāt: the a is taken into the e, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति). arthāt before y: t becomes d, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते). yaḥ before u: the visarga becomes y and is dropped, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य).

  • प्रेयो वृणीते preyo vṛṇīte → प्रेयः + वृणीते

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    The visarga becomes u before the voiced v, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च), and a + u give o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

Junctions outside the general rules

  • ते · उभे — pragṛhya: te ubhe: the dual te is pragṛhya, P. 1.1.11 (ईदूदेद्द्विवचनं प्रगृह्यम्), and does not combine with the following vowel, P. 6.1.125 (प्लुतप्रगृह्या अचि नित्यम्).

Morphology

श्रेयः
Comparative in -īyas, P. 5.3.57 (द्विवचनविभज्योपपदे तरबीयसुनौ), of praśasya ‘praiseworthy’, which is replaced by śra- before it, P. 5.3.60 (प्रशस्यस्य श्रः): literally ‘the better’.
प्रेयः
Comparative of priya ‘dear’, which becomes pra- before -īyas, P. 6.4.157 (प्रियस्थिरस्फिरोरुबहुलगुरुवृद्धतृप्रदीर्घवृन्दारकाणां प्रस्थस्फवर्बंहिगर्वर्षित्रब्द्राघिवृन्दाः): literally ‘the dearer’. The verse sets two comparatives against each other, not good against evil.
सिनीतः
Present dual of si ‘bind’, ninth class (stem sinā-, weak sinī-). The two things ‘bind’ a person: they lay a claim on him.
हीयते
Passive of hā ‘leave’: ‘is left’, ‘falls short’. With the ablative arthāt: ‘is deprived of the goal’.
आददानस्य
Present participle middle of ā-dā ‘take [for oneself]’, in the genitive of the person concerned: ‘for the one taking [the good] it is well’.

Anvaya

श्रेयः अन्यत्, उत प्रेयः एव अन्यत् । ते उभे नानार्थे [सती] पुरुषम् सिनीतः । तयोः श्रेयः आददानस्य साधु भवति । यः उ प्रेयः वृणीते [सः] अर्थात् हीयते ।

śreyaḥ anyat, uta preyaḥ eva anyat | te ubhe nānārthe satī puruṣam sinītaḥ | tayoḥ śreyaḥ ādadānasya sādhu bhavati | yaḥ u preyaḥ vṛṇīte saḥ arthāt hīyate |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Three sentences. Two nominal clauses with the copula understood (anyac chreyaḥ, anyad … preyaḥ: ‘one thing is the good, another the pleasant’); a statement about both, with te ubhe as subject and sinītaḥ a dual verb; then a contrast: the genitive participle ādadānasya with sādhu bhavati (‘it goes well for the one taking’), and a relative clause, ya u preyo vṛṇīte, whose understood correlative is the subject of hīyate.

Ambiguities

In what sense do the two ‘bind’?
Śaṅkara: everyone who acts at all acts for one or the other, and so is said to be bound by both: अतः श्रेयःप्रेयःप्रयोजनकर्तव्यतया ताभ्यां बद्ध इत्युच्यते सर्वः पुरुषः. Raṅgarāmānuja: they bring a person under their control, पुरुषं स्ववशताम् आपादयतः इत्यर्थः.
utaiva or uteva
The base, GRETIL, the vishvAsa mūla, Śaṅkara and Raṅgarāmānuja read uta eva, ‘and quite [other]’. Madhva’s text reads uteva, uta iva, ‘and as it were [other]’ (corrections.json). The base reading makes the difference absolute; Madhva’s would soften it, though his bhāṣya says nothing on the word.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 25

The first half has the regular 22 syllables (11 + 11). The second half counts 25: the third pāda, tayoḥ śreya ādadānasya sādhu bhavati, has 14; the fourth, hīyate ’rthād ya u preyo vṛṇīte, the regular 11. The hiatuses śreya ā- and ya u are counted as written.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: अन्यच्छ्रेयोऽन्यदुतेव प्रेयस् — Madhva’s text reads uteva (uta iva, ‘and as it were’) for utaiva (uta eva, ‘and indeed’).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Technology and digital distractions

Much of what competes for attention today is designed to be preferred in the moment — the next video, the next notification. The verse’s word for it, preyas, is exactly that: ‘the more preferred’. It does not call it evil. It only observes that what we prefer now and what is good for us are different things, and that a life drifts toward whichever one is allowed to choose. This is a reflection on the teaching, not part of it.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Name one choice this week where the pleasant and the good pointed in different directions.

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Is the good always unpleasant at first, or does the verse only say that the two are different?

A contemplation exercise Before one ordinary decision today, ask plainly: which of these is the pleasant, and which the good?

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
