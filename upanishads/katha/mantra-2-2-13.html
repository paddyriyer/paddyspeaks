Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 13 of 15

The eternal among the eternal

The eternal among the eternal, the conscious among the conscious, the one who grants the desires of many: those wise ones who see him abiding in themselves have lasting peace, and not others.

5 Faithful English translation

Eternal among the eternal, conscious among the conscious, who, one, grants the desires of many — the wise who perceive him abiding in [their] self, theirs [is] everlasting peace, not of others.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

He is the eternal among things eternal, the conscious among the conscious, the one who grants what the many desire. The wise who see him dwelling within themselves have lasting peace; others do not.

Key concepts आत्मन् self, Self शान्ति peace धीर the wise जीव individual self ईश्वर the Lord

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse is a close twin of the one before it. It calls the inner self ‘the eternal among the eternal, the conscious among the conscious’. In other words, whatever lasts and whatever is aware, he is the deepest form of it. He alone answers the wishes of all the many beings. The wise who find him within themselves gain a peace that lasts; others do not. Śaṅkara explains that all awareness comes from this self, just as water only burns your hand because fire has heated it. Raṅgarāmānuja says there are many eternal conscious souls, and the one Lord is the eternal conscious being who gives them what they need. Madhva insists that one must know God as dwelling within, not as being the same as oneself.

An analogy Many lamps glow, but every glow in the room comes from the one source of power they are wired to.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

He is eternal among the eternal, conscious among the conscious, and alone grants the desires of the many; the wise who see him within have lasting peace, and others do not.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the source of all awareness

He is the imperishable among the imperishable, नित्यः अविनाशी नित्यानाम् अविनाशिनाम्, and the conscious among the conscious, from Brahmā down. As burning in water and the like comes from fire, so awareness in all others comes from the consciousness of the self. As the all-knowing Lord he grants without effort the fruits of action, and the desires that come of his own grace, कर्मानुरूपं कामान् कर्मफलानि स्वानुग्रहनिमित्तांश्च कामान्. For those who see him the peace is everlasting and is their own self, शान्तिः उपरतिः शाश्वती नित्या स्वात्मभूतैव स्यात्.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: one among many eternal souls

The one eternal conscious being gives the many eternal conscious beings what they seek, without effort, नित्यश्चेतन एक एव सन् बहूनां नित्यानां चेतनानाम् अपेक्षितार्थान् अनायासेन प्रयच्छति; the rest is plain, शिष्टं स्पष्टम्.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: within, not one with the soul

He compares the phrasing to ‘the self alone was this in the beginning’, read with regard to beginninglessness, आत्मैवेदमग्र आसीत् इत्यादिवदनादित्वापेक्षया. Then verses cited by him (इति च): knowing Hari as abiding in the self, one is freed, आत्मनि स्थं हरिं जानन् मुच्यते नात्र संशयः; knowing him as one with the soul, one falls into blind darkness, जीवैक्येन तु तं जानन् तमस्यन्धे पतेद् ध्रुवम्.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

He is the consciousness in all the conscious, as fire is the burning in heated water; the peace of those who see him is their own self, स्वात्मभूतैव स्यात्.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.2.13

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The many souls are themselves eternal and conscious; the one Lord, eternal and conscious, provides for them, नित्यश्चेतन एक एव सन् बहूनां नित्यानां चेतनानाम् अपेक्षितार्थान् अनायासेन प्रयच्छति.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.2.13

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

To know Hari within is release; to know him as identical with the soul is ruin, जीवैक्येन तु तं जानन् तमस्यन्धे पतेद् ध्रुवम् (a verse cited by him).

Where they disagree

‘Conscious among the conscious’ says for Śaṅkara that all awareness is his; for Raṅgarāmānuja and Madhva that there are many conscious souls and one supreme conscious being over them. Madhva states outright that seeing him as one with the soul is the error.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • नित्यो नित्यानां nityo nityānāṃ → नित्यः + नित्यानाम्

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

  • चेतनश्चेतनानामेको cetanaścetanānāmeko → चेतनः + चेतनानाम् + एकः

    visarga before c; m before a vowel

    Visarga before c becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः), and s before c becomes ś, P. 8.4.40 (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः); the final m stays before a vowel and is joined to it.

  • यो विदधाति yo vidadhāti → यः + विदधाति

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

  • येऽनुपश्यन्ति ye'nupaśyanti → ये + अनुपश्यन्ति

    e + a

    After e at the end of a word an initial a is merged into it, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति); the avagraha marks it.

  • नेतरेषाम् netareṣām → न + इतरेषाम्

    a + i

    a + i give e, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • आत्मस्थम् ātmastham

    upapada-tatpuruṣa · आत्मन् + स्थ

    Vigraha आत्मनि तिष्ठतीति आत्मस्थः

    abiding in the self

Morphology

विदधाति
Present of vi + √dhā, ‘set out, arrange, grant’, a reduplicating root (dadhāti).
नित्यानाम्
A partitive genitive: ‘the eternal among the eternal’, as cetanaś cetanānām, ‘the conscious among the conscious’. All witnesses of this edition read nityo nityānām; Śaṅkara glosses it so, नित्यानाम् अविनाशिनाम्.
शाश्वती
Feminine of śāśvata, agreeing with śāntiḥ; 2.2.12 had śāśvatam sukham.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

As in 2.2.12: a string of nominatives with partitive genitives (nityo nityānām, cetanaś cetanānām), closed by the relative clause yo vidadhāti kāmān with ekaḥ bahūnām set against each other, then the same second line with śāntiḥ śāśvatī in place of sukhaṃ śāśvatam.

Ambiguities

‘Conscious among the conscious’
Śaṅkara: he is the source of consciousness in all who are conscious, as fire is the cause of burning in water and other things that are not fire, अग्निनिमित्तमिव दाहकत्वमनग्नीनामुदकादीनामात्मचैतन्यनिमित्तमेव चेतयितृत्वमन्येषाम्. Raṅgarāmānuja: the one eternal conscious being who grants the many eternal conscious beings what they need, नित्यश्चेतन एक एव सन् बहूनां नित्यानां चेतनानाम् अपेक्षितार्थान् अनायासेन प्रयच्छति.
How is he ‘in the self’ to be seen?
Madhva: whoever knows Hari as abiding in the self is released, आत्मनि स्थं हरिं जानन् मुच्यते नात्र संशयः; whoever knows him as one with the soul falls into blind darkness, जीवैक्येन तु तं जानन् तमस्यन्धे पतेद् ध्रुवम् (verses cited by him, इति च). For Śaṅkara, the same seeing is the self’s own peace.

Metre

irregular · syllables counted as written: 24 + 22

Counted as written, 24 and 22 syllables. The second line is a regular triṣṭubh (11 + 11). The first has 12 + 12: nityo nityānāṃ cetanaś cetanānām and eko bahūnāṃ yo vidadhāti kāmān each have twelve, a jagatī-length line inside a triṣṭubh verse.

Textual variations between witnesses

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Psychology and inner life

Desires granted bring satisfaction; the verse speaks of peace, which it ties to seeing rather than to getting. The difference between the two is familiar from ordinary life. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What is the difference between having what you want and being at peace?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Is ‘conscious among the conscious’ one consciousness in many, or one conscious being among many? What would decide it?

A contemplation exercise Before asking for something today, notice whether you are seeking a thing or seeking peace.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
