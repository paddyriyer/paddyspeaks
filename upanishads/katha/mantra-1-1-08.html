Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 8 of 29

What an unfed guest takes away

The household continues: a guest left fasting in one’s house destroys the host’s hopes, merit, children and cattle.

5 Faithful English translation

Hope and expectation, [the fruit of] company and pleasant speech, the merit of sacrifices and works, and sons and cattle, all — this he takes away from the man of little understanding in whose house a brāhmaṇa stays without eating.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘Hope and expectation, the good of friendship and kind words, the merit of rites and good works, sons and cattle — all of it is lost to the foolish man in whose house a brāhmaṇa stays unfed.’

Key concepts अतिथि guest कर्मन् action, ritual work धर्म dharma

7 Explain it to a beginner

The household’s warning goes on. If a guest stays in your house without food, the consequences are drastic: everything you hope for and expect, the good that comes from friendship and kind words, the merit of your religious rites and charitable works, even your children and cattle — all of it is lost. The list is deliberately sweeping. It covers the future, social life, religious merit and family. The point is not a magical punishment so much as a judgement: a person who can leave a guest hungry has already lost what makes those good things good. And the warning is addressed to Death himself, who has let a boy wait three nights at his door.

An analogy Like a company that neglects a single customer so badly that word spreads and its whole reputation, built over years, collapses.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the mantra say?

A brāhmaṇa who stays unfed in a foolish man’s house takes away his hopes and expectations, the fruit of company and kind speech, the merit of sacrifices and works, and his sons and cattle.

Traditional Interpretation

What do the terms mean?

Śaṅkara: āśā is अनिर्ज्ञातप्राप्येष्टार्थप्रार्थना आशा, pratīkṣā निर्ज्ञातप्राप्यार्थप्रतीक्षणं प्रतीक्षा; iṣṭa and pūrta are इष्टं यागजं फलं पूर्तम् आरामादिक्रियाजं फलम्. Raṅgarāmānuja: sūnṛtā is सत्यप्रियवाचम्, speech both true and kind. Śaṅkara’s conclusion: तस्मादनुपेक्षणीयः सर्वावस्थास्वप्यतिथिरित्यर्थः, a guest must never be neglected.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What is vṛṅkte?

A class-7 present of vṛj, ‘remove’: Śaṅkara glosses वृङ्क्ते वर्जयति विनाशयतीत्येतत्. Raṅgarāmānuja gives the grammar (nasal infix, Dhātupāṭha references).

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • पुत्रपशूंश्च putrapaśūṃśca → पुत्रपशून् + च

    n before c

    A final n before c becomes anusvāra followed by ś, P. 8.3.7 (नश्छव्यप्रशान्) with P. 8.3.4 (अनुनासिकात्‌ परोऽनुस्वारः).

  • एतद्वृङ्क्ते etadvṛṅkte → एतत् + वृङ्क्ते

    t before a voiced consonant

    t becomes d before a voiced sound, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

  • यस्यानश्नन्वसति yasyānaśnanvasati → यस्य + अनश्नन् + वसति

    savarṇa-dīrgha

    a + a → ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः); the final n stands before v.

Junctions outside the general rules

  • च · इष्टापूर्ते — unjoined hiatus (as printed): The base prints ca iṣṭāpūrte unjoined (Śaṅkara’s mūla); the vishvAsa mūla joins ceṣṭāpūrte (corrections.json).

    one whose understanding is small

Morphology

वृङ्क्ते
Present middle of vṛj (class 7, nasal infix), ‘wrench away, remove’. Raṅgarāmānuja notes the class-7 form and an alternative derivation.
आशाप्रतीक्षे
Dual of a dvandva of two feminines. Śaṅkara distinguishes them: अनिर्ज्ञातप्राप्येष्टार्थप्रार्थना आशा and निर्ज्ञातप्राप्यार्थप्रतीक्षणं प्रतीक्षा.

Anvaya

यस्य अल्पमेधसः पुरुषस्य गृहे ब्राह्मणः अनश्नन् वसति, [तस्य] आशाप्रतीक्षे सङ्गतम् सूनृताम् च इष्टापूर्ते सर्वान् पुत्रपशून् च एतत् [सः] वृङ्क्ते ॥

yasya alpamedhasaḥ puruṣasya gṛhe brāhmaṇaḥ anaśnan vasati, tasya āśāpratīkṣe saṅgatam sūnṛtām ca iṣṭāpūrte sarvān putrapaśūn ca etat saḥ vṛṅkte ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A long list of objects, summed up by etat (‘all this’), then the verb vṛṅkte and the genitive of the person deprived, with a relative clause (‘in whose house a brāhmaṇa stays unfed’). The subject of vṛṅkte is the unfed guest (or the fault of not feeding him).

Ambiguities

Who or what takes these away?
Grammatically the subject of vṛṅkte is the brāhmaṇa who stays unfed; Raṅgarāmānuja makes it the sin of his fasting, एतत् अनशनरूपं पापं वृङ्क्ते.
saṅgatam
Śaṅkara: the fruit of association with the good; Raṅgarāmānuja: the company of the good itself (सत्सङ्गमम्).

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 23 + 24

Irregular triṣṭubh: 23 + 24 syllables as written. The first line counts the unjoined hiatus ca iṣṭāpūrte (22 if joined); the last pāda (yasyānaśnan vasati brāhmaṇo gṛhe) has twelve syllables, jagatī-like.

Textual variations between witnesses

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Corporate life

Institutions that neglect the people who come to them in need may keep their assets for a while, but lose what those assets were for. This is a modern reflection, not the text’s claim.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Whose need in your life is easiest to overlook?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why would neglecting one guest destroy merit gained in quite other ways?

A contemplation exercise Offer food or drink to someone today who would not expect it from you.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
