Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 11 of 15

The sun is not soiled by the eye

As the sun, the eye of the whole world, is not soiled by the outward faults of the eye, so the one inner self of all beings is not soiled by the sorrow of the world, being outside it.

5 Faithful English translation

As the sun, the eye of the whole world, is not soiled by the eye’s outward faults, so the one inner self of all beings is not soiled by the sorrow of the world, [being] outside [it].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

The sun is the eye of the whole world, yet it is not stained by what is wrong with anyone’s eyes. So the one self within all beings is not stained by the world’s sorrow, for it stands outside it.

Key concepts अन्तरात्मन् the inner self सूर्य the Sun आत्मन् self, Self शोक grief, sorrow जीव individual self अविद्या avidyā

7 Explain it to a beginner

If the same self lives in every being, does it suffer every being’s pain? This verse answers with the sun. The sun lights up everything we see, so it is like the eye of the whole world. Yet when someone’s eyes are sore or they look at something dirty, the sun is not made sore or dirty. In the same way, the one self inside all beings is not stained by the sorrows of the world. Śaṅkara explains that suffering belongs to the confused picture we lay over the self, as a rope can be mistaken for a snake without the rope ever becoming one. Raṅgarāmānuja says the supreme Self is by its very nature free of fault, though it dwells in all. Madhva says God stays untouched because he is independent, while souls, who depend on him, are the ones who suffer.

An analogy Sunlight falls on a rubbish heap and on a garden alike, and comes away from neither any dirtier.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

As the sun, the world’s eye, is not soiled by the faults of eyes, so the one inner self of all beings is not soiled by the world’s sorrow: it is outside it.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: sorrow is superimposed

The objection: if one self is the self of all, its sorrow would be the supreme’s. The answer: the world experiences sorrow born of desire and action through ignorance superimposed on itself, लोको ह्यविद्यया स्वात्मन्यध्यस्तया कामकर्मोद्भवं दुःखमनुभवति, but it is not in the self in truth, न तु सा परमार्थतः स्वात्मनि. As a snake, silver, water or dirt seen in a rope, shell, desert or sky are not faults of those things, यथा रज्जुशुक्तिकोषरगगनेषु सर्परजतोदकमलानि, so the self is outside the false superimposition, विपरीतबुद्ध्यध्यासबाह्यो हि स इति.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: free of evil by nature

Though both are selves, the faults of the individual self do not arise in the supreme Self, आत्मत्वाविशेषेऽपि जीवात्मवत् दोषाः परमात्मनि न भवन्ति इत्येतत् सदृष्टान्तमाह. As the sun presides over the eye and is within it, yet is not touched by the eye’s discharges, यथा सूर्यः चक्षुरधिष्ठातृतया तदन्तर्गतोऽपि, बहिर्निर्गतैश्चक्षुर्मलादिभिः न स्पृश्यते, so the supreme Self dwelling in all is untouched, being by nature free of evil and different from all else. He cites the Aitareya, आदित्यश्चक्षुर्भूत्वाऽक्षिणी प्राविशत्, as cited by him.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: the independent is not soiled by the dependent

He reads the sun as the deity of the inner eye, with the outer eye as its reflection: सूर्यो यथाऽऽन्तरं चक्षुः प्रतिबिम्बोऽस्य बाह्यकः; the inner eye is not soiled by the faults of the outer, बाह्यचक्षुर्गतैर्दोषैरन्तश्चक्षुर्न लिप्यते. So the Supreme Person is ‘outside’ the souls by his independence, एवं बाह्यस्स्वतन्त्रत्वाज्जीवेभ्यः पुरुषोत्तमः, and is not soiled by the sorrows of the dependent soul, अस्वतन्त्रस्य जीवस्य दुःखैर्नैव हि लिप्यते.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

One word, two senses

bāhya closes both lines: the faults are ‘outward’ (of the eye), and the self is ‘outside’ (the world’s sorrow). Each commentator explains the second ‘outside’ by his own doctrine.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.2.11

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The world’s sorrow is superimposed on the self by ignorance and is not in it in truth, न तु सा परमार्थतः स्वात्मनि.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.2.11

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The supreme Self, though present in all as their self, is untouched by their faults because it is by nature free of evil and different from all else, विलक्षणत्वात् इत्यर्थः.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.2.11

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The Lord is outside the souls by his independence and is not soiled by the sorrows of the dependent soul, अस्वतन्त्रस्य जीवस्य दुःखैर्नैव हि लिप्यते.

Where they disagree

All three deny that the inner self suffers the world’s sorrow, for different reasons: the sorrow is a superimposition (Śaṅkara); the supreme Self is free of evil by nature though it dwells in the sufferer (Raṅgarāmānuja); the sufferer is another, dependent being (Madhva).

    the inner self of all beings

Morphology

लिप्यते
Passive present of √lip, ‘smear’: ‘is smeared, stained’. The agent stands in the instrumental (bāhyadoṣaiḥ, lokaduḥkhena).
चाक्षुषैः
A secondary derivative of cakṣus with vṛddhi of the first syllable: ‘relating to the eye’.
बाह्यः
bāhya, ‘outside’, from bahis; it closes both lines in different senses: the eye’s faults are ‘outward’, and the self is ‘outside’ the world’s sorrow.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

A yathā … tathā comparison. In the first line sūryaḥ is the subject of the passive na lipyate, with sarvalokasya cakṣuḥ in apposition and cākṣuṣair bāhyadoṣaiḥ as the instrumental agent. The second line repeats the frame with sarvabhūtāntarātmā as subject and lokaduḥkhena as agent; bāhyaḥ at the end gives the reason.

Ambiguities

Why does the question arise?
Śaṅkara: if the one self is the self of all, the sorrow of the world would seem to belong to the supreme, एकस्य सर्वात्मत्वे संसारदुःखित्वं परस्यैव स्यादिति प्राप्ते, इदमुच्यते. Raṅgarāmānuja: though the supreme Self is equally a self, the faults of the individual self do not reach it, आत्मत्वाविशेषेऽपि जीवात्मवत् दोषाः परमात्मनि न भवन्ति.
In what sense is the self ‘outside’?
Śaṅkara: outside the false superimposition, as a rope is outside the snake seen in it, विपरीतबुद्ध्यध्यासबाह्यो हि स इति. Raṅgarāmānuja: outside everything else by nature, being free of evil and different from all, विलक्षणत्वात् इत्यर्थः. Madhva: the Lord is outside the souls by his independence, एवं बाह्यस्स्वतन्त्रत्वाज्जीवेभ्यः पुरुषोत्तमः.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Ethics and wellbeing

People who care for others in pain often speak of being present without being swept away. The image of light that touches everything and is soiled by nothing gives a picture of that steadiness. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Can you be fully present to someone’s pain without being stained by it?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Śaṅkara’s rope and snake, Raṅgarāmānuja’s nature free of evil, Madhva’s independence: which explanation needs the most, and which the least?

A contemplation exercise Sit in sunlight for a minute and notice how it falls on everything around you without being changed by any of it.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
