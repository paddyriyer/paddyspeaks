Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 21 of 29

Even the gods doubted this: choose another boon

Yama answers the third boon by refusing it: the matter is subtle, the gods themselves were in doubt, and he asks Naciketas to release him from it.

5 Faithful English translation

Even by the gods, here, it was doubted of old; for not easily known well [is it]: subtle [is] this principle. Choose another boon, Naciketas; do not press me; release this [boon] for me.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Even the gods once doubted about this. It is not easy to know; it is a subtle thing. Choose some other boon, Naciketas. Do not hold me to it — let me off this one.

Key concepts मृत्यु death आत्मन् self, Self धर्म dharma विद्या vidyā

7 Explain it to a beginner

Naciketas has asked Death for his third gift: to know what becomes of a person after death. Death does not answer. Instead he pushes the question back. Even the gods argued about this long ago, he says; it is not easy to understand, because the thing in question is very fine and hard to grasp. So choose something else, he says, and do not hold me to my promise. This is not a refusal out of meanness. The commentators say Death is testing the boy: is he really ready for such knowledge, or will he be satisfied with something easier? A teacher who hands out the deepest teaching to anyone who asks may waste it. The rest of the vallī shows the test.

An analogy Like a master craftsman who tells an eager apprentice, ‘This joint is too hard for you — learn something simpler first,’ to see whether he will insist.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does Yama say?

That the gods themselves doubted about this, that it is subtle and not easily known, and that Naciketas should choose another boon and not press him.

Traditional Interpretation

Why does Death refuse?

Śaṅkara: to test whether the boy is fit for the knowledge of the self that is the means to the highest good — किमयमेकान्ततो निःश्रेयससाधनात्मज्ञानार्हो न वेत्येतत्परीक्षणार्थमाह. The subject is subtle — अणुः सूक्ष्मः एषः आत्माख्यः धर्मः — and not easily understood even when heard many times by ordinary people.

Traditional Interpretation

Why ‘do not press me’?

Śaṅkara’s image: do not press me as a creditor presses a debtor — उपरोधं मा कार्षीः अधमर्णमिवोत्तमर्णः — and release the boon: अतिसृज विमुञ्च एनं वरं मा मां प्रति. Yama had promised three boons; he now asks to be let off the third.

Comparative Vedānta

What is meant by dharma?

Madhva’s whole comment on the verse is a gloss of this word: धारकत्वात् धर्मो भगवान्, the Lord, called dharma because he upholds all. Raṅgarāmānuja takes it of the self the boy asked about: the gods were in doubt अस्मिन् मुक्तात्मस्वरूपे, about the nature of the freed self.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.21

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The subtle principle is the self (ātman); Death’s reluctance is a test of the pupil’s fitness for the knowledge that alone gives the highest good: किमयमेकान्ततो निःश्रेयससाधनात्मज्ञानार्हो न वेत्येतत्परीक्षणार्थमाह.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.1.21

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The question is about the nature of the freed self, and the gods, who see much, were in doubt about it: बहुदर्शिभिरपि देवैः अस्मिन् मुक्तात्मस्वरूपे विचिकित्सितम्.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.21

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The ‘subtle dharma’ is the Lord, so called as the upholder of all: धारकत्वात् धर्मो भगवान्. This line is Madhva’s whole comment on the verse.

Where they disagree

All three read the refusal as a test. They differ on what is too subtle to give away: the self (Śaṅkara), the nature of the freed self (Raṅgarāmānuja), or the Lord who upholds everything (Madhva).

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • देवैरत्रापि devairatrāpi → देवैः + अत्र + अपि

    visarga before a vowel; savarṇa-dīrgha

    devaiḥ before the vowel a: the visarga goes back to r and is kept (devair). atra + api: a + a fuse into ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • अणुरेष धर्मः aṇureṣa dharmaḥ → अणुः + एषः + धर्मः

    visarga as r; eṣaḥ before a consonant

    aṇuḥ before e shows its r (aṇur eṣa). eṣaḥ drops its visarga before a consonant, P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि).

  • नचिकेतो वृणीष्व naciketo vṛṇīṣva → नचिकेतः + वृणीष्व

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    The visarga (ru) becomes u before the voiced v, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च), and a + u gives o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • मा मोपरोत्सीरति mā moparotsīrati → मा + मा + उपरोत्सीः + अति

    guṇa; visarga as r

    mā + uparotsīḥ: ā + u give o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः). uparotsīḥ before the vowel of ati keeps its r.

  • सृजैनम् sṛjainam → सृज + एनम्

    vṛddhi

    a + e give ai, P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • सुविज्ञेयम् suvijñeyam

    prādi-tatpuruṣa · सु + विज्ञेय

    Vigraha सुष्ठु विज्ञेयम्

    well and easily to be known; the vigraha is Śaṅkara’s own gloss

Morphology

विचिकित्सितम्
Past participle of the desiderative of kit, vicikitsate ‘wishes to discern, doubts’; used impersonally with the instrumental devaiḥ: ‘it was doubted by the gods’.
उपरोत्सीः
Aorist of upa-rudh without augment, the injunctive form required after the prohibitive mā: ‘do not constrain’.
सृज
With ati, separated by mā (tmesis, common in Vedic): ati … sṛja, ‘give up, release’.
एनम्
The anaphoric pronoun ena-: ‘this one’ — the boon just asked.

Anvaya

देवैः अपि अत्र पुरा विचिकित्सितम् । एषः धर्मः अणुः, न हि सुविज्ञेयम् । नचिकेतः, अन्यम् वरम् वृणीष्व । मा मा उपरोत्सीः । एनम् [वरम्] मा अति सृज ।

devaiḥ api atra purā vicikitsitam | eṣaḥ dharmaḥ aṇuḥ, na hi suvijñeyam | naciketaḥ, anyam varam vṛṇīṣva | mā mā uparotsīḥ | enam varam mā ati sṛja |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Three statements and three commands. The first two clauses are impersonal (vicikitsitam, sc. asti) and give Yama’s reason; hi ties the second to the first. The commands are an imperative (vṛṇīṣva), a prohibition with mā and the injunctive (mā … uparotsīḥ), and a second imperative split by tmesis (ati mā sṛja enam).

Ambiguities

mā mā
Śaṅkara reads the first mā as the prohibitive and the second as the enclitic ‘me’ (मा मां मा उपरोत्सीः); Raṅgarāmānuja instead takes the repetition as emphasis: मा मा इति निषेधे वीप्सायां द्विर्वचनम्, ‘do not, do not press me’.
suvijñeyam or sujñeyam
The base, the vishvAsa mūla and Śaṅkara (who glosses सुष्ठु विज्ञेयम्) read suvijñeyam; GRETIL reads sujñeyam and Madhva’s text sujñeyo (masculine, agreeing with dharmaḥ). The meaning is hardly changed; the metre favours the shorter form (see metre).
What is the dharma here?
Śaṅkara: the self, the thing asked about (आत्माख्यः धर्मः). For Madhva the word means the Lord, as the upholder of all — धारकत्वात् धर्मो भगवान्. Raṅgarāmānuja keeps the sense of the self (the nature of the freed self).

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 24 + 22

Triṣṭubh (4 × 11). As printed the half-lines count 24 and 22: the first pāda (devair atrāpi vicikitsitaṃ purā) has 12, and the second has 12 with suvijñeyam but the regular 11 with the sujñeyam of GRETIL and Madhva’s text.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading GRETIL (after Olivelle 1998): न हि सुज्ञेयम् अणुर् — GRETIL reads sujñeyam, ‘easy to know’, where AS and the vishvAsa mūla read suvijñeyam, ‘easy to know well’ (Śaṅkara: सुष्ठु विज्ञेयम्). The shorter form keeps the triṣṭubh pāda at eleven syllables; Madhva’s text reads sujñeyaḥ.
  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: न हि सुज्ञेयोऽणुरेष धर्मः — Madhva’s text reads sujñeyaḥ for suvijñeyam (see also the next entry); GRETIL also has the shorter su-jñeya.
  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: न हि सुज्ञेयोऽणुरेष धर्मः — Madhva’s text has the masculine sujñeyo, agreeing with aṇur eṣa dharmaḥ, for the base’s neuter suvijñeyam.
  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: मा मोपरोत्सीरति मा सृजैवम् — Madhva’s text reads ati mā sṛja evam, ‘do not press me so’, for ati mā sṛjainam, ‘release this [boon] for me’, which Śaṅkara glosses (अतिसृज विमुञ्च एनं वरं).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Ethical leadership

Good teachers and good leaders sometimes say ‘not yet’ — not to hoard knowledge, but to see whether the person asking really wants it and can carry it. The difference between a test and a brush-off lies in what happens when the student insists. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Has anyone ever made you wait for something you asked for? Was it a test, or a refusal?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Is there knowledge that can do harm if given to someone not ready for it? Who should decide?

A contemplation exercise Think of one question you have stopped asking because the answer seemed too hard. Write it down plainly.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
