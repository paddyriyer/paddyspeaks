Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 19 of 25

Neither slayer nor slain

If the slayer thinks he slays, and the slain thinks he is slain, neither understands: this one does not slay and is not slain.

5 Faithful English translation

‘If the slayer thinks to slay, if the slain thinks [himself] slain, both of them do not understand: this one does not slay, is not slain.’

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘If the killer thinks he kills, and the killed thinks he is killed, neither of them understands. The self does not kill, and it is not killed.’

Key concepts आत्मन् self, Self जीव individual self मृत्यु death अविद्या avidyā

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse follows straight from the last. If someone thinks, ‘I am going to kill him,’ and the victim thinks, ‘I have been killed,’ both are mistaken about what they are. The self does not kill and cannot be killed. Śaṅkara says both people are looking only at the body and calling it the self. Raṅgarāmānuja says the same: they identify the self with the body. This is not a licence for violence; the verse speaks about what the self is, not about whether an act is right, and a few verses later the Upaniṣad says that someone who has not stopped doing wrong cannot reach this knowledge. The Bhagavad Gītā uses nearly the same lines when Kṛṣṇa speaks to Arjuna on the battlefield.

An analogy Like two actors who forget they are acting: the one with the stage sword thinks he has killed, the other thinks he has died.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

That whoever thinks of the self as slaying or slain does not understand it: the self neither slays nor is slain.

Traditional Interpretation

Why does neither understand?

Śaṅkara: both take the body for the self; the self does not act, being changeless, and is not acted on, being like space: आकाशवदविक्रियत्वादेव. So the round of dharma and adharma belongs only to the one who does not know the self, अतोऽनात्मज्ञविषय एव धर्माधर्मादिलक्षणः संसारः नात्मज्ञस्य.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Raṅgarāmānuja use the verse?

With 1.2.18 it is a ground for the Brahmasūtra topic on the individual self’s non-origin, इमौ मन्त्रौ प्रस्तुत्य वियत्पादे चिन्तितम्: the self is not produced, आत्मा न उत्पद्यते. Its ‘origin’ in scripture is a change in the contraction and expansion of its knowledge, not in its nature, ज्ञानसंकोचविकासलक्षणस्वभावान्यथाभावरूपा उत्पत्तिः.

Traditional Interpretation

And Madhva?

His comment is one line: since even the individual self has no death of its own, both fail to understand, जीवस्यापि स्वतो मरणाभावादुभौ तौ न विजानीतः.

Contemporary Reflection

Is this a licence to kill?

No reading among the commentaries treats it so. The verse is about what the self is; the Kaṭha’s conditions for knowing the self, in 1.2.24, begin with ceasing from wrongdoing. (A modern reflection.)

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.2.19

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The changeless self is neither agent nor object of killing; action and its results belong to ignorance of the self: अतोऽनात्मज्ञविषय एव धर्माधर्मादिलक्षणः संसारः नात्मज्ञस्य.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.2.19

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The verse concerns the individual self, eternal and unproduced, आत्मा न उत्पद्यते; its changes are changes in the extent of its knowledge, ज्ञानसंकोचविकासलक्षणस्वभावान्यथाभावरूपा उत्पत्तिः.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.2.19

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The individual self does not die of itself: जीवस्यापि स्वतो मरणाभावादुभौ तौ न विजानीतः.

Where they disagree

All three agree that the self is not slain; they differ on whether it is the one self (Śaṅkara) or each individual self (Raṅgarāmānuja, Madhva), and on what its ‘birth’ means.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • चेन्मन्यते cenmanyate → चेत् + मन्यते

    t before a nasal

    A final t before m becomes n, P. 8.4.45 (यरोऽनुनासिकेऽनुनासिको वा).

  • हन्तुं हतश्चेन्मन्यते hantuṃ hataścenmanyate → हन्तुम् + हतः + चेत् + मन्यते

    anusvāra; visarga before c

    Final m → anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः); visarga before c becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः), and s before c becomes ś, P. 8.4.40 (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः); t before m → n as above.

  • विजानीतो नायं vijānīto nāyaṃ → विजानीतः + न + अयम्

    visarga and vowel sandhi

    aḥ before a voiced consonant becomes o, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च); a + a → ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

हन्ता
Agent noun of han in -tṛ: ‘slayer’.
विजानीतः
Present 3 du. of jñā (class 9, jānāti) with vi: ‘the two understand’. The dual matches उभौ तौ.
हन्यते
Present passive of han, set against the active हन्ति: the self is neither the agent nor the object of killing.

Anvaya

हन्ता चेत् हन्तुम् मन्यते, हतः चेत् [आत्मानम्] हतम् मन्यते, तौ उभौ न विजानीतः । अयम् न हन्ति न हन्यते ॥

hantā cet hantum manyate, hataḥ cet ātmānam hatam manyate, tau ubhau na vijānītaḥ | ayam na hanti na hanyate ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two conditional clauses with cet, in parallel (hantā cen manyate hantum; hataś cen manyate hatam), then the main clause ubhau tau na vijānītaḥ, and a reason in two short sentences (nāyaṃ hanti, na hanyate), active against passive.

Ambiguities

Who are ‘the two’ who do not understand?
Śaṅkara: anyone who takes the body alone for the self, शरीरमात्रात्मदृष्टिः, whether he thinks of killing or of being killed. Raṅgarāmānuja: both think so देहात्मदृष्ट्या, identifying the self with the body. Madhva: both are wrong because even the individual self does not die of itself, जीवस्यापि स्वतो मरणाभावादुभौ तौ न विजानीतः.
Does the verse make killing indifferent?
It denies that the self is the agent or the victim of killing; it says nothing about the act. Śaṅkara draws the conclusion that the round of merit and demerit belongs to the one who does not know the self, अतोऽनात्मज्ञविषय एव धर्माधर्मादिलक्षणः संसारः नात्मज्ञस्य. The Kaṭha itself requires a seeker to have ceased from wrongdoing (1.2.24).

    kathaṃ sa puruṣaḥ pārtha kaṃ ghātayati hanti kam

    The Gītā draws a further question from the same teaching: how can one who knows the self as indestructible kill, or cause to kill?

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Violence and identity

Much cruelty begins by reducing a person to a body or a role. The verse insists that neither the one who harms nor the one harmed is only that. Read beside 1.2.24, it is an argument for restraint, not for indifference. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin When someone hurts you, what exactly is hurt?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why would knowing the self as changeless take one outside merit and demerit, in Śaṅkara’s view?

A contemplation exercise The next time you feel attacked, name the role that was attacked, and notice who is naming it.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
