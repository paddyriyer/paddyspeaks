Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 8 of 25

Not by an inferior teacher

Taught by an inferior man, the self is not easily known; taught by one ‘not other’, the wandering ends; it is subtler than the subtle and beyond reasoning.

5 Faithful English translation

‘This [self], taught by an inferior man, is not easily known well, being thought of in many ways. When it is taught by one who is not other, there is no [further] going here; for it is subtler than the atomic in measure, not to be reasoned out.’

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘Taught by an ordinary person, this self is not easily understood, for people think of it in so many ways. But when someone who is one with it teaches it, there is no more wandering about it; for it is subtler than the subtlest thing, and beyond the reach of argument.’

Key concepts तर्क tarka, reasoning आत्मन् self, Self आचार्य ācārya, teacher ज्ञान knowledge ब्रह्मन् Brahman

7 Explain it to a beginner

Death explains why the right teacher matters. If an ordinary person teaches about the self — someone who has only read or heard about it — the pupil will not understand it well, because people have so many conflicting opinions about it: that it exists or not, that it acts or not, and so on. But when the teacher is one who is truly at one with what he teaches, the pupil’s wandering between opinions comes to an end. Why can a teacher not be replaced by clever thinking? Because the self is finer than the finest thing, and argument alone cannot reach it: one person argues for something small, and another argues for something smaller still, and there is no end. The three commentators disagree sharply on who the right teacher is.

An analogy Like learning a path through a marsh: a guide who has only read maps will argue about the way, while one who has walked it simply leads.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

That the self, taught by an inferior man, is not easily known, because people think of it in many ways; that when it is taught by ‘one who is not other’ there is no further ‘going’; and that it is subtler than the subtle and beyond reasoning.

Traditional Interpretation

Why is it not known when an inferior man teaches it?

Śaṅkara: because disputants think of it in many ways — बहुधा अस्ति नास्ति कर्ताकर्ता शुद्धोऽशुद्ध इत्याद्यनेकधा चिन्त्यमानः वादिभिः. The ‘inferior man’ is one of ordinary understanding, हीनेन प्राकृतबुद्धिनेत्येतत्. The knowledge of the self’s oneness is the final end of knowledge: ज्ञानस्य ह्येषा परा निष्ठा यदात्मैकत्वविज्ञानम्.

Traditional Interpretation

Why can reasoning not reach it?

Śaṅkara: if one person establishes the self as atomic, another reasons it subtler still, and another subtler again; reasoning has no resting place, न हि तर्कस्य निष्ठा क्वचिद्विद्यते. Raṅgarāmānuja: since the self is subtler than the subtle, its nature is beyond the reach of reasoning: यतोऽणोरप्यणीयान् आत्मा, अतः तत्स्वरूपं तर्कागोचरम्.

Comparative Vedānta

Who is the teacher ‘not other’?

For Śaṅkara, a teacher who sees no difference and is one with the Brahman he teaches: अपृथग्दर्शिनाचार्येण प्रतिपाद्यब्रह्मात्मभूतेन. For Raṅgarāmānuja, one devoted solely to the self taught, who has realised it: तदेकान्तिना ब्रह्मात्मसाक्षात्कारिणा. For Madhva the ‘non-other’ is precisely the one who does not know the difference between the Lord and himself, and from him no knowledge comes: अन्यो भगवानन्योऽहमित्यजानन्ननन्यः. He supports this from the next verse, प्रोक्ताऽन्येनैव सुज्ञानाय प्रेष्ठ इति वाक्यशेषात्, and cites the Brahmavaivarta: जीवानां चैव विष्णोश्च यो न वेत्ति भिदां पुमान्.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.2.8

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Taught by a teacher who is himself the Brahman he teaches, the self leaves nothing further to know and no further birth: अतः गन्तव्याभावान्न गतिरत्रावशिष्यते.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.2.8

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The teacher who is ‘not other’ is wholly devoted to the self he teaches and has seen it: तदेकान्तिना ब्रह्मात्मसाक्षात्कारिणा. An inferior teacher is one who takes the body for the self, देहात्माभिमानिना.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya on 1.2.4–8

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The ‘non-other’ is the one who does not know that the Lord is other and the self other; taught by him there is no knowledge: तेन प्रोक्ते गतिर्ज्ञानं नास्ति. The next verse, ‘taught by another’, completes the sense.

Where they disagree

The verse is one of the sharpest points of disagreement in the Kaṭha. For Śaṅkara the ‘non-other’ teacher is the one who knows non-difference, and his teaching ends all difference; for Raṅgarāmānuja he is one wholly devoted to the self he has realised; for Madhva the ‘non-other’ is one who denies the difference between the Lord and the self, and from such a teacher no knowledge comes.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • नरेणावरेण nareṇāvareṇa → नरेण + अवरेण

    savarṇa-dīrgha

    a + a give ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • प्रोक्त एष सुविज्ञेयो prokta eṣa suvijñeyo → प्रोक्तः + एषः + सुविज्ञेयः

    aḥ before e; eṣaḥ before a consonant

    proktaḥ before e: the visarga becomes y, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), and is dropped, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य). eṣaḥ drops its visarga before a consonant, P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि).

  • सुविज्ञेयो बहुधा suvijñeyo bahudhā → सुविज्ञेयः + बहुधा

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    P. 6.1.114 (हशि च), P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • गतिरत्र नास्ति gatiratra nāsti → गतिः + अत्र + न + अस्ति

    visarga as r; savarṇa-dīrgha

    gatiḥ before a vowel shows r (the visarga after i comes from s, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः)). na + asti: P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • अणीयान्ह्यतर्क्यमणुप्रमाणात् aṇīyānhyatarkyamaṇupramāṇāt → अणीयान् + हि + अतर्क्यम् + अणुप्रमाणात्

    yaṇ; final m before a vowel

    hi + a: i becomes y before a vowel, P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि). atarkyam keeps its m before a.

Junctions outside the general rules

  • अस्ति · अणीयान् — hiatus at the pāda boundary: nāsti aṇīyān: i before a is not turned into y. The words close the third pāda and open the fourth, and both syllables are counted (11 + 11). GRETIL writes nāsty aṇīyān (corrections.json), which, read as written, would leave the third pāda with 10.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • अनन्यप्रोक्ते ananyaprokte

    tṛtīyā-tatpuruṣa (Śaṅkara’s first reading) · अनन्य + प्रोक्त

    Vigraha अनन्येन प्रोक्ते

    taught by one who is not other; Śaṅkara also reads it as ‘the non-other [self] being taught’, स्वात्मभूते अनन्यस्मिन् आत्मनि प्रोक्ते

  • अणुप्रमाणात् aṇupramāṇāt

    bahuvrīhi · अणु + प्रमाण

    Vigraha अणुः प्रमाणं यस्य तस्मात्

    than what has the measure of an atom

Morphology

प्रोक्तः
pra + ukta, past participle of vac ‘speak’ (vac > uc-): ‘proclaimed, taught’.
अणीयान्
Comparative of aṇu ‘minute’ in -īyas: ‘more minute’, with the ablative of comparison aṇupramāṇāt: ‘subtler than the subtlest measure’.
अतर्क्यम्
A gerundive of tark ‘reason, conjecture’ with the negative a-: ‘not to be reasoned out’. It is neuter, though the self (eṣaḥ) is masculine; Śaṅkara restates it in the masculine, अतर्क्यम् अतर्क्यः, and Raṅgarāmānuja makes the self’s nature its subject, तत्स्वरूपं तर्कागोचरम्.
चिन्त्यमानः
Present passive participle of cint ‘think’ (tenth class): ‘being thought of [variously]’ — the reason why the self taught by an inferior teacher is not easily known.

Anvaya

अवरेण नरेण प्रोक्तः एषः, बहुधा चिन्त्यमानः, सुविज्ञेयः न [भवति] । अनन्यप्रोक्ते अत्र गतिः न अस्ति । [एषः] हि अणुप्रमाणात् अणीयान्, अतर्क्यम् [च] ।

avareṇa nareṇa proktaḥ eṣaḥ, bahudhā cintyamānaḥ, suvijñeyaḥ na bhavati | ananyaprokte atra gatiḥ na asti | eṣaḥ hi aṇupramāṇāt aṇīyān, atarkyam ca |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Three statements. The first is negative, with the participle proktaḥ giving the condition and cintyamānaḥ the reason. The second is a locative absolute (ananyaprokte) with a short main clause, gatir atra nāsti, whose sense depends on how the compound and gati are read. The third (hi) gives the reason for both: the self is subtler than the subtle and beyond reasoning.

Ambiguities

ananyaprokte gatir atra nāsti — Śaṅkara’s three readings
Śaṅkara gives three. (1) When it is taught by a teacher who sees no difference, अनन्येन अपृथग्दर्शिनाचार्येण प्रतिपाद्यब्रह्मात्मभूतेन प्रोक्ते, there is no more ‘going’, that is, speculation whether it is or is not: अनेकधा अस्तिनास्तीत्यादिलक्षणा चिन्ता गतिः. (2) When the self, not other than oneself, is taught, there is no other thing to know, अन्यावगतिर्नास्ति ज्ञेयस्यान्यस्याभावात्, or no more going round in births: संसारगतिर्वात्र नास्त्यनन्य आत्मनि प्रोक्ते. (3) Reading agatiḥ: there is no failure to understand, अगतिः अनवबोधोऽपरिज्ञानमत्र नास्ति.
Raṅgarāmānuja’s readings
Raṅgarāmānuja: taught by one who is devoted solely to the self taught and has realised it, उच्यमानात्मनोऽनन्येन तदेकान्तिना ब्रह्मात्मसाक्षात्कारिणा, the understanding that results is not found when an inferior man teaches. He adds two more readings — no wandering in worldly existence, अत्र = संसारे, गतिः - चङ्क्रमणं नास्तीत्यर्थः, or no understanding when one teaches oneself, अनन्यप्रोक्ते - स्वयमवगते — and a variant text anyaprokte, ‘taught by another [inferior man]’: अन्यप्रोक्ते इति पाठे.
Madhva’s reading
Madhva reverses the sense of ananya: the ‘non-other’ is the one who does not know that the Lord and the self are different, अन्यो भगवानन्योऽहमित्यजानन्ननन्यः; taught by such a one, there is no knowledge, तेन प्रोक्ते गतिर्ज्ञानं नास्ति. His text also reads nareṇāvaraḥ, sujñeyo and gatir asya (corrections.json).

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 22

Two half-lines of 22 syllables (11 + 11), regular, with the hiatus nāsti aṇīyān at the pāda boundary counted as written.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: न नरेणावरः प्रोक्त — Madhva’s text reads nareṇāvaraḥ, ‘inferior [when] taught by a man’, for nareṇāvareṇa, ‘by an inferior man’.
  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: नरेणावरः प्रोक्त एष सुज्ञेयो — Madhva’s text reads sujñeyo for suvijñeyo, as at 1.1.21.
  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: अनन्यप्रोक्ते गतिरस्य नास्त्यणीयान् — Madhva’s text reads gatir asya, ‘there is no access to it’, for gatir atra, ‘there is no access here’.
  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: अनन्यप्रोक्ते गतिरस्य नास्त्यणीयान् — Madhva’s text reads gatir asya, ‘there is no access to it’, for gatir atra, ‘there is no access here’.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Learning and apprenticeship

In many fields there are two kinds of teacher: one who has collected opinions about a subject and one who has lived inside it. The first can make a subject seem endlessly debatable; the second can end a student’s confusion with a few words. The verse adds a warning about argument: on some questions, cleverness only produces a more refined version of the last guess. This is a reflection, not part of the commentaries.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Who have you learned from who seemed to know a subject from the inside?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why might more argument make some questions harder rather than easier?

A contemplation exercise Before your next argument about something important, ask yourself whether you have experience of it, or only opinions.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
