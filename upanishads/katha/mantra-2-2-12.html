Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 12 of 15

The one controller

The one controller, the inner self of all beings, who makes one form many: those wise ones who see him abiding in themselves have eternal happiness, and not others.

5 Faithful English translation

The one controller, the inner self of all beings, who makes one form manifold — the wise who perceive him abiding in [their] self, theirs [is] everlasting happiness, not of others.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

There is one who rules all, the self within every being, who makes the one form many. The wise who see him dwelling within themselves have lasting happiness; others do not.

Key concepts वशिन् vaśin, the controller अन्तरात्मन् the inner self ईश्वर the Lord आत्मन् self, Self धीर the wise आनन्द bliss एकत्व oneness

7 Explain it to a beginner

Death now names the self within all beings as the one ruler of everything. He makes one thing into many, as one lump of clay becomes many pots. The wise who look within and find him there have a happiness that does not fade, and others do not. Śaṅkara says the one self only appears as many because of names and forms laid over it, and the joy found is the self’s own bliss. Raṅgarāmānuja, whose copy reads ‘one seed’, says God turns the one unformed seed of matter into the whole world, and that those who see him as their inner ruler gain freedom. Madhva says the souls that look like God’s many forms are utterly different from him, because they depend on him. All three agree that the happiness comes from seeing him within.

An analogy One composer’s single theme heard in a hundred variations: each variation is different, the theme is one.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

The one controller, the inner self of all beings, makes one form many; lasting happiness belongs to the wise who see him abiding within themselves, and to no one else.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: one self made many by adjuncts

The supreme Lord is all-pervading and independent, one, with none equal or greater, स हि परमेश्वरः सर्वगतः स्वतन्त्रः एकः, न तत्समोऽभ्यधिको वान्योऽस्ति; the whole world is in his power. He makes his own one self many through adjuncts of name and form. He abides in the self as a face abides in a mirror, आदर्शस्थं मुखमिति यद्वत्, not as a body is held in a place. Those who turn from outward things and see him have lasting happiness, the bliss of the self, शाश्वतं नित्यं सुखम् आत्मानन्दलक्षणं भवति; others do not, though it is their own self, because ignorance stands between, नेतरेषां बाह्यासक्तबुद्धीनामविवेकिनां स्वात्मभूतमपि, अविद्याव्यवधानात्.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: the one seed and the inner ruler

He reads ekaṃ bījam: the one seed, primal matter in its undivided state, स्वेन एकीभूताविभागावस्थं तमोलक्षणं बीजम्, which the Lord makes into the many-formed world of mahat and the rest, महदादिबहुविधप्रपञ्चरूपेण. He cites the Subāla for the seed becoming one with the god, तमः परे देव एकी भवति, as cited by him. Those who see him as their own inner ruler, स्वान्तर्यामिणं ये पश्यन्ति, तेषामेव मुक्तिरित्यर्थः.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: the soul is utterly other

His comment after this mantra cites a verse he attributes to the Mahākaurma: the soul shows consciousness, Hari is the supreme conscious one, चेतनाभासको जीवः परमश्चेतनो हरिः, and being independent he is not touched by fault, स्वतन्त्रत्वात् स्वतन्त्रो हि नैव दोषेण लिप्यते (cited by Madhva). Then: he alone is the inner self of all beings, स एक एव सर्वभूतान्तरात्मा; the soul called his counterpart came to be, तस्य रूपं रूपं प्रतिरूपाख्यो जीवो बभूव, and it is utterly different from the supreme Self, being dependent, बहिश्चासौ परमात्मनो नितरां भिन्नः.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

The printed reading of Raṅgarāmānuja

The mūla printed with Raṅgarāmānuja’s comment, and his own gloss, read bījam, ‘seed’, where all our witnesses read rūpam, ‘form’. His gloss shows he commented on bījam; no witness of this edition has it.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The Lord makes his own one self many through name and form; seeing him within is the self’s own bliss, kept from others by ignorance, अविद्याव्यवधानात्.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.2.12

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The Lord turns the one seed of primal matter into the world; those who see him as their inner ruler are freed, स्वान्तर्यामिणं ये पश्यन्ति, तेषामेव मुक्तिरित्यर्थः.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.2.12

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The soul, called the Lord’s counterpart, is utterly different from him because it depends on him: बहिश्चासौ परमात्मनो नितरां भिन्नः.

Where they disagree

The ‘one form made many’ is the one self under adjuncts (Śaṅkara), or primal matter made into the world, on the reading ‘one seed’ (Raṅgarāmānuja); Madhva’s comment turns to the souls as the Lord’s dependent counterparts. All three tie lasting happiness to seeing him within.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • एको वशी eko vaśī → एकः + वशी

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

  • तमात्मस्थं tamātmasthaṃ → तम् + आत्मस्थम्

    m before a vowel

    Final m stays before a vowel and is joined to it.

  • येऽनुपश्यन्ति ye'nupaśyanti → ये + अनुपश्यन्ति

    e + a

    After e at the end of a word an initial a is merged into it, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति); the avagraha marks it.

  • धीरास्तेषां dhīrāsteṣāṃ → धीराः + तेषाम्

    visarga before t

    Visarga before t becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः).

  • नेतरेषाम् netareṣām → न + इतरेषाम्

    a + i

    a + i give e, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

Junctions outside the general rules

  • सर्वभूतान्तरात्मा · एकम् — hiatus at the pāda boundary: sarvabhūtāntarātmā ekam: ā before e is not fused. The words end the first pāda and begin the second, and both are counted (11 + 11); fused (sarvabhūtāntarātmaikam) the half-line would have 21. Every witness keeps the vowels apart.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • सर्वभूतान्तरात्मा sarvabhūtāntarātmā

    ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa (with a karmadhāraya in each member) · सर्व + भूत + अन्तर + आत्मन्

    Vigraha सर्वाणि भूतानि सर्वभूतानि, अन्तरः आत्मा अन्तरात्मा, सर्वभूतानाम् अन्तरात्मा

    the inner self of all beings

  • आत्मस्थम् ātmastham

    upapada-tatpuruṣa · आत्मन् + स्थ

    Vigraha आत्मनि तिष्ठतीति आत्मस्थः

    abiding in the self

Morphology

वशी
vaśin, a possessive in -in from vaśa, ‘will, control’: ‘having control’. Raṅgarāmānuja: सोऽस्यास्तीति वशी.
बहुधा
An adverb in -dhā, ‘in many ways’, from bahu.
शाश्वतम्
From śaśvat, ‘ever’, with vṛddhi: ‘everlasting’.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

The first line is a string of nominatives (ekaḥ vaśī sarvabhūtāntarātmā) closed by a relative clause, yaḥ … karoti. They are taken up by tam in the second line, object of the relative clause ye … anupaśyanti dhīrāḥ, whose correlative teṣām introduces a verbless clause: ‘theirs is lasting happiness’. na itareṣām denies it to the rest.

Ambiguities

What is the ‘one form’ made many?
Śaṅkara: his own one, uniform self of pure consciousness, made manifold through the adjuncts of name and form, एकमेव सदैकरसमात्मानं विशुद्धविज्ञानघनरूपं नामरूपाद्यशुद्धोपाधिभेदवशेन बहुधा अनेकप्रकारेण यः करोति. Raṅgarāmānuja reads ‘one seed’ (ekaṃ bījam) in the mūla printed with his comment, and explains it as the undivided primal matter, tamas, made into the many-formed world, स्वेन एकीभूताविभागावस्थं तमोलक्षणं बीजम्.
What does vaśī mean?
Śaṅkara: the whole world is in his power. Raṅgarāmānuja gives that sense and also ‘controlled by his devotees’, भक्तवश्यः इति वा अर्थः.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 22

Two lines of 22 syllables (11 + 11), counting sarvabhūtāntarātmā and ekam apart, as all witnesses write them.

Textual variations between witnesses

The witnesses differ here only in spelling (listed in section 14).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Psychology and inner life

Many traditions locate lasting contentment inward rather than in what changes outside. The verse adds a condition, seeing, not just believing. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What kind of happiness have you known that did not depend on anything outside you?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry rūpam or bījam: how does one word change what ‘making one many’ means?

A contemplation exercise For a few minutes, look for what is steady beneath your changing thoughts, without naming it.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
