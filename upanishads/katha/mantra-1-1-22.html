Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 22 of 29

Then no other boon is equal to it

Naciketas turns Death’s argument round: if even the gods doubted, and Death himself says it is hard, then no one else can teach it and nothing else is worth choosing.

5 Faithful English translation

Even by the gods here it was doubted, [as you say]; and you, O Death, say that [it is] not easily known. And a teacher of it other [than one] like you is not to be found; no other boon is equal to this, any at all.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

You say even the gods doubted about this, and you yourself, Death, call it hard to know. Then where would I find another teacher like you? No other boon is equal to this one.

Key concepts मृत्यु death आचार्य ācārya, teacher विद्या vidyā

7 Explain it to a beginner

Death has just said that the matter is too hard and that even the gods were confused by it. Naciketas does not argue back. He simply uses Death’s own words against him. If even the gods doubted, he says, and if Death himself calls it hard to know, then this is exactly the knowledge worth having, and Death is exactly the one to give it. Where else would I find a teacher who knows death from the inside? No other gift could be equal to this one. The boy’s reply shows the steadiness Death was testing for: the difficulty does not put him off; it convinces him.

An analogy Like a student told ‘this course is very hard’, who answers, ‘Then I am in the right place — and you are the right teacher.’

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does Naciketas answer?

He repeats Death’s words — even the gods doubted, and you say it is hard to know — and concludes that no teacher like Death can be found and no boon is equal to this.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Śaṅkara read the reply?

Because the matter is beyond even the learned, no teacher of it like Death can be found: वक्ता च अस्य धर्मस्य त्वादृक् त्वत्तुल्यः अन्यः पण्डितश्च न लभ्यः. And this boon is the cause of the highest good — अयं तु वरो निःश्रेयसप्राप्तिहेतुः — while every other has a passing result: अनित्यफलत्वादन्यस्य सर्वस्यैवेत्यभिप्रायः.

Comparative Vedānta

The Viśiṣṭādvaita reading

Raṅgarāmānuja repeats the point in his own words, naming the object: त्वं च मृत्यो न सुविज्ञेयम् इति यदात्मस्वरूपम् उक्तवान् — what Death called hard to know is the nature of the self.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • यन्न सुज्ञेयमात्थ yanna sujñeyamāttha → यत् + न + सुज्ञेयम् + आत्थ

    t before n; m before a vowel

    yat + na: t becomes the nasal n before n, P. 8.4.45 (यरोऽनुनासिकेऽनुनासिको वा). sujñeyam keeps its m before the vowel of āttha.

  • वक्ता चास्य vaktā cāsya → वक्ता + च + अस्य

    savarṇa-dīrgha

    ca + asya: a + a give ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • त्वादृगन्यो tvādṛganyo → त्वादृक् + अन्यः

    voicing; aḥ before a voiced consonant

    tvādṛk before a vowel becomes tvādṛg, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते). anyaḥ before n becomes anyo (P. 6.1.114 (हशि च) with guṇa).

  • वरस्तुल्य एतस्य varastulya etasya → वरः + तुल्यः + एतस्य

    visarga before t; aḥ before a vowel

    varaḥ before t becomes varas, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः). tulyaḥ before e: the visarga becomes y, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), which is dropped, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य), leaving tulya etasya.

Anvaya

मृत्यो, अत्र देवैः अपि विचिकित्सितम् किल । त्वम् च यत् [तत्] सुज्ञेयम् न [इति] आत्थ । अस्य वक्ता च त्वादृक् अन्यः न लभ्यः । एतस्य तुल्यः अन्यः वरः कश्चित् न [अस्ति] ।

mṛtyo, atra devaiḥ api vicikitsitam kila | tvam ca yat tat sujñeyam na iti āttha | asya vaktā ca tvādṛk anyaḥ na labhyaḥ | etasya tulyaḥ anyaḥ varaḥ kaścit na asti |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Naciketas repeats Death’s first clause word for word, marked with kila (‘so you say’), adds Death’s second (‘not easily known’) as reported speech with yat … āttha, and draws two conclusions in parallel nominal sentences, each with na.

Ambiguities

yat
Śaṅkara takes yat as causal, ‘because’ (यत् यस्मात्): because you say it is hard to know, no other teacher can be found. It can also be the relative ‘what you say’.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 23 + 22

Triṣṭubh. The half-lines count 23 and 22: the first pāda repeats the twelve-syllable first pāda of 1.1.21 (devair atrāpi vicikitsitaṃ kila), the others have 11.

Textual variations between witnesses

The witnesses differ here only in spelling (listed in section 14).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Ethical leadership

Being told that something is difficult can be read as a warning or as a recommendation. Naciketas takes the second view, and chooses the teacher because of the difficulty. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin When something is called ‘too hard’, does that make you want it less or more?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why might the best teacher of a subject be the one who finds it hardest to explain?

A contemplation exercise Name one person whose knowledge you could not find anywhere else. Consider asking them the question you have been putting off.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
