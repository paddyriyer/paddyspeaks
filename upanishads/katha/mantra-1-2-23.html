Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 23 of 25

Not by instruction: by the one he chooses

This self is not attained by instruction, nor by intelligence, nor by much learning; by the one whom he chooses is he attained; to him this self reveals its own form.

5 Faithful English translation

‘This self is not to be attained by instruction, not by intelligence, not by much learning. Whom alone this one chooses, by him [it] is to be attained; to him this self uncovers its own form.’

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘This self is not won by teaching, nor by cleverness, nor by much learning. It is won by the one it chooses; to that one the self reveals its own true form.’

Key concepts आत्मन् self, Self मेधा medhā, retentive intelligence विद्या vidyā ईश्वर the Lord

7 Explain it to a beginner

This is one of the best-known verses in the Upaniṣads, and the Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad repeats it word for word. The self cannot be gained by lectures, by sharp intelligence, or by having read a great deal. It is gained by the one whom it chooses, and to that person it shows its true form. But who does the choosing? The Sanskrit leaves it open, and the traditions divide here. Śaṅkara says the seeker chooses: whoever longs for the self alone, and nothing else, finds that the self reveals itself, so the self is reached through the self. Raṅgarāmānuja says the Lord chooses: he chooses the person who loves him, and the worshipper's love awakens the Lord's love in return. Either way, cleverness alone is not enough.

An analogy Like friendship: no amount of research about a person makes them your friend; at some point they must open the door.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

That the self is not attained by instruction, intelligence or learning, but by the one whom ‘this one’ chooses, and that to him the self reveals its own form.

Traditional Interpretation

Who chooses, for Śaṅkara?

The seeker, who chooses his own self: यमेव स्वमात्मानम् एष साधको वृणुते प्रार्थयते. By that self, the chooser, the self is attained: तेनैवात्मना वरित्रा स्वयमात्मा लभ्यः. One without desire asks for the self alone, निष्कामश्चात्मानमेव प्रार्थयते, and the self reveals its own true nature, पारमार्थिकीं स्वां तनूं.

Comparative Vedānta

Who chooses, for Raṅgarāmānuja?

The Supreme chooses the seeker, साधकं प्रार्थयते, and is attainable by the one he chooses, प्रार्थनीयपुंसा लभ्यः. Only the one dearest to him is so chosen, and he is dearest who loves him: the worshipper’s love for the Lord produces the Lord’s love for him, which brings attainment, भगवतः उपासके प्रीतिमुत्पाद्य तत्प्राप्तिहेतुः भवतीत्यर्थः. Then the Supreme gives himself, स्वात्मानं प्रयच्छति.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Does Madhva comment?

Not in our source: Madhva gives this verse without comment, and his mūla keys pravacena (a dropped syllable) for pravacanena (corrections.json, witness-error). The vishvAsa mūla writes labhyaḥ tasyaiṣa with the visarga (orthographic).

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Is the verse found elsewhere?

The Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad has it word for word (3.2.3) and follows it with a verse of its own on the same theme: not by one lacking strength, nor through negligence (3.2.4).

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.2.23

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The chooser is the seeker, the chosen his own self; the self is attained by the self alone: आत्मनैवात्मा लभ्यत इत्यर्थः. Learning without that desire for the self does not reach it.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, following Vyāsārya on pravacana, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.2.23

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The chooser is the Supreme, the chosen the seeker who loves him; the worshipper’s love awakens the Lord’s, भगवतः उपासके प्रीतिमुत्पाद्य तत्प्राप्तिहेतुः भवतीत्यर्थः, and the Lord then reveals and gives himself.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara makes the seeker the chooser and the self the chosen: attainment is the self’s self-disclosure to one who wants nothing else. Raṅgarāmānuja makes the Lord the chooser and the loving worshipper the chosen: attainment is by divine choice answering love. Madhva’s bhāṣya, in our source, is silent on the verse.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • नायमात्मा nāyamātmā → न + अयम् + आत्मा

    vowel sandhi; m before a vowel

    a + a → ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः); final m stays before a vowel.

  • लभ्यो न labhyo na → लभ्यः + न

    visarga sandhi

    aḥ → o before n, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

  • यमेवैष वृणुते yamevaiṣa vṛṇute → यम् + एव + एषः + वृणुते

    vowel sandhi; eṣaḥ before a consonant

    Final m stays before a vowel; a + e → ai, P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि); eṣaḥ loses its visarga before a consonant, P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि).

  • लभ्यस्तस्यैष आत्मा labhyastasyaiṣa ātmā → लभ्यः + तस्य + एषः + आत्मा

    visarga before t; vowel sandhi; eṣaḥ before a vowel

    Visarga before t becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः); a + e → ai, P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि); before a vowel the visarga of eṣaḥ becomes y, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), and is dropped, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य); the hiatus stays.

  • तनूंस्वाम् tanūṃsvām → तनूम् + स्वाम्

    anusvāra

    Final m → anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः); the base writes the two words without a space.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

वृणुते
Present middle of vṛ (class 5, vṛṇoti / vṛṇute) ‘choose’; the same verb with vi- in विवृणुते means ‘uncover, open’. The verse plays on the two: the one chosen (vṛṇute) is the one to whom the self opens (vivṛṇute).
लभ्यः
Gerundive of labh ‘obtain’ in -ya: ‘to be obtained, attainable’; Śaṅkara glosses ‘to be known’.
तनूम्
tanū ‘body, form, person’; here, with स्वाम्, the self’s ‘own form’ — Śaṅkara: its true nature.

Anvaya

अयम् आत्मा न प्रवचनेन लभ्यः न मेधया न बहुना श्रुतेन । एषः यम् एव वृणुते तेन लभ्यः । तस्य एषः आत्मा स्वाम् तनूम् विवृणुते ॥

ayam ātmā na pravacanena labhyaḥ na medhayā na bahunā śrutena | eṣaḥ yam eva vṛṇute tena labhyaḥ | tasya eṣaḥ ātmā svām tanūm vivṛṇute ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Three negated instrumentals (pravacanena, medhayā, bahunā śrutena) with labhyaḥ, then a correlative pair yam … tena: ‘whom he chooses, by him [it is] attainable’, and a closing sentence with tasya … vivṛṇute. The subject of vṛṇute, eṣaḥ, is not specified — the crux of the verse.

Ambiguities

Who chooses whom?
Śaṅkara: the seeker chooses the self — his own self — and by that self alone is it attained: यमेव स्वमात्मानम् एष साधको वृणुते प्रार्थयते, आत्मनैवात्मा लभ्यत इत्यर्थः. Raṅgarāmānuja: the Supreme chooses the seeker: एषः - परमात्मा, साधकं प्रार्थयते, and the one chosen is the one most dear to him. Madhva, in our source, does not comment on this verse.
pravacana — teaching others, or one’s own reflection?
Śaṅkara: taking up many Vedas, अनेकवेदस्वीकरणेन. Raṅgarāmānuja, following Vyāsārya: reflection, प्रवचनं मननम्, since teaching others could not be thought a means at all.
vivṛṇute or vṛṇute
Raṅgarāmānuja knows a reading vṛṇute in the last line and says the sense is the same, वृणुते इति पाठेऽपि स एवार्थः. None of our witnesses has it.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 23

Counted as written, 22 and 23 syllables: the last pāda, tasyaiṣa ātmā vivṛṇute tanūṃ svām, has twelve because eṣa ātmā stays in hiatus.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Witness error Madhva’s mūla: नायमात्मा प्रवचेन लभ्यो — Madhva’s digital text keys प्रवचेन for pravacanena, ‘by instruction’, a syllable dropped.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Learning and understanding

Credentials, reading and intelligence are real goods, and the verse does not despise them; it says they are not sufficient. Many people who have learned something deeply say that it was given as much as earned. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What have you understood that no amount of reading could have given you?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry If the seeker chooses (Śaṅkara), what is the role of grace? If the Lord chooses (Raṅgarāmānuja), what is the role of effort?

A contemplation exercise Choose one question you care about and, for a week, return to it daily without looking anything up.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
