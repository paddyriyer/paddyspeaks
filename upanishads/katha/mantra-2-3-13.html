Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 13 of 18

‘It is’, and its true nature

It is to be grasped as ‘it is’, and by its true nature — of the two. For one who has grasped it as ‘it is’, its true nature becomes clear.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

It must first be grasped simply as ‘it is’, and then in its true nature. For one who has grasped that it is, its true nature becomes clear.

Key concepts ब्रह्मन् Brahman आत्मन् self, Self प्रसाद clarity, calm; grace मनस् mind

7 Explain it to a beginner

The previous verse said the self is known only by one who affirms ‘it is’. This verse says that affirmation is a first step. First grasp it simply as ‘it is’; then its true nature becomes clear to you. The commentators read the key word, tattvabhāva, in three ways. Śaṅkara takes it as the self’s true nature: first we know it as the being behind all things, and then its nature beyond all qualities turns towards us. Raṅgarāmānuja takes the same word as ‘the mind’, the faculty that makes reality known: once scripture tells us that God is, and we reflect on it, the mind becomes clear and pure. Madhva, in verses he cites, reads it as a name of Viṣṇu, who makes known the nature of the world and of souls, and by whose grace one knows his greatness.

An analogy First you learn that someone is in the next room; only then, as you go in, do you see who they are.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

It is to be grasped as ‘it is’ and by its true nature; for one who has grasped it as ‘it is’, its true nature becomes clear.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: from ‘it is’ to what it truly is

Rejecting the view that it is not, the self is first to be grasped as ‘it is’ through its adjuncts, अस्तीत्येव आत्मा उपलब्धव्यः सत्कार्यबुद्ध्याद्युपाधिभिः. Since the effect is nothing apart from the cause, as the Chāndogya says (quoted by him), there is then the self’s true nature without adjuncts. The genitive is one of selection, निर्धारणार्था षष्ठी: of the two, for one who first grasped it as ‘it is’, the true nature, free of all adjuncts, becomes clear, प्रसीदति अभिमुखीभवति.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: scripture, then the clearing mind

tattvabhāva is the inner organ, तत्त्वं भावयति इति तत्त्वभावः - अन्तःकरणम्. The supreme Self is to be grasped as ‘it is’ by it as well: what the Vedānta sentences say is, is to be reflected on and meditated on by the mind too, मनसाऽपि अस्तीत्येवं मनननिदिध्यासने कर्तव्ये इत्यर्थः. Both means are meant, उभाभ्यां शब्दमनोरूपाभ्याम्, and for one who has known it so the mind becomes clear, मनः प्रसन्नं भवति, निर्दुष्टं भवति इत्यर्थः.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: tattvabhāva is Viṣṇu

In verses he cites (इति च), Viṣṇu makes the reality of matter and souls known, प्रकृतेः पुरुषाणां च तत्त्वं भावयति स्फुटम्, and so is called tattvabhāva, तत्त्वभावस्ततो विष्णुः; by his grace his greatness is known.

Comparative Vedānta

The Chāndogya behind Śaṅkara’s reading

Śaṅkara’s step from the effect to its cause rests on the Chāndogya’s clay: वाचारम्भणं विकारो नामधेयं मृत्तिकेत्येव सत्यम्.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.3.13

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

First grasped as ‘it is’ through its adjuncts, the self then shows its true nature without them, प्रसीदति अभिमुखीभवति.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.3.13

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

What scripture says ‘is’ must also be reflected on and meditated on by the mind, which is then made pure, मनः प्रसन्नं भवति, निर्दुष्टं भवति इत्यर्थः.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.3.12–13

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

tattvabhāva is Viṣṇu, who makes the reality of matter and souls known, तत्त्वभावस्ततो विष्णुः (in verses cited by him).

Where they disagree

The three read tattvabhāva differently: the self’s own true nature (Śaṅkara), the mind that makes reality known (Raṅgarāmānuja), Viṣṇu who makes it known (Madhva). The second line accordingly says that the true nature turns towards the knower, that the mind is cleared, or that by the Lord’s grace his greatness is known.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • अस्तीत्येवोपलब्धव्यस्तत्त्वभावेन astītyevopalabdhavyastattvabhāvena → अस्ति + इति + एव + उपलब्धव्यः + तत्त्वभावेन

    i + i; i before an unlike vowel; a + u; visarga before t

    Two like vowels merge into the long vowel, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः). i before an unlike vowel becomes y, P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि). a + u give o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः). Visarga before t becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः).

  • चोभयोः cobhayoḥ → च + उभयोः

    a + u

    a + u give o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • तत्त्वभावेन tattvabhāvena

    ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa (Śaṅkara’s reading) · तत्त्व + भाव

    Vigraha तत्त्वस्य भावः

    the being of the reality, its true nature; Raṅgarāmānuja and Madhva take bhāva as agent, ‘what makes the reality known’ (see the commentary)

Morphology

उपलब्धव्यः
Gerundive of upa + √labh with -tavya, P. 3.1.96 (तव्यत्तव्यानीयरः): ‘to be grasped’.
उपलब्धस्य
Past participle upalabdha in the genitive. Raṅgarāmānuja reads the participle actively, ‘of one who has known’, and compares the idiom ‘bhuktā brāhmaṇāḥ’ (‘the brahmins have eaten’), भुक्ता ब्राह्मणाः.
उभयोः
Genitive dual of ubha. Śaṅkara: a genitive of selection, ‘of the two’ (the conditioned and the unconditioned grasping), निर्धारणार्था षष्ठी; Raṅgarāmānuja: the two means, scripture and the mind.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

The first line has the gerundive upalabdhavyaḥ with two manners, asti ity eva and tattvabhāvena, joined by ca, and the genitive ubhayoḥ, whose relation is disputed. The second line has a genitive of the person (asti ity evopalabdhasya) and the subject tattvabhāvaḥ with prasīdati.

Ambiguities

What is tattvabhāva?
Śaṅkara: the self’s true nature, free of every adjunct, which ‘becomes clear’, that is, turns towards the knower, प्रसीदति अभिमुखीभवति. Raṅgarāmānuja: ‘that which makes the reality known’, the inner organ, तत्त्वं भावयति इति तत्त्वभावः - अन्तःकरणम्, and ‘becomes clear’ means the mind is cleared, मनः प्रसन्नं भवति, निर्दुष्टं भवति इत्यर्थः. Madhva, in verses he cites, takes it as Viṣṇu, who makes known the nature of matter and souls, तत्त्वभावस्ततो विष्णुः.
‘Of the two’
Śaṅkara: of the two ways of grasping, first as ‘it is’ through its adjuncts, then its true nature, सोपाधिकनिरुपाधिकयोरस्तित्वतत्त्वभावयोः. Raṅgarāmānuja: by the two means, उभाभ्यां शब्दमनोरूपाभ्याम्, scripture and the mind.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Learning and apprenticeship

Much understanding begins with accepting that something is there before one can say what it is. The verse makes that order explicit: first ‘it is’, then its nature. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What did you accept as real before you understood it?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Is tattvabhāva what is known, the faculty that knows, or the one who makes it known? What does each reading make the second line say?

A contemplation exercise Hold one thing today simply as ‘it is’, without describing it.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
