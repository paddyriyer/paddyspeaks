Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 22 of 25

Bodiless among bodies

Knowing the self as bodiless among bodies, steady among the unsteady, great and all-pervading, the wise one does not grieve.

5 Faithful English translation

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘The wise, who know the self — without a body among bodies, steady among things that never stay, vast and everywhere — do not grieve.’

Key concepts आत्मन् self, Self धीर the wise शोक grief, sorrow

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse gives the reason why knowing the self ends grief. Bodies come and go; none of them stays. But the self that lives in them has no body of its own, stays steady, and is vast and present everywhere. A wise person who has understood this does not grieve. Śaṅkara says the self is like space: it is inside every body without being one. He adds that the verse says 'self' on purpose, to remind us that this is not something far away but our own self. Raṅgarāmānuja reads the verse as speaking of the supreme Lord, who has no body made by past actions. Either way the point is practical: grief clings to what changes, and the verse points the mind to what does not.

An analogy Like a river’s name: the water in it is never the same from one day to the next, yet it is the same river.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

That the wise one who knows the self — bodiless among bodies, steady among the unstable, great and pervading — does not grieve.

Traditional Interpretation

Why does such knowledge end grief?

Śaṅkara: grief has no ground in one who knows the self as this: न ह्येवंविधस्वात्मविदः शोकोपपत्तिः. The bodies of gods, ancestors and men are impermanent; the self in them is eternal and unchanging, अवस्थितं नित्यम् अविकृतमित्येतत्.

Comparative Vedānta

How does Raṅgarāmānuja read it?

Briefly: bodiless means without a body made by karma, कर्मकृतशरीररहितम्; the self dwells in unstable bodies as their permanent element, and ‘great, pervading’ means possessed of great glory, महावैभवशालिनं. The next verse, he says, gives the means of reaching such a self.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Does Madhva comment?

In our source Madhva gives this verse and the next two without comment; the bhāṣya resumes at 1.3.1. His mūla joins śarīreṣv anavastheṣv (corrections.json).

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.2.22

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The self is one’s own self, like space in every body: आत्मग्रहणं स्वतोऽनन्यत्वप्रदर्शनार्थम्. Knowing it as ‘I am this’ leaves no ground for grief.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.2.22

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The Supreme dwells in bodies without a body made by karma, कर्मकृतशरीररहितम्, and knowing him the wise do not grieve.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara stresses that the bodiless self is one’s own; Raṅgarāmānuja that it is the Supreme, free of karma-made bodies. Madhva, in our source, does not comment.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • अशरीरं शरीरेषु aśarīraṃ śarīreṣu → अशरीरम् + शरीरेषु

    anusvāra

    Final m → anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • अनवस्थेष्ववस्थितम् anavastheṣvavasthitam → अनवस्थेषु + अवस्थितम्

    vowel sandhi

    u before a dissimilar vowel becomes v, P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि).

  • महान्तं विभुमात्मानं mahāntaṃ vibhumātmānaṃ → महान्तम् + विभुम् + आत्मानम्

    anusvāra; m before a vowel

    Final m → anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः); final m stays before a vowel.

  • धीरो न dhīro na → धीरः + न

    visarga sandhi

    aḥ → o before n, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

Junctions outside the general rules

  • शरीरेषु · अनवस्थेषु — vedic hiatus: śarīreṣu anavastheṣu: final u before a is not joined; the vishvAsa mūla and Madhva’s text join it (śarīreṣv anavastheṣv, corrections.json, orthographic), and Śaṅkara’s lemma keeps it apart as the base does. The unjoined u keeps the first pāda at eight syllables.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • अशरीरम् aśarīram

    nañ-bahuvrīhi · अ + शरीर

    Vigraha अविद्यमानं शरीरं यस्य तम्

    one who has no body

  • अनवस्थेषु anavastheṣu

    nañ-bahuvrīhi · अन् + अवस्था

    Vigraha अविद्यमाना अवस्था येषां तेषु

    in those that have no stability

Morphology

मत्वा
Absolutive of man ‘think’: ‘having known, having recognised’; Śaṅkara supplies the content, ‘I am this’.
अनवस्थेषु
From an- + avasthā ‘standing firm’; as the last member of a bahuvrīhi the final ā is shortened (anavastha).
अशरीरम्
‘Bodiless’, set against शरीरेषु: the self abides in bodies without being one.

Anvaya

धीरः अनवस्थेषु शरीरेषु अवस्थितम् अशरीरम् महान्तम् विभुम् आत्मानम् मत्वा न शोचति ॥

dhīraḥ anavastheṣu śarīreṣu avasthitam aśarīram mahāntam vibhum ātmānam matvā na śocati ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

One sentence. The first line and महान्तं विभुम् are accusative qualifiers of ātmānam, the object of the absolutive matvā; the main clause is dhīro na śocati. The line sets pairs against each other: aśarīram / śarīreṣu, anavastheṣu / avasthitam.

Ambiguities

Whose self is ‘bodiless’?
Śaṅkara: the one self, in its own nature like space, स्वेन रूपेणाकाशकल्प आत्मा; the word ātman is used to show it is not other than one’s own self, आत्मग्रहणं स्वतोऽनन्यत्वप्रदर्शनार्थम्. Raṅgarāmānuja: without a body made by karma, कर्मकृतशरीररहितम्, continuing his reading of 1.2.20–21 as the Supreme.
mahāntaṃ vibhum
Śaṅkara: vibhu answers the doubt that ‘great’ might be relative: it means pervading, विभुं व्यापिनम्. Raṅgarāmānuja takes the pair together as ‘possessed of great glory’, महावैभवशालिनं.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16

Counted as written, 16 and 16 syllables; written with the sandhi of the other witnesses, the first pāda would have seven.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Grief and change

Grief follows attachment to what changes, and almost everything changes. The verse does not forbid love of the changing; it asks whether anything in us stays, and whether knowing that could steady us. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What in your life has stayed the same while everything around it changed?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why does Śaṅkara insist that ‘ātman’ here means one’s own self and not something other?

A contemplation exercise When you next feel loss, name what changed, and then name what in you is aware of the change.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
