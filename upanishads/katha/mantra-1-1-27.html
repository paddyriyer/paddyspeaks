Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 27 of 29

‘No one is satisfied by wealth’

Wealth never satisfies; and having seen Death, the boy will have wealth and life anyway, for as long as Death rules. The boon worth choosing is the one he asked.

5 Faithful English translation

A human being is not to be satisfied by wealth. We shall obtain wealth if we have seen you. We shall live as long as you shall rule. But the boon to be chosen by me [is] that same one.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

No one is ever satisfied by wealth. And since I have seen you, wealth will come to me; I shall live as long as you rule. The only boon I choose is the one I asked.

Key concepts मृत्यु death भोग enjoyment, use एषणा desire, ‘the three desires’

7 Explain it to a beginner

Naciketas now answers the offers of wealth and long life one by one. No one, he says, is ever really satisfied by money; there is always the wish for more. And anyway, he has met Death face to face. If he wanted wealth, it would come to him; and he will live as long as Death allows, so a long life is not something he needs to ask for. What he wants is the gift he already named. Śaṅkara points out the plain truth behind the first line: nobody in the world has ever been made content by getting richer. The boy is not being stubborn; he is being precise about what he wants.

An analogy Like someone offered a bigger salary who answers, ‘More money never ended anyone’s wanting — and I came here to learn.’

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the boy say?

That wealth never satisfies a person; that having seen Death he will get wealth, and live as long as Death rules; and that the boon he will choose is the one he asked.

Traditional Interpretation

Why does wealth not satisfy?

Śaṅkara: न हि लोके वित्तलाभः कस्यचित्तृप्तिकरो दृष्टः — no one in the world was ever seen satisfied by getting wealth. Raṅgarāmānuja says the same — न हि वित्तेन लब्धेन कस्यचित् तृप्तिः दृष्टचरी — and cites the verse he gives from the Viṣṇu Purāṇa, desire is not quieted by enjoying its objects: न जातुकामः कामानामुपभोगेन शाम्यति (as cited by him).

Traditional Interpretation

What is the boon?

For Śaṅkara the boon is the knowledge of the self: वरस्तु मे वरणीयः स एव यदात्मविज्ञानम्. A mortal who has met Death need not fear poverty or a short life: कथं हि मर्त्यस्त्वया समेत्याल्पधनायुर्भवेत्.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.1.27

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Wealth brings no satisfaction — न हि वित्तेन लब्धेन कस्यचित् तृप्तिः दृष्टचरी — and the boon to be chosen is the one first asked, about the state after death (येयं प्रेते).

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

The two agree on the verse; they differ, as throughout, on what the asked boon is about (the self, or the freed self). Madhva’s bhāṣya on the passage 1.1.26–29 comments only on ‘the great passing-on’ of 1.1.29.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • तर्पणीयो मनुष्यो लप्स्यामहे tarpaṇīyo manuṣyo lapsyāmahe → तर्पणीयः + मनुष्यः + लप्स्यामहे

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    aḥ before m and l becomes o: the visarga (ru) becomes u, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च), and a + u give o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • चेत्त्वा cettvā → चेत् + त्वा

    none

    cet before t: no change; written together.

  • वरस्तु varastu → वरः + तु

    visarga before t

    varaḥ before t becomes varas, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

अद्राक्ष्म
s-aorist of dṛś, 1st pl.: ‘we have seen’.
ईशिष्यसि
Future of īś, normally middle (īśiṣyase, as Śaṅkara glosses); the active ending here is a Vedic irregularity, which Raṅgarāmānuja calls an exchange of voice: व्यत्ययेन परस्मैपदम्.
लप्स्यामहे
Future middle of labh, 1st pl.; the plural ‘we’ is the boy speaking of himself.

Anvaya

मनुष्यः वित्तेन न तर्पणीयः । त्वा अद्राक्ष्म चेत् वित्तम् लप्स्यामहे । त्वम् यावत् ईशिष्यसि [तावत्] जीविष्यामः । मे वरणीयः वरः तु सः एव ।

manuṣyaḥ vittena na tarpaṇīyaḥ | tvā adrākṣma cet vittam lapsyāmahe | tvam yāvat īśiṣyasi tāvat jīviṣyāmaḥ | me varaṇīyaḥ varaḥ tu saḥ eva |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A general statement (na … tarpaṇīyo manuṣyaḥ), two future clauses each with a subordinate condition (cet) or relative of time (yāvat), and a contrastive conclusion (varas tu …). The boy uses the first person plural for himself.

Ambiguities

cet
Śaṅkara keeps the conditional: if we wanted wealth, we could have it, since we have seen you. Raṅgarāmānuja takes it as: since we have seen you, what difficulty is there in getting wealth?

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 22

Triṣṭubh, 4 × 11 (22 + 22), regular.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: वित्तमद्राक्ष्म चेत्त्वाम् — Madhva’s text reads tvām, the full accusative, for the enclitic tvā.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Wealth and consumption

‘More’ is a target that moves as we approach it; the verse names a fact that every generation rediscovers. The boy’s choice is not against wealth but for something wealth cannot reach. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Can you remember a time when getting something you wanted did not satisfy you for long?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry If wealth cannot satisfy, why does the wish for it not end?

A contemplation exercise Before buying something non-essential this week, wait a day, and notice whether the wish is still there.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
