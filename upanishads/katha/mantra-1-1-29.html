Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 29 of 29

‘Tell us that about which they doubt’

Naciketas ends the exchange by repeating his question: tell us what concerns the great passing-on, about which men doubt; he chooses no boon but this hidden one.

5 Faithful English translation

That about which they doubt this, O Death, which [is] in the great passing-on — tell us that. This boon which has entered the hidden — other than that Naciketas does not choose.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Tell us, Death, that about which people are in doubt — what happens in the great passing-on. This boon goes into what is hidden; Naciketas chooses no other.

Key concepts मृत्यु death आत्मन् self, Self मोक्ष liberation साम्पराय sāmparāya, the passing-on

7 Explain it to a beginner

The first chapter ends where it began. Death has tried warnings, flattery and every pleasure he could think of. Naciketas has turned them all down. Now he simply asks his question again, more plainly. Tell us, he says, about the thing people are unsure of: what happens in the great passing-on, after death. The last line says that this boon goes deep into something hidden, and that Naciketas will choose nothing else. Śaṅkara notes that this last line is the Upaniṣad itself speaking, closing the scene. The test is over; the teaching can begin in the next vallī.

An analogy Like a student who, after every distraction has been offered and refused, sets the same single question back on the table.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does Naciketas ask?

To be told that about which people doubt, the knowledge concerning the great passing-on; the boon that enters the hidden is the only one he chooses.

Traditional Interpretation

What is the great passing-on?

Śaṅkara: the knowledge that settles the doubt about the self after death, concerning the other world and its great purpose — साम्पराये परलोकविषये महति महत्प्रयोजननिमित्ते. The boon has entered what is hidden and hard to discern: योऽयं प्रकृत आत्मविषयः वरः गूढं गहनं दुर्विवेचनं प्राप्तः.

Traditional Interpretation

Who speaks the last line?

Both commentators: the Upaniṣad, closing the scene — Śaṅkara इति श्रुतेर्वचनमिति, Raṅgarāmānuja नचिकेता न वृणीते स्म इति श्रुतेर्वचनम्.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.29

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The boy asks again for the knowledge of the self that settles the doubt after death; the boon is hidden because the self is hard to discern: गूढं गहनं दुर्विवेचनं प्राप्तः.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.1.29

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The doubt is about the freed self — मुक्तात्मस्वरूपे संशेरते — and the boon has entered the hidden truth of the self.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya on 1.1.26–29

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The great passing-on is release: महति साम्पराये मुक्तौ. The question, as he read it at 1.1.20, is about the Lord who governs the freed.

Where they disagree

For Śaṅkara the doubt is whether the self exists after death; for Raṅgarāmānuja, what the freed self is like; for Madhva, the great passing-on is release itself, and the question is about the Lord in it.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • यस्मिन्निदं yasminnidaṃ → यस्मिन् + इदम्

    doubling of n

    After a short vowel, final n is doubled before a vowel, P. 8.3.32 (ङमो ह्रस्वादचि ङमुण्नित्यम्): yasminn idam. idam before v becomes idaṃ, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • योऽयं yo'yaṃ → यः + अयम्

    aḥ before a; avagraha

    yaḥ before a becomes yo, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च) with P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः), and the initial a is elided, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति), marked by the avagraha.

  • तस्मान्नचिकेता वृणीते tasmānnaciketā vṛṇīte → तस्मात् + नचिकेताः + वृणीते

    t before n; āḥ before a voiced consonant

    tasmāt before n becomes tasmān, P. 8.4.45 (यरोऽनुनासिकेऽनुनासिको वा). naciketāḥ before v drops its visarga, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि) and P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

विचिकित्सन्ति
Present of the desiderative of kit, ‘they are in doubt’, taking up vicikitsā of 1.1.20 and vicikitsitam of 1.1.21.
नचिकेताः
Nominative of naciketas; the verse ends in the third person — the Upaniṣad itself speaking, as Śaṅkara says: इति श्रुतेर्वचनमिति.

Anvaya

मृत्यो, यस्मिन् [प्रेते जनाः] इदम् विचिकित्सन्ति, महति साम्पराये यत् [निर्णयविज्ञानम्], तत् नः ब्रूहि । यः अयम् वरः गूढम् अनुप्रविष्टः, तस्मात् अन्यम् नचिकेताः न वृणीते ।

mṛtyo, yasmin prete janāḥ idam vicikitsanti, mahati sāmparāye yat nirṇayavijñānam, tat naḥ brūhi | yaḥ ayam varaḥ gūḍham anupraviṣṭaḥ, tasmāt anyam naciketāḥ na vṛṇīte |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two relative clauses (yasmin …, yat …) gathered by the correlative tat as the object of brūhi; then a further relative (yo ’yaṃ varaḥ …) taken up by the ablative tasmāt, with the main clause in the third person.

Ambiguities

Who speaks the last line?
Śaṅkara: the last half-verse is the Upaniṣad’s own statement about Naciketas — इति श्रुतेर्वचनमिति. Raṅgarāmānuja says the same: नचिकेता न वृणीते स्म इति श्रुतेर्वचनम्. It can also be read as Naciketas speaking of himself in the third person.
What is doubted?
Śaṅkara: whether one is or is not after death (अस्ति नास्तीत्येवम्प्रकारं). Raṅgarāmānuja: the nature of the freed self — मुक्तात्मस्वरूपे संशेरते.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 23 + 22

Triṣṭubh. The half-lines count 23 and 22: the second pāda (yat sāmparāye mahati brūhi nas tat) has 12 syllables; Madhva’s text, which lacks yat, has the regular 11.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: यस्मिन्निदं विचिकित्सन्ति मृत्यो — Madhva’s text lacks yat before sāmparāye (‘[tell us] that which [concerns] the great passing-on’).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Ego and self-image

After every offer the boy asks the same question again, more plainly. Persistence of this kind is not stubbornness but clarity about what one is actually looking for. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What question would you keep asking even if you were offered everything to stop?
