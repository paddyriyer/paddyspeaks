Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 7 of 25

A wonder its teacher

Many never hear of the self, and many who hear do not know it; its teacher and its knower are rare.

5 Faithful English translation

‘[That] which is not obtainable even for hearing by many, which many, even hearing, would not know — a wonder [is] its speaker, skilled [is] its obtainer; a wonder [is] the knower taught by a skilled one.’

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘Many never even get to hear of it; many who hear of it do not understand it. Rare is the one who can teach it, and gifted the one who receives it; rare is the one who knows it, having been taught by someone gifted.’

Key concepts आत्मन् self, Self आचार्य ācārya, teacher ज्ञान knowledge विद्या vidyā

7 Explain it to a beginner

Death now explains why so few people know what Naciketas has asked about. Most never even hear of it. Of those who hear, many do not understand. Someone able to teach it is a rare thing, almost a wonder; so is someone able to take it in. And a person who truly knows it, because a capable teacher taught him, is rarer still. This is not said to discourage the boy. It is said to show how unusual his position is: he has asked the question, he is standing before a teacher who knows the answer, and he has proved that he is ready to listen. Three rare things have come together in one place, and the teaching depends on all three.

An analogy Like a rare piece of music: few have heard it, fewer understand it, and both a performer who can play it and a listener who can follow it are uncommon.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does Death say?

That the self is something many never hear of and many, hearing, do not understand; that its teacher and its learner are rare and gifted; and that a knower taught by a gifted teacher is a wonder.

Traditional Interpretation

Why is it so rare?

Śaṅkara: because most people are like those just described, and only one in thousands who seek the good comes to know the self, as Naciketas has: यस्तु श्रेयोर्थी स सहस्रेषु कश्चिदेवात्मविद्भवति त्वद्विधः. Those who do not know it are the unfortunate, the unprepared: अभागिनः असंस्कृतात्मानो न विजानीयुः.

Traditional Interpretation

What does Raṅgarāmānuja add?

That even hearing of the supreme self is the fruit of great merit, श्रवणलाभोऽपि महासुकृतफलमिति भावः, and that among those who hear, understanding is not easy: न हि श्रोतृणां सर्वेषाम्, परमात्मप्रतिपत्तिः सुलभेति भावः.

Comparative Vedānta

Where else is the verse used?

The Gītā echoes it closely (BG 2.29): one sees it as a wonder, another speaks of it as a wonder, and even having heard, no one knows it. Śaṅkara quotes the Kaṭha verse in his Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya (3.4.51) as scripture showing how hard the self is to know: श्रुतिः दुर्बोधत्वमात्मनो दर्शयति.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.2.7

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The self is known only by a rare pupil taught by a skilled teacher: आश्चर्यः ज्ञाता कश्चिदेव कुशलानुशिष्टः कुशलेन निपुणेनाचार्येणानुशिष्टः सन्.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.2.7

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The subject is the supreme self, यः प्रसिद्धः परमात्मा, which many cannot even hear of; a skilled teacher and a skilled obtainer are both rare.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara takes the subject as the self (ātman), Raṅgarāmānuja as the supreme self (paramātman); both agree on the rarity of teacher and pupil. Madhva comments on the passage 1.2.4–8 only at 1.2.8.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • श्रवणायापि बहुभिर्यो न śravaṇāyāpi bahubhiryo na → श्रवणाय + अपि + बहुभिः + यः + न

    savarṇa-dīrgha; visarga as r; aḥ before a voiced consonant

    śravaṇāya + api: a + a give ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः). bahubhiḥ before y: after a vowel other than a or ā the visarga shows as r before a voiced sound. yaḥ before n: P. 6.1.114 (हशि च), P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • शृण्वन्तोऽपि बहवो यं न śṛṇvanto'pi bahavo yaṃ na → शृण्वन्तः + अपि + बहवः + यम् + न

    aḥ before a; aḥ before a voiced consonant; final m

    śṛṇvantaḥ + api: P. 6.1.113 (अतो रोरप्लुतादप्लुते), P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः), P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति). bahavaḥ before y: P. 6.1.114 (हशि च). yam before n: P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • आश्चर्यो वक्ता कुशलोऽस्य āścaryo vaktā kuśalo'sya → आश्चर्यः + वक्ता + कुशलः + अस्य

    aḥ before a voiced consonant; aḥ before a

    āścaryaḥ before v: P. 6.1.114 (हशि च), P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः). kuśalaḥ + asya: P. 6.1.113 (अतो रोरप्लुतादप्लुते), P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति).

  • लब्धा आश्चर्यो labdhā āścaryo → लब्धा + आश्चर्यः

    unjoined ā + ā

    Normally ā + ā fuse into ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः); here the text keeps them apart at the end of a pāda (see the junction below).

Junctions outside the general rules

  • लब्धा · आश्चर्यः — hiatus at the pāda boundary: labdhā āścaryaḥ: ā before ā is not fused. The words stand at the end of the third and the start of the fourth pāda, and both syllables are counted (11 + 11); fused, the half-line would have 21. Śaṅkara, quoting the verse in his Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya (3.4.51), writes them joined: लब्धाऽऽश्चर्यो.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • कुशलानुशिष्टः kuśalānuśiṣṭaḥ

    tṛtīyā-tatpuruṣa · कुशल + अनुशिष्ट

    Vigraha कुशलेन अनुशिष्टः

    taught by a skilled [teacher]; Śaṅkara: कुशलेन निपुणेनाचार्येणानुशिष्टः

Morphology

लभ्यः
Gerundive of labh, with -ya after a root ending in a labial with short a before it, P. 3.1.98 (पोरदुपधात्‌): ‘to be had’.
विद्युः
Optative third plural of vid ‘know’ (second class): vid-yā- with -us for the plural ending, P. 3.4.108 (झेर्जुस्), the ā dropped before it: vidyuḥ. Śaṅkara glosses it both as a present, न विदन्ति, and as an optative, न विजानीयुः.
वक्ता
vaktṛ, ‘speaker’, an agent noun in -tṛ, P. 3.1.133 (ण्वुल्तृचौ); so too labdhṛ ‘obtainer’ and jñātṛ ‘knower’.
आश्चर्यः
āścarya ‘a marvel’, used here of persons: a speaker or knower of the self is a wonder, that is, rare. Raṅgarāmānuja: दुर्लभः इत्यर्थः.

Anvaya

यः बहुभिः श्रवणाय अपि न लभ्यः, यम् बहवः शृण्वन्तः अपि न विद्युः, अस्य वक्ता आश्चर्यः, लब्धा कुशलः, कुशलानुशिष्टः ज्ञाता आश्चर्यः ।

yaḥ bahubhiḥ śravaṇāya api na labhyaḥ, yam bahavaḥ śṛṇvantaḥ api na vidyuḥ, asya vaktā āścaryaḥ, labdhā kuśalaḥ, kuśalānuśiṣṭaḥ jñātā āścaryaḥ |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two relative clauses with yaḥ and yam describe the self without naming it; the main clauses answer with asya, ‘of it’, and three nominal sentences in which agent nouns are the subjects: its speaker is a wonder, its obtainer skilled, the knower taught by a skilled one a wonder. The copula is understood throughout.

Ambiguities

Who is ‘skilled’?
Śaṅkara takes kuśalaḥ with labdhā: the one who, having heard, attains it is skilled and one among many, कुशलः निपुण एवानेकेषु लब्धा कश्चिदेव भवति. Raṅgarāmānuja makes both the teacher and the obtainer skilled, and both rare: कुशलः प्राप्ता च, दुर्लभः इत्यर्थः.
āścaryo vaktā or āścaryo ’sya vaktā
Madhva’s text adds asya to the first word-group, ‘a wonder is its speaker’ (corrections.json), which makes the third pāda 12 syllables. The base has asya only once, with labdhā; Śaṅkara’s gloss, अस्य वक्तापि आश्चर्यः, shows he took that asya with vaktā as well.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 23 + 22

The halves count 23 and 22. The first pāda, śravaṇāyāpi bahubhir yo na labhyaḥ, has 12; the rest have 11. The hiatus labdhā āścaryaḥ at the pāda boundary is counted as written.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: आश्चर्योऽस्य वक्ता — Madhva’s text reads āścaryo ’sya vaktā, ‘wonderful is its teacher’, adding asya, which the base has only with labdhā.
11 Connections with other scriptures

  • Bhagavad Gītā 2.29 Direct textual parallel

    आश्चर्यवत्पश्यति कश्चिदेनमाश्चर्यवद्वदति तथैव चान्यः

    āścaryavatpaśyati kaścidenamāścaryavadvadati tathaiva cānyaḥ

    The Gītā takes up the Kaṭha’s words: one sees it as a wonder, another speaks of it as a wonder, another hears of it as a wonder, and having heard, no one knows it.

  • Bhagavad Gītā 7.3 Modern thematic association

    मनुष्याणां सहस्रेषु कश्चिद्यतति सिद्धये

    manuṣyāṇāṃ sahasreṣu kaścidyatati siddhaye

    ‘Among thousands of people, one strives for perfection.’ Śaṅkara’s comment on the Kaṭha verse uses the same measure: स सहस्रेषु कश्चिदेवात्मविद्भवति.

  • Śaṅkara on Brahmasūtra 3.4.51 Doctrinal comparison

    श्रवणायापि बहुभिर्यो न लभ्यः शृण्वन्तोऽपि बहवो यं न विद्युः

    śravaṇāyāpi bahubhiryo na labhyaḥ śṛṇvanto'pi bahavo yaṃ na vidyuḥ

    Śaṅkara quotes the whole verse in the Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya to show that knowledge may arise only after obstacles are worn away, even in a later life.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Learning and apprenticeship

Anyone who has learned a demanding craft knows the verse’s three rarities: the chance to hear of it at all, a teacher who can really pass it on, and a learner able to receive it. When the three meet, something is transmitted that books alone do not carry. The verse asks the student to value the meeting, not only the information. This is a reflection, not part of the commentaries.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Who taught you something that you could not have learned alone?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why should both teaching and learning this be called ‘a wonder’?

A contemplation exercise Thank, in words or in writing, one teacher whose teaching you now carry.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
