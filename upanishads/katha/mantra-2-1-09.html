Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 9 of 15

Whence the sun rises

That from which the sun rises and into which it sets, on which all the gods are fixed and beyond which no one goes: this is that.

5 Faithful English translation

From where the sun rises and where it goes to setting, on that all the gods are fixed; that indeed no one goes beyond. This truly is that.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

That from which the sun rises and into which it sets, on which all the gods are fixed, that no one passes beyond. This is that.

Key concepts ब्रह्मन् Brahman प्राण breath, life-force सूर्य the Sun देवता deity

7 Explain it to a beginner

The sun seems to come from somewhere every morning and go somewhere every evening. This verse says that the place it comes from and the place it returns to are the same reality the whole teaching has been pointing at. All the powers of the world, which the text calls the gods, rest on it like spokes on the hub of a wheel, and nothing can step outside it. Śaṅkara reads the source of the sun as the life-breath of the universe, and the life-breath as Brahman itself; another Upaniṣad says the sun rises from breath. Raṅgarāmānuja reads it as Brahman, the inner ruler that no one can get away from, any more than one can get away from one's own shadow. The verse ends, like those before it, with ‘this is that’: this is what Naciketas asked about.

An analogy Like the hub of a wheel: every spoke is fixed in it, and however fast the wheel turns, no spoke goes beyond it.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

That from which the sun rises and into which it sets, on which all the gods are fixed, no one goes beyond. This is that — the Brahman Naciketas asked about.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: prāṇa, which is Brahman

Śaṅkara takes the source of the sun as prāṇa, यस्मात्प्राणात्, and the gods fixed in it as the deities outside and the faculties within, अग्न्यादयः अधिदैवं वागादयश्चाध्यात्मं, like spokes in the hub of a wheel, अरा इव रथनाभौ. That prāṇa is itself Brahman, सोऽपि ब्रह्मैव, and no one, going beyond it, becomes other than it: नातीत्य तदात्मकतां तदन्यत्वं गच्छति.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: Brahman, the inner controller

For Raṅgarāmānuja the sun rises from Brahman and sets in it; the gods are established in that self, तस्मिन् आत्मनि प्रतिष्ठिताः; and no one can pass beyond Brahman that is the self of all, सर्वात्मकं ब्रह्म, कोऽपि न अतिक्रमेत, because the inner controller cannot be stepped over, like one's shadow.

Madhva

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

The unjoined vowels

The base writes sūryaḥ astam and sarve arpitāḥ with the vowels apart, where the ordinary rules give sūryo 'stam and sarve 'rpitāḥ; GRETIL, the vishvAsa mūla and Madhva's text write the first joined. Apart, both pādas keep eight syllables.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Both read the verse of Brahman; Śaṅkara reaches it through prāṇa and reads ‘no one goes beyond’ as ‘no one is other than it’, Raṅgarāmānuja as ‘no one escapes its rule’. Madhva's bhāṣya has no comment on the verse in our source.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • यतश्चोदेति yataścodeti → यतः + च + उदेति

    visarga before c; a + u

    The visarga becomes s before a voiceless consonant, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः), and s becomes ś before c, P. 8.4.40 (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः); a + u give o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • अर्पितास्तदु arpitāstadu → अर्पिताः + तत् + उ

    visarga before t; t before a vowel

    The visarga becomes s before t, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः); a final t becomes d before a vowel, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

  • नात्येति nātyeti → न + अत्येति

    a + a

    Two like vowels fuse into the long vowel, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • एतद्वै etadvai → एतत् + वै

    t before a voiced consonant

    A final t becomes d before a voiced sound, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

Junctions outside the general rules

  • सूर्यः · अस्तम् — unjoined hiatus (as printed): The base prints sūryaḥ astam with the visarga kept and the vowels apart; the ordinary rule would give sūryo 'stam, as GRETIL, the vishvAsa mūla and Madhva's text write it (corrections.json, orthographic). Unjoined, the second pāda (astaṃ yatra ca gacchati) keeps its eight syllables.
  • सर्वे · अर्पिताः — unjoined hiatus (as printed): sarve arpitāḥ: the ordinary rule would elide the a, sarve 'rpitāḥ, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति); the vishvAsa mūla writes it so. The base keeps the vowels apart, which gives the third pāda its eight syllables.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

तम्
An accusative with arpitāḥ, ‘fixed onto him’, where a locative would be usual; both commentators explain it with a locative — Raṅgarāmānuja तस्मिन् आत्मनि प्रतिष्ठिताः, Śaṅkara by the spokes fixed in the hub of a wheel.
अत्येति
ati + eti, from √i ‘go’: ‘goes beyond, passes over’. The preverb is written with the verb.
कश्चन
The interrogative kaḥ with the particle cana makes an indefinite, ‘anyone’; with the negative, ‘no one’.

Anvaya

यतः च सूर्यः उदेति, यत्र च अस्तम् गच्छति, तम् सर्वे देवाः अर्पिताः [सन्ति]; तत् उ कश्चन न अत्येति । एतत् वै तत् ।

yataḥ ca sūryaḥ udeti, yatra ca astam gacchati, tam sarve devāḥ arpitāḥ santi; tat u kaścana na atyeti | etat vai tat |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two relative clauses (yataḥ … udeti, yatra … gacchati) are picked up by the correlative tam, on which the gods are fixed; a further clause says that no one passes beyond tat. The refrain etad vai tat closes the verse, as at 2.1.3–8.

Ambiguities

From what does the sun rise?
The verse names no source. Śaṅkara takes it as prāṇa, यस्मात्प्राणात्, which is itself Brahman, सोऽपि ब्रह्मैव; the Bṛhadāraṇyaka (1.5.23) quotes the same line and says the sun rises from prāṇa. Raṅgarāmānuja takes it as Brahman directly.
‘No one goes beyond it’
Śaṅkara: no one becomes other than it, नातीत्य तदात्मकतां तदन्यत्वं गच्छति; Raṅgarāmānuja: no one can transgress the inner controller, as no one can step over his own shadow, छायावत् अन्तर्यामिण: दुर्लङ्घत्वात्.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 15 + 16 + 4

Counted as written, 15, 16 and 4 syllables. The first pāda (yataś codeti sūryaḥ) has seven; it has eight if sūryaḥ is read with three syllables (sū-ri-yaḥ), a resolution common in Vedic verse but marked by no witness. The unjoined hiatus sūryaḥ astam and sarve arpitāḥ keeps the second and third pādas at eight. The refrain etad vai tat (4) stands outside the metre.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Science and consciousness

Sunrise and sunset are not events on the sun but on the turning earth: the sun goes nowhere. The verse, in its own way, also looks past the rising and setting to what does not move. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What in your life stays the same while everything around it rises and sets?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why does Śaṅkara read the source of the sun as prāṇa before he calls it Brahman? What does the Bṛhadāraṇyaka parallel add?

A contemplation exercise Watch a sunrise or sunset once this week, and notice what in you is watching.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
