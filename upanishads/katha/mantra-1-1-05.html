Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 5 of 29

‘Among many I go first’

Alone, the boy reflects on his worth and wonders what Death could want with him.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘Among many I go first, among many in the middle. What could Yama want with me, that he will do today?’

Key concepts मृत्यु death

7 Explain it to a beginner

The boy has been given to Death in a moment of anger. Alone, he thinks it over. ‘Among many I go first; among many I go in the middle.’ The commentators read this in two ways. One: he has always been a good son and pupil, among the best or at least the average, never the worst, so why should he be thrown away? The other: whenever people go to Death, he will be at the front or in the middle, not hanging back, because he is not afraid. Then he asks: what could Death want with me? He does not argue with his father’s words. He takes them as something that must now happen, and wonders what it will mean.

An analogy Like a soldier who, ordered somewhere unexpectedly, does not protest but asks quietly what he could possibly be needed for there.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the mantra say?

The boy says that among many he goes first or in the middle, and asks what Yama’s purpose could be that he will accomplish with him today.

Traditional Interpretation

Is the boy complaining?

Śaṅkara: alone, the son lamented — स एवमुक्तः पुत्रः एकान्ते परिदेवयाञ्चकार. He concludes that his father spoke in anger, not with any purpose — नूनं प्रयोजनमनपेक्ष्यैव क्रोधवशादुक्तवान् पिता — yet resolves that the father’s word must not be made false: तथापि तत्पितुर्वचो मृषा मा भूदिति.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Raṅgarāmānuja read it?

As fearlessness: सर्वेषां मृत्युसदनगन्तृणां पुरतो मध्ये वा गच्छामि — among all who go to Death’s house I go in front or in the middle. What troubles him is only his own use: पूर्णकामस्य मृत्योः मादृशेन बालिशेन किं प्रयोजनं स्यात्, what purpose could Death, who wants nothing, have for a child like me?

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva

Madhva’s bhāṣya has no separate comment on this mantra; the verse he cites for 1.1.5–7 begins with the boy’s journey: स जगाम यमं बालो ब्रह्मचारी यमस्य तु.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.5

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

A lament in private that ends in resolve: the father’s word must stand — तथापि तत्पितुर्वचो मृषा मा भूदिति.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Prakāśikā 1.1.5

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Not a lament but fearless reflection: the boy goes first or in the middle among those who go to Death, and wonders only what use he could be: पूर्णकामस्य मृत्योः मादृशेन बालिशेन किं प्रयोजनं स्यात्.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara hears a lament, Raṅgarāmānuja calm courage. Both readings are possible from the words; neither changes the outcome.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • बहूनामेमि bahūnāmemi → बहूनाम् + एमि

    final m before a vowel

    A final m before a vowel stays m and joins the next word.

  • प्रथमो बहूनाम् prathamo bahūnām → प्रथमः + बहूनाम्

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    aḥ becomes o before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

  • यन्मयाद्य yanmayādya → यत् + मया + अद्य

    nasal assimilation; savarṇa-dīrgha

    A final t before m becomes n, P. 8.4.45 (यरोऽनुनासिकेऽनुनासिको वा); mayā + adya → mayādya, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

कर्तव्यम्
Gerundive of kṛ, ‘what is to be done’, used as a noun: ‘purpose, business’. Śaṅkara glosses it as prayojana.
स्वित्
An interrogative particle adding doubt: kiṃ svit, ‘what, I wonder?’

Anvaya

[अहम्] बहूनाम् प्रथमः एमि, बहूनाम् मध्यमः एमि । यमस्य किम् स्वित् कर्तव्यम् [अस्ति] यत् [सः] अद्य मया करिष्यति ॥

aham bahūnām prathamaḥ emi, bahūnām madhyamaḥ emi | yamasya kim svit kartavyam asti yat saḥ adya mayā kariṣyati ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two parallel statements (‘among many I go first / in the middle’) and a question with a relative clause. The speaker is the boy, alone; the verse is a soliloquy.

Ambiguities

First among whom, going where?
Śaṅkara: among many pupils or sons I conduct myself as the best or at least the middling, never the worst — the boy reflects on his worth. Raṅgarāmānuja: among all who go to Death’s house I go at the front or in the middle, not behind — I am not afraid. The words allow both.
Lament or calm?
Śaṅkara calls it a lament in private (paridevana). Raṅgarāmānuja says the boy spoke ‘free of fear and grief’ (विगतसाध्वसशोकः), and only wondered what use a child could be to Death.

Metre

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Emotional attachments

When someone we depend on treats us carelessly, we can protest, or we can ask what the new situation requires of us. The boy does the second. This is a modern reflection, not the text’s claim.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin When things go badly for you because of someone else, what is your first thought?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Is it possible to accept a situation without accepting that it was right?

A contemplation exercise Think of one difficult situation you are in and ask, as the boy does, what it might be for.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
