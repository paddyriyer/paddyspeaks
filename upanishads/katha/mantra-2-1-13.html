Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 13 of 15

A smokeless flame

The thumb-sized person is like a flame without smoke, the lord of what was and what will be; he is today and he is tomorrow. This is that.

5 Faithful English translation

The person of the measure of a thumb [is] like a smokeless light, the ruler of what has been and will be; he alone [is] today, and he [is] tomorrow. This truly is that.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

The person no bigger than a thumb is like a flame without smoke, lord of past and future. He is the same today and the same tomorrow. This is that.

Key concepts अङ्गुष्ठमात्र aṅguṣṭhamātra, thumb-sized पुरुष person ईश्वर the Lord आत्मन् self, Self अमृत immortality, the deathless

7 Explain it to a beginner

The verse repeats the thumb-sized person of the last verse and adds two images. He is like a flame without smoke: pure light, with nothing clouding it. And he does not change with time: he is here today and will be the same tomorrow, the lord of all that was and all that will be. Śaṅkara takes this as a reply to the doubt Naciketas raised at the start, when he said some people think nothing remains after death; it also answers the view that everything lasts only a moment. Raṅgarāmānuja reads it a little differently: everything that exists today and everything that will exist tomorrow has this person for its inner self. The verse ends, like the others, with ‘this is that’.

An analogy Like a lamp burning clean oil in a still room: a steady flame with no smoke, the same light from one hour to the next.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

The thumb-sized person is like a smokeless flame, lord of past and future; he is the same today and tomorrow. This is that.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the same today and tomorrow

The one discerned in this way by yogins in the heart, यस्त्वेवं लक्षितो योगिभिर्हृदये, the lord of past and future, is eternal and unchanging, स एव नित्यः कूटस्थः; present now in living beings, he will be so tomorrow; no other like him will be born. By its own words the scripture here answers the view ‘some say he is not’ (1.1.20), and the doctrine of momentariness too: स्ववचनेन श्रुत्या प्रत्युक्तः, तथा क्षणभङ्गवादश्च.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: the self of all times

Like a fire of dry fuel, शुष्केन्धनानलवत्, he shines without smoke. ‘He is today, he is tomorrow’ means that all things of today and of tomorrow, अद्यतनपदार्थजातं श्वस्तनपदार्थजातं, all things of the three times, have him for their self.

Comparative Vedānta

Madhva

Madhva's passage filed under 2.1.12–14 concerns 2.1.14 only; in our source his bhāṣya says nothing on this verse.

Comparative Vedānta

The same words in the Bṛhadāraṇyaka

The Bṛhadāraṇyaka (1.5.23), which also has the sunrise line of 2.1.9, has the words स एवाद्य स उ श्व of prāṇa: the vow the gods made, kept today and tomorrow.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.1.13

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The self is eternal and unchanging, स एव नित्यः कूटस्थः; the verse refutes both the denial of the self after death and the doctrine of momentariness.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.1.13

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Everything in the three times has the supreme Person for its self, कालत्रयवर्तिपदार्थ जातमपि तदात्मकमित्यर्थः.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara reads ‘today and tomorrow’ as the unchanging persistence of the one self; Raṅgarāmānuja as the Person being the self of all things at every time. Madhva's bhāṣya has no comment on the verse in our source.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • ज्योतिरिवाधूमकः jyotirivādhūmakaḥ → ज्योतिः + इव + अधूमकः

    s-stem before a vowel; a + a

    The final s of jyotis becomes r before a vowel (P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः)); a + a give ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • ईशानो भूतभव्यस्य īśāno bhūtabhavyasya → ईशानः + भूतभव्यस्य

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

  • स एवाद्य sa evādya → सः + एव + अद्य

    saḥ before a vowel; a + a

    The visarga of saḥ is lost before a vowel and the vowels stay apart; a + a give ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • स उ श्वः sa u śvaḥ → सः + उ + श्वः

    saḥ before a vowel

    The visarga of saḥ is lost before a vowel, and the vowels stay apart.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • भूतभव्यस्य bhūtabhavyasya

    samāhāra-dvandva · भूत + भव्य

    Vigraha भूतं च भव्यं च तयोः समाहारः

    what has been and what will be, together

Morphology

अधूमकः
a- ‘without’ + dhūma ‘smoke’ + the suffix -ka: ‘smokeless’. It is masculine, agreeing with puruṣaḥ, though it describes jyotiḥ, a neuter; Śaṅkara remarks that the neuter adhūmakam would be fitting, since it refers to the light: अधूमकमिति युक्तं ज्योतिःपरत्वात्.
श्वः
An adverb, ‘tomorrow’ (śvas), set against adya, ‘today’.

Anvaya

अङ्गुष्ठमात्रः पुरुषः अधूमकः ज्योतिः इव [अस्ति], भूतभव्यस्य ईशानः; सः एव अद्य, सः उ श्वः । एतत् वै तत् ।

aṅguṣṭhamātraḥ puruṣaḥ adhūmakaḥ jyotiḥ iva asti, bhūtabhavyasya īśānaḥ; saḥ eva adya, saḥ u śvaḥ | etat vai tat |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Nominal sentences without a verb: puruṣaḥ … jyotir iva adhūmakaḥ, īśānaḥ; then two short clauses, sa evādya and sa u śvaḥ, with time-adverbs as predicates. The refrain closes the verse.

Ambiguities

‘He is today, he is tomorrow’
Śaṅkara: the same eternal, unchanging one is present now and will be tomorrow, and no other like him will be born, नान्यस्तत्समोऽन्यश्च जनिष्यत इत्यर्थः; Raṅgarāmānuja: the things of today and of tomorrow, of all three times, have him for their self, कालत्रयवर्तिपदार्थ जातमपि तदात्मकमित्यर्थः.
Why ‘smokeless’?
Raṅgarāmānuja: shining like a fire of dry fuel, without smoke; Śaṅkara comments only on the gender of the word. The image suggests a light with nothing obscuring it.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 15 + 15 + 4

Counted as written, 15, 15 and 4 syllables. The second pāda, jyotir ivādhūmakaḥ, has seven, and eight if iva and adhūmakaḥ are kept apart, as GRETIL writes them (corrections.json); the fourth, sa evādya sa u śvaḥ, has seven, and eight with eva adya apart. The refrain etad vai tat (4) stands outside the metre.

Textual variations between witnesses

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Psychology and inner life

Moods, thoughts and circumstances change from one day to the next, yet something lets a person say ‘I’ of all of them. The verse points to that continuity and gives it the image of a steady, clear flame. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What in you was the same yesterday as it is today?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Śaṅkara hears in ‘he is today, he is tomorrow’ an answer to the doubt of 1.1.20. Why would that doubt need answering here, so late in the teaching?

A contemplation exercise Light a candle or lamp and watch the flame for two minutes without doing anything else.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
