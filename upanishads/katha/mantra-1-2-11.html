Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 11 of 25

‘You saw it all and let it go’

Death praises Naciketas: he saw the fulfilment of desire, the support of the world, the endless reward of the rite, the far shore of fearlessness, the great and praised, the wide-reaching, the abiding — and, steady and wise, let it all go.

5 Faithful English translation

Having seen the attainment of desire, the support of the world, the endlessness of the rite, the far shore of fearlessness, the praiseworthy-great, the wide-going, the abiding, you, wise [one], O Naciketas, with steadfastness have let [them] go.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

You saw the fulfilment of every desire, the ground on which the world stands, the endless reward of ritual, the far shore where there is no fear, a station great and praised, wide-reaching and secure — and, Naciketas, wise and steady, you let it all go.

Key concepts हिरण्यगर्भ Hiraṇyagarbha, ‘golden embryo’ त्याग giving up, relinquishment धीर the wise

7 Explain it to a beginner

Death now praises the boy. He lists what Naciketas has been shown: the fulfilment of every wish, the ground the whole world stands on, the endless reward that rituals promise, a far shore where there is no fear, a high and praised position that reaches far and stays secure. And Naciketas, steady and wise, let all of it go. The list is deliberately grand. Śaṅkara reads it as the highest heaven a person can reach by meditation, the station of the cosmic Creator, and says the boy refused even that because he wanted only the highest. Raṅgarāmānuja offers two readings, in one of which the later words describe liberation itself. Madhva says the boy saw the Lord, and so gave up the rest. In every reading the praise is for one thing: the boy could see something magnificent and still say, not this.

An analogy Like a student offered the directorship of a famous institute who turns it down because it is not the question they came to ask.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does Death praise?

Naciketas saw a series of great things — the attainment of desire, the support of the world, the endless fruit of the rite, the far shore of fearlessness, a great, wide-reaching and abiding station — and with steadfastness let them go.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the station of Hiraṇyagarbha

Śaṅkara reads the list as the highest fruit of meditation, where every desire is fulfilled — अत्र हि सर्वे कामाः परिसमाप्ताः — the fruit of the rite being Hiraṇyagarbha’s station, क्रतोः उपासनायाः फलं हैरण्यगर्भं पदम्. Naciketas, wanting only the highest, gave up the whole range of worldly enjoyment: परमेवाकाङ्क्षन्नतिसृष्टवानसि सर्वमेतत्संसारभोगजातम्.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: why the boy may hear

Raṅgarāmānuja takes the verse as unfolding the wish at the close of 1.2.9 for a questioner like Naciketas: it shows his fitness to hear — पूर्वमन्त्रोक्तं नचिकेतसः श्रवणाधिकारं विवृणोति. The boy saw the fruit of rites and the greatness of liberation, and in his wisdom gave up worldly desires: लौकिकान् कामान् प्रज्ञाशालित्वात् त्यक्तवानसि इत्यर्थः. Or the whole list describes the supreme Self: सर्वं परमात्मविषयतया योजनीयम्.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: Urugāya is the Lord

Madhva takes urugāya as the Lord’s name, and concludes that Naciketas’ question is not about the individual soul: उरुगायमित्युक्तत्वाच्च न जीवविषयोऽयं प्रश्नः. Having seen Urugāya, the boy let go of the attainment of desires — उरुगायं दृष्ट्वा कामस्याप्तिमत्यस्राक्षीः. He adds that a boy who had died and come to Yama could not be asking whether the dead exist: न च मृत्वा यमं प्राप्तस्य नचिकेतसो मृतोऽस्ति न वेति संशयो युज्यते.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

anantyam or ānantyam

AS and GRETIL read anantyam; the vishvAsa mūla, Madhva’s text and Raṅgarāmānuja read ānantyam, the regular abstract, which Śaṅkara supplies as his gloss: अनन्त्यम् आनन्त्यम्. The sense, ‘endlessness’, is the same (corrections.json).

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.2.11

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The list describes Hiraṇyagarbha’s station, the summit of what meditation can win; Naciketas rejected even that for the highest: परमेवाकाङ्क्षन्नतिसृष्टवानसि सर्वमेतत्संसारभोगजातम्.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.2.11

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Two readings: worldly fruit set against the greatness of liberation, or the whole list said of the supreme Self — सर्वं परमात्मविषयतया योजनीयम्. Either way the verse shows the boy fit to hear.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.2.11

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The boy saw Urugāya, the Lord: उरुगायं दृष्ट्वा कामस्याप्तिमत्यस्राक्षीः. The word urugāya shows that the question concerns the Lord, not the soul.

Where they disagree

For Śaṅkara the whole list is what was given up; for Madhva its key word names what was seen; Raṅgarāmānuja allows both. All three praise the same renunciation.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • कामस्याप्तिं kāmasyāptiṃ → कामस्य + आप्तिम्

    a + ā

    Like vowels join long, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः). GRETIL writes kāmasya āptiṃ with the vowels apart (corrections.json; orthographic).

  • क्रतोरनन्त्यम् kratoranantyam → क्रतोः + अनन्त्यम्

    visarga as r

    The final s of kratoḥ (P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः)) is r before a vowel after o.

  • स्तोममहदुरुगायं stomamahadurugāyaṃ → स्तोममहत् + उरुगायम्

    t voiced before a vowel

    stomamahat before u becomes stomamahad, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

  • धीरो नचिकेतोऽत्यस्राक्षीः dhīro naciketo'tyasrākṣīḥ → धीरः + नचिकेतः + अत्यस्राक्षीः

    aḥ before a voiced consonant; aḥ before a

    dhīraḥ before n becomes dhīro, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च) and P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः). naciketaḥ before a becomes naciketo and the a is elided (avagraha), P. 6.1.113 (अतो रोरप्लुतादप्लुते), P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः), P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • स्तोममहत् stomamahat

    karmadhāraya (Śaṅkara) · स्तोम + महत्

    Vigraha स्तोमं च तन्महच्च

    praiseworthy and great; Raṅgarāmānuja’s text writes stomaṃ mahad, Madhva reads ‘greater than praises’ (see ambiguities)

  • उरुगायम् urugāyam

    karmadhāraya (as Śaṅkara glosses it) · उरु + गाय

    Vigraha उरुः गायः

    wide going, far-reaching — Śaṅkara: विस्तीर्णां गतिम्; Madhva takes it as a name of the Lord

  • अभयस्य abhayasya

    nañ-tatpuruṣa · न + भय

    Vigraha न भयम्

    fearlessness

Morphology

अनन्त्यम्
Abstract noun from ananta ‘endless’ with -ya. The regular form lengthens the first vowel, ānantyam, which the vishvAsa mūla, Madhva’s text and Raṅgarāmānuja read and which Śaṅkara gives as his gloss: अनन्त्यम् आनन्त्यम्. The syllable count is the same either way.
अत्यस्राक्षीः
Sigmatic aorist of ati-√sṛj with the augment a-, 2nd sg.: ‘you have released, passed over’. The same verb closes 1.2.3.
प्रतिष्ठाम्
Used twice. Śaṅkara takes the first as the support of the world (आश्रयं सर्वात्मकत्वात्) and the second as one’s own unsurpassed abiding (स्थितिमात्मनोऽनुत्तमामपि).

Anvaya

नचिकेतः, [त्वम्] कामस्य आप्तिम्, जगतः प्रतिष्ठाम्, क्रतोः अनन्त्यम्, अभयस्य पारम्, स्तोममहत्, उरुगायम्, प्रतिष्ठाम् [च] दृष्ट्वा धीरः [सन्] धृत्या अत्यस्राक्षीः ।

naciketaḥ, tvam kāmasya āptim, jagataḥ pratiṣṭhām, kratoḥ anantyam, abhayasya pāram, stomamahat, urugāyam, pratiṣṭhām ca dṛṣṭvā dhīraḥ san dhṛtyā atyasrākṣīḥ |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

One sentence. A series of accusatives, not joined by ca, depends on the absolutive dṛṣṭvā ‘having seen’; then come the manner (dhṛtyā), the predicate adjective dhīraḥ, the vocative and the main verb atyasrākṣīḥ. How many things the list names, and whether it describes one goal or several, the verse leaves open.

Ambiguities

What Naciketas saw
Śaṅkara: the whole list describes Hiraṇyagarbha’s station, the highest fruit of meditation — क्रतोः उपासनायाः फलं हैरण्यगर्भं पदम् — which the boy saw and still gave up. Raṅgarāmānuja gives two readings: the first items are the fruit of rites and the later ones describe liberation; or the whole list is said of the supreme Self — सर्वं परमात्मविषयतया योजनीयम्. Madhva: the boy saw Urugāya, the Lord, and let go of the attainment of desires — उरुगायं दृष्ट्वा कामस्याप्तिमत्यस्राक्षीः.
stomamahat
Śaṅkara: one compound, ‘praiseworthy and great’ — स्तोमं च तन्महच्च निरतिशयत्वात् स्तोममहत्. Raṅgarāmānuja’s text writes stomaṃ mahad, two words, and he takes stoma as the mass of great qualities, अपहतपाप्मत्वसत्यसंकल्पत्वादिमहागुणगणरूपस्तोमं. Madhva: what even praises cannot wholly reach, greater than praises — स्तोमैरपि सर्वात्मना प्राप्तुमशक्यम् । स्तोमेभ्योऽपि महान्तम्.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 21 + 24

Triṣṭubh, 21 + 24 syllables: pādas of 10, 11, 11 and 13. As joined, the first pāda (kāmasyāptiṃ jagataḥ pratiṣṭhāṃ) has ten; GRETIL’s kāmasya āptiṃ, with the vowels apart, counts eleven. The last pāda (dṛṣṭvā dhṛtyā dhīro naciketo ’tyasrākṣīḥ) has thirteen in every witness. ānantyam for anantyam does not change the count.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading The vishvAsa mūla: प्रतिष्ठां क्रतोरानन्त्यमभयस्य पारम् — The vishvAsa mūla and Madhva’s text read ānantyam, the regular abstract (‘endlessness’), AS and GRETIL anantyam; Śaṅkara glosses the base’s word with the regular form (अनन्त्यम् आनन्त्यम्).
  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: प्रतिष्ठां क्रतोरानन्त्यमभयस्य पारम् — Madhva’s text reads ānantyam for anantyam, as the vishvAsa mūla does.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Ego and self-image

Turning down a small temptation is easy to admire. Turning down a magnificent and legitimate prize — real influence, deserved praise — is harder to recognise as wisdom, because it can look like a lack of ambition. The verse praises exactly this: seeing something great clearly and still deciding it is not what one is looking for. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Have you ever said no to something good because it was not what you were looking for?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why might the verse list the greatest prizes rather than obvious vices? What does refusal mean when what is refused is genuinely good?

A contemplation exercise Name one good thing you pursue mainly because others admire it. Ask whether you would still want it if no one knew.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
