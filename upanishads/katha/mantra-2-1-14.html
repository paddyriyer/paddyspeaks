Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 14 of 15

Rain on a mountain

As rain falling on high ground runs off down the slopes, so one who sees the dharmas as separate runs after them.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

As rain that falls on a peak runs away down the slopes in every direction, so one who sees things as separate runs after them, scattered.

Key concepts धर्म dharma एकत्व oneness संसार saṃsāra, the round of births अविद्या avidyā

7 Explain it to a beginner

Picture rain falling on a mountain top. It does not stay together: it splits into streams and runs away down every slope until it is lost. The verse says the same happens to a person who sees things as cut off from one another. That person's attention is scattered after them, and never comes to rest. The commentators each say what ‘things’ means here. Śaṅkara says it means the selves of beings, really one self, seen as many, one in each body; seeing them so, one takes body after body. Raṅgarāmānuja says it means the attributes of the one supreme Self, seen as belonging to different beings. Madhva says it means God's own qualities and actions, imagined as separate from him and from each other.

An analogy Like rain on a mountain peak: one shower, but it breaks into a hundred streams and is lost in the valleys.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

As rain on high ground runs off down the slopes in all directions, so one who sees the dharmas apart runs after them.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: seeing selves apart, one takes body after body

The verse again rejects the seeing of difference in Brahman, पुनरपि भेददर्शनापवादं ब्रह्मण आह. As water on a high place runs down into low places and, scattered, is lost, so whoever sees the dharmas, which are not different from the self, as separate in each body, पृथगेव प्रतिशरीरं पश्यन्, follows them: he takes on separate bodies again and again.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: the Lord's attributes seen apart

Rain on a mountain-top falls into the lower hills and runs on. So one who sees the attributes of the supreme Self, such as being the inner controller of gods and of men, परमात्मगतदेवान्तर्यामित्वमनुष्यान्तर्यामित्वादिधर्मान्, as resting in different substrates, falls like a mountain stream into the hollow of saṃsāra, संसारकुहरे पतति.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: not only difference-in-identity

Madhva says the verse is spoken to remove the doubt that only difference-in-identity is denied, not difference, भेदाभेद एव निषिध्यते न तु भेद इत्याशङ्कां निवर्तयितुम्: it names the separate fault of seeing difference among the Lord's attributes — his limbs, qualities and acts, भगवद्धर्माणामवयवगुणकर्मणां. Rain on a peak runs down; so one who sees Viṣṇu's attributes apart runs into blind darkness, एवं विष्णोर्धर्मान् पृथक् पश्यन्नन्धन्तमो विधावति.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.1.14

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The dharmas are not different from the self, आत्मनोऽभिन्नान्; seeing them as separate in each body is the cause of rebirth.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.1.14

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The dharmas are the supreme Self's own attributes as the inner controller of all beings; whoever sees them as belonging to separate substrates संसारकुहरे पतति, falls into the hollow of saṃsāra.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.1.12–14

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The dharmas are the Lord's limbs, qualities and acts; to see them as different from one another or from him is a fault in its own right, भगवद्धर्माणामवयवगुणकर्मणां भेददर्शने पृथग् दोषमाह.

Where they disagree

All three read the image as a warning against seeing apart what belongs together; they differ on what belongs together — the selves of all beings as one self (Śaṅkara), the attributes of the one inner controller (Raṅgarāmānuja), the qualities and acts of the Lord with the Lord himself (Madhva).

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • यथोदकं yathodakaṃ → यथा + उदकम्

    ā + u

    ā + u give o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • पश्यंस्तानेवानुविधावति paśyaṃstānevānuvidhāvati → पश्यन् + तान् + एव + अनुविधावति

    n before t; a + a

    A final n before t takes s and becomes anusvāra, P. 8.3.7 (नश्छव्यप्रशान्); a + a give ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • एवं धर्मान्पृथक् evaṃ dharmānpṛthak → एवम् + धर्मान् + पृथक्

    m before a consonant

    A final m becomes anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः); n and k stay unchanged before a voiceless consonant and are written joined to the next word.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

पश्यन्
The present participle of paśya- (the present of √dṛś), nominative masculine singular; it is the subject of anuvidhāvati: ‘seeing them apart, he runs after them’.
अनुविधावति
The verb of the comparison, vi-dhāvati, ‘runs off in all directions’, returns with anu-, ‘after’: the one who sees apart runs after what he sees.
पर्वतेषु
Usually ‘among the mountains’. Śaṅkara glosses it by its root sense, parvan, ‘joint, break’: पर्ववत्सु निम्नप्रदेशेषु, places broken into low ground.

Anvaya

यथा दुर्गे वृष्टम् उदकम् पर्वतेषु विधावति, एवम् धर्मान् पृथक् पश्यन् तान् एव अनुविधावति ।

yathā durge vṛṣṭam udakam parvateṣu vidhāvati, evam dharmān pṛthak paśyan tān eva anuvidhāvati |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A comparison: yathā … vidhāvati (the image) and evam … anuvidhāvati (what it means). The subject of the second clause is the participle paśyan; the verb of the image returns with anu- in the application.

Ambiguities

What are the dharmas?
Śaṅkara: the selves of beings, not different from the self, seen as separate in each body, आत्मनोऽभिन्नान् पृथक् पश्यन् पृथगेव प्रतिशरीरं पश्यन्; Raṅgarāmānuja: the supreme Self's attributes, such as being the inner controller of gods and of men, seen as resting in separate substrates; Madhva: the Lord's limbs, qualities and acts, seen as different from one another and from him.
What is the running?
Śaṅkara: one takes on separate bodies again and again, शरीरभेदमेव पृथक् पुनः पुनः प्रतिपद्यत इत्यर्थः; Raṅgarāmānuja: one falls, like a waterfall from the mountain, into the hollow of saṃsāra, संसारकुहरे पतति; Madhva: one runs into blind darkness.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Psychology and inner life

Attention that is pulled in many directions at once is tiring and leaves little behind, like water spread thin over a slope. The verse ties that scattering to a way of seeing — everything as separate and so each thing to be chased — rather than to the number of things in the world. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin When does your attention feel like rain running off in many directions?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Madhva says this verse is needed because 2.1.10–11 might seem to deny only difference-in-identity. What, for him, does 2.1.14 add?

A contemplation exercise For one hour today, do one thing at a time, and notice when you are pulled to run after another.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
