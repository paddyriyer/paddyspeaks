Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 13 of 29

The second boon: the fire that leads to heaven

Naciketas asks Death, who knows it, to teach him the fire whose builders reach immortality in heaven.

5 Faithful English translation

‘You, O Death, know the fire that leads to heaven; declare it to me who have faith. Those whose world is heaven enjoy immortality. This I choose with the second boon.’

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘You know the fire that leads to heaven, Death. Teach it to me, for I have faith. Those who reach heaven gain immortality — this is my second wish.’

Key concepts अग्नि Agni, fire अमृत immortality, the deathless श्रद्धा faith, trust लोक world, realm वर boon

7 Explain it to a beginner

Having described heaven, Naciketas makes his second request. You, Death, he says, know the fire ritual that takes people to heaven. Teach it to me, because I have faith. People who reach heaven by it gain immortality. That is my second wish. The fire here is a real Vedic ritual: an altar built of bricks on which the sacred fire is laid. Naciketas asks for the knowledge of it, not only its performance. Note his faith: he presents himself as a student ready to learn, and that readiness is what the Upaniṣad values.

An analogy Like a student who, before asking the deepest question, first asks the teacher to show the craft that the teacher is known for.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What is asked?

The knowledge of the fire that leads to heaven, from Death, who knows it, for a student who has faith. Its fruit: those who reach heaven by it enjoy immortality.

Traditional Interpretation

What does Śaṅkara say the fire is?

The means of reaching the heaven just described: एवं गुणविशिष्टस्य स्वर्गलोकस्य प्राप्तिसाधनभूतम् अग्निं. The immortality is the gods’ deathlessness, not the self-knowledge of the third boon: अमृतत्वम् अमरणतां देवत्वं भजन्ते.

Comparative Vedānta

Does the Viśiṣṭādvaita reading differ?

Raṅgarāmānuja takes heaven as release (see 1.1.12) and the fire as serving it through meditation: स्थण्डिलरूपाग्ने: स्वर्गप्रयोजनकत्वञ्चउपासनाद्वारेति उत्तरत्र स्फुटम्. The faithful one is faithful to release: मोक्षश्रद्धावते.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Madhva read ‘fire’?

Madhva’s half-verse on 1.1.12–13: the fire is Hari, present in the Nāciketa fire and called Agni for being foremost (agrya): अग्र्यत्वादग्निनामासौ नाचिकेताग्निगो हरिः.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.13

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

A request for a ritual knowledge whose fruit is heaven and relative immortality, अमरणतां देवत्वं. It belongs to the realm of action; the third boon will ask for what lies beyond action.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.1.13

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The fire serves release by way of meditation, and those ‘whose world is heaven’ have reached the highest abode: परमपदं प्राप्ता इत्यर्थः.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.12–13

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The ‘fire’ asked for is Hari, who is in the Nāciketa fire: नाचिकेताग्निगो हरिः. What is sought through the ritual is the Lord.

Where they disagree

All three take the fire as a real ritual; they differ on its fruit. Śaṅkara: heaven and the gods’ immortality. Raṅgarāmānuja: release, through meditation. Madhva: Hari as the true object behind the fire.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • त्वमग्निम् tvamagnim → त्वम् + अग्निम्

    final m before a vowel

    Final m before a vowel stays m and joins the next word.

  • स्वर्ग्यमध्येषि svargyamadhyeṣi → स्वर्ग्यम् + अध्येषि

    final m before a vowel

    As above.

  • स्वर्गलोका अमृतत्वम् svargalokā amṛtatvam → स्वर्गलोकाः + अमृतत्वम्

    āḥ before a vowel

    The visarga of -āḥ is lost before a vowel and the hiatus stays: P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), P. 8.3.22 (हलि सर्वेषाम्) for consonants.

  • भजन्त एतद्द्वितीयेन bhajanta etaddvitīyena → भजन्ते + एतत् + द्वितीयेन

    e before a vowel; t before d

    e before a vowel becomes ay and the y is dropped, P. 6.1.78 (एचोऽयवायावः) and P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य); final t before d becomes d, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • स्वर्गलोकाः svargalokāḥ

    bahuvrīhi · स्वर्ग + लोक

    Vigraha स्वर्गः लोकः येषां ते

    those whose world is heaven (the sacrificers who reach it)

Morphology

अध्येषि
Present of adhi-i ‘go over, learn, know’; Śaṅkara glosses ‘you remember, you know’.
स्वर्ग्यम्
svarga + the suffix ya ‘for the sake of’: ‘conducive to heaven’. Raṅgarāmānuja cites the rule: ‘स्वर्गादिभ्यो यद्वक्तव्यः’ इति प्रयोजनम् इत्यर्थे यत्.
श्रद्दधानाय
Present middle participle of śrad-dhā ‘put faith’, dative: ‘to [me who am] faithful’.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

sa tvam … adhyeṣi ‘you, that one, know’ is followed by the request prabrūhi tam ‘declare it’. A general statement about the heaven-dwellers (svargalokā amṛtatvaṃ bhajante) gives the reason, and etad dvitīyena vṛṇe vareṇa closes the request formally.

Ambiguities

What kind of immortality?
Śaṅkara: the immortality of the gods, a long but not absolute deathlessness: अमृतत्वम् अमरणतां देवत्वं भजन्ते. Raṅgarāmānuja: those whose world is heaven are those who have reached the highest abode, परमपदं प्राप्ता इत्यर्थः.
tam or tvam?
The vishvAsa mūla reads tvaṃ, ‘you’, for taṃ, ‘it’ (the fire); Śaṅkara comments on taṃ (corrections.json).

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 22

Two lines of 22 syllables, regular.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading The vishvAsa mūla: प्रब्रूहि त्वँ श्रद्दधानाय — The vishvAsa mūla reads tvaṃ, ‘you’, for taṃ, ‘it [the fire]’ (prabrūhi taṃ śraddadhānāya mahyam); Śaṅkara comments on taṃ (तं प्रब्रूहि), and GRETIL and Madhva’s text read taṃ.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Learning and apprenticeship

Naciketas asks the master for his craft and offers his trust in return. Good learning still begins that way: with a request to someone who knows, and a willingness to take the teaching seriously before judging it. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Whom would you ask to teach you something they are known for?
