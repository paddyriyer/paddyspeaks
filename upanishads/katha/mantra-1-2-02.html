Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 2 of 25

The wise and the dull

Good and pleasant come to everyone mixed; the wise separate them and choose the good, the dull choose the pleasant for getting and keeping.

5 Faithful English translation

‘The better and the dearer come to a man; the wise one, going all round them, separates those two. The wise one indeed chooses the better in preference to the dearer; the dull one chooses the dearer, for the sake of getting and keeping.’

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘The good and the pleasant both come to a person. The wise look at them from every side and tell them apart. The wise choose the good over the pleasant; the dull choose the pleasant, for the sake of getting and keeping.’

Key concepts श्रेयस् śreyas, the good प्रेयस् preyas, the pleasant योगक्षेम yogakṣema, getting and keeping धीर the wise विद्या vidyā भोग enjoyment, use मृत्यु death

7 Explain it to a beginner

Death goes on. The good and the pleasant do not arrive labelled; they come to every person together, mixed up with each other. The wise person walks all round them, looks at them from every side, and tells them apart. Having done that, the wise choose the good, even over something pleasant. The dull choose the pleasant, because it promises to get them things and to keep what they have. The difference between the two kinds of people is not that one is offered better things. Both are offered the same. The difference is in the looking: one stops to sort out what is in front of him, the other takes what feels comfortable now. The commentators compare the wise person to a swan, which in old Indian poetry is said to separate milk from water.

An analogy Like a swan, which Indian poets said could separate milk from the water it was mixed with — the image both commentators use.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

That the good and the pleasant come to everyone together; that the wise examine and separate them and choose the good; and that the dull choose the pleasant for the sake of acquiring and keeping.

Traditional Interpretation

How do the wise separate them?

Śaṅkara: as a swan separates milk from water, हंस इवाम्भसः पयः, weighing their greater and lesser worth in the mind, मनसा आलोच्य गुरुलाघवं विविनक्ति पृथक्करोति धीरः धीमान्. Raṅgarāmānuja uses the same image: नीरक्षीरे हंस इव पृथक्करोति.

Traditional Interpretation

Who is the wise one, and who the dull?

For Raṅgarāmānuja the dhīra is the one who delights in understanding, धिया रमते इति धीरः - प्रज्ञाशाली; the dull one chooses the pleasant for getting and keeping, मन्दमतिः योगक्षेमाद्धेतोः प्रेयो वृणीते. Śaṅkara: the dull one cannot tell the real from the unreal, सदसद्विवेकासामर्थ्यात्, and chooses the pleasant, पशुपुत्रादिलक्षणं, cattle, sons and the like.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What does Madhva’s text change?

It lacks hi in the third pāda, which makes the metre regular, and reads yogakṣemān, accusative plural, for yogakṣemād: the dull one ‘chooses gains and their keeping’ rather than ‘chooses for the sake of’ them. Madhva’s bhāṣya does not comment on this verse (corrections.json).

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.2.2

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Both choices are in a person’s power, सत्यं स्वायत्ते; the slow-witted fail to choose the good because for them good and pleasant come mixed, and only discrimination, विविनक्ति पृथक्करोति, separates them.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.2.2

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The wise one, rich in understanding, chooses the good as the more honoured, प्रेयोपेक्षया अभि - अभ्यर्हितं श्रेय एव वृणीते; the dull one chooses the pleasant for bodily gain and its preservation.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

There is no disagreement on this verse: both commentators read it as discrimination between the good and the pleasant, with the same image of the swan. Madhva does not comment on it.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • श्रेयश्च प्रेयश्च śreyaśca preyaśca → श्रेयः + च + प्रेयः + च

    visarga before c

    The visarga becomes s before a voiceless consonant, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः), and the s becomes ś beside the palatal c, P. 8.4.40 (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः).

  • मनुष्यमेतस्तौ manuṣyametastau → मनुष्यम् + एतः + तौ

    final m before a vowel; visarga before t

    manuṣyam keeps its m before the vowel of etaḥ. etaḥ before t: the visarga becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः).

  • श्रेयो हि धीरोऽभि śreyo hi dhīro'bhi → श्रेयः + हि + धीरः + अभि

    aḥ before a voiced consonant and before a

    śreyaḥ before h: the visarga becomes u, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च), and a + u give o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः). dhīraḥ before a: the visarga becomes u, P. 6.1.113 (अतो रोरप्लुतादप्लुते), o results by P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः), and the a of abhi is taken into it, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति).

  • प्रेयसो वृणीते preyaso vṛṇīte → प्रेयसः + वृणीते

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    As above: P. 6.1.114 (हशि च), P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः). The same rule gives preyo mando and mando yoga-.

  • योगक्षेमाद्वृणीते yogakṣemādvṛṇīte → योगक्षेमात् + वृणीते

    t before a voiced consonant

    Final t becomes d before the voiced v: P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • योगक्षेमात् yogakṣemāt

    samāhāra-dvandva · योग + क्षेम

    Vigraha योगः च क्षेमः च

    getting and keeping; Raṅgarāmānuja: शरीरस्योपचयो योगः, क्षेमः परिपालनम्

Morphology

एतः
ā + itaḥ, present dual of i ‘go’ with the preverb ā: ‘the two come to’. Śaṅkara spells the analysis out: आ इतः एतः प्राप्नुतः.
तौ
Masculine dual, though śreyas and preyas are neuter. Śaṅkara supplies a masculine noun: तौ श्रेयःप्रेयःपदार्थौ, ‘those two things’.
विविनक्ति
Present of vi-vic ‘separate’, seventh class (nasal infix: vi-na-k-ti).
अभि
The preverb of vṛṇīte, set apart from it (tmesis): abhi … vṛṇīte, ‘chooses in preference’. Śaṅkara joins them, अभिवृणीते; Raṅgarāmānuja glosses the word by itself: अभि - अभ्यर्हितं.

Anvaya

श्रेयः च प्रेयः च मनुष्यम् एतः । धीरः तौ सम्परीत्य विविनक्ति । धीरः हि प्रेयसः श्रेयः अभि वृणीते । मन्दः योगक्षेमात् प्रेयः वृणीते ।

śreyaḥ ca preyaḥ ca manuṣyam etaḥ | dhīraḥ tau samparītya vivinakti | dhīraḥ hi preyasaḥ śreyaḥ abhi vṛṇīte | mandaḥ yogakṣemāt preyaḥ vṛṇīte |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Four clauses: both things come to everyone (etaḥ, a dual verb with two subjects joined by ca … ca); the wise one examines and separates them (an absolutive before the finite viviṇakti); and two parallel choices, the wise choosing the better over the dearer (the ablative preyasaḥ with abhi … vṛṇīte), the dull choosing the dearer (an ablative of cause, yogakṣemāt).

Ambiguities

Why do most people choose the pleasant?
Śaṅkara raises the question himself: यद्युभे अपि कर्तुं स्वायत्ते पुरुषेण, किमर्थं प्रेय एवादत्ते बाहुल्येन लोक इति. His answer: for the slow-witted the two come mixed, as it were, in their means and their results — व्यामिश्रीभूते इव — and only the wise separate them.
yogakṣema
Both commentators read the compound as the gaining and keeping of bodily welfare: Śaṅkara शरीराद्युपचयरक्षणनिमित्तमित्येतत्; Raṅgarāmānuja शरीरस्योपचयो योगः, क्षेमः परिपालनम्. Madhva’s text reads the accusative plural yogakṣemān, making them the object chosen rather than its motive (corrections.json).

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 23

The first half has 22 syllables (11 + 11). The second counts 23: the third pāda, śreyo hi dhīro ’bhi preyaso vṛṇīte, has 12, the fourth 11. Madhva’s text lacks hi and so has a regular 11 in the third pāda (corrections.json).

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: श्रेयो धीरोऽभिप्रेयसो वृणीते — Madhva’s text lacks hi in śreyo hi dhīraḥ.
  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: मन्दो योगक्षेमान् वृणीते — Madhva’s text reads yogakṣemān, accusative plural, for yogakṣemād, ablative (‘for the sake of getting and keeping’).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Wealth and consumption

Yogakṣema, ‘getting and keeping’, is a fair description of much of modern economic life: acquiring, then insuring, maintaining and protecting what has been acquired. The verse does not condemn it — the body has to be fed and housed. It observes that when getting and keeping become the reason for every choice, the good is no longer what decides. This is a reflection, not part of the commentaries.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin When did you last stop to look at a choice ‘from every side’ before making it?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why should the good and the pleasant arrive mixed together rather than clearly labelled?

A contemplation exercise For one purchase or commitment this week, write down whether it is for getting, for keeping, or for something else.
