Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 13 of 25

‘Having heard this, the mortal rejoices’

A mortal who hears this, takes it fully to heart, draws out the subtle one that accords with dharma and reaches it, rejoices, for he has found what is worth rejoicing in. For Naciketas, Death thinks, the house stands open.

5 Faithful English translation

Having heard this, having fully grasped [it], a mortal — having drawn out the subtle one according with dharma, having reached this [one] — he rejoices, for [he has] obtained what is to be rejoiced in. Open [is] the house toward Naciketas, I think.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

A mortal who hears this and takes it fully to heart, who draws out this subtle being, true to dharma, and reaches it, rejoices, for he has found what is truly worth rejoicing in. For Naciketas, I think, the house stands open.

Key concepts आत्मन् self, Self आनन्द bliss मोक्ष liberation

7 Explain it to a beginner

Death describes what happens to someone who truly learns this teaching. First they hear it. Then they take it fully to heart. Then they draw out something subtle, the way one might pull a fine thread from a tangle, and they reach it. And then they rejoice — not over a possession, but because they have found the one thing worth rejoicing in. The commentators differ on what is drawn out. Śaṅkara says it is the Self, separated in understanding from the body. Raṅgarāmānuja says the body is what is set aside. Madhva says the Lord is known as distinct from the soul. Then Death turns to the boy: the house, he says, stands open for you. Śaṅkara explains the words as meaning, I think you are ready for freedom. After many tests, this is the first plain word of welcome.

An analogy Like panning for gold: you hear where it lies, you work the gravel, you lift out the small bright grain — and the joy is in the grain, not in the pan.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse describe?

A sequence — hearing, grasping fully, drawing out, reaching, rejoicing — and Death’s judgement that the house stands open for Naciketas.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the stages of learning

One hears the truth of the Self from the teacher and embraces it as one’s own — तच्छ्रुत्वा आचार्यसकाशात् सम्यगात्मभावेन परिगृह्य; separates the subtle Self from the body; reaches it, and rejoices, having found the Self that is worth rejoicing in — मोदनीयं हि हर्षणीयमात्मानं लब्ध्वा. The house is Brahman, its door open to Naciketas: मोक्षार्हं त्वां मन्ये इत्यभिप्रायः.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: reflection, and the freed self

Taking it fully to heart is reflection and what follows — मननादिकं कृत्वा इत्यर्थः. The subtle one is the supreme Self, reached in a particular place, देशविशेषे प्राप्य; the knower, finding his own nature, rejoices, and Raṅgarāmānuja reads this with the Chāndogya’s freed self, स तत्र पर्येति जक्षत् क्रीडन् रममाणः. The open house is the abode that is Brahman: तस्यैष आत्मा विशति ब्रह्मधाम.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: the Lord known as distinct

Madhva: प्रवृह्य जीवात् पृथक्कृत्य. He supports this with a verse he cites from the Mahāvarāha: knowing Viṣṇu, who stands in the freed soul, as distinct from the soul, the freed one rejoices in him for ever — मुक्तजीवे स्थितं विष्णुं विदित्वा जीवतः पृथक् । मोदते मोदनीयं तं प्राप्य मुक्तस्सदैव च.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

enam for etam

Madhva’s text reads enam for etam; both pronouns refer to the same subtle one (corrections.json). The absolutive āpya stands without a preverb.

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The body produced by karma is set aside, the supreme Self is reached — देशविशेषे प्राप्य — and the freed self rejoices in its own nature.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.2.13

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The Lord is known as distinct from the soul — प्रवृह्य जीवात् पृथक्कृत्य — and the freed soul rejoices in him for ever, on a verse Madhva cites from the Mahāvarāha.

Where they disagree

What is ‘drawn out’: the Self from the body (Śaṅkara), the body set aside (Raṅgarāmānuja), the Lord distinguished from the soul (Madhva). Raṅgarāmānuja and Madhva place the joy in a freed self that remains distinct from the Lord.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • एतच्छ्रुत्वा etacchrutvā → एतत् + श्रुत्वा

    t before ś

    t becomes c before ś, P. 8.4.40 (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः), and the ś after it may become ch, P. 8.4.63 (शश्छोऽटि).

  • धर्म्यमणुमेतमाप्य dharmyamaṇumetamāpya → धर्म्यम् + अणुम् + एतम् + आप्य

    final m before a vowel

    A final m before a vowel is written joined to it; the sound does not change.

  • स मोदते sa modate → सः + मोदते

    saḥ before a consonant

    saḥ loses its ending before a consonant, P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि).

  • विवृतं सद्म vivṛtaṃ sadma → विवृतम् + सद्म

    m before a consonant

    P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

Morphology

सम्परिगृह्य
Absolutive in -ya of sam-pari-√grah; -ya replaces -tvā after preverbs, P. 7.1.37 (समासेऽनञ्पूर्वे क्त्वो ल्यप्‌).
आप्य
Absolutive in -ya of √āp, used without a preverb; Śaṅkara glosses it with the usual form, आप्य प्राप्य.
प्रवृह्य
Absolutive of pra-√vṛh ‘tear out, draw out’. The verb returns at the close of the Upaniṣad (2.3.17), where the Self is to be drawn from the body like the stalk from a reed.
मोदनीयम्
Gerundive in -anīya of √mud, ‘to be rejoiced in’, echoing the verb modate.
नचिकेतसम्
Accusative of the person toward whom: Śaṅkara supplies ‘toward you’ — नचिकेतसं त्वां प्रत्यपावृतद्वारं विवृतम् अभिमुखीभूतं मन्ये.

Anvaya

मर्त्यः एतत् श्रुत्वा सम्परिगृह्य, धर्म्यम् अणुम् एतम् प्रवृह्य आप्य, सः मोदनीयम् हि लब्ध्वा मोदते । नचिकेतसम् [प्रति] सद्म विवृतम् [इति] मन्ये ।

martyaḥ etat śrutvā samparigṛhya, dharmyam aṇum etam pravṛhya āpya, saḥ modanīyam hi labdhvā modate | naciketasam prati sadma vivṛtam iti manye |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A chain of five absolutives (śrutvā, samparigṛhya, pravṛhya, āpya, labdhvā) leads to the one finite verb modate, with sa resuming the subject martyaḥ. The last pāda is a separate sentence in the first person — (aham) manye — with vivṛtaṃ sadma as what Death thinks and naciketasam as the one toward whom the house is open.

Ambiguities

What is drawn out?
Śaṅkara: the subtle Self, which accords with dharma, is separated from the body — धर्मादनपेतं धर्म्यं प्रवृह्य उद्यम्य पृथक्कृत्य शरीरादेः. Raṅgarāmānuja: dharmyam is the body and the like, produced by karma, which is separated and given up — धर्म्यम् - कर्मसाध्यं शरीरादि, प्रवृह्य - पृथक्कृत्य परित्यज्य इत्यर्थः — and the subtle one reached is the supreme Self. Madhva: the Lord is distinguished from the soul — प्रवृह्य जीवात् पृथक्कृत्य.
The open house
Śaṅkara: Brahman is a house whose door stands open to Naciketas — मोक्षार्हं त्वां मन्ये इत्यभिप्रायः. Raṅgarāmānuja: the abode that is Brahman stands open and may be entered, as the Muṇḍaka says, तस्यैष आत्मा विशति ब्रह्मधाम.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 23

Triṣṭubh, 22 + 23 syllables: pādas of 11, 11, 11 and 12. The last pāda (vivṛtaṃ sadma naciketasaṃ manye) has twelve in every witness.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: प्रवृह्य धर्म्यमणुमेनमाप्य — Madhva’s text reads enam for etam (‘this [subtle one]’); both are pronouns of the same reference.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Learning and apprenticeship

The verse describes learning as a sequence, not an event: hearing, taking to heart, separating what matters, reaching it, and only then joy. Much modern study stops at the first step. The teacher’s closing words — the house is open for you — are what every good apprenticeship hopes to say in the end. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Think of something you heard many times before you really understood it. What changed?

Go deeper
