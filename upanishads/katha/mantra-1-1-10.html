Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 10 of 29

The first boon: a father at peace

Naciketas asks first that his father be calm and free of anger towards him, and recognise and greet him when he returns.

5 Faithful English translation

That Gautama may be calmed in his thought, glad at heart, free of anger towards me, O Death; that he may greet me, sent forth by you, recognising [me]: this I choose as the first of the three boons.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘Death, let my father Gautama be at peace about me, glad at heart and no longer angry with me; and when you send me back, let him know me and greet me. That is the first of my three boons.’

Key concepts शान्ति peace मृत्यु death वर boon

7 Explain it to a beginner

Offered three wishes by Death himself, what does the boy ask for first? Not wealth, not long life, not knowledge. He asks that his father be at peace: no longer anxious about him, no longer angry, glad at heart. And he asks that when Death sends him home, his father will recognise him and greet him. The first boon is about mending a relationship. The father spoke in anger and probably regrets it; the son wants to come home to a father who can welcome him. The commentators note that this is a son’s duty: to set his father’s mind at rest. Only after this does Naciketas turn to larger questions.

An analogy Like a child who, after a quarrel that ended in a slammed door, wishes most of all to come home and be greeted kindly.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the mantra say?

Naciketas chooses as his first boon that his father be calm, glad and free of anger towards him, and recognise and greet him when he is sent home.

Traditional Interpretation

What exactly does he ask?

Śaṅkara: that his father’s worry be stilled — शान्तसङ्कल्पः उपशान्तः सङ्कल्पो यस्य मां प्रति — and that he recognise him on return, प्रतीतः लब्धस्मृतिः; the purpose is the father’s contentment: एतत्प्रयोजनं त्रयाणां वराणां प्रथमम् आद्यं वरं वृणे प्रार्थये यत्पितुः परितोषणम्.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Greet, or bless?

Raṅgarāmānuja first explains the father as मद्विषयचिन्तारहितः प्रसन्नमनाः, free of worry about me and glad; then offers abhivadet as ‘bless’: आशिषं प्रयुङ्क्ताम्, because in the smṛtis abhivadana is used of a blessing. (His comment on this mantra is filed under 1.1.9 in the imported text.)

Traditional Interpretation

The Dvaita reading

Madhva’s cited verse names the first boon सौमनस्यं पितुश्चैव, the father’s goodwill.

Where they disagree

All three agree on the content of the first boon; they differ only on whether abhivadet means greeting or blessing (Raṅgarāmānuja allows both).

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • सुमना यथा sumanā yathā → सुमनाः + यथा

    āḥ before a voiced consonant

    After ā the visarga is dropped before a voiced sound: P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि) and P. 8.3.22 (हलि सर्वेषाम्).

  • माभिमृत्यो mābhimṛtyo → मा + अभि + मृत्यो

    savarṇa-dīrgha

    mā + abhi → mābhi, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • प्रतीत एतत् pratīta etat → प्रतीतः + एतत्

    aḥ before a vowel other than a

    aḥ → a before a vowel other than a: the visarga becomes y, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), and is dropped, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य); the hiatus stays.

  • एतत्त्रयाणां etattrayāṇāṃ → एतत् + त्रयाणाम्

    consonant before t

    The final t stands before t; final m → anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • शान्तसङ्कल्पः śāntasaṅkalpaḥ

    bahuvrīhi · शान्त + सङ्कल्प

    Vigraha शान्तः सङ्कल्पः यस्य सः

    bahuvrīhi · वीत + मन्यु

    Vigraha वीतः मन्युः यस्य सः

    whose anger has gone

  • त्वत्प्रसृष्टम् tvatprasṛṣṭam

    tṛtīyā-tatpuruṣa · युष्मद् + प्रसृष्ट

    Vigraha त्वया प्रसृष्टः

    sent forth by you

Morphology

मा
Enclitic accusative of aham, ‘me’ (not the prohibitive particle mā). With abhi it means ‘towards me’; in the second line it is the object of abhivadet.
वृणे
Present middle of vṛ, ‘I choose’ (class 9 vṛṇīte; here the Vedic class-5 form vṛṇe).

Anvaya

मृत्यो, गौतमः मा अभि यथा शान्तसङ्कल्पः सुमनाः वीतमन्युः स्यात्, त्वत्प्रसृष्टम् मा प्रतीतः [सन्] अभिवदेत् — एतत् त्रयाणाम् प्रथमम् वरम् वृणे ॥

mṛtyo, gautamaḥ mā abhi yathā śāntasaṅkalpaḥ sumanāḥ vītamanyuḥ syāt, tvatprasṛṣṭam mā pratītaḥ san abhivadet — etat trayāṇām prathamam varam vṛṇe ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two wishes introduced by yathā (‘so that’), the first with three predicate adjectives, the second with the participle pratītaḥ; then the summary ‘this I choose as the first of the three boons’.

Ambiguities

abhivadet: greet or bless?
Śaṅkara: that he may speak to me, recognising me. Raṅgarāmānuja gives that and an alternative: ‘may bless me’, since in the smṛtis abhivadati is used of giving a blessing.
What is the anxious thought?
Śaṅkara: the father’s worry ‘what will my son do, having gone to Yama?’ — यमं प्राप्य किं नु करिष्यति मम पुत्रः. A later Dvaita commentator on Madhva (Vyāsatīrtha): his resolve against the boy.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 23

Triṣṭubh, 22 + 23 syllables as written: the second line’s last pāda (etat trayāṇāṃ prathamaṃ varaṃ vṛṇe) has twelve syllables, jagatī-like.

Textual variations between witnesses

The witnesses differ here only in spelling (listed in section 14).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Emotional attachments

Given any wish, the boy first asks to be reconciled with the person who hurt him. Repairing a relationship comes before every other ambition. This is a modern reflection, not the text’s claim.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin If you could ask for one thing for someone you have quarrelled with, what would it be?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why might the Upaniṣad put reconciliation before knowledge of the fire and of the self?

A contemplation exercise Take one step today towards setting someone’s mind at rest about you.
