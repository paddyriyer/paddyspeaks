Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 12 of 15

The thumb-sized person

A person the size of a thumb stands in the middle of the body; knowing him as lord of what was and what will be, one no longer shrinks away. This is that.

5 Faithful English translation

A person of the measure of a thumb stands in the middle, in the body. [Knowing him as] the ruler of what has been and will be, one does not thereafter shrink away. This truly is that.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

A person no bigger than a thumb dwells in the middle of the body. Whoever knows him as lord of past and future no longer shrinks from anything. This is that.

Key concepts अङ्गुष्ठमात्र aṅguṣṭhamātra, thumb-sized पुरुष person ईश्वर the Lord आत्मन् self, Self गुहा the cave

7 Explain it to a beginner

The teaching now gives the self a place and a size, so that it can be thought about. It is like a person no bigger than a thumb, standing in the middle of the body, in the heart. This is not a measurement: the commentators say the size is borrowed from the small space of the heart in which it is meditated on, as the space inside a short hollow reed seems the size of the reed. Yet this small one is the lord of all that has been and all that will be. Śaṅkara says that one who knows him so loses the anxious wish to guard oneself, because there is nothing left to fear. Raṅgarāmānuja reads the same words of the Lord, who out of love does not shrink from living in a body with all its faults.

An analogy Like the space inside a short length of hollow reed: it seems the size of the reed, though it is the same space that is everywhere.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

A person the size of a thumb stands in the middle of the body; as lord of past and future, he is the one knowing whom one no longer shrinks away. This is that.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the measure of the heart

The heart-lotus is the size of a thumb, अङ्गुष्ठपरिमाणं हृदयपुण्डरीकं; conditioned by the inner organ within its opening, the self is called thumb-sized, as space within a thumb-long joint of bamboo, अङ्गुष्ठमात्रवंशपर्वमध्यवर्त्यम्बरवत्. He is puruṣa because all is filled by him, पूर्णमनेन सर्वमिति, and stands in the body, मध्ये आत्मनि शरीरे तिष्ठति. The rest is as before (2.1.5): ईशानं भूतभव्यस्य विदित्वा, न तत इत्यादि पूर्ववत्.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara on ‘does not shrink away’ (2.1.5)

At 2.1.5, to which he refers here, Śaṅkara explains that after this knowledge one no longer wishes to guard oneself, न गोपायितुमिच्छति, अभयप्राप्तत्वात्, because fearlessness has been reached.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: the Lord in the meditator's body

The person is the lord of all sentient and insentient things in all three times, कालत्रयवर्तिनिखिलचेतनाचेतनेश्वरः पुरुषः, abiding thumb-sized in the middle of the meditator's body, उपासकशरीरमध्ये. He does not shrink away: from great love he sees even the body's faults as something to enjoy, वात्सल्यातिशयात् देहगतानपि दोषान् भोग्यतया पश्यतीत्यर्थः. Against the objection that the thumb-sized one is the individual soul, he appeals to the Brahmasūtra (1.3.24) and concludes मन्त्रः परमात्मपर एव. He also discusses, and rejects, readings that make īśāna a name of Rudra, quoting earlier Viśiṣṭādvaita authors (Vyāsārya) on the Bhāṣya.

Comparative Vedānta

Madhva

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.1.12

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The thumb-size is the measure of the heart in which the all-filling self is conditioned by the inner organ, तच्छिद्रवर्त्यन्तःकरणोपाधिरङ्गुष्ठमात्रः; it is Brahman, the lord of all time.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.1.12

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The supreme Lord dwells thumb-sized in the meditator's body, उपासकशरीरमध्ये, and out of love does not recoil from its faults; the verse is about him, not the individual soul, मन्त्रः परमात्मपर एव.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Both read the person as the supreme Self, not the individual soul, as the Brahmasūtra (1.3.24) does. They differ on who ‘does not shrink away’: the knower who has reached fearlessness (Śaṅkara, by reference to 2.1.5), or the Lord who does not recoil from the body (Raṅgarāmānuja). Madhva's bhāṣya says nothing on this verse in our source.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • पुरुषो मध्य puruṣo madhya → पुरुषः + मध्ये

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

  • मध्य आत्मनि madhya ātmani → मध्ये + आत्मनि

    e before a vowel

    e becomes ay before a vowel, P. 6.1.78 (एचोऽयवायावः), and the y is then dropped, leaving the vowels apart.

  • ततो विजुगुप्सते tato vijugupsate → ततः + विजुगुप्सते

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

  • एतद्वै etadvai → एतत् + वै

    t before a voiced consonant

    A final t becomes d before a voiced sound, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • भूतभव्यस्य bhūtabhavyasya

    samāhāra-dvandva · भूत + भव्य

    Vigraha भूतं च भव्यं च तयोः समाहारः

    what has been and what will be, taken together: all time

Morphology

अङ्गुष्ठमात्रः
Not a compound: aṅguṣṭha, ‘thumb’, with the suffix -mātra (mātrac) of measure, P. 5.2.37 (प्रमाणे द्वयसज्दघ्नञ्मात्रचः): ‘having the measure of a thumb’.
ईशानम्
A present participle of √īś, ‘rule’, used as a noun. AS and the vishvAsa mūla read the accusative īśānam, which Śaṅkara takes as the object of a ‘knowing’ carried over from 2.1.5 (‘having known him as the ruler’). GRETIL, Madhva's text and Raṅgarāmānuja's quotation read the nominative īśānaḥ, ‘[he is] the ruler’, as all witnesses do in 2.1.13 (corrections.json).
विजुगुप्सते
The desiderative of √gup, ‘guard’, with vi-: literally ‘wishes to guard oneself away’, hence ‘shrinks from, recoils’. The same line closes 2.1.5.

Anvaya

अङ्गुष्ठमात्रः पुरुषः आत्मनि मध्ये तिष्ठति । [तम्] भूतभव्यस्य ईशानम् [विदित्वा] ततः न विजुगुप्सते । एतत् वै तत् ।

aṅguṣṭhamātraḥ puruṣaḥ ātmani madhye tiṣṭhati | tam bhūtabhavyasya īśānam viditvā tataḥ na vijugupsate | etat vai tat |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A simple sentence (puruṣaḥ tiṣṭhati) with the locatives madhye ātmani. The second line, as at 2.1.5, has an accusative īśānam with no verb to govern it; Śaṅkara supplies viditvā, ‘having known’. With the nominative reading īśānaḥ, the line reads ‘[he is] the ruler of past and future’, and the subject of na vijugupsate is unstated. The refrain closes the verse.

Ambiguities

Who is the thumb-sized person?
Both commentators take him as the supreme Self, given the measure of the heart in which he is meditated on. Raṅgarāmānuja argues it at length: an objector cites the thumb-sized soul that Yama drew out of Satyavān's body; he answers from the Brahmasūtra (1.3.24) that the verse is about the supreme Self, मन्त्रः परमात्मपर एव, because the lordship over past and future fits no other. Śaṅkara argues the same in his Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya on that sūtra.
Who does not shrink away?
Śaṅkara (on 2.1.5, to which he refers): the knower, who no longer wishes to guard himself, having reached fearlessness. Raṅgarāmānuja: the Lord himself, who from his great love does not shrink from the faults of the body he dwells in, वात्सल्यातिशयात् देहगतानपि दोषान् भोग्यतया पश्यतीत्यर्थः.
īśānam or īśānaḥ
The accusative (AS, vishvAsa, Śaṅkara) needs a verb supplied; the nominative (GRETIL, Madhva's text, Raṅgarāmānuja's quotation) makes the half-line a statement about the person.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16 + 4

Counted as written, 16, 16 and 4 syllables: two regular half-lines; the refrain etad vai tat (4) stands outside the metre.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading GRETIL (after Olivelle 1998): ईशानो भूतभव्यस्य न — GRETIL reads īśāno, nominative (‘[he is] the lord of what was and will be’), where AS and the vishvAsa mūla read īśānaṃ, accusative, on which Śaṅkara comments (ईशानं भूतभव्यस्य विदित्वा). Madhva’s text also reads īśāno; in 2.1.13 every witness has the nominative.
  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: ईशानो भूतभव्यस्य — Madhva’s text reads īśāno, nominative, for īśānaṃ, as GRETIL does.

    Śaṅkara quotes this verse in the Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya and decides that it is the supreme Self, because no one else rules past and future without restriction.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Psychology and inner life

People often picture the self as located somewhere — behind the eyes, in the chest. The verse uses such an image on purpose and the commentators insist it is a measure for meditation, not a size: a place to direct attention, not an object found there. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Where do you feel ‘you’ are, when you stop to notice?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why would a text that calls the self infinite give it the size of a thumb? How do the Brahmasūtra and the commentators explain it?

A contemplation exercise Sit quietly for a few minutes with your attention resting in the centre of the chest, without trying to find anything there.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
