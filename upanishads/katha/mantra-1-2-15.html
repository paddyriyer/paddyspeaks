Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 15 of 25

The word all the Vedas proclaim: Om

Death answers in brief: the goal that all the Vedas declare, that all austerities speak of, for which people live as students of the Veda — that word, in short, is Om.

5 Faithful English translation

The goal which all the Vedas declare, and which all austerities speak of, desiring which they practise brahmacarya — that goal I tell you in brief: Om, it is this.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

The goal that all the Vedas proclaim, that every austerity speaks of, for whose sake people live the disciplined life of students — I will tell it to you in a word. It is Om.

Key concepts पद the goal; the word ओम् Om वेद Veda तपस् austerity; disciplined effort ब्रह्मचारिन् brahmacārin, Vedic student

7 Explain it to a beginner

Naciketas asked for what lies beyond all opposites. Death begins his answer with a summary. There is one goal, he says, that all the Vedas proclaim. All the hard disciplines of religious life speak of it. People spend years as students, living simply and chastely, because they want it. And I will tell it to you briefly: it is Om. Om is the sacred syllable spoken at the start of Vedic recitation. Śaṅkara explains that the goal is both what Om names and what Om stands for, as a symbol through which one approaches something too great to grasp directly. Raṅgarāmānuja says Om names the supreme Lord, and that its sounds also point to the individual self. The verse is almost the same as one in the Bhagavad Gītā, which also promises to tell the goal in brief.

An analogy Like a library catalogue that, after thousands of entries, gives a single call number: everything else was pointing there.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does Death answer?

He names the goal that all the Vedas declare, of which all austerities speak and for which people practise brahmacarya, and tells it in brief: it is Om.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: named and symbolised

All the Vedas teach this goal without division or contradiction — अविभागेन अविरोधेन आमनन्ति प्रतिपादयन्ति; austerities are for its sake; brahmacarya is life in the teacher’s house or another discipline for reaching Brahman. The goal is ‘Om’ in two senses: तदेतदोमिति ओंशब्दवाच्यमोंशब्दप्रतीकं च — what the word Om denotes, and what Om stands as a symbol for.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: the goal of all the Vedas

Before teaching at length from 1.2.18, Death praises the goal so that the hearer will attend closely. padam is what is reached, पद्यते गम्यते इति व्युत्पत्त्या. Om names Brahman, as the Gītā says — ओं तत्सदिति निर्देशो ब्रह्मणस्त्रिविधस्स्मृतः. Raṅgarāmānuja uses the verse against the view that the whole Upaniṣad concerns only the purified individual self: what all the Vedas teach is the supreme Self.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

The Gītā’s version

The second half is nearly the Gītā’s 8.11 — यदिच्छन्तो ब्रह्मचर्यं चरन्ति तत्ते पदं सङ्ग्रहेण — where Kṛṣṇa ends with pravakṣye, ‘I shall tell’. The Kaṭha’s line carries three more syllables, om ity etat (see metre).

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Om names Brahman, the goal that all the Vedas teach — what is reached, पद्यते गम्यते इति व्युत्पत्त्या; so the Upaniṣad teaches the supreme Self, not the purified soul alone.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Both take Om as the brief statement of the goal; Raṅgarāmānuja adds that its sounds name the supreme and the individual self, and uses the verse against reading the Upaniṣad as about the individual self alone. Madhva’s bhāṣya on the passage 1.2.14–17 consists of one verse he cites without naming its source (इति च), about the imperishable of 1.2.16–17.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • वेदा यत्पदमामनन्ति vedā yatpadamāmananti → वेदाः + यत् + पदम् + आमनन्ति

    āḥ before a voiced consonant

    āḥ loses its visarga before y, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि) and P. 8.3.22 (हलि सर्वेषाम्); padam is written joined to the vowel that follows.

  • यद्वदन्ति yadvadanti → यत् + वदन्ति

    t voiced before a voiced consonant

    P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

  • यदिच्छन्तो ब्रह्मचर्यं yadicchanto brahmacaryaṃ → यत् + इच्छन्तः + ब्रह्मचर्यम्

    t voiced; aḥ before a voiced consonant

    yat before a vowel becomes yad, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते); icchantaḥ before b becomes icchanto, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च) and P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • ब्रवीम्योमित्येतत् bravīmyomityetat → ब्रवीमि + ओम् + इति + एतत्

    yaṇ

    bravīmi + om give bravīmy om, and iti + etat give ity etat, P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि). GRETIL writes bravīmi om apart (corrections.json; orthographic).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • ब्रह्मचर्यम् brahmacaryam

    tatpuruṣa · ब्रह्मन् + चर्य

    Vigraha ब्रह्मणे चर्यम्

    conduct for the sake of Brahman — Śaṅkara: life in the teacher’s house, or another discipline undertaken to reach Brahman

Morphology

आमनन्ति
Present of ā-√mnā ‘repeat, hand down by heart’, the root of āmnāya, the Veda as transmitted.
पदम्
From √pad ‘go’: a step, the place reached, a word. Śaṅkara: पदनीयं गमनीयम्; Raṅgarāmānuja: पद्यते गम्यते इति व्युत्पत्त्या.
ते
Enclitic dative of tvam, ‘to you’.

Anvaya

सर्वे वेदाः यत् पदम् आमनन्ति, सर्वाणि तपांसि च यत् वदन्ति, यत् इच्छन्तः ब्रह्मचर्यम् चरन्ति, तत् पदम् ते सङ्ग्रहेण ब्रवीमि — ओम् इति एतत् ।

sarve vedāḥ yat padam āmananti, sarvāṇi tapāṃsi ca yat vadanti, yat icchantaḥ brahmacaryam caranti, tat padam te saṅgraheṇa bravīmi — om iti etat |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Three relative clauses (yat … āmananti, yad vadanti, yad icchantaḥ … caranti) build the description; the correlative tat … padam picks them up in the main clause, ‘that goal I tell you in brief’; om ity etat, ‘it is this: Om’, gives the answer in apposition.

Ambiguities

padam: goal or word
The word means both the place reached and a word. Śaṅkara takes it as what is to be reached, and says the goal is Om in two ways, as what the word Om names and as what it stands for — ओंशब्दवाच्यमोंशब्दप्रतीकं च. Raṅgarāmānuja: the goal taught by all the Vedas, directly or indirectly; Om is the word that names it in brief, and its sounds a and m name the supreme and the individual self — प्रणवावयवयोः अकारमकारयोः परजीववाचितया.
The austerities
Śaṅkara: the austerities exist for its sake, यत्प्राप्त्यर्थानीत्यर्थः. Raṅgarāmānuja reports Vyāsārya’s view that tapāṃsi here means the later parts of the Veda, in which austerity is prominent: तपः प्रधाना उपरितनभागाः.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 25

Triṣṭubh, 22 + 25 syllables: pādas of 11, 11, 11 and 14. The last pāda is an eleven-syllable line (tat te padaṃ saṅgraheṇa bravīmi) to which om ity etat adds three syllables; the Gītā’s version (8.11) ends the line at pravakṣye, with eleven. GRETIL’s bravīmi om, written apart, counts one more.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Learning and apprenticeship

Teachers know the value of a summary that comes before the detail: it tells the learner what all the detail is for. Death does exactly this, naming the goal in a single syllable before explaining it. Years of study and discipline, the verse suggests, mean more when the learner knows what they point to. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin If your life’s work had to be summed up in one word, what would you want it to be?

Go deeper
