Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 15 of 15

Pure water into pure water

As pure water poured into pure water becomes just the same, so becomes the self of the sage who knows, Gautama.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

As clear water poured into clear water becomes just the same, so, Gautama, becomes the self of the sage who knows.

Key concepts आत्मन् self, Self मोक्ष liberation एकत्व oneness ज्ञान knowledge

7 Explain it to a beginner

The vallī ends with a simple, beautiful image. Pour clear water into clear water, and it does not stay apart: it becomes just like the water it joins. So, Death tells Naciketas, becomes the self of the sage who truly knows. The great commentators read this one image in three ways, and here their teachings on liberation show most clearly. Śaṅkara says the knower's self becomes one with the supreme Self, with nothing left to divide them; he calls the Veda kinder than a thousand mothers and fathers for teaching it. Raṅgarāmānuja says the purified self becomes equal to the pure supreme Self, alike in purity. Madhva says it becomes like the Lord, never the same as him; even the highest of souls reaches only likeness.

An analogy Like a cup of clear water poured into a clear pool: one can no longer point to where the cupful is.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

As pure water poured into pure water becomes just such, so becomes the self of the sage who knows.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: of one taste

For the knower whose seeing of difference made by adjuncts is destroyed, who sees the self as विशुद्धविज्ञानघनैकरसमद्वयमात्मानं, pure water poured into pure water is of one taste, not otherwise, एकरसमेव नान्यथा; so the self of the sage who knows oneness becomes. Therefore one should give up the divisive views of bad reasoners and of those who deny the Veda, and honour the seeing of the self's oneness taught by the Veda, which wishes us more good than a thousand mothers and fathers: मातृपितृसहस्रेभ्योऽपि हितैषिणा वेदेनोपदिष्टमात्मैकत्वदर्शनं.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: equal to the pure Self

The verse states the fruit of knowing that all has one self, सर्वत्रैकात्मकत्वज्ञानस्य फलमाह. Pure water joined to pure water becomes like it, तत्सदृशमेव भवति, न कथञ्चिदपि विसदृशम्; so the self of one who knows, purified by the knowledge of the supreme Self, becomes equal to the pure supreme Self, विशुद्धेन परमात्मना समानो भवतीत्यर्थः. Death calls ‘Gautama’ with joy, hinting at the greatness of what is to be reached.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: like him, not the same

The self of the knowing sage — even Vāyu — becomes such, not the same: विजानतो मुनेरात्मा वायुरपि तादृगेव भवति न तु स एव भवति; how much less other souls, किम्वन्ये जीवाः. He cites verses he attributes to the Bhaviṣyat-parvan: the released Vāyu reaches likeness to Viṣṇu, मुक्तो वायुश्च सादृश्यमेवं विष्णोस्तु गच्छति, and not his form, न तु तद्रूपतां याति.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

The word tādṛk

tādṛk, ‘looking like that’, can mean ‘just such’ in kind or ‘just like’ in resemblance; the Sanskrit does not decide between identity and likeness, and each school takes it in the sense its doctrine of release requires.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.1.15

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The knower's self becomes of one taste with the self, एकरसमेव नान्यथा: release is the realised oneness of the self, and the Veda's teaching of it is to be honoured, आत्मैकत्वदर्शनं शान्तदर्पैरादरणीयमित्यर्थः.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.1.15

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The released self becomes equal to the supreme Self in purity, विशुद्धेन परमात्मना समानो भवतीत्यर्थः — like it, never unlike it, and still a self.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.1.15

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The released self, even the highest, Vāyu, attains likeness to Viṣṇu, not identity, न तु स एव भवति.

Where they disagree

Identity (Śaṅkara: of one taste, not otherwise), equality in purity (Raṅgarāmānuja: samāna), likeness and not identity (Madhva: tādṛg eva, na tu sa eva). The image of water in water serves all three; the word tādṛk does not settle the question.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • यथोदकं yathodakaṃ → यथा + उदकम्

    ā + u

    ā + u give o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • तादृगेव tādṛgeva → तादृक् + एव

    k before a vowel

    A final k becomes g before a vowel, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

  • मुनेर्विजानत आत्मा munervijānata ātmā → मुनेः + विजानतः + आत्मा

    visarga after e before a voiced sound; aḥ before a vowel

    The visarga from s after a vowel other than a becomes r before a voiced sound (P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः)); the visarga of -aḥ is lost before a vowel other than a, and the vowels stay apart.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

तादृक्
From tad + dṛś, ‘looking like that’: ‘such, of that kind’. The pausal form of tādṛś is tādṛk; before eva it becomes tādṛg. It is the word on which the readings divide: ‘just that’ or ‘just like that’.
विजानतः
The present participle of vi-√jñā, ‘know, discern’, genitive singular, agreeing with muneḥ.
गौतम
Vocative of Gautama, ‘descendant of Gotama’: Death addresses Naciketas by his family name, as at 2.2.6.

Anvaya

यथा शुद्धे [उदके] आसिक्तम् शुद्धम् उदकम् तादृक् एव भवति, एवम् [हे] गौतम, विजानतः मुनेः आत्मा [तादृक् एव] भवति ।

yathā śuddhe udake āsiktam śuddham udakam tādṛk eva bhavati, evam he gautama, vijānataḥ muneḥ ātmā tādṛk eva bhavati |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A comparison: yathā … bhavati (the image) and evam … bhavati (the application). tādṛg eva, the predicate of the first clause, is understood in the second. gautama is a vocative.

Ambiguities

‘Becomes just such’: the same, or like?
Śaṅkara: of one taste, not otherwise — the self of the knower is one with the self, एकरसमेव नान्यथा, तादृगेव भवति; Raṅgarāmānuja: the purified self becomes equal to the pure supreme Self, विशुद्धेन परमात्मना समानो भवतीत्यर्थः; Madhva: it becomes like him, not the same, न तु स एव भवति. This is the verse on which the three schools' doctrines of release most plainly part.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 18 + 16

Counted as written, 18 and 16 syllables. The first half-line has two syllables over the norm: yathodakaṃ śuddhe śuddham (8) āsiktaṃ tādṛg eva bhavati (10). The second half-line is regular.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Psychology and inner life

People describe deep understanding as merging, belonging, or coming home, and also as meeting something other than oneself. The single image of water poured into water holds both, which may be why three schools could each find their teaching in it. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Have you ever felt so at home somewhere that you could not tell where you ended and it began?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Śaṅkara, Raṅgarāmānuja and Madhva all accept the image of water poured into water. What does each keep, and what does each refuse, in it?

A contemplation exercise Pour a little water into a glass of water and watch it closely; then sit for a minute with what you saw.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
