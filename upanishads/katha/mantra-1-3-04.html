Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 4 of 17

The horses and the roads

The senses are the horses, their objects the roads; the self joined with body, senses and mind is called the enjoyer.

5 Faithful English translation

‘They call the senses the horses, the objects the roads for them; the [self] joined with body, senses and mind the wise call “the enjoyer”.’

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘The senses, they say, are the horses, and the things of the senses are the roads they run on. The self joined with body, senses and mind is what the wise call “the one who experiences”.’

Key concepts रथ ratha, the chariot इन्द्रिय indriya, a sense आत्मन् self, Self मनस् mind भोग enjoyment, use

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse finishes the picture of the chariot. The senses — seeing, hearing, touching, tasting, smelling — are the horses that pull it. The things they go after, sights and sounds and the rest, are the roads the horses run on. Then comes the key point: the self is called 'the one who experiences' only when it is joined with a body, senses and a mind. Both Śaṅkara and Raṅgarāmānuja stress this. Śaṅkara says the self by itself does not enjoy or suffer; it seems to, because of the instruments it is joined with. Raṅgarāmānuja says that without the body and senses the self would make no journey at all. The picture explains how we come to be pulled about by the world, and prepares the next verses, which say what happens when the driver is skilled or unskilled.

An analogy Like a passenger in a car who says ‘we’re going too fast’: it is the car that moves, yet the passenger speaks as the one who goes.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

That the senses are the horses and their objects the roads, and that the wise call the self joined with body, senses and mind ‘the enjoyer’.

Traditional Interpretation

Why is the self called the enjoyer?

Śaṅkara: the self alone is not an enjoyer; its enjoying is made by its adjuncts, the intellect and the rest, बुद्ध्याद्युपाधिकृतमेव तस्य भोक्तृत्वम्, and another scripture shows the self by itself does not enjoy, citing ध्यायतीव लेलायतीव. Only so can the self later be known as Viṣṇu’s highest place, वैष्णवस्य पदस्यात्मतया प्रतिपत्तिरुपपद्यते.

Comparative Vedānta

Raṅgarāmānuja

He reads ātman in the compound as the body and manas as standing also for the intellect, already named as the charioteer, मनः शब्दः तत्कार्यबुद्धेरपि उपलक्षकः; the self alone has neither agency nor enjoyment, न हि केवलस्यात्मनः कर्तृत्वं भोक्तृत्वं वा अस्तीति भावः. On this point he and Śaṅkara agree in words.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

The roads

gocara is a range or pasture; Śaṅkara glosses it as the roads, the objects such as form, मार्गान् रूपादीन्विषयान्; Raṅgarāmānuja the same with sound and the rest, मार्गान् शब्दादिविषयान्.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.3.4

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Enjoyment belongs to the self only through its adjuncts, बुद्ध्याद्युपाधिकृतमेव तस्य भोक्तृत्वम्; the self in itself does not enjoy.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.3.4

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The self is agent and enjoyer, कर्तृत्वभोक्तृत्ववान्, but not apart from body, senses and mind: न हि केवलस्यात्मनः कर्तृत्वं भोक्तृत्वं वा अस्तीति भावः.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Both deny enjoyment to the self ‘alone’; for Śaṅkara the self is in truth never an enjoyer, for Raṅgarāmānuja it really is agent and enjoyer when embodied. In our source Madhva prints 1.3.3–1.3.11 without comment and then comments once, marking it ॥ १०-१२ ॥, on the deities who preside over the senses, their objects, the mind and the intellect; he does not gloss the chariot image itself.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • हयानाहुर्विषयांस्तेषु hayānāhurviṣayāṃsteṣu → हयान् + आहुः + विषयान् + तेषु

    n before a vowel; visarga; n before t

    n stays before a vowel; final s of āhuḥ becomes ru, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः), r before a voiced sound; final n before t becomes ru with anusvāra, P. 8.3.7 (नश्छव्यप्रशान्), giving -āṃs t-.

  • भोक्तेत्याहुर्मनीषिणः bhoktetyāhurmanīṣiṇaḥ → भोक्ता + इति + आहुः + मनीषिणः

    vowel sandhi; visarga

    ā + i → e, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः); i before a dissimilar vowel → y, P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि); final s → ru, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः), r before m.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • आत्मेन्द्रियमनोयुक्तम् ātmendriyamanoyuktam

    tṛtīyā-tatpuruṣa on a dvandva · आत्मन् + इन्द्रिय + मनस् + युक्त

    Vigraha आत्मा च इन्द्रियाणि च मनश्च आत्मेन्द्रियमनांसि, तैः युक्तम्

    joined with body, senses and mind

  • गोचरान् gocarān

    upapada-tatpuruṣa · गो + चर

    Vigraha गावः चरन्ति अस्मिन्निति गोचरः

    a range, a pasture; here the roads the horses travel

Morphology

आहुः
A perfect of the defective root ah ‘say’, used with present meaning: ‘they say, they call’.
आत्मेन्द्रियमनोयुक्तम्
Inside the compound ātma-indriya → ātmendriya (a + i → e) and manas- → mano- before y. Both commentators take ātman here as the body (Śaṅkara’s gloss शरीरेन्द्रियमनोभिः; Raṅgarāmānuja आत्मशब्दो देहपरः).
भोक्ता
Agent noun in -tṛ from bhuj ‘enjoy, experience’, nominative singular, kept in the nominative inside the quotation with iti.

Anvaya

इन्द्रियाणि हयान् आहुः, विषयान् तेषु गोचरान् [आहुः] । आत्मेन्द्रियमनोयुक्तम् [आत्मानम्] भोक्ता इति मनीषिणः आहुः ॥

indriyāṇi hayān āhuḥ, viṣayān teṣu gocarān āhuḥ | ātmendriyamanoyuktam ātmānam bhoktā iti manīṣiṇaḥ āhuḥ ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two statements with āhuḥ. In the first, indriyāṇi hayān is a double accusative; viṣayān teṣu gocarān continues it, Śaṅkara supplying viddhi. In the second, the compound ātmendriyamanoyuktam is the object and bhoktā, in the nominative, is quoted with iti.

Ambiguities

Is the self an enjoyer?
Both commentators say it is so only as joined with body, senses and mind. Śaṅkara: न हि केवलस्यात्मनो भोक्तृत्वमस्ति ; बुद्ध्याद्युपाधिकृतमेव तस्य भोक्तृत्वम्. Raṅgarāmānuja: without body and the rest the detached self has no agency in going, उदासीनस्य आत्मनो गमनरूपलौकिकवैदिकक्रियाकर्तृत्वमेव नास्ति; ‘enjoyer’ means agent and enjoyer, कर्तृत्वभोक्तृत्ववान्.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Attention

Senses as horses and their objects as roads: attention goes where the road invites unless something holds the reins. The image is older than any screen, and does not need one to be true. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Which ‘roads’ do your senses run down most often without your choosing?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry If the self alone is not an enjoyer, who is it that enjoys?

A contemplation exercise For a few minutes, notice each time your attention is pulled to something, and where it was pulled.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
