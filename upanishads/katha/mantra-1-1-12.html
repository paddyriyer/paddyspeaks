Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 12 of 29

Heaven without fear

Naciketas describes the heaven he asks about: no fear, no death, no old age, no hunger or thirst, no grief.

5 Faithful English translation

‘In the heaven-world there is no fear at all; you are not there; [one] does not fear because of old age. Having crossed both hunger and thirst, gone beyond grief, [one] rejoices in the heaven-world.’

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘In heaven there is nothing to fear. You are not there, and no one is afraid of growing old. Beyond hunger and thirst, beyond sorrow, one rejoices in heaven.’

Key concepts लोक world, realm मृत्यु death शोक grief, sorrow अमृत immortality, the deathless

7 Explain it to a beginner

Naciketas now prepares his second request. Before asking for the fire that leads to heaven, he describes heaven: a place where there is no fear at all, where death has no hold, where no one dreads growing old, where hunger and thirst are left behind and sorrow is passed by. It is a picture of the ordinary hopes of human beings for what lies after death. The Upaniṣad will later look beyond even this, but here the boy speaks of the best that people usually imagine, and asks how to reach it.

An analogy Like a traveller describing the safe harbour before asking for the chart that leads to it.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse describe?

A heaven without fear, without death’s power, without fear of age, beyond hunger, thirst and grief. It is the introduction to the second boon, asked in 1.1.13.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Śaṅkara read it?

As the familiar heaven, free from fear caused by disease: स्वर्गे लोके रोगादिनिमित्तं भयं किञ्चन किञ्चिदपि नास्ति, where death does not suddenly prevail, न च तत्र त्वं मृत्यो सहसा प्रभवसि. The one who reaches it, having crossed hunger and thirst, is beyond mental pain: शोकमतीत्य गच्छतीति शोकातिगः.

Comparative Vedānta

Is this heaven release?

Raṅgarāmānuja: yes, the word here names the place of release, स्वर्गशब्दः मोक्षस्थानपरः, and he defends the claim at length on 1.1.19, reporting the Śrībhāṣya: स्वर्गशब्देनात्र परमपुरुषार्थलक्षणमोक्षोऽभिधीयते.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Why ‘hunger and thirst’?

aśanāyā and pipāsā are desideratives of ‘eat’ and ‘drink’: the wanting, not the eating. The verse uses the pair as the mark of embodied need; the Chāndogya uses the same pair for the self that is free of them.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.12

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The ordinary heaven, the reward of ritual, described in its best colours; it is not yet the knowledge of the self, which only the third boon will bring. न च तत्र त्वं मृत्यो सहसा प्रभवसि.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, reporting the Śrībhāṣya, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.1.12 and on 1.1.19

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Heaven here is release: अत्रापि स्वर्गशब्दः मोक्षस्थानपरः. Raṅgarāmānuja argues that the immortality ‘enjoyed’ in 1.1.13 and the crossing of birth and death in 1.1.17 cannot be said of the perishable heaven.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

The question is whether svarga means heaven or release. Śaṅkara keeps heaven as the fruit of ritual, below knowledge; Raṅgarāmānuja makes it release itself. Madhva has no comment on this verse alone; his comment on the fire (1.1.12–13) concerns Hari in the fire, not the heaven described here.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • किञ्चनास्ति kiñcanāsti → किञ्चन + अस्ति

    vowel sandhi (savarṇa-dīrgha)

    a + a → ā: P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • त्वं न tvaṃ na → त्वम् + न

    final m before a consonant

    Final m before a consonant becomes anusvāra: P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • तीर्त्वा अशनायापिपासे tīrtvā aśanāyāpipāse → तीर्त्वा + अशनायापिपासे

    hiatus

    ā before a is left unjoined in the text (see the junction listed below).

  • शोकातिगो मोदते śokātigo modate → शोकातिगः + मोदते

    visarga before a voiced consonant

    aḥ before a voiced consonant becomes o: P. 6.1.114 (हशि च) with P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

Junctions outside the general rules

  • तीर्त्वा · अशनायापिपासे — vedic hiatus: tīrtvā aśanāyā-: final ā before a is not merged; the text (and Śaṅkara’s quotation of it) keeps the two words apart.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • अशनायापिपासे aśanāyāpipāse

    itaretara-dvandva · अशनाया + पिपासा

    Vigraha अशनाया च पिपासा च

    hunger and thirst

  • शोकातिगः śokātigaḥ

    upapada-tatpuruṣa · शोक + अतिग

    Vigraha शोकम् अतिगच्छति इति

    one who goes beyond grief

  • स्वर्गलोके svargaloke

    karmadhāraya · स्वर्ग + लोक

    Vigraha स्वर्गः एव लोकः

    the world that is heaven

Morphology

अशनायापिपासे
aśanāyā ‘desire to eat’ and pipāsā ‘desire to drink’ (desiderative of pā): a feminine dual dvandva, object of tīrtvā.
शोकातिगः
ati-√gam with the suffix ḍa (-ga): ‘going beyond’. Śaṅkara: शोकमतीत्य गच्छतीति शोकातिगः.
बिभेति
Present of bhī (third class, reduplicated). Its subject is not stated: ‘[one] does not fear’.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

Three short negative statements (no fear, not you, no fear of old age) and one positive one (having crossed hunger and thirst, beyond grief, one rejoices). The subject of bibheti and modate is not named: whoever reaches that world.

Ambiguities

What ‘heaven’ is meant?
Śaṅkara takes it as the heaven-world, free of disease and fear: स्वर्गे लोके रोगादिनिमित्तं भयं किञ्चन किञ्चिदपि नास्ति. Raṅgarāmānuja says the word here means the place of release: स्वर्गशब्दः मोक्षस्थानपरः.
na tatra tvam: ‘you are not there’
Śaṅkara softens it: death does not suddenly prevail there, न च तत्र त्वं मृत्यो सहसा प्रभवसि.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Fear and security

Most of what people work and save for is a smaller version of this heaven: a life without fear, without want, without the dread of old age. The verse names those hopes honestly before the Upaniṣad asks whether any place can fulfil them. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What would your own ‘heaven without fear’ look like?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Is freedom from fear something a place can give, or only a state of mind?
