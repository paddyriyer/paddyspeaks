Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 5 of 17

The unskilled driver

For one without understanding, whose mind is never held, the senses are out of control, like a poor driver’s vicious horses.

5 Faithful English translation

‘But he who is without understanding, with a mind always unheld — his senses are uncontrollable, like the bad horses of a charioteer.’

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘When the driver lacks understanding and never holds the mind steady, the senses run out of control, like a poor driver’s unbroken horses.’

Key concepts इन्द्रिय indriya, a sense मनस् mind विज्ञान understanding, the knowing self

7 Explain it to a beginner

Now the chariot picture is put to work. Imagine a driver who does not really know how to drive and who lets the reins hang loose all the time. The horses do whatever they like: they bolt, swerve and follow every distraction. That, says the verse, is what happens when a person's intellect lacks understanding and the mind is never steadied: the senses run wherever they are pulled. Śaṅkara takes 'the one without understanding' to be the intellect itself, the driver, and the loose mind to be slack reins. Raṅgarāmānuja comments on this verse and the next together: when the driver and the reins are sound, the horses obey; when they are not, they do not. The verse describes; it does not scold. It explains why good intentions so often fail.

An analogy Like a learner driver on an icy road with the wheel slipping through their hands: the engine is fine; what is missing is the hold.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

That for one without understanding, whose mind is always unheld, the senses are out of control, like the vicious horses of a charioteer.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara

The charioteer called intellect, if unskilled and undiscerning in acting and refraining, अनिपुणोऽविवेकी प्रवृत्तौ च निवृत्तौ च, with the mind, the reins, unheld and unsettled, अप्रगृहीतेन असमाहितेन, finds the senses cannot be checked, अशक्यनिवारणानि, like untamed horses, अदान्ताश्वाः.

Comparative Vedānta

Raṅgarāmānuja

He comments on 1.3.5–6 together: these two verses give the purpose of describing body and the rest as a chariot, शरीरादेः रथत्वादिरूपणस्य प्रयोजनमाह. In the world horses are controlled when the charioteer and reins are sound; so the senses are controlled when understanding and mind are sound, and not otherwise, नान्यथा इत्यर्थः.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Madhva

In our source Madhva prints 1.3.3–1.3.11 without comment and then comments once, marking it ॥ १०-१२ ॥, on the deities who preside over the senses, their objects, the mind and the intellect; he does not gloss this verse.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara and Raṅgarāmānuja read the verse alike as a description of control lost. 10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • यस्त्वविज्ञानवान्भवत्ययुक्तेन yastvavijñānavānbhavatyayuktena → यः + तु + अविज्ञानवान् + भवति + अयुक्तेन

    visarga; vowel sandhi

    Visarga before t becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः); u and i before a dissimilar vowel become v and y, P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि); n of -vān stays before bh.

  • तस्येन्द्रियाण्यवश्यानि tasyendriyāṇyavaśyāni → तस्य + इन्द्रियाणि + अवश्यानि

    vowel sandhi

    a + i → e, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः); i before a dissimilar vowel → y, P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि).

  • दुष्टाश्वा इव duṣṭāśvā iva → दुष्टाश्वाः + इव

    visarga sandhi

    āḥ before a vowel other than a: the visarga becomes y, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), and the y is dropped, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य); the hiatus remains.

    not under control

  • दुष्टाश्वाः duṣṭāśvāḥ

    karmadhāraya · दुष्ट + अश्व

    Vigraha दुष्टाः अश्वाः

    bad, unbroken horses

Morphology

अविज्ञानवान्
vijñāna with the possessive suffix -vat (nom. sg. -vān), negated: ‘one who has no discernment’.
सारथेः
Genitive singular of the i-stem sārathi: -eḥ.

Anvaya

यः तु अविज्ञानवान् सदा अयुक्तेन मनसा भवति, तस्य इन्द्रियाणि सारथेः दुष्टाश्वाः इव अवश्यानि [भवन्ति] ॥

yaḥ tu avijñānavān sadā ayuktena manasā bhavati, tasya indriyāṇi sāratheḥ duṣṭāśvāḥ iva avaśyāni bhavanti ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A relative clause (yas tu … bhavati) with its correlative tasya; the main clause has no verb, indriyāṇy avaśyāni being a nominal sentence, with the simile duṣṭāśvā iva sāratheḥ.

Ambiguities

Who is ‘he’?
Śaṅkara on 1.3.5–6: the charioteer called intellect, बुद्ध्याख्यः सारथिः. On 1.3.7–8 he takes the same words of the rider who has such a charioteer. Raṅgarāmānuja, commenting on 1.3.5–6 together, speaks of understanding and mind, as charioteer and reins, being sound or not: सारथिप्रग्रहत्वेन रूपितयोः विज्ञान-मनसोः सामीचीन्ये.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 17 + 16

Four pādas of eight syllables.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Self-mastery

The verse locates the trouble not in the horses but in the hold on the reins. That is a hopeful diagnosis: horses cannot be changed, but a grip can be learned. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin When did your senses last ‘bolt’ with you?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why does the verse blame the lack of understanding rather than the senses?

A contemplation exercise Choose one small habit of distraction and notice the moment just before it begins.
