Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 9 of 29

Three nights, three boons

Yama greets the boy with honour and offers a boon for each night he waited without food.

5 Faithful English translation

Since you have stayed three nights in my house without eating, O brāhmaṇa, a guest worthy of reverence — reverence be to you, O brāhmaṇa; may there be well-being for me! Therefore choose three boons, [one] for each.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘Brāhmaṇa, you are a guest who deserves honour, and you have stayed three nights in my house without food. Reverence to you! Let it be well with me. So choose three boons, one for each night.’

Key concepts अतिथि guest मृत्यु death वर boon

7 Explain it to a beginner

Death comes home and finds the boy who has waited three nights at his door without food. He greets him with real respect: you are a guest worthy of honour; reverence to you. Then he adds a wish for himself: may it go well with me. He knows he has done wrong by a guest, and that this matters. To set things right he offers three boons, one for each night of fasting. Here the story turns. A boy given away in anger to Death is now offered anything he wishes by Death himself. What will he ask for? The next verses tell us, and the third request will become the subject of the whole Upaniṣad.

An analogy Like a host who returns late to find a guest has waited for days, apologises sincerely, and says: name three things I can do for you.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the mantra say?

Yama greets the boy with reverence, wishes well-being for himself, and offers three boons, one for each night the boy stayed without food.

Traditional Interpretation

Why does Yama wish well for himself?

Śaṅkara: to be free of the fault of a guest’s fasting in his house; even if all would be well by the boy’s favour, Yama offers more to win him fully: त्वदधिकसम्प्रसादनार्थम् … अनशनेनोषितामेकैकां रात्रिं प्रति त्रीन् वरान् वृणीष्व.

Traditional Interpretation

What does Raṅgarāmānuja add?

That the boons are for Yama’s sake, not the boy’s: तव लिप्साभावेऽपि मदनुग्रहार्थम् अनशनरात्रिसमसंख्याकान् त्रीन् वरान् वृणीष्व इति भावः.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Madhva read the three boons?

Madhva’s verse on this passage: इत्युक्तस्स यमस्तं तु सम्पूज्यादाद् वरत्रयम् — so addressed, Yama honoured him and gave three boons. The verse he cites next (from what he calls the Gatisāra) names them in advance: the father’s goodwill, Hari in the Nāciketa fire, and Viṣṇu present in the released — सौमनस्यं पितुश्चैव नाचिकेताग्निगं हरिम् … मुक्ते स्थितञ्च तं विष्णुमिति प्रादात् वरत्रयम् (reported as cited by Madhva).

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.9

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Three boons, one for each night of fasting: अनशनेनोषितामेकैकां रात्रिं प्रति त्रीन् वरान् वृणीष्व.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Prakāśikā 1.1.9

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Yama offers the boons for his own good, the boy having no desire: तव लिप्साभावेऽपि मदनुग्रहार्थम्.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.8–10

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Yama honours the boy and grants three boons (इत्युक्तस्स यमस्तं तु सम्पूज्यादाद् वरत्रयम्), which a verse Madhva cites reads as the father’s goodwill, Hari in the Nāciketa fire and Viṣṇu in the released: सौमनस्यं पितुश्चैव नाचिकेताग्निगं हरिम्.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara and Raṅgarāmānuja explain why there are three boons; Madhva’s cited verse already reads the second and third as knowledge of Viṣṇu, not of a fire-rite and a question about the dead.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • रात्रीर्यदवात्सीर्गृहे rātrīryadavātsīrgṛhe → रात्रीः + यत् + अवात्सीः + गृहे

    visarga before voiced sounds; t before a vowel

    īḥ → īr before a voiced sound, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः); yat → yad before a vowel, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

  • ब्रह्मन्नतिथिर्नमस्यः brahmannatithirnamasyaḥ → ब्रह्मन् + अतिथिः + नमस्यः

    n doubled before a vowel

    A final n after a short vowel is doubled before a vowel, P. 8.3.32 (ङमो ह्रस्वादचि ङमुण्नित्यम्).

  • नमस्तेऽस्तु namaste'stu → नमः + ते + अस्तु

    visarga before t; e + a

    aḥ → as before t, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः); te + astu → te ’stu, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति).

Junctions outside the general rules

  • मे · अनश्नन् — unjoined hiatus (as printed): The base prints me anaśnan unjoined (Śaṅkara’s mūla and gloss गृहे मे मम अनश्नन्); the vishvAsa mūla and Raṅgarāmānuja write me ’naśnan with the a elided (corrections.json).

brahman, namasyaḥ atithiḥ tvam yat me gṛhe tisraḥ rātrīḥ anaśnan avātsīḥ, tasmāt brahman te namaḥ astu, me svasti astu | tasmāt ekaikāṃ rātrim prati trīn varān vṛṇīṣva ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A causal clause (‘since you stayed…’), two wishes in the imperative (‘reverence be to you’, ‘well-being be to me’) and the command ‘choose three boons’. Prati, ‘for each’, links the three boons to the three nights.

Ambiguities

Why three boons?
Śaṅkara: one for each night spent fasting — अनशनेनोषितामेकैकां रात्रिं प्रति त्रीन् वरान् वृणीष्व. Raṅgarāmānuja says the same and adds that Yama offers them for his own sake, though the boy wants nothing: तव लिप्साभावेऽपि मदनुग्रहार्थम्.
Who says ‘reverence to you’?
A later Dvaita commentator on Madhva (Vedeśatīrtha) asks whether the words could be the wife’s, and concludes from the context that they are Yama’s.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 20

Triṣṭubh, 22 + 20 syllables as written: the first line counts the unjoined hiatus me anaśnan (21 if elided); the second has two short pādas of ten as printed.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Witness error GRETIL (after Olivelle 1998): रात्रीर् यद् अवत्सीर् गृहे — GRETIL reads अवत्सीर्, short a; the s-aorist of vas ‘dwell’ is avātsīḥ, as AS, the vishvAsa mūla and Madhva’s text read and Śaṅkara glosses.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Ethical leadership

A powerful figure who has wronged someone apologises, names the wrong, and offers real redress rather than excuses. This is a modern reflection, not the text’s claim.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin When you have wronged someone, how do you try to make it right?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why does Yama ask for well-being for himself in the same breath as honouring the boy?

A contemplation exercise Make a concrete amends today for one small wrong you have done.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
