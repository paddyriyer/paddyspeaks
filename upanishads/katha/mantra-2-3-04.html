Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 4 of 18

Before the body falls

If one can know it here, before the body falls apart, [one is freed]; otherwise one becomes fit for embodiment in the created worlds.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

If one comes to know it here, before the body falls away, one is freed; if not, one is bound to take a body again in the worlds of creation.

Key concepts ब्रह्मन् Brahman संसार saṃsāra, the round of births लोक world, realm ज्ञान knowledge

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse adds urgency. If a person comes to know the highest reality here, in this life, before the body wears out and falls away, that person is set free. If not, the verse says, one will take a body again somewhere in the many worlds of creation. Śaṅkara draws the lesson plainly: make the effort now, before death, because here the self can be seen clearly, as a face in a mirror. Raṅgarāmānuja reads the first word a little differently, as ‘if one was unable’, so that the verse runs straight on: if one fails to know it here, then one is bound to be born again. Either way the advice is the same: the time to seek this knowledge is this life, not some later one.

An analogy A traveller is told to read the map before the light fails, not after dark.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

If one can know it here, before the body falls apart, [one is freed]; otherwise one becomes fit for embodiment in the created worlds.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: strive before the body falls

If, while still living, one is able to know Brahman, the cause of that fear, before the body falls, one is freed from the bondage of saṃsāra; if not, one takes a body in the worlds where beings are created. So one must strive for knowledge of the self before the body falls, तस्माच्छरीरविस्रंसनात्प्रागात्मावबोधाय यत्न आस्थेयः, because here the self is seen clearly, like a face in a mirror, and not in other worlds except Brahmā’s, यस्मादिहैवात्मनो दर्शनमादर्शस्थस्येव मुखस्य स्पष्टमुपपद्यते.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: if unable, then rebirth

If one is unable to know Brahman here before the body falls, अशकश्चेत् अशक्नुवांश्चेत्, then from that want of knowledge one becomes subject to birth, old age and decay in all the created worlds, सृज्यमानसर्वलोकेषु जन्मजरादिमत्वलक्षणशीर्यमाणत्वाय भवति इत्यर्थः. Therefore one should strive for knowledge of the self before the body falls, तस्मात् शरीरपातात्प्रागेव आत्मज्ञानाय यतेत इति भावः.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva

Madhva’s bhāṣya has no comment of its own on this mantra; the comment filed under 2.3.3–2.3.5 concerns the images of 2.3.5. His text reads svargeṣu for sargeṣu.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

aśakat

aśakat is formally an a-aorist of √śak, ‘was able’, P. 3.1.55 (पुषादिद्युताद्यॢदितः परस्मैपदेषु). Raṅgarāmānuja, taking it as ‘unable’, notes a Vedic irregularity of the stem-class suffix, विकरणव्यत्ययः छान्दसः.

Comparative Vedānta

Knowledge in this life

The Bṛhadāraṇyaka makes the same point: if we know it while here, well; if not, great is the loss, इहैव सन्तोऽथ विद्मस्तद्वयं न चेदवेदिर्महती विनष्टिः.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.3.4

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Knowing Brahman here, before death, one is freed; failing that, one takes a body again; so the effort must be made now: तस्माच्छरीरविस्रंसनात्प्रागात्मावबोधाय यत्न आस्थेयः.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.3.4

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

If one fails to know Brahman before the body falls, one is born again and decays in the created worlds; hence the effort before death: तस्मात् शरीरपातात्प्रागेव आत्मज्ञानाय यतेत इति भावः.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara and Raṅgarāmānuja draw the same practical lesson but construe the verse differently: Śaṅkara reads aśakat as ‘was able’ with an unexpressed consequence, Raṅgarāmānuja as ‘was unable’, with the second line as its direct result. Madhva’s bhāṣya has no comment on this mantra.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • चेदशकद्बोद्धुं cedaśakadboddhuṃ → चेत् + अशकत् + बोद्धुम्

    t before a vowel; t before a voiced consonant; m before a consonant

    A final t becomes d before a vowel or a voiced consonant, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते); final m before a consonant becomes anusvāra, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • प्राक्शरीरस्य prākśarīrasya → प्राक् + शरीरस्य

    k before ś

    A final k stays before ś; the optional change of ś to ch after a stop is not made here.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

अशकत्
The a-aorist of √śak (śakḷ, a root with ḷ as marker), P. 3.1.55 (पुषादिद्युताद्यॢदितः परस्मैपदेषु): ‘was able’. Raṅgarāmānuja glosses it as ‘unable’, अशकश्चेत् अशक्नुवांश्चेत्, and calls the change of the stem-class suffix a Vedic licence, विकरणव्यत्ययः छान्दसः.
विस्रसः
visras-, ‘falling apart, decay’, a root noun from vi + √sraṃs; ablative singular after prāk.
बोद्धुम्
Infinitive of √budh, ‘to know, awaken to’.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

A conditional sentence whose two halves are read differently. With Śaṅkara, the condition (iha ced aśakad boddhum …) has an unexpressed consequence, ‘one is freed’, and tataḥ begins the opposite case, ‘otherwise’. With Raṅgarāmānuja, aśakat means ‘was unable’, and tataḥ … kalpate is its direct consequence.

Ambiguities

Able or unable?
Śaṅkara reads aśakat as ‘was able’ and supplies the consequence: one is freed from bondage, संसारबन्धनाद्विमुच्यते; if one is not able, then one takes a body, न चेदशकद्बोद्धुम् , ततः अनवबोधात्, one takes a body, शरीरं गृह्णातीत्यर्थः. Raṅgarāmānuja reads it as ‘unable’, अशकश्चेत् अशक्नुवांश्चेत्, so the second line follows directly: from that want of knowledge one becomes subject to birth and decay in all the created worlds.
sargeṣu or svargeṣu
Madhva’s text reads svargeṣu lokeṣu, ‘in the heavenly worlds’, for sargeṣu, ‘in the created worlds’ (corrections.json). Śaṅkara explains sarga as the worlds in which beings are created, सृज्यन्ते येषु स्रष्टव्याः प्राणिन इति सर्गाः.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16

Regular: 16 and 16 syllables.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Witness error The vishvAsa mūla: चेदशकद्बोद्धुं प्राक्षरीरस्य विस्रसः — The vishvAsa mūla writes प्राक्षरीरस्य; the words are prāk śarīrasya, ‘before the body’s falling apart’, as the others read and Śaṅkara glosses.
  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: ततः स्वर्गेषु लोकेषु — Madhva’s text reads svargeṣu lokeṣu, ‘in the heavenly worlds’, for sargeṣu lokeṣu, ‘in the created worlds’, which Śaṅkara glosses (सृज्यन्ते येषु स्रष्टव्याः प्राणिन इति सर्गाः).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Science and wonder

Many traditions tell people to settle the questions that matter while there is time. The verse sets its own question in the span of one life. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.
