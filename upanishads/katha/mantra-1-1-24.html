Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 24 of 29

Or any boon you think equal: be great on the earth

Death widens the offer: if Naciketas thinks any other boon equal, let him choose it, with wealth and long life; he may rule on the earth, and Death will give him a share of every desire.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

If you think any boon equal to this, choose it, and wealth and long life with it. Be great on the wide earth, Naciketas; I will make you the enjoyer of every desire.

Key concepts मृत्यु death भोग enjoyment, use लोक world, realm वर boon

7 Explain it to a beginner

Death does not give up. If none of the gifts he listed is good enough, he says, then name any gift you think is equal to your question, and take wealth and long life with it. Rule over the wide earth. I will make you someone who enjoys every pleasure he could want. Śaṅkara points out why Death can promise this: as a god, what he wills comes true. The offer is now open-ended — anything at all, except the one thing the boy asked for. The test is becoming harder, because now the boy cannot say that the offer was too small.

An analogy Like being told ‘name your price’ — the moment when it becomes clear whether there is a price at all.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does Death add?

Any boon the boy thinks equal, together with wealth and long life; kingship on the great earth; and a share in every desire.

Traditional Interpretation

How can Death promise every desire?

Śaṅkara: कामानां दिव्यानां मानुषाणां च त्वा त्वां कामभाजं कामभागिनं कामार्हं करोमि, for, as Death says of himself, सत्यसङ्कल्पो ह्यहं देवः — a god whose will comes true. ‘Be’ means be king: महत्यां भूमौ राजा नचिकेतः त्वम् एधि भव.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

The grammar of edhi

Raṅgarāmānuja: edhi is the imperative, second person singular, of as — अस्तेः लोण्मध्यमपुरुषैकवचनम्; ‘king’ is to be supplied (राजेति शेषः).

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • एतत्तुल्यं etattulyaṃ → एतत् + तुल्यम्

    none (t before t)

    etat before t stays: etat tulyam, written together. tulyam before the consonant y becomes tulyaṃ, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • नचिकेतस्त्वमेधि naciketastvamedhi → नचिकेतः + त्वम् + एधि

    visarga before t; m before a vowel

    naciketaḥ before t becomes naciketas, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः). tvam keeps its m before the vowel of edhi.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • चिरजीविकाम् cirajīvikām

    karmadhāraya · चिर + जीविका

    Vigraha चिरं जीविका

    long living, a long life

  • महाभूमौ mahābhūmau

    karmadhāraya · महत् + भूमि

    Vigraha महती भूमिः

    the great earth; Śaṅkara glosses महत्यां भूमौ

  • कामभाजम् kāmabhājam

    upapada-tatpuruṣa · काम + भाज्

    Vigraha कामान् भजति इति

    one who enjoys his desires; Śaṅkara: कामभागिनं कामार्हं

Morphology

एधि
Imperative 2nd sg. of as ‘to be’; Raṅgarāmānuja notes the form — अस्तेः लोण्मध्यमपुरुषैकवचनम्.
त्वा
Enclitic accusative of the 2nd person pronoun; Śaṅkara glosses त्वां.

Anvaya

नचिकेतः, यदि एतत् तुल्यम् [अन्यम्] वरम् मन्यसे, [तम्] वृणीष्व, वित्तम् चिरजीविकाम् च [वृणीष्व] । त्वम् महाभूमौ [राजा] एधि । त्वा कामानाम् कामभाजम् करोमि ।

naciketaḥ, yadi etat tulyam anyam varam manyase, tam vṛṇīṣva, vittam cirajīvikām ca vṛṇīṣva | tvam mahābhūmau rājā edhi | tvā kāmānām kāmabhājam karomi |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A conditional (yadi manyase) with an imperative apodosis (vṛṇīṣva), whose objects are extended loosely (vittaṃ cirajīvikāṃ ca); then an imperative of ‘be’ with a locative of place; then a first-person promise with a double accusative (tvā … kāmabhājaṃ karomi) and a partitive genitive.

Ambiguities

edhi
From as, ‘be [king] on the great earth’: Śaṅkara supplies राजा, and Raṅgarāmānuja names the form. The letters also allow edh ‘grow, prosper’, but no commentator used here takes it so.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 22

Triṣṭubh, 4 × 11 (22 + 22), regular.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Wealth and consumption

‘Name anything you think is equal’ is the strongest form of temptation, because it removes the excuse that the offer was too small. What it tests is whether anything is, for us, beyond price. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Is there anything you would not exchange for any amount of money?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry What makes a question worth more than every answer to other questions?

A contemplation exercise Notice, today, one moment when you settle for something easier than what you really wanted to ask for.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
