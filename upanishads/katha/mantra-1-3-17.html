Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 17 of 17

Recited at a śrāddha

Whoever, purified, has this supreme secret recited in an assembly of brāhmaṇas or at the time of a śrāddha — that leads to endless fruit. The repeated last words close the first adhyāya.

5 Faithful English translation

Whoever, purified, should cause this supreme secret to be heard in an assembly of brāhmaṇas, or at the time of a śrāddha — that serves for endlessness; that serves for endlessness. Thus.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Whoever, with a pure mind, recites this supreme secret to an assembly of brāhmaṇas, or at the time of the rite for the ancestors — that brings endless fruit. That brings endless fruit.

Key concepts अनन्त infinite ब्राह्मण Brāhmaṇa श्रुति śruti, ‘what is heard’

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse ends the first half of the Kaṭha. It says that whoever, with a pure mind, recites this supreme secret teaching to a gathering of learned people, or at the time of the rite for the ancestors called śrāddha, gains something endless by it. The last words are said twice. Śaṅkara explains that the repetition simply marks the end of a section, the way a reciter signals that a chapter is complete. He reads ‘that’ as the ancestral rite itself, which becomes endlessly fruitful when the teaching is recited at it. The verse belongs to a common pattern: a teaching closes by praising its own recitation. It reports what the text promises; it says nothing about how such a promise should be understood today, which is left to the reader and to tradition.

An analogy Like the closing line a storyteller repeats at the end of a tale, so that everyone knows it is over.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

Whoever, purified, recites this supreme secret in an assembly of brāhmaṇas or at the time of a śrāddha, that leads to endless fruit; the last words are repeated.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the śrāddha becomes endless; the repetition closes the adhyāya

Whoever recites this text in its words and in its meaning, ग्रन्थतोऽर्थतश्च, in an assembly of brāhmaṇas, purified, or at the time of a śrāddha to those eating — तत् श्राद्धम् अस्य आनन्त्याय अनन्तफलाय कल्पते. The repetition marks the end of the adhyāya: द्विर्वचनमध्यायपरिसमाप्त्यर्थम्.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: the assembly and purity

His short comment glosses the assembly of brāhmaṇas, ब्राह्मणसमाजे, and prayataḥ as pure. His entry for this verse is printed after the colophon of the third vallī and before the colophon of his own comment on it.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

How the first adhyāya ends

Repeating the last words of a section is a common way of marking its end in Vedic prose and verse; Śaṅkara says so here. The second adhyāya begins with 2.1.1.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • य इमं ya imaṃ → यः + इमम्

    aḥ before a vowel other than a

    The visarga after a becomes y before a vowel other than a, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), and the y is dropped, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य), leaving the hiatus ya imam.

  • श्रावयेद्ब्रह्मसंसदि śrāvayedbrahmasaṃsadi → श्रावयेत् + ब्रह्मसंसदि

    t before a voiced consonant

    A final t becomes d before b, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

  • तदानन्त्याय tadānantyāya → तत् + आनन्त्याय

    t before a vowel

    A final t becomes d before a vowel, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

  • कल्पत इति kalpata iti → कल्पते + इति

    e before a vowel

    e becomes ay before i, P. 6.1.78 (एचोऽयवायावः), and the y is dropped, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • ब्रह्मसंसदि brahmasaṃsadi

    ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa · ब्रह्मन् + संसद्

    Vigraha ब्राह्मणानां संसत्

    an assembly of brāhmaṇas (Śaṅkara's analysis; brahman here stands for the brāhmaṇas)

  • श्राद्धकाले śrāddhakāle

    ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa · श्राद्ध + काल

    Vigraha श्राद्धस्य कालः

    the time of a śrāddha

Morphology

श्रावयेत्
Optative of the causative of √śru, ‘hear’: ‘should make [others] hear’, that is, recite to them.
इति
The final words are repeated, with iti, to mark the end of the first adhyāya: Śaṅkara, द्विर्वचनमध्यायपरिसमाप्त्यर्थम्.

Anvaya

यः प्रयतः [सन्] इमम् परमम् गुह्यम् ब्रह्मसंसदि श्राद्धकाले वा श्रावयेत्, तत् आनन्त्याय कल्पते; तत् आनन्त्याय कल्पते इति ।

yaḥ prayataḥ san imam paramam guhyam brahmasaṃsadi śrāddhakāle vā śrāvayet, tat ānantyāya kalpate; tat ānantyāya kalpate iti |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A relative clause (yaḥ … śrāvayet) gives the condition; the main clause tat ānantyāya kalpate gives the result, with the dative of purpose. The main clause is repeated with iti to close the adhyāya. The object of śrāvayet is masculine (imam … guhyam), agreeing with an understood noun; Śaṅkara supplies grantham, ‘text’.

Ambiguities

What does tat refer to?
Śaṅkara: the śrāddha at which it is recited, तत् श्राद्धम् अस्य आनन्त्याय अनन्तफलाय कल्पते, brings endless fruit. Raṅgarāmānuja's short comment glosses only the assembly and prayataḥ.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 26

Anuṣṭubh, 16 + 16, with the closing repetition tad ānantyāya kalpata iti (10 syllables) added: 16 and 26 as written.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Aging and mortality

Remembering the dead with words that matter is an old practice in many cultures. The verse links a teaching about what does not die to the rite that remembers those who have died. Whatever one makes of its promise, the pairing is worth noticing. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Why might a teaching about death and the deathless be fitting at a rite for the dead?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Śaṅkara stresses reciting the text ‘in its words and in its meaning’. What is lost when only the words are passed on?
