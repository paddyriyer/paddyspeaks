Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 5 of 15

The honey-eater near at hand

Whoever knows this honey-eater, the living self, as near at hand, the ruler of past and future, no longer shrinks from anything. This is that.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘Whoever knows this enjoyer of life’s honey, the living self, as close at hand — the lord of what has been and what will be — no longer shrinks from anything. This is that.’

Key concepts जीव individual self आत्मन् self, Self ईश्वर the Lord कर्मन् action, ritual work भोग enjoyment, use

7 Explain it to a beginner

The verse calls the self in each of us the 'honey-eater': the one who tastes the sweet and bitter fruits of what we do. Then it says something surprising: this same living self, known as close at hand, is the ruler of past and future. Whoever knows this, it says, no longer shrinks from anything. Śaṅkara explains why: we try to protect ourselves only while we feel small and threatened, and once the self is known as beyond time and change, there is nothing left to protect it from. Raṅgarāmānuja reads the last word differently: such a person no longer looks down on anyone, even those who do wrong, because the Lord dwells close to every self. Madhva stresses that the Lord is near the self, and therefore not the same as it.

An analogy A person who learns that the house they were guarding against thieves was never theirs to lose stops checking the locks.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

Whoever knows this honey-eater, the living self, as near, the ruler of past and future, no longer shrinks away. This is that.

Traditional Interpretation

No more need to guard oneself

Śaṅkara: as long as one stands amid fear and thinks the self impermanent, one wants to protect it, यावद्धि भयमध्यस्थोऽनित्यमात्मानं मन्यते तावद्गोपायितुमिच्छत्यात्मानम्; once it is known as eternal and without a second, तदा कः किं कुतो वा गोपायितुमिच्छेत्.

Comparative Vedānta

Raṅgarāmānuja: the Lord beside the self

The honey-eater is the individual self that enjoys the fruits of action, the one named in ‘the two who drink ṛta’ (1.3.1); whoever knows it and the Lord near it does not censure even a wrongdoer, तं दुष्कृतकारिणमपि न निन्देदित्यर्थः. He grounds the sense ‘censure’ in grammar (see morphology).

Comparative Vedānta

Madhva: near means other

Madhva’s comment turns on antikāt: ‘near the living self’, जीवस्यान्तिके, for nothing is near itself, न हि स्वस्य स्वयं जीवोऽन्तिके भवति; nearness and distance belong to another thing, वस्त्वन्तरस्य हि दूरत्वमन्तिकत्वं वा.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Text and witnesses

Madhva’s text reads ya idaṃ madhvadaṃ veda and joins vedātmānam; Raṅgarāmānuja comments on the same idam. The second line recurs in the Bṛhadāraṇyaka (4.4.15).

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.1.5

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The living self, the enjoyer, is itself the ruler of all time; known so, as eternal and without a second, नित्यमद्वैतमात्मानं, it leaves nothing to guard against.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.1.5

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The honey-eater is the individual self, कर्मफलभोक्तारं जीवात्मानम्, and the Lord of the conscious and unconscious stands near it; the knower of both censures no one.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.1.5

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

‘Near’ implies another: the ruler is near the living self and so distinct from it, न हि स्वस्य स्वयं जीवोऽन्तिके भवति.

Where they disagree

The three part over antikāt and īśānam: Śaṅkara reads the living self and the ruler as one; Raṅgarāmānuja places the Lord near the self as its inner ruler; Madhva argues from ‘near’ that the two are different. They also differ on vijugupsate (not guarding oneself, or not censuring others).

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • य इमं ya imaṃ → यः + इमम्

    visarga lost before a vowel

    After a, ru becomes y before a vowel, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), and the y is dropped, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य); the hiatus is not joined again.

  • जीवमन्तिकात् jīvamantikāt → जीवम् + अन्तिकात्

    m before a vowel

    Final m stays m before a vowel; before a consonant it becomes anusvāra, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • ततो विजुगुप्सते tato vijugupsate → ततः + विजुगुप्सते

    aḥ → o

    The ru of -aḥ becomes u before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च), and a + u → o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • एतद्वै etadvai → एतत् + वै

    voicing

    Final t becomes d before a voiced sound, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

Junctions outside the general rules

  • वेद · आत्मानम् — hiatus at the pāda boundary: veda ātmānam: a before ā is not fused. The words end the first pāda and begin the second, and both are counted (8 + 8); fused, as Madhva’s text writes it (vedātmānam, corrections.json), the half-line would have 15.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • मध्वदम् madhvadam

    upapada-tatpuruṣa · मधु + अद

    Vigraha मधु अत्तीति मध्वदः

    one who eats honey (the fruits of action)

  • भूतभव्यस्य bhūtabhavyasya

    samāhāra-dvandva · भूत + भव्य

    Vigraha भूतं च भव्यं च

    past and future (taken together)

Morphology

वेद
The old perfect of vid ‘know’, used with present meaning (‘knows’); Pāṇini allows these forms in place of the present, P. 3.4.83 (विदो लटो वा).
विजुगुप्सते
Desiderative of gup with vi, middle. Raṅgarāmānuja cites P. 3.1.5 (गुप्तिज्किद्भ्यः सन्), where the desiderative of gup has the sense of censure, and reads ‘does not censure’; Śaṅkara keeps the root sense, ‘does not wish to guard [himself]’.
इमम्
Madhva’s text reads idam (neuter) for imam (corrections.json); Raṅgarāmānuja comments on the same idam and calls it a Vedic change of gender, इदम् इति लिङ्गव्यत्ययः छान्दसः.

Anvaya

यः इमम् मध्वदम् जीवम् आत्मानम् भूतभव्यस्य ईशानम् अन्तिकात् वेद, [सः] ततः न विजुगुप्सते । एतत् वै तत् ॥

yaḥ imam madhvadam jīvam ātmānam bhūtabhavyasya īśānam antikāt veda, saḥ tataḥ na vijugupsate | etat vai tat ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A relative clause (yaḥ … veda) with the object madhvadam and its appositions ātmānaṃ jīvam and īśānaṃ bhūtabhavyasya, and the adverb antikāt; the correlative subject of na vijugupsate is supplied. The refrain closes the verse.

Ambiguities

Who is ‘near’ whom?
Śaṅkara: knowing the living self itself as the ruler of all time, close at hand, अन्तिके समीपे ईशानम् ईशितारं भूतभव्यस्य. Raṅgarāmānuja: knowing the living self and, near it, the Lord of all that is conscious and unconscious in the three times, तस्यान्तिके कालत्रयवर्तिचिदचिदीश्वरं च यो वेद. Madhva: the ruler is near the living self, and so other than it, न हि स्वस्य स्वयं जीवोऽन्तिके भवति.
vijugupsate
Śaṅkara: does not wish to protect himself, being free of fear, अभयप्राप्तत्वात्. Raṅgarāmānuja: does not censure, even one who does wrong, तं दुष्कृतकारिणमपि न निन्देदित्यर्थः.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16 + 4

Two half-lines of 16 syllables (four pādas of eight, counting veda ātmānam unjoined), then the refrain etad vai tat, four syllables.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: य इदं मध्वदं — Madhva’s text reads ya idaṃ madhvadaṃ veda, neuter idam, for ya imaṃ madhvadaṃ veda, ‘who knows this honey-eater’.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Fear

Much of what we do is self-protection. The verse locates the root of that guardedness in what we take ourselves to be, and suggests it relaxes when that changes. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What are you most careful to protect about yourself?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why might knowing the ruler of past and future as ‘near’ change how one treats a wrongdoer?

A contemplation exercise Notice one moment today when you become defensive, and ask what exactly felt threatened.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
