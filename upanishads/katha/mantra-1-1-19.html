Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 19 of 29

The second boon given; the third asked for

Yama closes the second boon — this is your fire to heaven, and people will call it by your name — and invites the third.

5 Faithful English translation

‘This is your fire, O Naciketas, leading to heaven, which you chose with the second boon. People will call this fire yours indeed. Choose the third boon, O Naciketas.’

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘This, Naciketas, is the fire that leads to heaven, the one you chose as your second gift. People will call it by your name. Now, Naciketas, choose your third gift.’

Key concepts अग्नि Agni, fire लोक world, realm वर boon

7 Explain it to a beginner

Yama closes the second gift and opens the way to the third. This, he says, is the fire that leads to heaven, the one you chose; and people will call it by your name. Now choose your third gift. Śaṅkara adds a touching detail: Yama feels he owes the boy until the third boon is given, as one feels in debt to an honoured guest. The scene is set for the most important question of the Upaniṣad, which Naciketas will ask in the next verse. Everything so far has been preparation for it.

An analogy Like a host who has served two courses and now, before the last one, asks the guest what he would most like.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does Yama say?

The second boon, the fire to heaven, is given and will bear Naciketas’s name among people; now he must choose the third.

Traditional Interpretation

Why must the third be given?

Śaṅkara: Yama counts the naming as a fourth boon given from pleasure, एष वरो दत्तो मया चतुर्थस्तुष्टेन, and feels himself in debt until the third is given: तस्मिन्ह्यदत्ते ऋणवानेवाहमित्यभिप्रायः.

Comparative Vedānta

What does Raṅgarāmānuja add here?

He uses this point for a long argument that ‘heaven’ in the second boon means release, reporting the Śrībhāṣya: स्वर्गशब्देनात्र परमपुरुषार्थलक्षणमोक्षोऽभिधीयते, because those who reach it are said to have immortality and to cross birth and death, and because a boy who scorns perishable fruits would not ask for a perishable heaven. He then states the objections and answers them.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.19

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The ritual section ends; the boons so far concern action and its fruits. Yama feels indebted until the third is given: तस्मिन्ह्यदत्ते ऋणवानेवाहमित्यभिप्रायः.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, reporting the Śrībhāṣya, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.1.19

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Heaven throughout the second boon is release: स्वर्गशब्देनात्र परमपुरुषार्थलक्षणमोक्षोऽभिधीयते. He also reads the people’s naming plainly: जनाः तवैव नाम्ना एनमग्निं प्रवक्ष्यन्ति इत्यर्थः.

Dvaita · Madhva (citing the Pādma), Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya on 1.1.12–18

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Madhva has no separate comment on this verse; the verse he cites from the Pādma states the naming of the fire: यमोऽनुवादसन्तुष्टो वह्नेस्तन्नामतामपि. His text reads nāciketaḥ, the name of the fire.

Where they disagree

The verse itself is a hinge; the schools differ on what has just been given. Śaṅkara: a ritual leading to a high heaven. Raṅgarāmānuja: a means of release. Madhva: the fire in which Hari is present (1.1.12–13).

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • एष तेऽग्निर्नचिकेतः eṣa te'gnirnaciketaḥ → एषः + ते + अग्निः + नचिकेतः

    eṣaḥ before a consonant; e before a

    eṣaḥ loses its visarga before a consonant, P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि); e before a absorbs it (shown by the avagraha), P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति); the visarga after i becomes r.

  • स्वर्ग्यो यमवृणीथा svargyo yamavṛṇīthā → स्वर्ग्यः + यम् + अवृणीथाः

    visarga before a voiced consonant; m before a vowel

    aḥ before a voiced consonant becomes o: P. 6.1.114 (हशि च) with P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः); m stays before a vowel.

  • जनासस्तृतीयम् janāsastṛtīyam → जनासः + तृतीयम्

    visarga before t

    Visarga before t becomes s: P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः).

  • नचिकेतो वृणीष्व naciketo vṛṇīṣva → नचिकेतः + वृणीष्व

    visarga before a voiced consonant

    aḥ before a voiced consonant becomes o: P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

जनासः
Vedic nominative plural in -āsaḥ (= janāḥ), formed with the augment asuk: P. 7.1.50 (आज्जसेरसुक्).
अवृणीथाः
Imperfect middle 2 sg. of vṛ ‘choose’ (ninth class).
ते
Enclitic dative or genitive of tvam; Śaṅkara takes it as dative, ‘for you’.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

Three sentences: a summary of the second boon with a relative clause (yam avṛṇīthā dvitīyena vareṇa), a statement about the future name of the fire, and the invitation tṛtīyaṃ varaṃ naciketo vṛṇīṣva. The vocative naciketaḥ frames the speech.

Ambiguities

naciketaḥ or nāciketaḥ?
Madhva’s text reads nāciketaḥ, ‘the Nāciketa [fire]’, an adjective of agniḥ, for the vocative naciketaḥ (corrections.json).

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 26

Counted as written, 22 and 26 syllables: the second line (etam agniṃ tavaiva pravakṣyanti janāsas tṛtīyaṃ varaṃ naciketo vṛṇīṣva) is four syllables long; janāsaḥ and the extra vocative may have been read with contractions, or the line is irregular.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: एष तेऽग्निर्नाचिकेतः स्वर्ग्यो — Madhva’s text reads nāciketaḥ, ‘the Nāciketa [fire]’, an adjective of agniḥ, for naciketaḥ, the vocative ‘O Naciketas’.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Patience before the real question

Naciketas has waited through two boons — family peace and a ritual — before the question that matters most to him. People often need to settle smaller things before they can ask the large one. (A modern reflection.)
