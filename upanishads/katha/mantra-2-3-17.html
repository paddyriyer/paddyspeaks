Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 17 of 18

Drawing out the reed from the grass

The person the size of a thumb, the inner self, always dwells in the hearts of people. One should draw him out from one’s own body, steadily, like the reed from the muñja grass; one should know him as the bright, the immortal.

5 Faithful English translation

The thumb-sized person, the inner self, always [is] seated in the heart of people. Him one should draw out from one’s own body, as the reed from the muñja grass, with steadiness. Him one should know as bright, immortal; him one should know as bright, immortal.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

The person the size of a thumb, the self within, dwells always in people’s hearts. Draw him out from your own body steadily, as one pulls the inner stalk from a blade of muñja grass, and know him as the bright and the immortal — know him as the bright and the immortal.

Key concepts अङ्गुष्ठमात्र aṅguṣṭhamātra, thumb-sized अन्तरात्मन् the inner self पुरुष person आत्मन् self, Self अमृत immortality, the deathless

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse gathers up what the vallī has taught. The ‘person the size of a thumb’, the self within, is always present in the heart. You are to draw him out from your own body as one draws the soft inner stalk out of a blade of muñja grass, carefully, without breaking it, and know him as bright and immortal. The last words are said twice, as the traditional sign that the teaching is ending. Śaṅkara says this means separating the self, which is pure consciousness, from the body, and knowing it as Brahman. Raṅgarāmānuja says it means knowing God as distinct from the individual soul, which is like his body, and that ‘steadiness’ means skill in knowledge. Madhva likewise says that Viṣṇu is to be known as different from the soul in which he dwells.

An analogy Pulling the inner stalk from a blade of grass takes a steady hand: pull too hard and it snaps, too lightly and it stays put.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

The thumb-sized person, the inner self, dwells always in people’s hearts; one should draw him out from one’s own body steadily, like the reed from the muñja grass, and know him as bright and immortal.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the summary of the vallīs

The verse concludes the sense of all the vallīs, इदानीं सर्ववल्ल्यर्थोपसंहारार्थमाह. The thumb-sized inner self, as already explained, is to be drawn out from one’s own body, set apart, उद्यच्छेत् निष्कर्षेत् पृथक्कुर्यादित्यर्थः, like the reed from the muñja grass, with steadiness, धैर्येण अप्रमादेन. One should know it, drawn out, as pure consciousness, तं शरीरान्निष्कृष्टं चिन्मात्रं विद्यात्, as bright and immortal Brahman. The repetition marks the end of the Upaniṣad, द्विर्वचनमुपनिषत्परिसमाप्त्यर्थम्.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: knowing the Lord as distinct from the soul

‘Own’ refers to the inner self just named, एवंपूर्वनिर्दिष्टान्तरात्मसम्बन्धिपरामर्शी स्वशब्दः: one is to know him as distinct from the conscious being that is his body, तच्छरीरभूतात् जनशब्दिताच्चेतनात्, प्रवृहेत् - विविच्य जानीयात्, as the Muṇḍaka says (quoted by him), जुष्टं यदा पश्यत्यन्यमीशम्. Steadiness is skill in knowledge, धैर्येण - ज्ञानकौशलेन. The repetition marks the end of the teaching.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: Viṣṇu drawn out from the jīva

The jīva is Viṣṇu’s body, under his control; and in a verse he cites (इति च), one should draw out the imperishable Viṣṇu from the jīva called one’s own body, स्वाख्याच्छरीराज्जीवात् तु प्रवृहेद् विष्णुमव्ययम्. Because the verse says ‘in the hearts of people’, Hari is shown to be other than the jīva, जनानां हृदये इत्युक्तत्वाच्च जीवात् पृथग् हरिरिति सिद्धम्; hence the difference of Viṣṇu from the jīva is taught, अतो विष्णोर्जीवाद्भेद उक्तः.

The Lord, the inner self, is to be known as distinct from the individual soul that is his body, विविच्य जानीयात्.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.3.16–17

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Viṣṇu is to be known as different from the jīva in whose heart he dwells, अतो विष्णोर्जीवाद्भेद उक्तः.

Where they disagree

For Śaṅkara the self is drawn out from the body and known as Brahman; for Raṅgarāmānuja and Madhva the Lord is drawn out, in knowledge, from the individual soul, which is his body, and known as distinct from it.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • पुरुषोऽन्तरात्मा puruṣo'ntarātmā → पुरुषः + अन्तरात्मा

    aḥ before a

    The visarga of -aḥ becomes u before a, giving o, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च) with P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः), and the following a is merged, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति); the avagraha marks it.

  • जनानां हृदये janānāṃ hṛdaye → जनानाम् + हृदये

    m before a consonant

    Final m → anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • तं स्वाच्छरीरात् taṃ svāccharīrāt → तम् + स्वात् + शरीरात्

    m before a consonant; t before ś

    Final m → anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः). t before ś becomes c, P. 8.4.40 (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः), and the ś after it becomes ch, P. 8.4.63 (शश्छोऽटि).

  • प्रवृहेन्मुञ्जादिवेषीकां pravṛhenmuñjādiveṣīkāṃ → प्रवृहेत् + मुञ्जात् + इव + इषीकाम्

    t before a nasal; t before a vowel; a + i

    A final t becomes n before a nasal, P. 8.4.45 (यरोऽनुनासिकेऽनुनासिको वा). A final t becomes d before a voiced sound, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते). a + i give e, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • विद्याच्छुक्रममृतं vidyācchukramamṛtaṃ → विद्यात् + शुक्रम् + अमृतम्

    t before ś; m before a vowel

    t before ś becomes c, P. 8.4.40 (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः), and the ś after it becomes ch, P. 8.4.63 (शश्छोऽटि). Final m stays before a vowel and is joined to it.

  • अमृतमिति amṛtamiti → अमृतम् + इति

    m before a vowel

    Final m stays before a vowel and is joined to it.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • अन्तरात्मा antarātmā

    karmadhāraya · अन्तर + आत्मन्

    Vigraha अन्तरश्चासौ आत्मा च

    the inner self

Morphology

अङ्गुष्ठमात्रः
aṅguṣṭha with the suffix mātrac of measure, P. 5.2.37 (प्रमाणे द्वयसज्दघ्नञ्मात्रचः): ‘having the measure of a thumb’, as at 2.1.12–13.
प्रवृहेत्
Optative of pra + √vṛh (also written bṛh), ‘tear out, pull out’: ‘one should draw out’.
इति
The last words are said twice and closed with iti. Śaṅkara: the repetition and iti mark the end of the Upaniṣad, द्विर्वचनमुपनिषत्परिसमाप्त्यर्थम्; Raṅgarāmānuja: the end of the teaching, द्विर्वचनम् उपदेशसमाप्त्यर्थम्.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

A verbless descriptive line (aṅguṣṭhamātraḥ … sanniviṣṭaḥ). Then an optative command, taṃ svāc charīrāt pravṛhet, with the comparison muñjād iveṣīkām and the instrumental dhairyeṇa; then taṃ vidyāc chukram amṛtam, an optative with a double accusative, said twice and closed by iti.

Ambiguities

From what is he to be ‘drawn out’?
Śaṅkara: from one’s own body; one is to set the self apart and know it, drawn out, as pure consciousness, तं शरीरान्निष्कृष्टं चिन्मात्रं विद्यात्, Brahman. Raṅgarāmānuja: ‘own’ refers to the inner self, so the body meant is the conscious being (jana) that is his body; he is to be known as distinct from it, तं जनानाम् अन्तरात्मानम्, तच्छरीरभूतात् जनशब्दिताच्चेतनात्, प्रवृहेत् - विविच्य जानीयात्. Madhva: Viṣṇu is to be drawn out from the jīva, which is his body (see the commentary).
Who is the thumb-sized person here?
Śaṅkara: the inner self as explained before (2.1.12–13). Raṅgarāmānuja and Madhva: the Lord as inner self, distinct from the individual soul. The Brahmasūtra discusses the thumb-sized one under 1.3.24.

Metre

irregular · syllables counted as written: 23 + 17 + 18

Counted as written, 23, 17 and 18 syllables. The first line is a triṣṭubh line of 11 + 12: sadā janānāṃ hṛdaye sanniviṣṭaḥ has twelve. The second line, taṃ svāc charīrāt pravṛhen muñjād iveṣīkāṃ dhairyeṇa, has seventeen and does not divide into regular pādas. The third repeats the nine-syllable taṃ vidyāc chukram amṛtam twice.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Self-mastery

Drawing a stalk from a blade of grass is the opposite of force: it needs patience and a steady hand. The verse puts the inward search in those terms. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What in you is like the soft stalk inside the hard grass?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Is the ‘body’ from which he is drawn the physical body, or the soul itself? What follows from each?

A contemplation exercise Do one careful task today with the steadiness the verse asks for.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
