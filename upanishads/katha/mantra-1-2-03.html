Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 3 of 25

The chain of wealth

Death praises Naciketas: he examined the dear and lovely desires and let them go, and did not take the chain of wealth in which many sink.

5 Faithful English translation

‘You, such [as you are], reflecting on dear desires and on [desires] of pleasing form, have let them go, Naciketas. You have not taken this chain made of wealth, in which many human beings sink.’

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘You have looked hard at desires, Naciketas, the dear ones and the lovely ones, and you have let them go. You did not take this chain of wealth, in which so many people drown.’

Key concepts मृत्यु death भोग enjoyment, use मोह delusion वर boon

7 Explain it to a beginner

Death now praises the boy. He has offered Naciketas everything that people usually want: long life, sons and grandsons, wealth, beautiful companions, music and chariots. The boy did not simply say no. He thought about these things, saw that they do not last, and let them go. Death also says that Naciketas did not take the chain of wealth in which so many people sink. Earlier Death had given him a jewelled chain as an extra gift; now the same word is used for a chain of riches that pulls people under. Wealth here is not only something one owns; it is something that can hold its owner down. The praise prepares for the teaching, because a pupil who can refuse such offers is ready to hear it.

An analogy Like a swimmer who is offered a heavy golden chain and declines it, not because gold is bad, but because he can see the water.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does Death praise?

That Naciketas, reflecting on the dear and lovely objects of desire, let them go, and did not take the ‘chain made of wealth’ in which many sink.

Traditional Interpretation

How did he let them go?

Śaṅkara: by reflecting on their faults, their impermanence and lack of substance — चिन्तयन् तेषामनित्यत्वासारत्वादिदोषान् — though tempted again and again, पुनः पुनः मया प्रलोभ्यमानोऽपि. Death exclaims at the boy’s intelligence: अहो बुद्धिमत्ता तव. Raṅgarāmānuja: seeing them as ending in pain and mixed with pain, दुःखोदर्कत्व-दुःखमिश्रत्वादिदोषयुक्ततया निरूपयन्.

Comparative Vedānta

Chain or road?

Śaṅkara and Raṅgarāmānuja read sṛṅkā here as a road, a contemptible one walked by the deluded: सृतिं कुत्सितां मूढजनप्रवृत्तां, कुत्सितगतिं मूढजनसेवितां. Madhva’s whole comment on the passage 1.2.1–3 is a gloss of this word as a chain: शृङ्कां शृङ्कलाम्. At 1.1.16 Śaṅkara had given both senses, a jewelled chain or a road: सृङ्कां शब्दवतीं रत्नमयीं मालाम्, यद्वा, सृङ्काम् अकुत्सितां गतिं कर्ममयीं.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What is odd about avāptaḥ?

It is a past participle, normally passive, used here with an accusative object as a past active verb, ‘you have not taken’. Śaṅkara supplies the auxiliary: न एताम् अवाप्तवानसि.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.2.3

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Naciketas has seen the impermanence of desires and refused the road of wealth trodden by the deluded: यस्यां सृतौ मज्जन्ति सीदन्ति बहवः अनेके मूढा मनुष्याः. The praise shows him fit for knowledge.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.2.3

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The boy saw that desires end in pain and gave them up; he did not take the base road of wealth: वित्तमयीम् - धनप्रायाम्, सृंकाम् - कुत्सितगतिं मूढजनसेवितां एतां न अवाप्तवानसि.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya on 1.2.1–3

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The sṛṅkā is a chain: शृङ्कां शृङ्कलाम्. This gloss is his whole comment on 1.2.1–3.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara and Raṅgarāmānuja read the sṛṅkā as a road of wealth; Madhva as a chain. The moral is the same: wealth is something one can sink in.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • स त्वं sa tvaṃ → सः + त्वम्

    saḥ before a consonant

    saḥ drops its visarga before a consonant, P. 6.1.132 (एतत्तदोः सुलोपोऽकोरनञ्समासे हलि); tvam before p becomes tvaṃ, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • प्रियरूपांश्च priyarūpāṃśca → प्रियरूपान् + च

    final n before c

    Final n before c becomes ru (later s, then ś beside the c), with anusvāra on the vowel: P. 8.3.7 (नश्छव्यप्रशान्).

  • कामानभिध्यायन्नचिकेतोऽत्यस्राक्षीः kāmānabhidhyāyannaciketo'tyasrākṣīḥ → कामान् + अभिध्यायन् + नचिकेतः + अत्यस्राक्षीः

    final n before a vowel; aḥ before a

    kāmān keeps its n before a. abhidhyāyan before a: n after a short vowel is doubled before a vowel, P. 8.3.32 (ङमो ह्रस्वादचि ङमुण्नित्यम्). naciketaḥ before a: P. 6.1.113 (अतो रोरप्लुतादप्लुते), P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः), P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति).

  • नैतां naitāṃ → न + एताम्

    vṛddhi

    a + e give ai, P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि); final m before a consonant becomes anusvāra, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • वित्तमयीमवाप्तो यस्यां vittamayīmavāpto yasyāṃ → वित्तमयीम् + अवाप्तः + यस्याम्

    final m before a vowel; aḥ before a voiced consonant

    vittamayīm keeps its m before a. avāptaḥ before y: P. 6.1.114 (हशि च), P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • बहवो मनुष्याः bahavo manuṣyāḥ → बहवः + मनुष्याः

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    P. 6.1.114 (हशि च) with P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • प्रियरूपान् priyarūpān

    bahuvrīhi · प्रिय + रूप

    Vigraha प्रियं रूपं येषां ते

    those whose form is pleasing

Morphology

अत्यस्राक्षीः
ati-sṛj ‘let go’, s-aorist second singular. The root takes the augment a after its vowel, P. 6.1.58 (सृजिदृशोर्झल्यमकिति), giving sraj-, lengthened in the aorist and with j > k before s: a-srāk-ṣ-īḥ.
वित्तमयीम्
vitta ‘wealth’ with the suffix -maya, feminine in -ī. Śaṅkara’s gloss धनप्रायाम्, ‘mostly wealth’, takes -maya in the sense ‘abounding in’ (P. 5.4.21 (तत्प्रकृतवचने मयट्)).
अवाप्तः
A past participle used with an accusative object, as a past active verb with asi understood: ‘you have not taken this’. Śaṅkara renders it so: न एताम् अवाप्तवानसि.
मज्जन्ति
Present of masj ‘sink’ (the root is given as masj; its s becomes j beside the j: majj-).

Anvaya

नचिकेतः, सः त्वम् प्रियान् प्रियरूपान् च कामान् अभिध्यायन् अत्यस्राक्षीः । यस्याम् बहवः मनुष्याः मज्जन्ति, एताम् वित्तमयीम् सृङ्काम् न अवाप्तः [असि] ।

naciketaḥ, saḥ tvam priyān priyarūpān ca kāmān abhidhyāyan atyasrākṣīḥ | yasyām bahavaḥ manuṣyāḥ majjanti, etām vittamayīm sṛṅkām na avāptaḥ asi |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two sentences addressed to Naciketas. In the first, the participle abhidhyāyan gives the manner of the main verb atyasrākṣīḥ: he let the desires go while, or by, reflecting on them. In the second, the participle avāptaḥ stands for a finite verb with an accusative object; the relative clause yasyāṃ … manuṣyāḥ describes the sṛṅkā.

Ambiguities

What is the sṛṅkā?
At 1.1.16 Death gave Naciketas a sṛṅkā, which Śaṅkara took as a tinkling jewelled chain, सृङ्कां शब्दवतीं रत्नमयीं मालाम्, or else a road, सृङ्काम् अकुत्सितां गतिं कर्ममयीं. Here Śaṅkara reads a road, and a base one: सृतिं कुत्सितां मूढजनप्रवृत्तां; Raṅgarāmānuja likewise, सृंकाम् - कुत्सितगतिं मूढजनसेवितां. Madhva glosses the word as a chain, शृङ्कां शृङ्कलाम्. A chain ‘in which many sink’ keeps the link with the necklace Death has just given.
priyān and priyarūpān
Śaṅkara divides the desires into the dear, sons and the like, and the lovely in form, the celestial women of 1.1.25. Raṅgarāmānuja takes both words of the same objects: dear in themselves and in their form, स्वतोरूपतश्च प्रियान्.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Wealth and consumption

The image of a chain of wealth is easy to recognise: the larger house that needs a larger income, the possessions that need insuring, maintaining and defending. People sometimes describe the moment of seeing this as being able to breathe again. The verse praises not poverty but the clarity that can look at what is offered and decline what would hold one down. This is a reflection, not part of the commentaries.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Is there something you own that you sometimes feel owns you?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Naciketas let desires go by reflecting on them, not by refusing to look. What is the difference?

A contemplation exercise Take one thing you want and list honestly what it would cost to keep it, not only to get it.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
