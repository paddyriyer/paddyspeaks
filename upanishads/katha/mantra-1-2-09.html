Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 9 of 25

Not by reasoning

This understanding is not reached by reasoning but by being taught by another; Death praises Naciketas’ true resolve and wishes for questioners like him.

5 Faithful English translation

‘This understanding is not to be attained by reasoning; taught by another, dearest, it is for good knowing. You have obtained it; ah, you are one whose resolve is true. May there be for us, Naciketas, a questioner like you.’

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘This understanding cannot be reached by argument. It is when another teaches it, my dear, that it is truly understood. You have found it — and how steadfast you are! May we always have a questioner like you, Naciketas.’

Key concepts तर्क tarka, reasoning आचार्य ācārya, teacher ज्ञान knowledge सत्य truth, the real मृत्यु death

7 Explain it to a beginner

Death finishes his praise of the boy before beginning the teaching itself. The understanding Naciketas is seeking cannot be reached by argument alone; it becomes clear when someone who knows it teaches it. Naciketas has already taken hold of it, in the sense that he has fixed his mind on it and refused every distraction. Death calls him “dearest” and says, with real warmth, that his resolve is true. Then he says something surprising for the god of death: he wishes he always had pupils like this one. Teachers know the feeling. A good question is a gift to the person who is asked it, and the rare student who keeps asking it, steadily, is what makes teaching worthwhile.

An analogy Like an old teacher who, after years of indifferent pupils, meets one who will not stop asking the real question, and says, ‘I wish all my students were like you.’

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does Death say?

That this understanding is not to be had by reasoning but is well understood when taught by another; that Naciketas has obtained it and is steadfast; and that Death wishes for questioners like him.

Traditional Interpretation

Why not by reasoning?

Śaṅkara: the understanding that arises from scripture, येयमागमप्रभवा मतिः, cannot be reached by one’s own conjecture alone, स्वबुद्ध्यभ्यूहमात्रेण; it is understood when taught by a teacher who knows scripture. In his Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya (2.1.6) he quotes this line to show that Brahman is known from scripture alone, like dharma: आगममात्रसमधिगम्य एव त्वयमर्थो धर्मवत्.

Traditional Interpretation

In what sense has Naciketas ‘obtained’ it already?

Śaṅkara: through the boon Death granted, मद्वरप्रदानेन. Raṅgarāmānuja: he has resolved to attain it, सिषाधयिषिततया निश्चितवानित्यर्थः; his resolve cannot be shaken, सत्या - अप्रकंप्या धृतिः यस्य स तथोक्तः.

Comparative Vedānta

Who is the ‘other’ teacher?

For Śaṅkara, a teacher learned in scripture as opposed to the logician; for Raṅgarāmānuja, a teacher other than oneself; for Madhva, commenting at 1.2.8, the one who knows the Lord as other than the self, the ‘other’ who completes the sense of ananya there: प्रोक्ताऽन्येनैव सुज्ञानाय प्रेष्ठ इति वाक्यशेषात्.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.2.9

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The knowledge of the self comes from scripture through a teacher, and reasoning alone can neither reach it nor overturn it: अत एव च येयमागमप्रभूता मतिः अन्येनैव आगमाभिज्ञेनाचार्येणैव तार्किकात्, प्रोक्ता सती सुज्ञानाय भवति.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.2.9

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Even one skilled in reasoning cannot know it by himself; taught by a teacher other than oneself it becomes the means of release: उपदिष्टैव मति: - मोक्षसाधनाय भवति.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya on 1.2.4–8

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Madhva uses this line as the completion of 1.2.8: the knowledge comes only from ‘another’, one who knows the Lord as other than the self, प्रोक्ताऽन्येनैव सुज्ञानाय प्रेष्ठ इति वाक्यशेषात्. His comment filed with 1.2.9–10 concerns 1.2.10.

Where they disagree

All three make a teacher necessary. They differ on who he is: one who knows scripture rather than the logician (Śaṅkara), a realised teacher other than oneself (Raṅgarāmānuja), or one who knows the difference of the Lord and the self (Madhva).

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • नैषा naiṣā → न + एषा

    vṛddhi

    a + e give ai, P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि).

  • मतिरापनेया matirāpaneyā → मतिः + आपनेया

    visarga as r

    matiḥ (from mati + s, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः)) keeps its r before a vowel.

  • प्रोक्तान्येनैव proktānyenaiva → प्रोक्ता + अन्येन + एव

    savarṇa-dīrgha; vṛddhi

    proktā + anyena: ā + a give ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः). anyena + eva: a + e give ai, P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि).

  • यां त्वमापः yāṃ tvamāpaḥ → याम् + त्वम् + आपः

    final m before a consonant and before a vowel

    yām before t: P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः). tvam keeps its m before ā.

  • सत्यधृतिर्बतासि satyadhṛtirbatāsi → सत्यधृतिः + बत + असि

    visarga as r; savarṇa-dīrgha

    satyadhṛtiḥ before b shows r. bata + asi: P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • त्वादृङ् नो भूयान्नचिकेतः tvādṛṅ no bhūyānnaciketaḥ → त्वादृक् + नः + भूयात् + नचिकेतः

    stop before a nasal; aḥ before a voiced consonant

    tvādṛk before n becomes the nasal ṅ, P. 8.4.45 (यरोऽनुनासिकेऽनुनासिको वा). naḥ before bh: P. 6.1.114 (हशि च), P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः). bhūyāt before n becomes bhūyān, by the same P. 8.4.45 (यरोऽनुनासिकेऽनुनासिको वा).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • सत्यधृतिः satyadhṛtiḥ

    bahuvrīhi · सत्य + धृति

    Vigraha सत्या धृतिः यस्य सः

  • सुज्ञानाय sujñānāya

    prādi-tatpuruṣa · सु + ज्ञान

    good, thorough understanding

Morphology

आपनेया
A gerundive in -eya whose root Śaṅkara leaves open. He glosses it first as ‘to be attained’, नापनीया न प्रापणीयेत्यर्थः, and then as ‘to be removed, destroyed’, नापनेतव्या वा ; नोपहन्तव्या, which takes it from apa-nī ‘lead away’. Raṅgarāmānuja has only the first: तर्केण प्रापणीया न इत्यर्थः.
आपः
Aorist second singular of āp ‘obtain’: a root marked with ḷ takes the thematic aorist, P. 3.1.55 (पुषादिद्युताद्यॢदितः परस्मैपदेषु); a + āp gives āp-: āpaḥ, ‘you have obtained’.
त्वादृक्
tvādṛś ‘like you’: dṛś after a pronoun, P. 3.2.60 (त्यदादिषु दृशोऽनालोचने कञ् च), with the pronoun’s final ā, P. 6.3.91 (आ सर्वनाम्नः); the final consonant becomes k, P. 8.2.62 (क्विन्प्रत्ययस्य कुः).
भूयात्
Precative (a wish) of bhū, P. 3.4.116 (लिङाशिषि): ‘may there be’.
प्रष्टा
praṣṭṛ ‘one who asks’, an agent noun of prach ‘ask’, P. 3.1.133 (ण्वुल्तृचौ). GRETIL’s प्रेष्टा is a copying error that repeats preṣṭha of the line before (corrections.json).
प्रेष्ठ
Superlative of priya ‘dear’, with priya replaced by pra- (the same rule as preyas in 1.2.1): ‘dearest’.

Anvaya

प्रेष्ठ, एषा मतिः तर्केण न आपनेया; अन्येन एव प्रोक्ता [सती] सुज्ञानाय [भवति] । याम् त्वम् आपः, [त्वम्] बत सत्यधृतिः असि । नचिकेतः, त्वादृक् प्रष्टा नः भूयात् ।

preṣṭha, eṣā matiḥ tarkeṇa na āpaneyā; anyena eva proktā satī sujñānāya bhavati | yām tvam āpaḥ, tvam bata satyadhṛtiḥ asi | naciketaḥ, tvādṛk praṣṭā naḥ bhūyāt |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two statements about the understanding, one negative (not by reasoning) and one positive (taught by another, it serves good knowing), with a vocative between them; a relative clause (yāṃ tvam āpaḥ) links them to Naciketas, followed by an exclamation of praise (satyadhṛtir batāsi) and a wish in the precative (tvādṛṅ no bhūyāt … praṣṭā).

Ambiguities

Who is the ‘other’ teacher?
Śaṅkara: another than the logician, a teacher who knows scripture, अन्येनैव आगमाभिज्ञेनाचार्येणैव तार्किकात्. Raṅgarāmānuja: a teacher other than oneself, स्वस्मादन्येनैव गुरुणा. Madhva cites this line to show that in 1.2.8 the ‘non-other’ who cannot teach is the one who does not see the Lord as other: प्रोक्ताऽन्येनैव सुज्ञानाय प्रेष्ठ इति वाक्यशेषात्.
āpaneyā: ‘to be attained’ or ‘to be removed’
Śaṅkara gives both: the understanding cannot be reached by reasoning, and it cannot be taken away or destroyed by it, नापनेतव्या वा ; नोपहन्तव्या. The second sense makes the verse a defence of scriptural knowledge against the logician, whom Śaṅkara describes as one who imagines whatever his own mind constructs: तार्किको ह्यनागमज्ञः स्वबुद्धिपरिकल्पितं यत्किञ्चिदेव कल्पयति.
‘May there be for us a questioner like you’
Śaṅkara: another son or pupil, भवत्वन्यः पुत्रः शिष्यो वा प्रष्टा; Death says it in compassion, to praise the knowledge he is about to give, अनुकम्पयन्नाह मृत्युर्नचिकेतसं वक्ष्यमाणविज्ञानस्तुतये. Raṅgarāmānuja: त्वादृशः शिष्यः अस्माकं भूयादित्यर्थः.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Learning and apprenticeship

Some understanding is passed on rather than worked out: a craft, a language, a way of seeing. A student who insists on deriving everything alone can become very clever and still miss it. The verse is not against thinking — Naciketas thinks hard — but it says that this kind of understanding arrives through a relationship. And it ends with the teacher’s gratitude for the student. This is a reflection, not part of the commentaries.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Is there something you understood only when someone showed you, after failing to work it out alone?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry What does it mean that the god of death wishes for more students like this boy?

A contemplation exercise Ask one real question this week of someone who knows more than you, and listen without arguing.
