Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 6 of 18

The separate arising of the senses

Knowing that the senses are separate, arising separately, and that rising and setting are theirs, the wise one does not grieve.

5 Faithful English translation

The separateness of the senses, and the rising and setting which [are theirs], of [the senses] arising separately, having understood, the wise one does not grieve.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Seeing that the senses are separate from the self, that they arise on their own, and that their rising and setting belong to them, the wise one does not grieve.

Key concepts इन्द्रिय indriya, a sense आत्मन् self, Self धीर the wise शोक grief, sorrow

7 Explain it to a beginner

Why does a wise person not grieve? This verse gives one reason. Our senses, seeing, hearing, touching and the rest, are distinct from the self. They come into being on their own, and they switch on and off: they are busy when we are awake and quiet when we sleep. Their rising and setting belong to them, not to the self that knows them. Śaṅkara says the self is unchanging and of one nature, so there is nothing in it that could be a cause of grief; he quotes another Upaniṣad, ‘the one who knows the self crosses beyond sorrow’. Raṅgarāmānuja makes the same point: the changes of the senses and the body do not belong to the self, whose nature is knowing. Understanding this difference is what frees one from grief.

An analogy The lamps in a house go on and off; the one who lives there is not switched off with them.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

Understanding that the senses are separate, arise separately, and have their own rising and setting, the wise one does not grieve.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the changes are not the self’s

The senses arise from their own causes, ākāśa and the rest, for grasping their objects, and are of a nature different from the self, which is pure consciousness alone. Their rising and setting, in waking and sleep, are theirs and not the self’s; knowing this by discrimination the wise one does not grieve, since the self is always of one nature and nothing in it can cause grief: आत्मनो नित्यैकस्वभावत्वाव्यभिचाराच्छोककारणत्वानुपपत्तेः. He quotes the Chāndogya: तरति शोकमात्मवित्.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: not in the self that is knowledge

‘Senses’ stands also for the body and the rest, देहादीनामपि उपलक्षणम्; yat is an indeclinable meaning yau, यत् इत्यव्ययं यावित्यर्थे. Their mutual difference, origin and destruction are not found in the self, whose single form is knowledge; knowing this one does not grieve: परस्परवैलक्षण्योत्पादविनाशाः, ज्ञानैकाकारे आत्मनि न सन्ति इति ज्ञात्वा न शोचति इत्यर्थः.

Comparative Vedānta

The wise one does not grieve

The close, matvā dhīro na śocati, ends 1.2.22 and 2.1.4 as well, महान्तं विभुमात्मानं मत्वा धीरो न शोचति. Śaṅkara’s quotation is from the Chāndogya, where Nārada says he has heard that the knower of the self crosses sorrow: भगवद्दृशेभ्यस्तरति शोकमात्मविदिति.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.3.6

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The senses and their changes are not the self, which is pure, single consciousness; knowing this one has no ground for grief: आत्मनो नित्यैकस्वभावत्वाव्यभिचाराच्छोककारणत्वानुपपत्तेः.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.3.6

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The differences, origin and destruction of the senses and body are not in the self, whose form is knowledge alone: ज्ञानैकाकारे आत्मनि न सन्ति इति ज्ञात्वा न शोचति इत्यर्थः.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara and Raṅgarāmānuja agree that the senses and their changes are distinct from the self and that seeing this ends grief; Śaṅkara speaks of the self as pure consciousness, Raṅgarāmānuja of the individual self whose form is knowledge. Madhva’s bhāṣya has no comment on this mantra.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • इन्द्रियाणां पृथग्भावमुदयास्तमयौ indriyāṇāṃ pṛthagbhāvamudayāstamayau → इन्द्रियाणाम् + पृथग्भावम् + उदयास्तमयौ

    m before a consonant; m before a vowel

    Final m becomes anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः); before a vowel it stays and is joined to it.

  • पृथगुत्पद्यमानानां pṛthagutpadyamānānāṃ → पृथक् + उत्पद्यमानानाम्

    k before a vowel; m before a consonant

    A final k becomes g before a vowel, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते); final m before a consonant becomes anusvāra, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • धीरो न dhīro na → धीरः + न

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • पृथग्भावम् pṛthagbhāvam

    tatpuruṣa (with the indeclinable pṛthak) · पृथक् + भाव

    Vigraha पृथक् भावः

    being apart, separateness

  • उदयास्तमयौ udayāstamayau

    itaretara-dvandva · उदय + अस्तमय

    Vigraha उदयः च अस्तमयः च

    rising and setting

Morphology

उत्पद्यमानानाम्
ud + √pad, present participle middle (śānac), genitive plural: ‘of [the senses] that arise’. It agrees with indriyāṇām in the first line.
यत्
yat may be taken as the neuter relative or, with Raṅgarāmānuja, as an indeclinable standing for the dual yau: ‘the rising and setting which [are theirs]’.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

One sentence with the absolutive matvā governing two objects, pṛthagbhāvam and udayāstamayau, both depending on the genitive indriyāṇām, which is picked up in the second line by pṛthag utpadyamānānām. The main clause is dhīro na śocati.

Ambiguities

Separate from what?
Śaṅkara: separate from the self, pure consciousness, and arising from their own causes, ākāśa and the rest; their rising and setting in waking and sleep belong to them, not the self, नात्मन इति मत्वा. Raṅgarāmānuja: the senses (standing for the body and the rest) differ from one another and arise and perish, and these things are not in the self, whose single form is knowledge, ज्ञानैकाकारे आत्मनि न सन्ति इति ज्ञात्वा न शोचति इत्यर्थः.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16

Regular: 16 and 16 syllables.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Science and wonder

Sleep researchers describe the senses going quiet and returning each night. The verse asks who it is that is there both times. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What is still there when your eyes are closed and the room is quiet?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Is grief always about something that rises and sets?

A contemplation exercise Before sleep tonight, notice the senses going quiet one by one.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
