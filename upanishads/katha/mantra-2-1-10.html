What is here is there

What is here is there, and what is there is here; from death to death goes the one who sees as if there were difference here.

5 Faithful English translation

What indeed is here, that [is] there; what [is] there, that likewise [is] here. From death to death he goes who sees here as if [there were] difference.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

What is here is there; what is there is also here. Whoever sees anything like difference here goes from death to death.

Key concepts नाना nānā, diversity ब्रह्मन् Brahman आत्मन् self, Self एकत्व oneness मृत्यु death अविद्या avidyā

7 Explain it to a beginner

Here Death says something that sounds simple but runs very deep. What we meet here, in this body and this world, is the same reality that exists ‘there’, beyond. Whoever sees it as split into different pieces goes from one death to another, round and round. The three great commentators read this differently, and this is one of the verses where they part. Śaṅkara says the self in us and Brahman are one, and seeing them as two is the mistake that keeps us in the cycle of birth and death. Raṅgarāmānuja says the one supreme Self is the inner self of beings here and in every other world, so it is wrong to think of a different self in each place. Madhva says the verse warns against imagining any difference between God's forms, or between God and his qualities.

An analogy Like the same sky seen through a window here and through another window far away: it is not two skies.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

What is here is there, and what is there is here; whoever sees difference, as it were, here goes from death to death.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the transmigrator is Brahman

The verse is spoken so that no one should think that what appears in all beings is a transmigrator other than the supreme Brahman, संसार्यन्यत्परस्माद्ब्रह्मण इति मा भूत्कस्यचिदाशङ्केतीदमाह. What appears here with adjuncts is, there, Brahman of the nature of pure consciousness, नित्यविज्ञानघनस्वभावं सर्वसंसारधर्मवर्जितं ब्रह्म. Deluded by ignorance, one thinks परस्मादन्योऽहं मत्तोऽन्यत्परं ब्रह्मेति and so goes through birth and death again and again. The sense: one should see ब्रह्मैवाहमस्मीति.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: one self of all, here and elsewhere

He raises the objection that the self is known as ‘I’, located here and not elsewhere, 'अहमिहैवास्मि' इति देशान्तरव्यावृत्ततया अनुसन्धीयते, so how can it be the self of all? The answer: the supreme Self that is the self here is also the self of those in other worlds, लोकान्तरस्थानामपि आत्मभूतम्, so ततश्च आत्मभेदो नास्ति इत्यर्थः. The knowers experience themselves as present in all things, citing the Ṛgveda, अहं मनुरभवं सूर्यश्च. Whoever sees difference in the supreme Self, परमात्मनि भेदमिव, goes संसारात् संसारं.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: no difference within the Lord

Madhva's comment cites verses (no source is named, इति च) saying that Viṣṇu in his manifestations and in bodies is the same as his original form, स एव मूलरूपश्च साक्षान्नारायणाभिधः, and that whoever sees any distinction between them, in quality or in nature, गुणतस्स्वरूपतो वापि विशेषं योऽत्र पश्यति, goes to blind darkness — as do those who hold difference-in-identity, भेदाभेदविदश्चात्र तमो यान्ति न संशयः. In his comment on 2.1.11 he adds that this first ‘ya iha nāneva paśyati’ denies a difference of nature in the Lord: प्रथमा य इह नानेव पश्यति इति स्वरूपभेदनिषेधार्थम्.

Comparative Vedānta

The Bṛhadāraṇyaka has the same line

The second line, and the line that follows in 2.1.11, are found word for word in the Bṛhadāraṇyaka: मनसैवानुद्रष्टव्यं नेह नानास्ति किञ्चन । मृत्योः स मृत्युमाप्नोति य इह नानेव पश्यति.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.1.10

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The self that appears as a transmigrator is Brahman; seeing oneself as other than Brahman, परस्मादन्योऽहं, is the ignorance that brings death after death.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.1.10

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The supreme Self is the one inner self of beings in every place and world, ततश्च आत्मभेदो नास्ति इत्यर्थः; the error is to suppose a difference in the supreme Self, परमात्मनि भेदमिव.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.1.10–11

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The difference denied is within the Lord — between his original form and his manifestations, स एव मूलरूपश्च साक्षान्नारायणाभिधः — and the doctrine of difference-in-identity is condemned with it.

Where they disagree

The same words, three denials: of any difference between self and Brahman (Śaṅkara); of different selves in different places, the supreme Self being the one inner self of all (Raṅgarāmānuja); of any difference within the Lord and his forms (Madhva). Each reading is the commentator's; the verse itself names only ‘here’, ‘there’ and ‘as if different’.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • यदेवेह yadeveha → यत् + एव + इह

    t before a vowel; a + i

    A final t becomes d before a vowel, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते); a + i give e, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • तदन्विह tadanviha → तत् + अनु + इह

    u before a vowel

    u becomes v before a dissimilar vowel, P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि).

  • य इह ya iha → यः + इह

    aḥ before a vowel other than a

    The visarga of -aḥ is lost before a vowel other than a, and the vowels then stay apart: ya iha.

  • नानेव nāneva → नाना + इव

    ā + i

    ā + i give e, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

अमुत्र
A locative adverb from the pronoun adas, ‘yonder’: ‘in that place, there’; set against iha, ‘here’.
पश्यति
paśyati serves as the present of √dṛś, ‘see’, which forms no present of its own.
इव
‘As if, like’. Śaṅkara takes nāneva as ‘as though different’ (the difference is only apparent). Madhva, in his comment on 2.1.11, reads iva as marking even the slightest difference, and difference-in-identity: अतो नानेव इतीवशब्दोऽत्यल्पविषयो भेदाभेदविषयश्च.

Anvaya

यत् एव इह [अस्ति] तत् अमुत्र, यत् अमुत्र तत् अनु इह । यः इह नाना इव पश्यति, सः मृत्योः मृत्युम् आप्नोति ।

yat eva iha asti tat amutra, yat amutra tat anu iha | yaḥ iha nānā iva paśyati, saḥ mṛtyoḥ mṛtyum āpnoti |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two balanced relative–correlative pairs without a verb (yad … tat, yad … tat) state the identity of ‘here’ and ‘there’. The second line is a relative clause (yaḥ … paśyati) with its correlative saḥ; mṛtyoḥ (ablative) and mṛtyum (accusative) frame āpnoti, ‘reaches death from death’.

Ambiguities

What is ‘here’ and what is ‘there’?
Śaṅkara: what appears here in body and organs as a transmigrator is the same Brahman, free of all its properties, that is there. Raṅgarāmānuja: the supreme Self that is the self here is the self of those in other worlds too, लोकान्तरस्थानामपि आत्मभूतम्. Madhva: the Lord in his manifestations is the same as his original form.
Whose ‘difference’ is denied?
For Śaṅkara, any difference between the self and Brahman; for Raṅgarāmānuja, a difference within the supreme Self (one self here, another there), परमात्मनि भेदमिव; for Madhva, a difference between the Lord's forms, or between him and his qualities, however slight — not the difference of the Lord from souls. This is where the three schools part.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 17

Counted as written, 16 and 17 syllables: the last pāda, ya iha nāneva paśyati, has nine, because ya and iha stand apart after the lost visarga and are counted as two syllables. The Bṛhadāraṇyaka (4.4.19) has the same pāda.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Psychology and inner life

Much unease comes from a sense of being cut off — from others, from the world, from a larger whole. Whatever one makes of the metaphysics, the verse ties suffering to a way of seeing rather than to the world itself. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Where in your life do you feel ‘here’ and ‘there’ are worlds apart?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Śaṅkara, Raṅgarāmānuja and Madhva each deny a difference in this verse. Which difference does each deny, and which does each keep?

A contemplation exercise Today, when you catch yourself thinking ‘that has nothing to do with me’, pause and ask what connects you to it.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
