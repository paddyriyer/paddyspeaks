Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 8 of 15

The fire in the fire-sticks

Jātavedas, placed in the two fire-sticks, well carried like an embryo by pregnant women, praised day by day by wakeful people bearing offerings — Agni: this is that.

5 Faithful English translation

‘Jātavedas, placed in the two fire-sticks, well carried like an embryo by pregnant women, to be praised day after day by wakeful people with oblations — Agni: this, truly, is that.’

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘The fire hidden in the two fire-sticks, carefully kept like a child in the womb, to be praised every day by watchful people who bring offerings: this fire is that.’

Key concepts अरणि araṇi, the fire-stick अग्नि Agni, fire ब्रह्मन् Brahman उपासना worship, meditation आचार्य ācārya, teacher

7 Explain it to a beginner

In the old Vedic ritual, fire was not lit with matches; it was drilled out of two wooden sticks rubbed together. Before the drilling, the fire was said to be hidden in the wood, like a child carried safely in its mother's womb. Every day, attentive people who brought offerings praised this fire. The verse takes that familiar picture and adds its refrain: this is that. Śaṅkara reads it on two levels at once. Outwardly it is the sacrificial fire; inwardly it is the light within the heart that people who meditate keep alive with their attention, as the priests keep the fire alive with ghee. Raṅgarāmānuja reads it as the ritual fire, which has Brahman as its inner self. Madhva, through a verse he cites, hears in the two fire-sticks the teacher and the pupil, between whom knowledge is kindled.

An analogy A spark that is already in the flint before anyone strikes it; striking only lets it show.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

Agni, hidden in the fire-sticks, kept safe like a child in the womb, praised every day by watchful people with offerings — this is that.

Traditional Interpretation

Two fires

Śaṅkara: Agni lies in the upper and lower fire-sticks in the sacrifice, and within, in the heart, for the yogins, अध्यात्मं च योगिभिः; he is cared for by priests and yogins as an embryo is by a mother who eats wholesome food; praised daily by ritualists and yogins ‘in the sacrifice and in the heart’, कर्मिभिर्योगिभिश्चाध्वरे हृदये च. That fire is the Brahman under discussion, तदेव प्रकृतं ब्रह्म.

Comparative Vedānta

Raṅgarāmānuja

He takes the fire as the ritual fire and Agni as ‘the leader’, अग्ननेता; ‘this is that’ means this fire has as its self the Brahman spoken of before, पूर्वोक्तब्रह्मात्मकम् इत्यर्थः.

Comparative Vedānta

Madhva: teacher and pupil

Madhva cites a verse (with इति च, no source named): the all-knowing Viṣṇu, highest of persons, is praised daily in the ‘fire-sticks’ of teacher and pupil, सर्वज्ञो भगवान् विष्णुररण्योर्गुरुशिष्ययोः.

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Viṣṇu is the one praised between teacher and pupil, सर्वज्ञो भगवान् विष्णुररण्योर्गुरुशिष्ययोः, in a verse he cites.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara reads the fire in both the sacrifice and the heart as Brahman itself; Raṅgarāmānuja reads it as a fire whose self is Brahman; Madhva, through a cited verse, reads the fire-sticks as teacher and pupil and the fire as Viṣṇu.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • अरण्योर्निहितो जातवेदा araṇyornihito jātavedā → अरण्योः + निहितः + जातवेदाः

    visarga → r; aḥ → o; āḥ before a voiced sound

    aranyoḥ → aranyor before a voiced sound, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः); nihitaḥ → nihito, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च) with P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः); after ā the ru becomes y, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), which is dropped before a consonant, P. 8.3.22 (हलि सर्वेषाम्).

  • गर्भ इव garbha iva → गर्भः + इव

    visarga lost before a vowel

    After a, ru becomes y before a vowel, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), and the y is dropped, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य).

  • दिवे दिव ईड्यो dive diva īḍyo → दिवे + दिवे + ईड्यः

    e before a vowel

    e becomes ay before a vowel, P. 6.1.78 (एचोऽयवायावः), and the y is dropped, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य), leaving diva īḍyaḥ.

  • जागृवद्भिर्हविष्मद्भिर्मनुष्येभिरग्निः jāgṛvadbhirhaviṣmadbhirmanuṣyebhiragniḥ → जागृवद्भिः + हविष्मद्भिः + मनुष्येभिः + अग्निः

    visarga → r

    The s of -bhiḥ becomes ru, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः), heard as r before a voiced sound.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • सुभृतः subhṛtaḥ

    prādi-tatpuruṣa · सु + भृत

    Vigraha सुष्ठु भृतः

    well carried

Morphology

जातवेदाः
Nominative of jātavedas, an ancient name of Agni; the commentators here gloss it simply as fire.
दिवे
dive dive, ‘day after day’, a Vedic repetition of the dative of div ‘day’; the second dive becomes diva before īḍyaḥ.
जागृवद्भिः
Instrumental plural of the perfect participle of jāgṛ ‘be awake’ (jāgṛvas), ‘those who have woken, who stay awake’.
मनुष्येभिः
Vedic instrumental plural in -ebhiḥ for manuṣyaiḥ.
अरण्योः
Locative dual of araṇi, the fire-stick; fire is drilled from the lower and upper stick. Madhva gives a derivation, अर्यते ण आभ्यामित्यरणी.

Anvaya

जातवेदाः अरण्योः निहितः, गर्भिणीभिः गर्भः इव सुभृतः, दिवे दिवे जागृवद्भिः हविष्मद्भिः मनुष्येभिः ईड्यः अग्निः [अस्ति] । एतत् वै तत् ॥

jātavedāḥ araṇyoḥ nihitaḥ, garbhiṇībhiḥ garbhaḥ iva subhṛtaḥ, dive dive jāgṛvadbhiḥ haviṣmadbhiḥ manuṣyebhiḥ īḍyaḥ agniḥ asti | etat vai tat ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A nominal sentence: jātavedāḥ with three predicates — araṇyor nihitaḥ, garbha iva subhṛto garbhiṇībhiḥ, and dive diva īḍyaḥ with three instrumental agents — resumed by agniḥ at the end. The refrain identifies it with ‘that’.

Ambiguities

Which fire?
Śaṅkara reads two levels: in the sacrifice, the fire in the upper and lower fire-sticks, अधियज्ञम्; within, the fire kept by yogins in the heart, अध्यात्मं च योगिभिः — ‘oblations’ are both ghee and meditation, आज्यादिमद्भिः ध्यानभावनावद्भिश्च. Either way, ‘this is that’: तदेव प्रकृतं ब्रह्म. Raṅgarāmānuja: the ritual fire, whose self is Brahman, पूर्वोक्तब्रह्मात्मकम् इत्यर्थः. Madhva, in a verse he cites: Viṣṇu, the all-knowing, in the ‘fire-sticks’ of teacher and pupil, सर्वज्ञो भगवान् विष्णुररण्योर्गुरुशिष्ययोः.
garbha iva, or ivet?
Raṅgarāmānuja comments on a particle it, ‘just so’, इत् इत्यवधारणे — the mūla printed with him reads garbha ivet. None of our witnesses has it.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 21 + 20 + 4

21 and 20 syllables: the pādas have 10, 11, 10 and 10 (araṇyor nihito jātavedā, garbha iva subhṛto garbhiṇībhiḥ, dive diva īḍyo jāgṛvadbhir, haviṣmadbhir manuṣyebhir agniḥ), against the eleven of the triṣṭubh; then the refrain etad vai tat, four syllables.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Attention

A fire in wood has to be drawn out and then kept; the verse’s ‘wakeful’ people are the ones who keep it. Whatever one tends daily with care is, in that sense, kept alive. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What do you keep alive by tending it every day?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why might the Upaniṣad describe the self with an image taken from the ritual fire?

A contemplation exercise Choose one thing to attend to daily for a week, at the same time each day, and notice what changes.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
