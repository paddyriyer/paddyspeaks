Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 23 of 29

Death’s first offer: sons, herds, land and life

Death tempts the boy away from his question with everything a householder could want: long-lived sons and grandsons, cattle, elephants, gold and horses, a great realm, and as many years as he likes.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Choose sons and grandsons who will live a hundred years, cattle in plenty, elephants, gold and horses. Choose a great kingdom on the earth, and live yourself for as many years as you like.

Key concepts मृत्यु death भोग enjoyment, use लोक world, realm वर boon

7 Explain it to a beginner

Death now changes tactics. Instead of arguing, he tempts. Ask for children and grandchildren who will each live a hundred years, he says; ask for herds of cattle, elephants, gold and horses; ask for a great kingdom; and live yourself as long as you want. Everything an ordinary person might dream of is laid in front of the boy. Śaṅkara notes the logic of the last offer: all the rest would be worthless if the boy himself did not live to enjoy it, so Death adds long life too. The commentators agree that this is still the test begun in the last verse: will Naciketas trade his question for comfort?

An analogy Like a recruiter who, when a candidate asks the one question that matters, slides a bigger salary across the table instead of answering.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does Death offer?

Long-lived sons and grandsons, many cattle, elephants and gold, horses, a great domain on the earth, and as many years of life as the boy wants.

Traditional Interpretation

Why these gifts?

Śaṅkara: having been answered, Death tempts him again — एवमुक्तोऽपि पुनः प्रलोभयन्नुवाच मृत्युः. Long life is added because the rest would be useless to a short-lived man: सर्वमप्येतदनर्थकं स्वयं चेदल्पायुरित्यत आह.

Comparative Vedānta

Is the temptation in earnest?

Raṅgarāmānuja: the knowledge of the freed self is not to be given to one whose mind is held by other things — विषयान्तरासक्तचेतसे एतादृशं मुक्तात्मतत्त्वं नोपदेशार्हम् — so Death tempts him to make his desire for release firm.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • भूमेर्महदायतनं bhūmermahadāyatanaṃ → भूमेः + महत् + आयतनम्

    visarga as r; t voiced before a vowel

    bhūmeḥ before the voiced m shows r (the visarga of a non-a vowel). mahat before a vowel becomes mahad, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

  • शरदो यावदिच्छसि śarado yāvadicchasi → शरदः + यावत् + इच्छसि

    aḥ before a voiced consonant; t voiced

    śaradaḥ before y becomes śarado, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च) with guṇa, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः). yāvat before i becomes yāvad, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • शतायुषः śatāyuṣaḥ

    bahuvrīhi · शत + आयुस्

    Vigraha शतं वर्षाणि आयूंषि येषां तान्

    those whose lives are a hundred years; the vigraha is Śaṅkara’s

  • पुत्रपौत्रान् putrapautrān

    itaretara-dvandva · पुत्र + पौत्र

    Vigraha पुत्रान् च पौत्रान् च

    sons and grandsons

  • हस्तिहिरण्यम् hastihiraṇyam

    samāhāra-dvandva · हस्तिन् + हिरण्य

    Vigraha हस्ती च हिरण्यं च

    elephants and gold, taken as one collective (neuter singular); the vigraha is Śaṅkara’s

Morphology

शरदः
Accusative plural of śarad ‘autumn’, i.e. ‘years’; the accusative of extent of time.
वृणीष्व
Imperative middle of vṛ (9th class) ‘choose’; repeated in the next verse.

Anvaya

शतायुषः पुत्रपौत्रान् बहून् पशून् हस्तिहिरण्यम् अश्वान् [च] वृणीष्व । भूमेः महत् आयतनम् वृणीष्व । स्वयम् च यावत् शरदः इच्छसि [तावत्] जीव ।

śatāyuṣaḥ putrapautrān bahūn paśūn hastihiraṇyam aśvān ca vṛṇīṣva | bhūmeḥ mahat āyatanam vṛṇīṣva | svayam ca yāvat śaradaḥ icchasi tāvat jīva |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Three imperatives, each with its objects: a list for the first (persons, beasts, wealth), one object for the second, and for the third an accusative of time with a relative of measure (śarado yāvad icchasi).

Ambiguities

bhūmer mahad āyatanam
Śaṅkara: a wide domain on the earth, a kingdom — महत् विस्तीर्णम् आयतनम् आश्रयं मण्डलं साम्राज्यं. Raṅgarāmānuja gives that and an alternative: a great house on the earth, with halls and palaces — विचित्रशालाप्रासादादियुक्तं गृहं वृणीष्व.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 24

Triṣṭubh. The half-lines count 22 and 24: the last pāda (svayaṃ ca jīva śarado yāvad icchasi) has 13 syllables, two more than the norm, as the text stands in every witness.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading The vishvAsa mūla: शतायुषः पुत्रपौत्रान्वृणीष्वा बहून्पशून् हस्तिहिरण्यमश्वान् — The vishvAsa mūla lengthens vṛṇīṣva to vṛṇīṣvā, a Vedic lengthening of the final vowel; the others read vṛṇīṣva.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Wealth and consumption

The list is the ordinary measure of a successful life — family, assets, property, health and length of days. The verse asks what we would give up a question for, and suggests that the offer itself reveals what the question is worth. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin If someone offered you everything on this list to stop asking one question, would you stop?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why does Death include long life last, after everything else?

A contemplation exercise Write down the three things you would most like to have. Then ask what each one would leave unanswered.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
