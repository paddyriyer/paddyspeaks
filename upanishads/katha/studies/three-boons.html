Study II · Kaṭha Upaniṣad

The three boons

Mantras 1.1.10–1.1.20: peace with the father, the fire that leads to heaven and why it bears Naciketas’ name, and the question of what remains after death

Anuvākas 1.1.10, 1.1.11, 1.1.12, 1.1.13, 1.1.14, 1.1.15, 1.1.16, 1.1.17, 1.1.18, 1.1.19, 1.1.20

Naciketas asks first for his father’s good will, then for the fire that leads to heaven, and last for knowledge about what is left of a person after death. Death grants the first two at once, adds a fourth gift unasked, and hesitates over the third. The commentators agree about the first boon and divide over what ‘heaven’ means in the second and what exactly is asked in the third.

The first boon

Direct Textual Meaning

Naciketas asks that his father be calm and kind towards him: शान्तसङ्कल्पः सुमना यथा स्याद्वीतमन्युर्गौतमो माभिमृत्यो — ‘that Gautama be at peace in his thoughts, glad, free of anger towards me, O Death’ — and that, sent back by Death, he be recognised and welcomed: त्वत्प्रसृष्टं माभिवदेत्प्रतीत.

Death grants it in the next mantra: the father will sleep well again — सुखं रात्रीः शयिता — having seen his son freed from the mouth of death. Śaṅkara spells out the recognition the boy asks for: स एवायं पुत्रो ममागतः — ‘this is my very son who has come back’.

The second boon: a heaven without fear

Direct Textual Meaning

The boy describes the heaven he means: स्वर्गे लोके न भयं किञ्चनास्ति — ‘in the world of heaven there is no fear at all’; there is no Death and no old age there. Then he asks for the fire that leads to it, and says what it gives: स्वर्गलोका अमृतत्वं भजन्त — ‘those whose world is heaven share in immortality’.

Heaven, or release?

Comparative Vedānta

Śaṅkara reads svarga as the heaven reached by the fire-rite, and the fire as the means to it — स्वर्गसाधनम्. The highest fruit of the rite, done with its knowledge, is the station of Virāj: वैराजं पदं ज्ञानकर्मसमुच्चयानुष्ठानेन प्राप्नोतीत्यर्थः.

Raṅgarāmānuja holds that in this passage svarga means the place of release: स्वर्गशब्दः मोक्षस्थानपरः. His reason is that a boy who will spurn every passing reward could not be asking for a passing one, and that ‘heaven’ can name the highest happiness: स्वर्गशब्दस्य प्रकृष्टसुखवचनतया निरवधिकानन्दरूपमोक्षस्य स्वर्गशब्दवाच्यत्वसम्भवात्. He presents this as the teaching of the bhāṣyakāra of his school.

Madhva cites a verse that takes a middle course: the one who builds the fire meditating on Viṣṇu reaches heaven and, after dwelling there, attains immortality in due course — उष्य मन्वन्तरं कालममृतत्वं भजेत्क्रमात्. He names the source as the Brahmasāra; the citation is reported as his.

The fire hidden in the cave

Comparative Vedānta

Death calls the fire अनन्तलोकाप्तिमथो प्रतिष्ठां — ‘the attaining of an endless world, and the support’ — and tells the boy to know it as निहितं गुहायाम्, ‘placed in the cave’.

Śaṅkara: the support is the fire as the support of the world in the form of Virāj, and the cave is the mind of the wise — विदुषां बुद्धौ निविष्टमित्यर्थः. Raṅgarāmānuja reads ‘the endless world’ as Viṣṇu’s: अनन्तस्य – विष्णोः लोकः. Madhva’s commentary goes further and makes Hari himself the fire meant: लोको विष्णोरनन्तस्य तज्ज्ञानान्नित्य आप्यते — ‘by knowing him, the eternal world of the endless Viṣṇu is reached’.

A fourth gift: the name and the necklace

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Pleased that the boy repeated the instruction exactly, Death gives more than he promised: तवैव नाम्ना भवितायमग्निः — ‘this fire shall bear your name’ — and a sṛṅkā of many forms. Śaṅkara counts this a fourth boon, वरं तव चतुर्थम्, and gives two senses of sṛṅkā: a jewelled, sounding necklace — शब्दवतीं रत्नमयीं मालाम् — or a path of action — अकुत्सितां गतिं कर्ममयीं.

Three times the Naciketa fire

Comparative Vedānta

Mantra 1.1.17 praises the one who has built the Naciketa fire three times, joined with three, doing three works. The numbers are filled differently.

Śaṅkara: the three with whom one is joined are mother, father and teacher — मातृपित्राचार्यैः — and the three works are sacrifice, study and giving — इज्याध्ययनदानानां कर्ता. Raṅgarāmānuja reads ‘three times Naciketa’ as one who studies the three anuvākas of the rite — अनुवाकत्रयाध्यायी. Madhva gives the three works as sacrifice, giving and austerity — त्रिकर्मकृत् यज्ञदानतपःकर्ता — and supports it from the Gītā; ‘joined with three’ he takes as being in accord with the Vedas — त्रिभिरेत्य सन्धिं वेदैरविरुद्धः.

A mantra not in every manuscript

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

After 1.1.18 the text printed with Raṅgarāmānuja’s commentary has one more mantra, absent from the base text and the other witnesses of this edition: it speaks of the one who builds the Naciketa fire knowing the fire-altar as having Brahman’s son for its self — ब्रह्मजज्ञात्मभूतां चितिं विदित्वा चिनुते नाचिकेतम्.

Raṅgarāmānuja himself notes that it is not found in some manuscripts — अयं च मन्त्रः केषुचित्कोशेषु न दृष्टः — but defends it because trusted earlier teachers commented on it. His numbering therefore runs one ahead of this edition’s from here to the end of the vallī.

The third question

Direct Textual Meaning

Naciketas asks: येयं प्रेते विचिकित्सा मनुष्ये अस्तीत्येके नायमस्तीति चैके — ‘this doubt about a man who has gone — some say “he is”, some say “he is not”’. He wants to know it, taught by Death; this is his third boon.

What exactly is being asked

Comparative Vedānta

Śaṅkara reads the doubt as whether there is a self apart from body, senses, mind and intellect that passes to another body: अस्ति शरीरेन्द्रियमनोबुद्धिव्यतिरिक्तो देहान्तरसम्बन्ध्यात्मा इत्येके. He also says why the text turns here: the first two boons belong to rites and their fruits, and what follows teaches the knowledge of the oneness of self and Brahman — ब्रह्मात्मैकत्वविज्ञानं.

Raṅgarāmānuja, following his school, reads ‘gone’ not as dying but as released: न शरीरवियोगमात्राभिप्रायम् अपि तु सर्वबन्धविनिर्मोक्षाभिप्रायम् — the question is about the nature of the released self, over which the schools disagree.

Madhva glosses the words directly: ‘those who know say that in a man who has gone — that is, has been released — the Lord is present as his controller; the ignorant say he is not’ — प्रेते मुक्ते मनुष्ये नियामकत्वेन भगवानस्तीति ज्ञानिनो वदन्ति. The Gatisāra verse he cites earlier names the third boon in the same way: मुक्ते स्थितञ्च तं विष्णुम्.

So the same words carry three questions: is there a self beyond the body (Śaṅkara); what is the released self (Raṅgarāmānuja); and who governs the released (Madhva’s school).

Asking the right thing

Contemporary Reflection

The order of the boons is itself instructive: first a repaired relationship, then a rite and its reward, and only then the question that cannot be paid for. A modern reader may see in it a familiar order of concerns — peace at home, security, and last, if ever, what one’s life is for.

This is a reflection, not the commentators’ reading; they see in the order the movement from rites to knowledge.

Open questions

  • What svarga means in 1.1.12–1.1.13: the heaven of the fire-rite (Śaṅkara), the place of release (Raṅgarāmānuja), or heaven as a stage on the way to release (the verses Madhva cites).
  • The extra mantra after 1.1.18 in the text printed with Raṅgarāmānuja: its manuscript basis needs a printed critical edition.
  • Whether ‘prete’ in 1.1.20 means a man who has died or a self that has been released.
