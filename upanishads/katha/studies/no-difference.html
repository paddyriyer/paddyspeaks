Study XII · Kaṭha Upaniṣad

What is here is there

Mantras 2.1.10–2.1.15: from death to death goes the one who sees difference here; ‘there is no difference here at all’; the thumb-sized person; rain on the heights; pure water poured into pure water

Anuvākas 2.1.10, 2.1.11, 2.1.12, 2.1.13, 2.1.14, 2.1.15

The vallī ends with its sharpest statements. What is here is there; whoever sees as if there were difference here goes from death to death. There is no difference here at all. A person the size of a thumb, lord of past and future, stands in the middle of the self. Water rained on the heights runs off down the slopes; pure water poured into pure water becomes just such. The three commentators read these words in three incompatible ways, each from his own commentary: Śaṅkara as the denial of any difference between the self and Brahman; Raṅgarāmānuja as one supreme self of all, with the released self becoming like it; Madhva as a denial of difference within the Lord, with the released self like him and never the same. This study sets the readings side by side and does not decide between them.

The words

Direct Textual Meaning

यदेवेह तदमुत्र यदमुत्र तदन्विह — ‘what is here is there; what is there is here as well’ — मृत्योः स मृत्युमाप्नोति य इह नानेव पश्यति — ‘he goes from death to death who sees here as if there were difference’. 2.1.11 repeats the warning after a new line: मनसैवेदमाप्तव्यं नेह नानास्ति किञ्चन — ‘by the mind alone is this to be reached; there is no difference here at all’. The Bṛhadāraṇyaka (4.4.19) has the same half-verse.

Two images close the vallī. यथोदकं दुर्गे वृष्टं पर्वतेषु विधावति — ‘as water rained on rough ground runs off among the hills’ — एवं धर्मान्पृथक्पश्यंस्तानेवानुविधावति — ‘so one who sees dharmas as separate runs after them’. And: यथोदकं शुद्धे शुद्धमासिक्तं तादृगेव भवति — ‘as pure water poured into pure water becomes just such’ — एवं मुनेर्विजानत आत्मा भवति गौतम — ‘so becomes the self of the sage who knows, Gautama’.

Every key word is open: ‘as if’ (iva) in ‘as if different’, ‘dharmas’, and tādṛg eva, ‘just such’ — of the same kind, or the very same?

No difference between the self and Brahman (Śaṅkara)

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: what appears here, joined to body and senses and seeming to be in saṃsāra, is itself the Brahman ‘there’, eternal consciousness free of saṃsāra. The one who sees ‘I am other than the supreme, Brahman is other than me’ sees as if there were difference — परस्मादन्योऽहं मत्तोऽन्यत्परं ब्रह्मेति नानेव भिन्नमिव पश्यति — and is born and dies again and again. One should see instead: विज्ञानैकरसं नैरन्तर्येणाकाशवत्परिपूर्णं ब्रह्मैवाहमस्मीति पश्येदिति वाक्यार्थः.

At 2.1.11 the warning reaches the least difference: whoever does not give up the blindness of ignorance goes from death to death स्वल्पमपि भेदमध्यारोपयन्नित्यर्थः — ‘superimposing even a little difference’. The ‘dharmas’ of 2.1.14 are not different from the self — धर्मान् आत्मनोऽभिन्नान् — and seeing them separately in each body sends one after body upon body. At 2.1.15 pure water in pure water is one in taste and nothing else — एकरसमेव नान्यथा — and so is the self of the sage who knows oneness: एकत्वं विजानतो मुनेः. He ends the vallī by commending the vision of the one self taught by the Veda: आत्मैकत्वदर्शनं शान्तदर्पैरादरणीयमित्यर्थः.

One self of all; the released become like it (Raṅgarāmānuja)

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja begins from an objection: the supreme self cannot be the self of all, since each self knows itself as ‘I, here’ — परमात्मनः सर्वात्मत्वं न सम्भवति. His answer: the supreme self that is the self here, known as ‘I’, is the self of those in other worlds too — यदेव परमात्मतत्त्वम्, इह - अत्रलोके; अहम् इति अनुसन्धीयमानतया आत्मभूतम्, तदेव - लोकान्तरस्थानामपि आत्मभूतम् इत्यर्थः — so there is no difference of self: ततश्च आत्मभेदो नास्ति इत्यर्थः. The one who sees difference ‘here’ sees it in the supreme self, and goes from saṃsāra to saṃsāra: इह - परमात्मनि भेदमिव, यः पश्यति, स तु संसारात् संसारं प्राप्नोति इत्यर्थः.

The ‘dharmas’ of 2.1.14 are the supreme self’s being the inner controller of gods, of humans and the rest, seen as if they rested in separate substrates: परमात्मगतदेवान्तर्यामित्वमनुष्यान्तर्यामित्वादिधर्मान् पृथगधिकरण निष्ठान् पश्यन्. At 2.1.15 pure water added to pure water becomes like it, never unlike — तत्सदृशमेव भवति, न कथञ्चिदपि विसदृशम् — and so the knower’s self, purified, becomes equal to the pure supreme self: विशुद्धेन परमात्मना समानो भवतीत्यर्थः.

No difference within the Lord (Madhva)

Traditional Interpretation

In the verses Madhva gives on 2.1.10, the difference denied is within Viṣṇu: between his original form and his manifestations, in qualities or in nature — मूलरूपश्च यो विष्णुः प्रादुर्भावादिगश्च यः … गुणतस्स्वरूपतो वापि विशेषं योऽत्र पश्यति — and those who see difference-in-non-difference there go to darkness as well: भेदाभेदविदश्चात्र तमो यान्ति न संशयः.

On 2.1.11 he explains ‘as if’ by a verse he cites from a work he names (‘इति शब्दनिर्णये’): the word iva marks a very small thing or difference-in-non-difference — अतो नानेव इतीवशब्दोऽत्यल्पविषयो भेदाभेदविषयश्च — and ‘there is no difference here at all’ denies difference between the Lord’s parts, qualities and acts and between them and him: नेह नानास्ति किञ्चन इति किञ्चनशब्दादवयवानां गुणानां क्रियाणां च परस्परं तद्वतां च भेदनिषेधार्थम्. On 2.1.12–14 he answers the doubt that only difference-in-non-difference was denied: भेदाभेद एव निषिध्यते न तु भेद इत्याशङ्कां निवर्तयितुम् — so seeing Viṣṇu’s dharmas as separate leads down: एवं विष्णोर्धर्मान् पृथक् पश्यन्नन्धन्तमो विधावति.

At 2.1.15 the knowing sage becomes like, never the same: विजानतो मुनेरात्मा वायुरपि तादृगेव भवति न तु स एव भवति. He supports it with verses he attributes to a ‘Bhaviṣyat-parvan’ (‘इति भविष्यत्पर्वणि’); they are reported here as cited by him.

The thumb-sized person

Academic History

Who is ‘the person the size of a thumb, standing in the middle of the self’? The Brahmasūtra takes up the question under ‘शब्दादेव प्रमितः’ (1.3.24), and Śaṅkara’s commentary on that sūtra answers that the supreme self is meant — परमात्मैवायमङ्गुष्ठमात्रपरिमितः पुरुषो भवितुमर्हति — because of the words ‘lord of past and future’.

In his Kaṭha commentary Śaṅkara explains the measure by the heart: the heart-lotus is a thumb in size, and the self conditioned by the inner organ seated in it is called thumb-sized — अङ्गुष्ठपरिमाणं हृदयपुण्डरीकं तच्छिद्रवर्त्यन्तःकरणोपाधिरङ्गुष्ठमात्रः. On 2.1.13, ‘he is today and he tomorrow’, he says the verse answers by its own words the view that the self does not exist, and the doctrine of momentariness: तथा क्षणभङ्गवादश्च.

Raṅgarāmānuja reaches the same answer by the same sūtra, at length, and rejects a reading in which the first half names the individual self and the second half declares it to be the Lord. He has the Lord dwelling thumb-sized in the worshipper’s body — उपासकशरीरमध्ये, अङ्गुष्ठपरिमाणस्सन् आस्ते — and reads ‘does not shrink from’ as the Lord’s affection: वात्सल्यातिशयात् देहगतानपि दोषान् भोग्यतया पश्यतीत्यर्थः. Madhva’s bhāṣya has no comment of its own on 2.1.12–13 beyond the passage on 2.1.12–14 above.

  • Brahmasūtra 1.3.24: शब्दादेव प्रमितः śabdādeva pramitaḥ
  • Śaṅkara on Brahmasūtra 1.3.24: परमात्मैवायमङ्गुष्ठमात्रपरिमितः पुरुषो भवितुमर्हति paramātmaivāyamaṅguṣṭhamātraparimitaḥ puruṣo bhavitumarhati

Just such

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

The last mantra turns on one word, tādṛk, ‘of that kind, such as that’, sharpened by eva: तादृगेव भवति. Śaṅkara reads the pair as sameness of taste — एकरसमेव नान्यथा. Raṅgarāmānuja reads it as likeness — तत्सदृशमेव भवति. Madhva reads it as likeness that excludes identity — न तु स एव भवति. By itself tādṛk says ‘such as that’; whether ‘just such’ means one in taste, equal, or like and not the same is something each school decides from its whole doctrine, not from this word alone.

The ‘as if’ of 2.1.10–11 (nāneva) is similarly open: for Śaṅkara the difference seen is not there at all; for Raṅgarāmānuja what is wrongly seen is a difference in the supreme self; for Madhva iva marks a small or a mixed difference. This edition gives each reading in its commentator’s words and makes none of them the meaning of the text.

Water into water

Contemporary Reflection

A modern reflection. The two water images are a pair. Rain falling on the heights scatters and runs downhill into every channel; pure water poured into pure water does not scatter at all. The verses contrast a mind that follows things apart with one that is at rest with what it knows.

Whether the second image means becoming one, becoming like, or becoming like and never one is the deepest disagreement among the commentators, and no reflection settles it. This is a reflection, not a claim of any commentator.

Open questions

  • Whether tādṛg eva in 2.1.15 means identity (Śaṅkara), equality in purity (Raṅgarāmānuja), or likeness excluding identity (Madhva).
  • Whether the ‘dharmas’ of 2.1.14 are the self’s own (Śaṅkara), the Lord’s being the inner controller of each being (Raṅgarāmānuja), or the Lord’s parts, qualities and acts (Madhva).
  • The works Madhva cites here by name — a Śabdanirṇaya and a Bhaviṣyat-parvan — are reported as cited by him; this edition has not verified the quotations in those works.
