The light by which all shines

Mantras 2.2.9–2.2.15: one fire and one wind taking the form of each thing; the sun not soiled by the eye’s faults; the one controller; the eternal among the eternal; ‘there the sun does not shine’

Anuvākas 2.2.9, 2.2.10, 2.2.11, 2.2.12, 2.2.13, 2.2.14, 2.2.15

The vallī closes with images of one and many. As one fire entering the world takes the form of each thing, as one wind does, so the one inner self of all beings takes the form of each, and is outside as well. As the sun, the eye of the whole world, is not soiled by the eye’s faults, the inner self of all is not soiled by the world’s sorrow. The one controller, eternal among the eternal, conscious among the conscious, who grants the desires of many: those who see him standing in themselves have lasting happiness and peace. The pupil asks how that can be known — does it shine, or shine clearly? The answer is a verse the Muṇḍaka also has: there the sun does not shine, nor moon and stars, nor lightning, still less this fire; all shines after him as he shines. Śaṅkara reads these images as one self unaffected by what is superimposed on it; Raṅgarāmānuja as the Supreme indwelling each thing yet free of its faults, with a radiant form of his own; Madhva as the Lord, wholly different from the jīvas who are his reflections.

The words

Direct Textual Meaning

अग्निर्यथैको भुवनं प्रविष्टो रूपं रूपं प्रतिरूपो बभूव — ‘as the one fire, entering the world, has taken the form of each form’ — एकस्तथा सर्वभूतान्तरात्मा रूपं रूपं प्रतिरूपो बहिश्च — ‘so the one inner self of all beings takes the form of each form, and is outside as well’. 2.2.10 repeats the verse with wind.

सूर्यो यथा सर्वलोकस्य चक्षुर्न लिप्यते चाक्षुषैर्बाह्यदोषैः — ‘as the sun, the eye of the whole world, is not soiled by the outer faults of the eyes’ — so the one inner self of all ‘is not soiled by the world’s sorrow, being outside it’. 2.2.12–13: the one controller, who makes one form many, and ‘eternal among the eternal, conscious among the conscious’ — नित्यो नित्यानां चेतनश्चेतनानाम् — who grants the desires of many; to the wise who see him standing in the self belongs lasting happiness and lasting peace, ‘not to others’.

2.2.14: कथं नु तद्विजानीयां किमु भाति विभाति वा — ‘how should I know it? Does it shine, or shine forth?’ 2.2.15 answers: न तत्र सूर्यो भाति न चन्द्रतारकं नेमा विद्युतो भान्ति कुतोऽयमग्निः — ‘there the sun does not shine, nor moon and stars; these lightnings do not shine; how then this fire?’ — तमेव भान्तमनुभाति सर्वं तस्य भासा सर्वमिदं विभाति — ‘all shines after him alone as he shines; by his light all this shines’.

One self, untouched by what is laid on it (Śaṅkara)

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara says the Śruti repeats the oneness of the self because it does not settle in minds unsettled by bad reasoning. Fire, one by nature, appears manifold according to what it burns — दाह्यभेदेन बहुविधो बभूव — and the self, entering every body, takes each form and is also outside, बहिश्च स्वेनाविकृतेन रूपेण आकाशवत्, in its own unchanged form like space.

To the objection that the one self of all would then suffer the world’s sorrow, he answers with the sun and with the rope taken for a snake: the sorrow arises from what the world superimposes on the self, and न त्वात्मा सर्वलोकात्मापि सन् विपरीताध्यारोपनिमित्तेन लिप्यते लोकदुःखेन. The controller is in the heart as a face is in a mirror — आदर्शस्थं मुखमिति यद्वत् — and those who see him have lasting happiness, आत्मानन्दलक्षणं.

At 2.2.15 the answer is that it both shines and shines forth. The sun and the rest shine after it as a firebrand burns after the fire, not by itself — यथा जलोल्मुकादि अग्निसंयोगादग्निं दहन्तमनुदहति न स्वतः, तद्वत् — so that तदेव ब्रह्म भाति च विभाति च.

The indweller, free of faults, with a radiant form (Raṅgarāmānuja)

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja reads the fire and wind as the Supreme, one yet present in each thing as its inner controller and pervading it from outside: प्रतिवस्तु प्रतिसंक्रान्तान्तर्यामिविग्रहः. The sun of 2.2.11 is the deity of the eye, in it yet untouched by its impurities, as the Aitareya says the sun entered the eyes; so the Supreme, the self of all, is untouched by their faults, being by nature free of evil: स्वाभाविकापहतपाप्मत्वादिगुणयुक्ततया.

In 2.2.12 the text printed with his commentary reads ‘one seed’ (एकं बीजं) for ‘one form’; he takes it as the undivided darkness that the Lord makes into the many-formed world, and gives as one meaning of vaśī ‘subject to his devotees’ — भक्तवश्यः इति वा अर्थः. In 2.2.13 the one eternal and conscious grants the wishes of the many eternal conscious selves.

At 2.2.15 he holds that the Supreme has a divine, auspicious form — प्रमाणप्रतिपन्नशुभाश्रयदिव्यमङ्गलविग्रहोऽस्ति — whose brilliance outshines all lights, and connects the verse with the Brahmasūtra ‘jyotirdarśanāt’ (1.3.40) and the Bhāṣya and Vyāsārya on it, which he quotes. He rejects the reading of the third quarter as saying the world has no shining apart from Brahman: अधिष्ठानब्रह्मरूपभानव्यतिरिक्तभानशून्यत्वम् अध्यस्तप्रपञ्चस्य तृतीयपदार्थ इति यत् परैरुच्यते, तदयुक्तम्. The sun’s light is not its own, he says, citing the Gītā (15.12) and the Bhāṣyakāra’s comment on it.

  • Aitareya Upaniṣad 1.2.4: आदित्यश्चक्षुर्भूत्वाक्षिणी ādityaścakṣurbhūtvākṣiṇī
  • Brahmasūtra 1.3.40: ज्योतिर्दर्शनात् jyotirdarśanāt
  • Bhagavad Gītā 15.12: यदादित्यगतं तेजो जगद्भासयतेऽखिलम् yadādityagataṃ tejo jagadbhāsayate'khilam

The Lord and his reflections (Madhva)

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva’s comment on 2.2.9–2.2.13 is mostly verse, with no source named except where noted. One fire enters the worlds and there is no other cooker; the many unconscious fires are his likenesses — पाकादिकर्ताऽथाप्यस्य देवस्य प्रतिरूपकाः. Likewise Janārdana alone is independent within all jīvas, and the conscious beings are his reflections: रूपं रूपं प्रति ह्यस्य प्रतिबिम्बाश्च चेतनाः. His lines on the wind of 2.2.10 follow that verse in his text, though the digital file used here files them with his comment on 2.2.9.

On 2.2.11 the inner eye, a deity, is not soiled by the faults of the outer eye; so the Supreme Person, independent, is outside the dependent jīvas and not touched by their sorrow — एवं बाह्यस्स्वतन्त्रत्वाज्जीवेभ्यः पुरुषोत्तमः. A verse he cites from a Mahākaurma says the independent Hari is not touched by fault; and in prose: बहिश्चासौ परमात्मनो नितरां भिन्नः — the jīva, his reflection, is wholly different from the Supreme, being dependent.

On 2.2.13 he cites a verse (‘इति च’, no source named) that one knowing Hari in the self is released, while one knowing him as one with the jīva falls into blind darkness: जीवैक्येन तु तं जानन् तमस्यन्धे पतेद् ध्रुवम्. On 2.2.14 the indescribable highest happiness is the Lord’s own form — एतदेव भगवद्रूपं परमं सुखम् — and a verse he cites from the Mahāvarāha says it cannot be known without his grace. His bhāṣya has no separate comment on 2.2.15.

The verse in other texts

Academic History

The Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad (2.2.11 in the numbering of the edition used here) has the same verse word for word. The Bhagavad Gītā has two echoes: न तद्भासयते सूर्यो न शशाङ्को न पावकः (15.6), of the abode from which there is no return, and (15.12) the light in sun, moon and fire is said to be the speaker’s own.

Raṅgarāmānuja brings both the Gītā verse and the Brahmasūtra into his reading of 2.2.15; Śaṅkara’s comment here uses neither. The parallels show only that the image of a light beyond sun, moon and fire is shared; they do not decide between the readings.

  • Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 2.2.11: न तत्र सूर्यो भाति न चन्द्रतारकं na tatra sūryo bhāti na candratārakaṃ
  • Bhagavad Gītā 15.6: न तद्भासयते सूर्यो न शशाङ्को न पावकः na tadbhāsayate sūryo na śaśāṅko na pāvakaḥ
  • Bhagavad Gītā 15.12: यच्चन्द्रमसि यच्चाग्नौ तत्तेजो विद्धि मामकम् yaccandramasi yaccāgnau tattejo viddhi māmakam

‘Shines after’

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

2.2.14 contrasts bhāti, ‘shines’, with vibhāti, ‘shines forth, shines clearly’; 2.2.15 adds anubhāti, ‘shines after’, and bhāntam, the present participle, ‘him as he shines’. Śaṅkara takes the pair in 2.2.14 as the pupil’s doubt whether it shines and is clearly seen, and answers that it does both.

Raṅgarāmānuja argues from the prefix anu-: one does not say that someone ‘goes after’ another if he does not go at all, so the sun and the rest must shine with a light of their own, derived from the Supreme — अनुभाति इत्यस्य शब्दस्यायोगात्. Śaṅkara’s image of the firebrand that burns after the fire, not by itself, reads the same prefix the other way.

A light not seen by

Contemporary Reflection

A modern reflection. The pupil asks whether the highest shines; the answer is that nothing shines on it, and everything shines by it. What lets things be seen is not itself one of the things seen.

The commentators agree on the image and differ on what it shows: one light that alone is real, a Supreme whose own form outshines every light, or the Lord on whom every light depends and from whom every self is distinct. This is a reflection, not a claim of any commentator.

Open questions

  • Whether 2.2.12 reads ‘one form’ (the base and the other witnesses) or ‘one seed’ (the text printed with Raṅgarāmānuja); the reading changes what is made many.
  • Whether anubhāti in 2.2.15 implies that the sun and the rest have a light of their own derived from the Supreme (Raṅgarāmānuja) or none apart from it (Śaṅkara’s firebrand).
  • The works Madhva cites here by name — a Mahākaurma and a Mahāvarāha — and the Bhāṣya and Vyāsārya passages Raṅgarāmānuja quotes are reported as cited by them; this edition has not verified them in those works.
