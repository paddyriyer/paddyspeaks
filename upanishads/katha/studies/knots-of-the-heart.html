Study XVIII · Kaṭha Upaniṣad

When the knots of the heart are cut

Mantras 2.3.12–2.3.18: not by speech, mind or eye — ‘it is’; when every desire in the heart is let go; when the knots of the heart are cut, ‘this much is the teaching’; the hundred and one channels; drawing out the inner self like the reed from the grass; Naciketas reaches Brahman

Anuvākas 2.3.12, 2.3.13, 2.3.14, 2.3.15, 2.3.16, 2.3.17, 2.3.18

The Upaniṣad ends by saying how its subject is known and what knowing it does. It is not reached by speech, mind or eye; it is grasped as ‘it is’. When all the desires lodged in the heart are let go, and when all the knots of the heart are cut, the mortal becomes immortal — ‘this much is the teaching’. Of the heart’s hundred and one channels, one rises to the crown; going up by it one reaches immortality, the others lead every way. The thumb-sized person, the inner self, is to be drawn out from one’s body as a reed is drawn from its sheath of grass. Naciketas, having received this knowledge and the whole rule of yoga from Death, reached Brahman, free of stain and of death — and so may anyone who knows the self in this way. Here the three commentaries give three accounts of release: here and now, with no going; in two stages, with the going as the Lord’s work; or as knowledge of the Lord as other than the self.

The words

Direct Textual Meaning

2.3.12: नैव वाचा न मनसा प्राप्तुं शक्यो न चक्षुषा — ‘not by speech, not by mind can it be reached, nor by the eye’ — अस्तीति ब्रुवतोऽन्यत्र कथं तदुपलभ्यते, ‘how is it grasped except by one who says “it is”?’ 2.3.13: अस्तीत्येवोपलब्धव्यस्तत्त्वभावेन चोभयोः — it is to be grasped as ‘it is’, and by its true nature, of the two; for one who has grasped it as ‘it is’, its true nature becomes clear.

2.3.14: यदा सर्वे प्रमुच्यन्ते कामा येऽस्य हृदि श्रिताः — ‘when all the desires lodged in one’s heart are let go’ — अथ मर्त्योऽमृतो भवत्यत्र ब्रह्म समश्नुते, ‘then the mortal becomes immortal; here he attains Brahman’. 2.3.15 repeats the turn with यदा सर्वे प्रभिद्यन्ते हृदयस्येह ग्रन्थयः, ‘when all the knots of the heart are cut here’, and ends एतावद्ध्यनुशासनम्, ‘this much, indeed, is the teaching’.

2.3.16: शतं चैका च हृदयस्य नाड्यस्तासां मूर्धानमभिनिःसृतैका — ‘a hundred and one are the channels of the heart; of them one goes out to the crown’; going up by it one reaches immortality, the others serve for going out in every direction. 2.3.17: the thumb-sized person, the inner self, ever seated in the heart of men — तं स्वाच्छरीरात्प्रवृहेन्मुञ्जादिवेषीकां धैर्येण, ‘one should draw him out from one’s own body with steadiness, as the reed from the muñja grass’; the last words are said twice. 2.3.18: Naciketas, having gained this knowledge told by Death and the whole rule of yoga, ब्रह्म प्राप्तो विरजोऽभूद्विमृत्युः — ‘reached Brahman, became free of stain, free of death’ — and so does any other who knows the self thus.

Here and now, with no going (Śaṅkara)

Traditional Interpretation

For Śaṅkara 2.3.12–13 answer the worry that what no sense grasps must not exist: the root of the world is to be grasped as ‘it is’, तस्माज्जगतो मूलमात्मा अस्तीत्येवोपलब्धव्यः; first through its adjuncts, then, when every adjunct has set, its true nature becomes manifest — पश्चात्प्रत्यस्तमितसर्वोपाधिरूप आत्मनः तत्त्वभावः.

Desires rest in the intellect, not in the self, बुद्धिर्हि कामानामाश्रयः नात्मा; when they fall away with the knowledge that there is nothing else to desire, the knower attains Brahman here, all bonds stilled like a lamp going out: अत्र इहैव प्रदीपनिर्वाणवत्सर्वबन्धनोपशमात् ब्रह्म समश्नुते ब्रह्मैव भवतीत्यर्थः. The knots are the notions of ignorance — अहमिदं शरीरं ममेदं धनं सुखी दुःखी चाहमित्येवमादिलक्षणाः, ‘I am this body, this wealth is mine, I am happy, I am unhappy’ — and ‘this much’ is the teaching of all the Vedāntas: अनुशासनम् अनुशिष्टः उपदेशः सर्ववेदान्तानामिति वाक्यशेषः.

So 2.3.16 is not about him: for the knower who has become Brahman while living there is no going, जीवत एव ब्रह्मभूतस्य विदुषो न गतिर्विद्यते. The channels concern those of lesser knowledge or other meditations, who go to the world of Brahmā, and the fruit of the fire-knowledge Naciketas asked for; the immortality reached by the channel to the crown is relative, अमृतत्वम् अमरणधर्मत्वमापेक्षिकम्, as a smṛti text he quotes says (the edition used here identifies it as Viṣṇu Purāṇa 2.8.97). In 2.3.17 to ‘draw out’ is to separate the self from the body, प्रवृहेत् उद्यच्छेत् निष्कर्षेत् पृथक्कुर्यादित्यर्थः, and the repetition marks the end of the Upaniṣad, द्विर्वचनमुपनिषत्परिसमाप्त्यर्थम्. 2.3.18 closes the story to praise the knowledge: Naciketas ‘reached Brahman’, that is, was released — ब्रह्म प्राप्तोऽभूत् मुक्तोऽभवदित्यर्थः.

Release in two stages, the going as the Lord’s work (Raṅgarāmānuja)

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja reads 2.3.12 as saying that the Supreme is known from scripture alone, तस्य उपनिषदेकगम्यत्वात् इति भावः. In 2.3.13 ‘its true nature’ (tattvabhāva) is the inner organ that dwells on the truth, तत्त्वं भावयति इति तत्त्वभावः - अन्तःकरणम्: grasped as ‘it is’ from the texts, the Supreme is to be dwelt on by the mind too, and the mind becomes clear, मनः प्रसन्नं भवति, निर्दुष्टं भवति इत्यर्थः.

‘Immortal’ in 2.3.14 means free of the weight of past and future wrongdoing, विश्लिष्टाश्लिष्ट पूर्वोत्तरदुरितभरो भवति इत्यर्थः, and ‘here he attains Brahman’ means the experience of Brahman in the time of meditation, अत्रैव उपासनवेलायां ब्रह्मानुभवति इत्यर्थः — a reading he supports from the Bhāṣya on the Brahmasūtra, which he quotes. The knots of 2.3.15 are attachment, aversion and the like, ग्रन्थिवत् दुर्मोचा: रागद्वेषादयो. ‘This much is the teaching’ marks what the meditator has to do: the going out by the crown channel and the path that follows are not his work but the Lord’s, pleased by the meditation — किन्तु उपासनप्रीतभगवत्कृत्यम् इति भावः.

2.3.16 then states the second, the supreme, release, पूर्वमुक्तां द्वितीयां परममुक्तिमाह: by the channel to the crown the knower goes up to the world of Brahman and attains release as the manifestation of his own nature, देशविशेषविशिष्ट ब्रह्मप्राप्तिपूर्वकस्वस्वरूपाविर्भावलक्षणां मुक्तिं प्राप्नोति इत्यर्थः. The knower can find that channel because he is favoured by the Supreme in the heart, हार्देन परमपुरुषेण अनुगृहीतो भवति. In 2.3.17 to ‘draw out’ is to discern, प्रवृहेत् - विविच्य जानीयात्: to know the inner self of men as distinct from the conscious beings who are his body, by skill in knowledge, धैर्येण - ज्ञानकौशलेन. Naciketas, in 2.3.18, reached Brahman and became one in whom the eight qualities are manifest, आविर्भूतगुणाष्टकोऽभूत् इत्यर्थः.

‘It is’ as a name of the Lord; the Lord other than the self (Madhva)

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva’s comment on 2.3.12–13 is in verses closing इति च, with no source named. Keśava, exceeding all, is named ‘He is’ and is to be known as such, अस्तीति नामकस्तस्माज्ज्ञातव्यस्स तथैव च; those who do not know his supremacy cannot reach him, अनाधिक्यं जानतां तु कथं स उपलभ्यते. ‘True nature’ (tattvabhāva) is Viṣṇu, who makes clear the nature of matter and of souls: प्रकृतेः पुरुषाणां च तत्त्वं भावयति स्फुटम्.

On 2.3.14–15 the one freed from desires does not go to death, कामानां तु तदा मुक्तो मृतिं नैवाभियास्यति, and the knots are those of false knowledge, मिथ्याज्ञानग्रन्थिभिस्तु नितरां मुच्यते यदा. On 2.3.17 the jīva is Viṣṇu’s body and under his control, and one should draw out the imperishable Viṣṇu from the jīva, one’s ‘own body’: स्वाख्याच्छरीराज्जीवात् तु प्रवृहेद् विष्णुमव्ययम्. So the verse teaches the difference between Viṣṇu and the jīva, अतो विष्णोर्जीवाद्भेद उक्तः.

In the digital text used here his bhāṣya has no comment of its own on 2.3.16 or 2.3.18: the passage filed under 2.3.16–2.3.17 concerns 2.3.17, and the 2.3.18 entry is the colophon of the Upaniṣad, after which the bhāṣya closes with its conclusion, अतः सर्वोत्तमो विष्णुरिति सिद्धम्, and a verse of salutation.

Verses shared with other Upaniṣads

Academic History

Three of these verses are found elsewhere. The Bṛhadāraṇyaka (4.4.7) quotes 2.3.14 as ‘a verse’, यदा सर्वे प्रमुच्यन्ते कामा येऽस्य हृदि श्रिताः. The Muṇḍaka (2.2.9) has the knot of the heart in its own words, भिद्यते हृदयग्रन्थिश्छिद्यन्ते सर्वसंशयाः. The Chāndogya (8.6.6) quotes the verse of the hundred and one channels, शतं चैका च हृदयस्य नाड्यस्तासां मूर्धानमभिनिःसृतैका; in the edition used here its third quarter reads अमृतत्वमेव where the Kaṭha has अमृतत्वमेति.

The Brahmasūtra takes up the channel to the crown in its fourth chapter: the knower goes out by the hundred-and-first, favoured by the one in the heart, हार्दानुगृहीतः शताधिकया. Raṅgarāmānuja expounds this sūtra on 2.3.16; it is the basis of his reading that the going is the Lord’s gift and not the meditator’s task.

  • Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.4.7: यदा सर्वे प्रमुच्यन्ते कामा येऽस्य हृदि श्रिताः yadā sarve pramucyante kāmā ye'sya hṛdi śritāḥ
  • Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 2.2.9: भिद्यते हृदयग्रन्थिश्छिद्यन्ते सर्वसंशयाः bhidyate hṛdayagranthiśchidyante sarvasaṃśayāḥ
  • Chāndogya Upaniṣad 8.6.6: शतं चैका च हृदयस्य नाड्यस्तासां मूर्धानमभिनिःसृतैका śataṃ caikā ca hṛdayasya nāḍyastāsāṃ mūrdhānamabhiniḥsṛtaikā
  • Brahmasūtra 4.2.17: हार्दानुगृहीतः शताधिकया hārdānugṛhītaḥ śatādhikayā

Three accounts of release

Comparative Vedānta

The verses say that the mortal becomes immortal ‘here’, and also that one goes up by a channel to immortality. Śaṅkara resolves this by assigning the two to two kinds of knower: the one who knows Brahman as the self is released here, with no going; the going belongs to lesser knowledge, and its immortality is relative. Raṅgarāmānuja resolves it by two stages of one release: freedom from the weight of wrongdoing and the experience of Brahman in meditation here, and the supreme release after the going, which the Lord brings about. Madhva’s comment, keyed to the verses on desire, the knots and the thumb-sized one, makes release depend on knowing the Lord as other than the self.

None of the three readings is the text’s own statement; each is a way of holding together what the verses say. The edition sets them side by side and does not choose.

The reed and the grass

Contemporary Reflection

A modern reflection. The Upaniṣad ends not with a doctrine but with a gesture: draw the reed out from the grass, steadily. Something is already inside, and the work is to separate it from what surrounds it — not to make it.

Death’s teaching, which began with a boy refusing every reward, ends by saying that the boy reached what he asked for, and that anyone may. The commentators differ on what is drawn out and from what; they agree that the teaching is meant for others besides Naciketas. This paragraph is a reflection, not a reading of the text.

Open questions

  • How ‘here he attains Brahman’ (2.3.14) and the going by the channel to the crown (2.3.16) belong together: two kinds of knower (Śaṅkara), two stages of one release (Raṅgarāmānuja), or a question Madhva’s comment does not take up in this place.
  • Whether tattvabhāva in 2.3.13 is the true nature of the self (Śaṅkara), the inner organ that dwells on the truth (Raṅgarāmānuja), or Viṣṇu who makes the nature of things clear (Madhva).
  • Madhva’s bhāṣya, in the digital text used here, has no comment of its own on 2.3.16 or 2.3.18.
