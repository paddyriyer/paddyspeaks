Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 14 of 25

‘Tell me what you see beyond dharma and adharma’

Naciketas asks again: tell me what you see as other than dharma and adharma, other than what is made and unmade, other than what has been and what is to be.

5 Faithful English translation

Other than dharma, other than adharma, other than this made-and-unmade, other than what has been and what is to be — that which you see, tell that.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Tell me what you see that is beyond right and wrong, beyond what is made and unmade, beyond what has been and what is still to be.

Key concepts धर्म dharma ब्रह्मन् Brahman कर्मन् action, ritual work

7 Explain it to a beginner

Death has just praised the boy and said the door stands open. Naciketas does not relax. He asks again, more sharply. Tell me, he says, what you see that is beyond right and wrong, beyond what is made and what is not yet made, beyond what has been and what is still to come. Every ordinary thing falls inside these pairs: our actions are good or bad, things are made or not yet made, events belong to the past or the future. The boy wants what lies outside all of them. Śaṅkara says he is asking one thing: what no part of time can measure, and what lies beyond all our dealings. Raṅgarāmānuja, following an earlier teacher of his school, hears three questions in the verse: about the way, the goal and the one who travels. Either way, the request is for something no list of actions could contain.

An analogy Like asking a mapmaker not for one more road, but for the paper the whole map is drawn on.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does Naciketas ask?

He asks Death to tell him what he sees as other than dharma and adharma, other than this made and unmade, other than what has been and what is to be.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: beyond every dealing

Naciketas speaks again, having heard the praise — एतच्छ्रुत्वा नचिकेताः पुनराह — if he is fit and Death is pleased. He asks for what is apart from scriptural duty with its fruit and agents, from sin, from effect and cause, and from all three times — कालत्रयेण यन्न परिच्छिद्यत इत्यर्थः: a thing beyond the range of all dealing, सर्वव्यवहारगोचरातीतं.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: why the question is asked

The verses just spoken said that the goal is reached by knowledge, not by dharma (1.2.10, 1.2.12, 1.2.13); the boy now asks so that the goal, the means and the one who attains may be made clear. Raṅgarāmānuja reports Vyāsārya’s reading under Brahmasūtra 1.4.6: dharma stands for the means, धर्मः उपायः, and the means asked about is unlike the familiar ones, प्रसिद्धोपायविलक्षण उपायः इत्यर्थः; adharma stands for the goal, a fruit unlike familiar fruits, प्रसिद्धसाध्यविलक्षणं फलमित्यर्थः; ‘this’ is the seeker, स एवोपेता.

Comparative Vedānta

One question or three

Śaṅkara hears one question, which restates the third boon. Raṅgarāmānuja, with Vyāsārya and Brahmasūtra 1.4.6 (‘and so the mention and the question are of three only’), hears the means, the goal and the attainer, and defends the reading against the objection that the four anyatra phrases must describe a single thing. Madhva’s bhāṣya on the passage 1.2.14–17 consists of one verse he cites without naming its source (इति च), about the imperishable of 1.2.16–17.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • धर्मादन्यत्राधर्मादन्यत्रास्मात् dharmādanyatrādharmādanyatrāsmāt → धर्मात् + अन्यत्र + अधर्मात् + अन्यत्र + अस्मात्

    t voiced before a vowel; a + a

    dharmāt and adharmāt become -d before a vowel, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते); anyatra + adharmāt and anyatra + asmāt join long, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • भूताच्च भव्याच्च bhūtācca bhavyācca → भूतात् + च + भव्यात् + च

    t before c

    t becomes c before c, P. 8.4.40 (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः).

  • तद्वद tadvada → तत् + वद

    t voiced before a voiced consonant

    P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • अधर्मात् adharmāt

    nañ-tatpuruṣa · न + धर्म

    Vigraha न धर्मः

    the opposite of dharma, wrong-doing

  • कृताकृतात् kṛtākṛtāt

    samāhāra-dvandva · कृत + अकृत

    Vigraha कृतं च अकृतं च

    the made and the unmade, taken together (hence singular)

Morphology

अन्यत्र
Adverb ‘elsewhere’, here construed with the ablative: ‘other than, apart from’. It is repeated four times.
भव्यात्
Gerundive of √bhū used as a noun: ‘what is to be’, the future.
पश्यसि
The present of √dṛś is formed from the stem paśya-.

Anvaya

धर्मात् अन्यत्र, अधर्मात् अन्यत्र, अस्मात् कृताकृतात् अन्यत्र, भूतात् च भव्यात् च अन्यत्र यत् तत् पश्यसि, तत् वद ।

dharmāt anyatra, adharmāt anyatra, asmāt kṛtākṛtāt anyatra, bhūtāt ca bhavyāt ca anyatra yat tat paśyasi, tat vada |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A request in one sentence. Four phrases of anyatra with an ablative describe the object (yat tat) of paśyasi; the imperative vada, with tad picking up the relative, closes it. The phrases are not joined by ca, so whether they describe one object or several is a question the commentators argue.

Ambiguities

One thing asked, or three?
Śaṅkara: one thing, beyond all that can be dealt with — beyond rites with their fruit and means, beyond effect and cause, beyond the three times: कालत्रयेण यन्न परिच्छिद्यत इत्यर्थः. Raṅgarāmānuja reports Vyāsārya’s reading, given under Brahmasūtra 1.4.6, that three things are asked: the means (धर्मः उपायः — a means unlike the familiar ones, प्रसिद्धोपायविलक्षण उपायः इत्यर्थः), the goal (प्रसिद्धसाध्यविलक्षणं फलमित्यर्थः) and the seeker (स एवोपेता). He also gives a second explanation, in which the first phrases ask about meditation and the last about the goal not bounded by time — कालापरिच्छिन्नमुपेयं पृष्टम् — and defends it at length.
kṛtākṛta
Śaṅkara: the made is the effect, the unmade the cause — कृतं कार्यमकृतं कारणम्.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 18 + 17

Anuṣṭubh, 18 + 17 syllables: pādas of 10, 8, 9 and 8. The repeated anyatra lengthens the first and third pādas; every witness has the same words.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Ethical leadership

Most of a working life is spent inside the pairs this verse names: right and wrong decisions, things built and not yet built, last quarter and next. Naciketas asks for what lies outside all of them. A leader need not answer that question to gain from asking it: it is a reminder that the scorecard is not the whole of what matters. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Is there anything about you that is not past, not future, and not something you did or failed to do?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why might the boy press his question just when he has been praised?

A contemplation exercise For one day, notice how often your thoughts are about the past or the future, and how often about now.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
