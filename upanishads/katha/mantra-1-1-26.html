Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 26 of 29

‘These last till tomorrow’: the refusal

Naciketas, unmoved, answers the temptations: such pleasures are uncertain of tomorrow and wear out the senses; all life, however long, is short; Death may keep his chariots, his dancing and song.

5 Faithful English translation

[Things] of tomorrow’s existence, O Ender; what [is] the vigour of all the senses of a mortal, this they wear out. Even all life [is] short indeed. Yours alone [be] the chariots; yours the dancing and singing.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

These things last only till tomorrow, Death, and they wear away the strength of every sense a mortal has. Even the longest life is short. Keep your chariots; keep your dancing and your song.

Key concepts मृत्यु death भोग enjoyment, use त्याग giving up, relinquishment

7 Explain it to a beginner

Death has offered the boy everything. Now Naciketas answers, calmly. These things, he says, are not sure to be there tomorrow. While they last they wear down the strength of all the senses. And even the longest life is short, so long life is no great gift either. Keep your horses and chariots, and your dancing and singing; they are yours. Śaṅkara says the boy stood unshaken like a deep lake. He also notes that even the life of Brahmā, the longest of all, is short. The boy is not refusing pleasure out of fear; he has simply seen through it.

An analogy Like a traveller offered a beautifully furnished room in a house that will be pulled down tomorrow.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does Naciketas answer?

That the things offered last only till tomorrow and wear out the senses, that all life is short, and that Death may keep his chariots, dancing and song.

Traditional Interpretation

How does the boy hear the temptation?

Śaṅkara: tempted thus by Death, Naciketas speaks unshaken, like a great lake — मृत्युना एवं प्रलोभ्यमानोऽपि नचिकेता महाह्रदवदक्षोभ्य आह. Such pleasures are harmful because they waste virtue, strength, wisdom, vigour and fame — धर्मवीर्यप्रज्ञातेजोयशःप्रभृतीनां क्षपयितृत्वात्. And all life, even Brahmā’s, is short: सर्वं यद्ब्रह्मणोऽपि जीवितम् आयुः अल्पमेव.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Two divisions, one meaning

Śaṅkara reads śvobhāvāḥ as one compound (‘whose being is doubtful tomorrow’); Raṅgarāmānuja divides śvo ’bhāvāḥ, ‘whose non-existence comes tomorrow’ — श्वः अभावः येषां ते तथोक्ताः, दिनद्वयस्थायिनो न भवन्ति इत्यर्थः. Both make the pleasures transient.

Comparative Vedānta

The same reading: the pleasures do not last two days, and even Brahmā’s life is short, much more ours — ब्रह्मणोऽपि जीवितं स्वल्पम्, किमुत अस्मदादिजीवितम् — so long life is not worth choosing either.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

No disagreement of substance; they divide the first word differently. Madhva’s bhāṣya on the passage 1.1.26–29 comments only on ‘the great passing-on’ of 1.1.29.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • श्वोभावा मर्त्यस्य śvobhāvā martyasya → श्वोभावाः + मर्त्यस्य

    āḥ before a voiced consonant

    āḥ drops its visarga before the voiced m, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि) and P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य).

  • यदन्तकैतत् yadantakaitat → यत् + अन्तक + एतत्

    voicing; vṛddhi

    yat before a vowel becomes yad, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते). antaka + etat: a + e give ai, P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि).

  • तवैव वाहास्तव tavaiva vāhāstava → तव + एव + वाहाः + तव

    vṛddhi; visarga before t

    tava + eva give tavaiva, P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि). vāhāḥ before t becomes vāhās, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • श्वोभावाः śvobhāvāḥ

    bahuvrīhi · श्वस् + भाव

    Vigraha श्वो भविष्यन्ति न वेति सन्दिह्यमान एव येषां भावो

    whose being is uncertain tomorrow; the vigraha is Śaṅkara’s. Raṅgarāmānuja divides śvo ’bhāvāḥ instead (see ambiguities)

  • सर्वेन्द्रियाणाम् sarvendriyāṇām

    karmadhāraya · सर्व + इन्द्रिय

    Vigraha सर्वाणि इन्द्रियाणि

    all the senses

  • नृत्यगीते nṛtyagīte

    itaretara-dvandva · नृत्य + गीत

    Vigraha नृत्यं च गीतं च

    dancing and singing (dual)

Morphology

जरयन्ति
Causative of jṝ ‘grow old’: ‘make old, wear out’ (Śaṅkara: अपक्षपयन्ति).
अन्तक
‘Ender’, a name of Death (Yama), vocative.

Anvaya

अन्तक, [एते] श्वोभावाः । मर्त्यस्य सर्वेन्द्रियाणाम् यत् एतत् तेजः [तत् ते] जरयन्ति । सर्वम् अपि जीवितम् अल्पम् एव । वाहाः तव एव [सन्तु], नृत्यगीते तव [स्ताम्] ।

antaka, ete śvobhāvāḥ | martyasya sarvendriyāṇām yat etat tejaḥ tat te jarayanti | sarvam api jīvitam alpam eva | vāhāḥ tava eva santu, nṛtyagīte tava stām |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Short nominal sentences. The first is a single predicate (śvobhāvāḥ) whose subject — the pleasures offered — is understood; the second uses a relative (yat … etat) for the object of jarayanti; the last two leave the verb ‘be’ to be supplied (tavaiva vāhāḥ, tava nṛtyagīte).

Ambiguities

śvobhāvāḥ or śvo ’bhāvāḥ
The letters allow two divisions. Śaṅkara reads one word, ‘whose being is doubtful tomorrow’: श्वो भविष्यन्ति न वेति सन्दिह्यमान एव येषां भावो भवनं. Raṅgarāmānuja divides śvaḥ abhāvāḥ, ‘whose non-being is tomorrow’ — श्वः अभावः येषां ते तथोक्ताः — that is, they will not last two days. Madhva’s text writes the avagraha (श्वोऽभावा). The sense is the same.
yad … etat
Śaṅkara construes yad etat with tejaḥ: ‘this vigour of all the senses’, which the pleasures wear out.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 22

Triṣṭubh, 4 × 11 (22 + 22), regular.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: तवैव वाहास्तव नृत्तगीतम् — Madhva’s text reads nṛttagītam, ‘dance and song’ (a collective singular), for nṛtyagīte, dual, ‘dancing and singing’.
  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: वाहास्तव नृत्तगीतम् — Madhva’s text reads nṛttagītam, ‘dance and song’ (a collective singular), for nṛtyagīte, dual, ‘dancing and singing’.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Aging and mortality

Things that wear out the one who enjoys them are poor bargains, however bright. The boy’s answer is not puritanism but arithmetic: what lasts till tomorrow cannot answer a question about what lasts. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Which of the things you enjoy most will still be with you in ten years?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Is a pleasure less valuable because it does not last? What else would make it valuable?

A contemplation exercise Notice one thing today that leaves you more tired than it found you, and one that leaves you clearer.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
