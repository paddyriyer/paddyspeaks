Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 5 of 15

Not by breath

No mortal lives by out-breath or in-breath, but by another on whom both depend.

5 Faithful English translation

Not by the out-breath, not by the in-breath does any mortal live; but they live by another, on whom these two rest.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

No mortal lives by the out-breath or the in-breath. All live by something else, on which these two breaths themselves depend.

Key concepts प्राण breath, life-force आत्मन् self, Self जीव individual self

7 Explain it to a beginner

We usually say that breathing keeps us alive. The verse disagrees: no one lives by the breath going out or the breath coming in. We live by something else, on which the breathing itself depends. Śaṅkara argues it like this: the breaths and senses are parts that work together, and things that are put together work for someone other than themselves, as a house is built for the person who lives in it. So the breaths cannot be the final reason we are alive. Raṅgarāmānuja says the whole life of every being depends on the supreme Self, who even keeps the breath going. Madhva says breath can carry living beings only because it rests on Viṣṇu.

An analogy Like a pair of oars that move the boat, though neither oar is the rower who pulls them.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

No mortal lives by out-breath or in-breath; all live by another, on whom those two rest.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: assembled things serve another

The breaths cannot be the cause of life, because what is assembled works for another, न ह्येषां परार्थानां संहत्यकारित्वाज्जीवनहेतुत्वमुपपद्यते, as houses do in the world, यथा गृहादीनां लोके. We live by another, different from the assembled breaths, इतरेणैव संहतप्राणादिविलक्षणेन, in whose presence and for whose sake they act.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: the life of all depends on him

The verse states the greatness of the Supreme as the cause of the breathing of every being, तस्य सर्वप्राणिप्राणनहेतुत्वरूपं महिमानमाह. On him the life of the two breaths depends, and so the life of all, यदधीनं प्राणापानयोरपि जीवनम् तदधीनमेव सर्वेषां जीवनमिति भावः.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: breath rests on Viṣṇu

His bhāṣya comments once on 2.2.3–5, in a verse that names no source: breath alone does not sustain conscious beings, न केवलं प्राण एव चेतनानां विधारकः, but sustains them by resting on Viṣṇu, किन्तु विष्णुं समाश्रित्य प्राणो जीवान् बिभर्त्ययम्, who is their chief and independent support.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.2.5

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Life is from the self, other than the assembled breaths, इतरेणैव संहतप्राणादिविलक्षणेन.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.2.5

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The supreme Self causes the breathing of all beings, तस्य सर्वप्राणिप्राणनहेतुत्वरूपं महिमानमाह.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.2.3–5

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Viṣṇu is the chief and independent support of conscious beings, अतो मुख्याश्रयो विष्णुश्चेतनानां स्वतन्त्रतः.

Where they disagree

All three read the ‘other’ as that on which breath depends: the self, unlike any assembled thing (Śaṅkara); the supreme Self who causes all breathing (Raṅgarāmānuja); Viṣṇu, on whom breath rests (Madhva).

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • नापानेन nāpānena → न + अपानेन

    a + a

    Two like vowels merge into the long vowel, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • मर्त्यो जीवति martyo jīvati → मर्त्यः + जीवति

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

  • यस्मिन्नेता yasminnetā → यस्मिन् + एतौ

    n before a vowel

    A final n after a short vowel is doubled before a vowel, P. 8.3.32 (ङमो ह्रस्वादचि ङमुण्नित्यम्).

  • एतावुपाश्रितौ etāvupāśritau → एतौ + उपाश्रितौ

    au before a vowel

    au becomes āv before a vowel, P. 6.1.78 (एचोऽयवायावः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

कश्चन
kaḥ, ‘who’, with the particle cana, ‘any(one) at all’; with the negative na, ‘no one’. The visarga becomes ś before c.
उपाश्रितौ
upa-ā-√śri, ‘lean on, rest on’, past participle in the dual: the two breaths rest on the one by whom all live.

Anvaya

कश्चन मर्त्यः न प्राणेन न अपानेन जीवति । [सर्वे] तु इतरेण जीवन्ति यस्मिन् एतौ उपाश्रितौ ।

kaścana martyaḥ na prāṇena na apānena jīvati | sarve tu itareṇa jīvanti yasmin etau upāśritau |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two contrasting clauses: a double negative (na … na …) with the singular jīvati, then the plural jīvanti with the instrumental itareṇa; the relative clause yasmin etau upāśritau describes this ‘other’.

Ambiguities

What is the ‘other’?
Śaṅkara: the self, unlike the breaths an unassembled being, for whose sake the assembled breaths work, as a house is for its dweller, यथा गृहादीनां लोके. Raṅgarāmānuja: the supreme Self, on whom even the life of the breaths depends, यदधीनं प्राणापानयोरपि जीवनम् तदधीनमेव सर्वेषां जीवनमिति भावः. Madhva: Viṣṇu, on whom breath rests to sustain beings.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Science and consciousness

A body can be kept breathing by a machine, and we still ask whether someone is there. The verse locates life not in the mechanics of breathing but in that for which the breathing goes on. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin If you are not your breath, what are you?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Is Śaṅkara’s argument from assembled things convincing to you? What would it take to refute it?

A contemplation exercise Watch three breaths and notice the one who is watching them.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
