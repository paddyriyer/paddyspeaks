Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 14 of 18

When all desires are let go

When all the desires that lodge in one’s heart are let go, then the mortal becomes immortal; here he attains Brahman.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

When all the desires that dwell in the heart fall away, then the mortal becomes immortal, and attains Brahman here and now.

Key concepts अमृत immortality, the deathless ब्रह्मन् Brahman मोक्ष liberation मृत्यु death

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse names what has to end for the self to be found: the desires that live in the heart. When all of them fall away, it says, the mortal becomes immortal and reaches Brahman right here. The Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad has the same verse word for word. Śaṅkara explains that desires belong to the mind, not to the self, and that when one knows the self there is nothing left to want. Then death, which for him means ignorance, desire and action, comes to an end, and one becomes Brahman in this very life, as a lamp goes out. Raṅgarāmānuja reads ‘immortal’ as being freed from the weight of past wrongdoing, with later wrongs no longer clinging, and ‘here’ as the time of meditation, when the meditator already experiences Brahman. Madhva’s comment, in verse, says that one freed from desire will not go towards death.

An analogy A boat tied to the shore by many ropes floats free only when the last rope is untied.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

When all desires lodged in the heart are let go, the mortal becomes immortal and attains Brahman here.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: desires belong to the intellect, not to the self

For one who sees the highest self, desires fall away because there is nothing else to desire, कामयितव्यस्यान्यस्याभावात्. They rest in the intellect, not the self, बुद्धिर्हि कामानामाश्रयः नात्मा, as another scripture says, ‘desire, resolve…’ (quoted by him). Death, which is ignorance, desire and action, is destroyed, and with it all going; here, as a lamp goes out, he becomes Brahman, अत्र इहैव प्रदीपनिर्वाणवत्सर्वबन्धनोपशमात् ब्रह्म समश्नुते ब्रह्मैव भवतीत्यर्थः.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: freed from sin, enjoying Brahman in meditation

When the heart’s longings for wrong objects are stilled, the meditator becomes immortal: past sins are destroyed and later ones do not cling, विश्लिष्टाश्लिष्ट पूर्वोत्तरदुरितभरो भवति इत्यर्थः. ‘Here’ is the time of meditation, अत्रैव उपासनवेलायां ब्रह्मानुभवति इत्यर्थः. He quotes the Bhāṣya on the sūtra समाना चाऽऽसृत्युपक्रमादमृतत्त्वं च अनुपोष्य to the same effect.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: the freed one will not go to death

In a verse in his comment: when the desires of the inner organ are given up, the one freed will not go to death, कामानां तु तदा मुक्तो मृतिं नैवाभियास्यति. His text reads hṛdi sthitāḥ and atha brahma (corrections.json).

Comparative Vedānta

The same verse in the Bṛhadāraṇyaka

The verse stands word for word in the Bṛhadāraṇyaka, यदा सर्वे प्रमुच्यन्ते कामा येऽस्य हृदि श्रिताः.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.3.14

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

With desire gone, the knower becomes Brahman in this life, ब्रह्मैव भवतीत्यर्थः, and there is no further going.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.3.14

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Immortality here is freedom from the clinging of sin; Brahman is experienced in meditation, अत्रैव उपासनवेलायां ब्रह्मानुभवति इत्यर्थः.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.3.14

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

One freed from the desires of the inner organ does not go to death, कामानां तु तदा मुक्तो मृतिं नैवाभियास्यति.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara reads ‘attains Brahman here’ as becoming Brahman in this life; Raṅgarāmānuja as experiencing Brahman in meditation while still embodied, release itself coming later; Madhva’s verse speaks of freedom from death without discussing ‘here’.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • कामा ये kāmā ye → कामाः + ये

    āḥ before a voiced consonant

    After ā the visarga becomes y before a voiced sound, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), and the y is dropped before a consonant, P. 8.3.22 (हलि सर्वेषाम्).

  • येऽस्य ye'sya → ये + अस्य

    e + a

    After e at the end of a word an initial a is merged into it, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति); the avagraha marks it.

  • मर्त्योऽमृतो भवत्यत्र martyo'mṛto bhavatyatra → मर्त्यः + अमृतः + भवति + अत्र

    aḥ before a; aḥ before a voiced consonant; i before an unlike vowel

    The visarga of -aḥ becomes u before a, giving o, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च) with P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः), and the following a is merged, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति); the avagraha marks it. -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च). i before an unlike vowel becomes y, P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

प्रमुच्यन्ते
Passive present of pra + √muc, ‘are released, fall away’: the desires are let go, not the person.
समश्नुते
sam + √aś (5th class), ‘reach, attain fully’, in the middle voice.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

A temporal clause (yadā sarve pramucyante kāmāḥ) with a relative clause on kāmāḥ (ye ’sya hṛdi śritāḥ), answered by atha with two main clauses: martyo ’mṛto bhavati and atra brahma samaśnute.

Ambiguities

‘Here’: in this life, or in meditation?
Śaṅkara: here, in this very life, all bonds ceasing as a lamp goes out, he becomes Brahman, अत्र इहैव प्रदीपनिर्वाणवत्सर्वबन्धनोपशमात् ब्रह्म समश्नुते ब्रह्मैव भवतीत्यर्थः. Raṅgarāmānuja: here, at the time of meditation, he experiences Brahman, अत्रैव उपासनवेलायां ब्रह्मानुभवति इत्यर्थः.
What is ‘becoming immortal’ here?
Śaṅkara: death, which is ignorance, desire and action, is destroyed, so there is no more going, गमनप्रयोजकस्य मृत्योर्विनाशाद्गमनानुपपत्तेः. Raṅgarāmānuja: past sins are destroyed and later ones do not cling, विश्लिष्टाश्लिष्ट पूर्वोत्तरदुरितभरो भवति इत्यर्थः, following the Brahmasūtra and the Bhāṣya he quotes.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16

Regular: 16 and 16 syllables.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: कामा येऽस्य हृदि स्थिताः — Madhva’s text reads hṛdi sthitāḥ, ‘standing in the heart’, for hṛdi śritāḥ, ‘resting on the heart’.
  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: येऽस्य हृदि स्थिताः — Madhva’s text reads hṛdi sthitāḥ, ‘standing in the heart’, for hṛdi śritāḥ, ‘resting on the heart’.
  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: मर्त्योऽमृतो भवत्यथ ब्रह्म — Madhva’s text reads bhavaty atha brahma, ‘then [he attains] Brahman’, for bhavaty atra brahma, ‘here [he attains] Brahman’.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Wealth and consumption

Desire usually feels like something we have to satisfy. The verse treats it as something that can be let go, and places freedom in that letting go rather than in having. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Which of your wishes would you be glad to be free of?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Is ‘here’ this life, or the hour of meditation? What would each reading ask of a reader?

A contemplation exercise Notice one desire today and watch it pass without acting on it.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
