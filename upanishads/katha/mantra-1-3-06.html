Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 6 of 17

The skilled driver

For one with understanding, whose mind is always held, the senses obey, like a good driver’s good horses.

5 Faithful English translation

‘But he who is possessed of understanding, with a mind always held — his senses are controllable, like the good horses of a charioteer.’

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘When the driver has understanding and always holds the mind steady, the senses obey, like a good driver’s well-trained horses.’

Key concepts इन्द्रिय indriya, a sense मनस् mind विज्ञान understanding, the knowing self

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse is the mirror of the one before it. Now picture a driver who knows the road and keeps a steady hold on the reins. The horses are strong — they have not changed — but they go where they are directed. In the same way, when a person's understanding is clear and the mind is held steady, the senses do what is asked of them. Śaṅkara points out that 'controlled' does not mean switched off: the senses can be sent forward or held back as needed, like horses that can be urged on or reined in. The good driver is not one with weaker horses, but one with a better grip. Read together, the two verses say that the same energies can carry us well or badly, depending on who holds the reins.

An analogy Like a rider on a spirited horse who has trained it well: the strength is the same as a runaway’s, but it carries the rider where she means to go.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

That for one with understanding and a mind always held, the senses are under control, like the good horses of a charioteer.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara

This is the reward of the opposite kind of charioteer, पूर्वोक्तविपरीतसारथिर्भवति तस्य फलमाह: the one who is skilled and discerning, निपुणः विवेकवान्, with the mind held and settled, प्रगृहीतमनाः समाहितचित्तः, has senses that can be set going or held back, like tamed good horses, दान्ताः सदश्वा.

Comparative Vedānta

Raṅgarāmānuja

His joint comment on 1.3.5–6: when understanding and mind, the charioteer and reins, are sound, the senses, the horses, are under control, अश्वत्वेन रूपितानि इन्द्रियाणि वश्यानि भवन्ति.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Madhva

In our source Madhva prints 1.3.3–1.3.11 without comment and then comments once, marking it ॥ १०-१२ ॥, on the deities who preside over the senses, their objects, the mind and the intellect; he does not gloss this verse.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • यस्तु yastu → यः + तु

    visarga sandhi

    Visarga before t becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः).

  • तस्येन्द्रियाणि tasyendriyāṇi → तस्य + इन्द्रियाणि

    vowel sandhi

    a + i → e, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • सदश्वा इव sadaśvā iva → सदश्वाः + इव

    visarga sandhi

    āḥ before a vowel other than a: the visarga becomes y, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), and the y is dropped, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य); the hiatus remains.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • सदश्वाः sadaśvāḥ

    karmadhāraya · सत् + अश्व

    Vigraha सन्तः अश्वाः

    good horses

Morphology

विज्ञानवान्
vijñāna with the possessive suffix -vat, nominative singular masculine -vān.
सदश्वाः
sat ‘good’ before a vowel keeps its d (sad-aśva) in the compound.

Anvaya

यः तु विज्ञानवान् सदा युक्तेन मनसा भवति, तस्य इन्द्रियाणि सारथेः सदश्वाः इव वश्यानि [भवन्ति] ॥

yaḥ tu vijñānavān sadā yuktena manasā bhavati, tasya indriyāṇi sāratheḥ sadaśvāḥ iva vaśyāni bhavanti ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

The exact counterpart of 1.3.5: a relative clause with tasya as correlative, and a nominal main clause with the simile sadaśvā iva sāratheḥ. Each negative of 1.3.5 (avijñānavān, ayuktena, avaśyāni, duṣṭāśvāḥ) is here positive.

Ambiguities

Control as restraint and as use
Śaṅkara: ‘controllable’ means the senses can be set going or held back, प्रवर्तयितुं निवर्तयितुं वा शक्यानि; the verse does not ask that they be stopped, only that they answer to the driver.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 17 + 16

17 and 16 syllables: the first pāda, yas tu vijñānavān bhavati, has nine. In 1.3.5 the vowels of tu and bhavati fuse with the following a- (yas tv avijñānavān bhavaty ayuktena), which keeps that pāda at eight; here there is no such junction.

Textual variations between witnesses

The witnesses differ here only in spelling (listed in section 14).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Self-mastery

Discipline here is not suppression but skill: the horses keep all their strength. That is a useful corrective to the idea that self-control means doing less. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Where in your life do your senses already ‘obey’ you well?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry What is the difference, in Śaṅkara’s gloss, between controlling the senses and stopping them?
