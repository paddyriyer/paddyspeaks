Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 2 of 17

The bridge and the further shore

The Nāciketa fire, a bridge for those who sacrifice, and the imperishable Brahman, the fearless further shore: may we be able to know both.

5 Faithful English translation

‘[That] which is the bridge of those who have sacrificed, the Nāciketa [fire], and that which is the imperishable, highest Brahman, the fearless further shore of those wishing to cross — may we be able [to know both].’

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘May we be able to know the Nāciketa fire, the bridge for those who sacrifice, and the imperishable, highest Brahman, the shore beyond fear for those who long to cross.’

Key concepts सेतु setu, bridge, dam अग्नि Agni, fire अक्षर akṣara, the imperishable; syllable ब्रह्मन् Brahman कर्मन् action, ritual work

7 Explain it to a beginner

This short verse looks back to the fire ritual Naciketas learned in the first part of the Upaniṣad and looks ahead to what lies beyond it. It names two things a person may come to know. The first is the Nāciketa fire, called a bridge: for people who perform rituals it carries them across hardship to a better life. The second is Brahman, the highest reality, which does not perish, called the far shore where there is no fear, for those who want to cross the whole stream of birth and death. Śaṅkara says the verse sets out both the lower goal of ritual and the higher goal of knowledge, and that both are to be known. The speaker says 'may we be able' to know them — a confident hope rather than a boast.

An analogy Like a ferry and the far bank: the ferry is real and useful, but what the traveller wants is the shore.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

It names the Nāciketa fire, the bridge of sacrificers, and the imperishable highest Brahman, the fearless further shore of those wishing to cross, and says ‘may we be able’ — to know them, as the commentators supply.

Traditional Interpretation

Two goals

Śaṅkara: the verse sets out the two Brahmans to be known, the lower and the higher, the refuge of ritualists and of knowers of Brahman, परापरे ब्रह्मणी कर्मिब्रह्मविदाश्रये वेदितव्ये इति वाक्यार्थः; these are the two the previous verse introduced as drinking in the cave.

Traditional Interpretation

The bridge

Śaṅkara: the fire is a bridge for those who sacrifice because it carries them across sorrow, दुःखसन्तरणार्थत्वात्. Raṅgarāmānuja: the support of those who sacrifice, the giver of the fruit of action, कर्मफलप्रदः, and the far shore is ‘firm and fearless’, निर्भयं दृढं तीरम्.

Comparative Vedānta

Madhva’s reading

Madhva’s comment begins with a gap in our source; what remains speaks of a limit for those who worship Viṣṇu and of Viṣṇu, standing on the far side of saṃsāra, in two forms, संसारस्य च पारस्थस्स विष्णुर्द्विस्वरूपकः.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

The form śakemahi

An optative of śak with a thematic stem; Raṅgarāmānuja explains it as a Vedic interchange of stem class, शके: व्यत्यनेन शप्. Madhva’s text has śakemasi, a Vedic first person plural (corrections.json).

The lower Brahman, reached by ritual, and the higher, the refuge of knowers, are both to be known: परापरे ब्रह्मणी कर्मिब्रह्मविदाश्रये वेदितव्ये इति वाक्यार्थः.

‘We are able to worship’ what the Nāciketa fire attains; so there is no need to fear that the Supreme is hard to worship, अतः दुरुपास्यत्वबुद्धया न भेतव्यम्.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara reads the verse as two objects of knowledge, a lower and a higher Brahman; Raṅgarāmānuja as reassurance that the Supreme can be worshipped. Madhva’s comment survives only in part in our source.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • सेतुरीजानानाम् seturījānānām → सेतुः + ईजानानाम्

    visarga sandhi

    Final s becomes ru, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः); before a vowel it stands as r.

  • ईजानानामक्षरं ījānānāmakṣaraṃ → ईजानानाम् + अक्षरम्

    m before a vowel

    Final m stays before a vowel; before a consonant it becomes anusvāra, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • अभयं तितीर्षतां पारं नाचिकेतं abhayaṃ titīrṣatāṃ pāraṃ nāciketaṃ → अभयम् + तितीर्षताम् + पारम् + नाचिकेतम्

    anusvāra

    Final m → anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • अभयम् abhayam

    nañ-bahuvrīhi · अ + भय

    Vigraha अविद्यमानं भयं यस्मिन् तत्

    that in which there is no fear

Morphology

ईजानानाम्
Perfect middle participle of yaj ‘sacrifice’ (ījāna), with the reduplicated weak stem īj-; Raṅgarāmānuja: ईजानानाम् इति कानजन्तश्शब्दः, formed with the suffix kānac.
तितीर्षताम्
Present participle of the desiderative of tṝ ‘cross’ (titīrṣa- ‘wish to cross’), genitive plural.
शकेमहि
Optative middle, first person plural, of śak ‘be able’, with the thematic a (śak-a-ī-mahi). The root is otherwise of the fifth class (śaknoti); Raṅgarāmānuja notes the Vedic interchange, शके: व्यत्यनेन शप्. Madhva’s text reads śakemasi.

Anvaya

यः ईजानानाम् सेतुः [तम्] नाचिकेतम् [अग्निम्], यत् [च] तितीर्षताम् अभयम् पारम् परम् अक्षरम् ब्रह्म [तत् च ज्ञातुम्] शकेमहि ॥

yaḥ ījānānām setuḥ tam nāciketam agnim, yat ca titīrṣatām abhayam pāram param akṣaram brahma tat ca jñātum śakemahi ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two relative clauses, each without its correlative: yaḥ setur ījānānām (the Nāciketa fire) and akṣaraṃ brahma yat param … abhayaṃ titīrṣatāṃ pāram (Brahman). The main verb śakemahi has nāciketam as its object; Śaṅkara supplies ‘to know’ and extends it to Brahman too.

Ambiguities

Who speaks?
The first person plural has no subject. Śaṅkara reads it as a claim, ‘we have been able’ (शक्तवन्तः), to know both the lower and the higher Brahman: परापरे ब्रह्मणी कर्मिब्रह्मविदाश्रये वेदितव्ये इति वाक्यार्थः. Raṅgarāmānuja: ‘we are able to worship’ what the Nāciketa fire leads to, so one need not fear that the Supreme is too hard to worship, अतः दुरुपास्यत्वबुद्धया न भेतव्यम्.
What is the bridge?
Śaṅkara: the Nāciketa fire, a bridge because it carries sacrificers across sorrow, दुःखसन्तरणार्थत्वात्. Raṅgarāmānuja: the support of sacrificers, the giver of the fruit of action, कर्मफलप्रदः.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 15 + 17

16 and 17 syllables: the third pāda, abhayaṃ titīrṣatāṃ pāram, has nine; the others have eight.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: नाचिकेतं शकेमसि — Madhva’s text reads śakemasi, with the Vedic first person plural ending -masi, for śakemahi, middle; both ‘may we be able’.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Means and ends

The verse keeps the ritual and the goal in one sentence without confusing them: the bridge is honoured, but it is not the shore. Much effort in any life goes into building bridges; the question is whether we remember where they lead. (A modern reflection.)
