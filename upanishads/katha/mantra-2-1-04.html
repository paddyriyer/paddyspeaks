Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 4 of 15

The knower of dream and waking

Knowing as the great, all-pervading self that by which one sees both the dream state and the waking state, the wise one does not grieve.

5 Faithful English translation

‘Having understood as the great, all-pervading self that by which one perceives both the state of dream and the state of waking, the wise one does not grieve.’

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘The one by which we see both our dreams and our waking days — knowing that as the great self that pervades all, the wise do not grieve.’

Key concepts आत्मन् self, Self धीर the wise शोक grief, sorrow ज्ञान knowledge

7 Explain it to a beginner

Every night we dream and every morning we wake. The dream world and the waking world are very different, yet the same 'I' is there in both, noticing each. This verse points to that witness. Śaṅkara says the Upaniṣad repeats the teaching of the previous verse because it is so subtle and easy to miss: just as one knower knows all colours and sounds, one knower sees both dreaming and waking. When a wise person understands this knower as the great self that pervades everything, grief loses its hold, because what grieves is always tied to one state or another, and this is not. The second line repeats, word for word, the end of an earlier verse (1.2.22), where the self was described as bodiless among bodies.

An analogy A cinema screen that carries a comedy in the afternoon and a tragedy in the evening, and is wet with neither the rain nor the tears.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

That by which one sees both dreaming and waking — knowing it as the great, all-pervading self, the wise do not grieve.

Traditional Interpretation

Why the teaching is repeated

Śaṅkara: because the self is very subtle and hard to know, the same point is made again, अतिसूक्ष्मत्वाद्दुर्विज्ञेयमिति मत्वैतमेवार्थं पुनः पुनराह; the rest is as in the previous verse, सर्वं पूर्ववत्. The understanding meant is direct: ‘I am the Supreme Self’, साक्षादहमस्मि परमात्मेति.

Comparative Vedānta

Raṅgarāmānuja: the Supreme within the senses

The one by whom people see the whole dream world and waking world is the Supreme Self, which has become, as it were, the mind and senses, इन्द्रियभावमापन्नेन येन परमात्मना; the second line he leaves as already explained, उक्तोऽर्थः.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

A repeated line

The second line, mahāntaṃ vibhum ātmānaṃ matvā dhīro na śocati, is the second line of 1.2.22.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.1.4

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The witness of dream and waking is the self; knowing it directly as ‘I am the Supreme Self’, साक्षादहमस्मि परमात्मेति, the wise one does not grieve.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.1.4

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The one by whom the world sees in both states is the Supreme Self acting through the senses, इन्द्रियभावमापन्नेन येन परमात्मना.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

For Śaṅkara the witness is one’s own self, identical with the Supreme; for Raṅgarāmānuja it is the Supreme as the inner ground of the senses, distinct from the seeing self. In our source Madhva’s comment filed with 2.1.4–5 concerns 2.1.5 only.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • स्वप्नान्तं जागरितान्तं svapnāntaṃ jāgaritāntaṃ → स्वप्नान्तम् + जागरितान्तम्

    anusvāra

    Final m → anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • चोभौ cobhau → च + उभौ

    guṇa

    a + u → o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • येनानुपश्यति yenānupaśyati → येन + अनुपश्यति

    savarṇa-dīrgha

    a + a → ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • धीरो न dhīro na → धीरः + न

    aḥ → o

    The ru of -aḥ becomes u before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च), and a + u → o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • स्वप्नान्तम् svapnāntam

    ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa · स्वप्न + अन्त

    Vigraha स्वप्नस्य अन्तः

    the ‘end’ (state, sphere) of dream; Śaṅkara takes anta as ‘middle’

  • जागरितान्तम् jāgaritāntam

    ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa · जागरित + अन्त

    Vigraha जागरितस्य अन्तः

    the state of waking

Morphology

अनुपश्यति
Present of dṛś (suppletive stem paśya-) with anu: ‘see along, see throughout’.
मत्वा
Absolutive of man ‘think’ (-tvā).
महान्तम्
Accusative singular of mahat; the strong stem mahānt- has the long vowel.

Anvaya

स्वप्नान्तम् जागरितान्तम् च उभौ येन [लोकः] अनुपश्यति, [तम्] महान्तम् विभुम् आत्मानम् मत्वा धीरः न शोचति ॥

svapnāntam jāgaritāntam ca ubhau yena lokaḥ anupaśyati, tam mahāntam vibhum ātmānam matvā dhīraḥ na śocati ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A relative clause (yena anupaśyati, objects svapnāntaṃ jāgaritāntaṃ ca ubhau) whose correlative is supplied — Raṅgarāmānuja: ‘tam is to be supplied with mahāntam’, महान्तम् इत्यत्र तम् इति शेषः. The main clause has the absolutive matvā with a double accusative (ātmānam, qualified by mahāntaṃ vibhum).

Ambiguities

By what does one see?
Śaṅkara: by the self, येन आत्मना अनुपश्यति लोकः, to be known as ‘I am the Supreme Self’, साक्षादहमस्मि परमात्मेति. Raṅgarāmānuja: by the Supreme Self, which has taken the role of the mind and senses, इन्द्रियभावमापन्नेन येन परमात्मना.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16

Two half-lines of 16 syllables, four pādas of eight.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Grief

Moods and states come and go — dreaming, waking, good days and bad. The verse suggests that grief loosens when one notices what is present through all of them. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin In your last vivid dream, who was having it?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why should knowing the witness of dream and waking end grief?

A contemplation exercise On waking tomorrow, before getting up, notice what was there both in the dream and now.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
