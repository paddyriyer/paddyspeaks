Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 11 of 18

This they call yoga

That firm holding of the senses they consider yoga; then one becomes heedful, for yoga comes and goes.

5 Faithful English translation

That they consider yoga — the firm holding of the senses. Heedful then one becomes; for yoga [is] arising and passing away.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

This steady holding of the senses is what they call yoga. At that time one must be watchful, for yoga comes and goes.

Key concepts योग yoga, the holding of the senses इन्द्रिय indriya, a sense अध्यात्मयोग inward yoga मनस् mind

7 Explain it to a beginner

The previous verse described the senses, mind and intellect all coming to rest. Here that state gets its name: yoga, the steady holding of the senses. The verse then gives a warning. At such a time one must be watchful, because yoga ‘comes and goes’: a state that arises can also slip away. Śaṅkara points out that people call it yoga, joining, though it is really a separation from everything that brings harm. He adds that the watchfulness has to be practised beforehand, since once the mind is fully still there is no room left for carelessness. Raṅgarāmānuja gives the same warning and a second reading of the last words: yoga is the means of gaining what is good and removing what is bad. Madhva reads the arising and passing away as coming from the Lord.

An analogy Holding a full cup steady is easy for a moment; the skill is not letting it tip when your attention wanders.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

The steady holding of the senses is called yoga; then one must be heedful, for yoga arises and passes away.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: a joining that is a separation

They call it yoga though it is really disjunction, योगमिति मन्यन्ते वियोगमेव सन्तम्: this state of the yogin is marked by separation from union with every harm, सर्वानर्थसंयोगवियोगलक्षणा हीयमवस्था योगिनः. Heedful means free from carelessness and always making effort towards composure, प्रमादवर्जितः समाधानं प्रति नित्यं यत्नवान्. Yoga has arising and passing away, उपजनापायधर्मक इत्यर्थः, so heedfulness must guard against its loss, अतः अपायपरिहारायाप्रमादः कर्तव्य इत्यभिप्रायः.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: a highest course that must be guarded

The holding of the outer and inner faculties named in the last verse is called yoga, and he quotes Vyāsārya: परमागतिर्योग. Yoga can lapse at any moment, so attention is needed, योगस्य प्रतिक्षणापायशालितया अवधानम् अपेक्षितमिति भावः; or, since yoga is the means to every aim, इष्टप्रभवानिष्टाप्ययलक्षणसर्वपुरुषार्थसाधनत्वात् योगस्य, one must be heedful in it.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: arising and passing away from the Lord

He glosses the last words: yoga is towards arising and passing away, प्रभवाप्ययौ प्रति हि योगः, and these come from the Lord, भगवतस्सकाशात् प्रभवाप्ययौ.

Comparative Vedānta

The Gītā’s definition

Śaṅkara’s phrase, a ‘joining’ that is a separation from harm, matches the Gītā’s definition of yoga as disjunction from union with sorrow: तं विद्याद्दुःखसंयोगवियोगं योगसञ्ज्ञितम्.

Yoga is separation from every harm, सर्वानर्थसंयोगवियोगलक्षणा हीयमवस्था योगिनः; because it can be lost, heedfulness must be practised.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.3.11

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Yoga is the highest course and the means to every human aim, and must be guarded because it can lapse at any moment, योगस्य प्रतिक्षणापायशालितया अवधानम् अपेक्षितमिति भावः.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.3.9–11

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The arising and passing away the verse speaks of come from the Lord, भगवतस्सकाशात् प्रभवाप्ययौ.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara and Raṅgarāmānuja read prabhavāpyayau of yoga itself, which comes and goes; Raṅgarāmānuja adds that it brings what is good and removes what is bad. Madhva’s short gloss ties the arising and passing away to the Lord.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • तां योगमिति tāṃ yogamiti → ताम् + योगम् + इति

    m before a consonant; m before a vowel

    Final m → anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः). Final m stays before a vowel and is joined to it.

  • स्थिरामिन्द्रियधारणाम् sthirāmindriyadhāraṇām → स्थिराम् + इन्द्रियधारणाम्

    m before a vowel

    Final m stays before a vowel and is joined to it.

  • अप्रमत्तस्तदा apramattastadā → अप्रमत्तः + तदा

    visarga before t

    Visarga before t becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः).

  • योगो हि yogo hi → योगः + हि

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • इन्द्रियधारणाम् indriyadhāraṇām

    ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa · इन्द्रिय + धारणा

    Vigraha इन्द्रियाणां धारणा

    the holding (steady) of the senses

  • प्रभवाप्ययौ prabhavāpyayau

    itaretara-dvandva · प्रभव + अप्यय

    Vigraha प्रभवश्च अप्ययश्च

    arising and passing away

Morphology

प्रभवाप्ययौ
A dvandva in the dual, set as predicate to the singular yogaḥ: ‘yoga is arising-and-passing-away’, that is, it has both. Śaṅkara takes it so, उपजनापायधर्मक इत्यर्थः; Raṅgarāmānuja gives that sense and a second, ‘the means of bringing about what is wanted and removing what is unwanted’ (see the commentary).
अप्रमत्तः
a- + pramatta, past participle of pra + √mad, ‘careless, distracted’: ‘not careless’.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

tām (the state of 2.3.10) is the object of manyante with yogam iti as its predicate; sthirām indriyadhāraṇām stands in apposition to tām. The second line has a short clause with the subject understood (apramattas tadā bhavati) and a verbless reason clause, yogo hi prabhavāpyayau.

Ambiguities

Why must one be heedful ‘then’?
Śaṅkara: when the intellect has stopped there is no room for carelessness, so the heedfulness is enjoined for the time before, तस्मात्प्रागेव बुद्ध्यादिचेष्टोपरमात् अप्रमादो विधीयते; or else, once the holding of the senses is firm, heedfulness is unhindered. Raṅgarāmānuja: attentiveness comes when the senses stop working, इन्द्रियाणां निर्व्यापारत्वे एव अवहितचित्तता भवति.
‘yogo hi prabhavāpyayau’
Śaṅkara and Raṅgarāmānuja: yoga arises and perishes, so watchfulness is needed, योगस्य प्रतिक्षणापायशालितया अवधानम् अपेक्षितमिति भावः (Raṅgarāmānuja). Raṅgarāmānuja also gives a second reading: yoga is the means to every human aim, bringing what is wanted and removing what is not, इष्टप्रभवानिष्टाप्ययलक्षणसर्वपुरुषार्थसाधनत्वात् योगस्य. Madhva reads the arising and passing away as coming from the Lord (see the commentary).

Metre

irregular · syllables counted as written: 16 + 17

Counted as written, 16 and 17 syllables. The first line is a regular anuṣṭubh line (8 + 8). In the second, apramattas tadā bhavati has nine syllables; yogo hi prabhavāpyayau has eight.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Self-mastery

Any discipline that is hard to reach is easy to lose. The verse names the stillness of the senses and in the same breath warns that it does not stay by itself. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What good habit of yours comes and goes, and what keeps it?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Śaṅkara calls yoga a ‘separation’. From what, and why might the word still be ‘joining’?

A contemplation exercise When a moment of quiet comes, notice what is most likely to end it.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
