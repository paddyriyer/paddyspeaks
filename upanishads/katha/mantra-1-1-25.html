Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 25 of 29

The women with chariots and music

Death’s last and richest offer: every desire hard to win in the world of mortals, and celestial women with chariots and instruments, to wait on him — only he must not ask about death.

5 Faithful English translation

Whatever desires [are] hard to obtain in the world of mortals, ask for all desires at will. These delightful women with chariots, with instruments — for such are not to be obtained by men — by these, given by me, have yourself served. Naciketas, do not ask about death.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Whatever pleasures are hard to come by in the world of mortals, ask for them all as you please. Here are these lovely women, with their chariots and their music — no man could win the like. Let them, my gift to you, wait upon you. Only, Naciketas, do not ask me about death.

Key concepts मृत्यु death भोग enjoyment, use वर boon

7 Explain it to a beginner

This is Death’s richest offer. Ask for every pleasure that is hard to find among mortals, he says, as much as you like. And look: here are beautiful women with chariots and music, such as no human being could ever win. Take them as my gift, and let them wait on you. Just do not ask me about death. Śaṅkara says Death treats the question as useless, like examining a crow’s teeth; the commentators all agree that the offer is the last and hardest part of the test. Having seen this, the reader waits for the boy’s answer in the next verse.

An analogy Like a negotiator who, after every other offer has failed, puts the most dazzling thing on the table and adds, almost in passing, ‘and let us not discuss that other matter.’

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What is offered?

Every desire hard to obtain among mortals, as many as he wants; celestial women with chariots and instruments, such as men cannot win, to wait on him; on condition that he does not ask about death.

Traditional Interpretation

Who are the women?

Śaṅkara: celestial apsarases, called rāmāḥ because they delight men — रमयन्ति पुरुषानिति रामाः — with chariots and instruments, सह रथैर्वर्तन्त इति सरथाः, सतूर्याः सवादित्राः, unobtainable by men without Death’s favour. They are offered as attendants: पादप्रक्षालनादिशुश्रूषां कारयात्मन इत्यर्थः.

Comparative Vedānta

What does ‘do not ask about death’ forbid?

For Śaṅkara, the question whether a man exists after death, which Death calls as idle as a crow’s teeth: प्रेत्यास्ति नास्तीति काकदन्तपरीक्षारूपं. For Raṅgarāmānuja, ‘after death’ means after release, so the forbidden question is about the nature of the freed self: मुक्तात्मस्वरूपमिति यावत्. For Madhva, it is the Lord who abides in release: मरणे स्थितं भगवन्तं मानुप्राक्षीः.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.25

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The forbidden question is whether one is or is not after death; Death dismisses it as idle — काकदन्तपरीक्षारूपं — to see whether the boy will let it go.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.1.25

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

maraṇam here is, by the context, release from the body and all bondage; the question is about the freed self — मुक्तात्मस्वरूपमिति यावत् — and the narrowed sense is justified by the context: प्रकरणवशेन विशेषवाचित्वं न दोषाय इति द्रष्टव्यम्.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya on 1.1.22–25

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Madhva’s whole comment on the passage glosses the last words: do not ask about the Lord who stands in ‘death’ — मरणे स्थितं भगवन्तं मानुप्राक्षीः. On 1.1.20 he has explained ‘death’ as release, since release too is a leaving of the body.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara keeps ‘death’ in its plain sense (survival after death); Raṅgarāmānuja reads it as release, and Madhva as the Lord present in release.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • कामा दुर्लभा मर्त्यलोके kāmā durlabhā martyaloke → कामाः + दुर्लभाः + मर्त्यलोके

    āḥ before a voiced consonant

    āḥ before a voiced sound drops its visarga: the y that it becomes is elided, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि) and P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य).

  • कामांश्छन्दतः kāmāṃśchandataḥ → कामान् + छन्दतः

    n before ch

    Final n before ch becomes ru with a nasalised vowel, P. 8.3.7 (नश्छव्यप्रशान्); the ś then joins the palatal, P. 8.4.40 (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः): kāmāṃś chandataḥ.

  • न हीदृशा na hīdṛśā → न + हि + ईदृशाः

    savarṇa-dīrgha

    hi + īdṛśāḥ: i + ī give ī, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • मानुप्राक्षीः mānuprākṣīḥ → मा + अनुप्राक्षीः

    savarṇa-dīrgha

    mā + anuprākṣīḥ: ā + a give ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

    given by me; Śaṅkara: मया प्रदत्ताभिः

Morphology

छन्दतः
Ablative adverb in -tas from chandas ‘wish’: ‘according to wish’ (Śaṅkara: इच्छातः).
परिचारयस्व
Causative middle imperative of pari-car: ‘have [yourself] attended’.
अनुप्राक्षीः
s-aorist of anu-prach without augment, the injunctive after mā: ‘do not ask after’.

Anvaya

ये ये कामाः मर्त्यलोके दुर्लभाः [ते] सर्वान् कामान् छन्दतः प्रार्थयस्व । इमाः सरथाः सतूर्याः रामाः — न हि ईदृशाः मनुष्यैः लम्भनीयाः — मत्प्रत्ताभिः आभिः परिचारयस्व । नचिकेतः, मरणम् मा अनुप्राक्षीः ।

ye ye kāmāḥ martyaloke durlabhāḥ te sarvān kāmān chandataḥ prārthayasva | imāḥ sarathāḥ satūryāḥ rāmāḥ — na hi īdṛśāḥ manuṣyaiḥ lambhanīyāḥ — matprattābhiḥ ābhiḥ paricārayasva | naciketaḥ, maraṇam mā anuprākṣīḥ |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A distributive relative (ye ye … durlabhāḥ) answered by sarvān kāmān; a presentation of the women in the nominative (imā rāmāḥ …) broken by a parenthesis (na hīdṛśā …), then taken up again by the instrumental ābhiḥ; and a closing prohibition with mā and the injunctive.

Ambiguities

maraṇam
Śaṅkara takes ‘death’ as the question about death — whether one exists after it: मरणसम्बद्धं प्रश्नं प्रेत्यास्ति नास्तीति काकदन्तपरीक्षारूपं मा अनुप्राक्षीः, calling it, from Death’s side, as idle as examining a crow’s teeth. Raṅgarāmānuja takes maraṇam anu as ‘after death’, i.e. the state of release — मुक्तात्मस्वरूपमिति यावत् — and defends the narrowing of the word: प्रकरणवशेन विशेषवाचित्वं न दोषाय इति द्रष्टव्यम्.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 21 + 23

Six pādas (a triṣṭubh stanza with an added half). The lines count 22, 21 and 23: the pāda imā rāmāḥ sarathāḥ satūryā has 10, and ābhir mat-prattābhiḥ paricārayasva has 12; the others have 11.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Technology and digital distractions

The last temptation is not wealth but pleasure that waits on us, endlessly and on demand — offered on condition that we stop asking the uncomfortable question. Many things are offered on the same terms today. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Which is harder to refuse: money, or pleasure that serves you?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why does Death attach a condition — ‘do not ask about death’ — to the richest offer?

A contemplation exercise For one evening, set aside one thing that entertains you on demand, and sit for a few minutes with a question you usually avoid.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
