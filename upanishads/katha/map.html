Naciketas and the boons · 1.1.1, 1.1.2, 1.1.3, 1.1.4, 1.1.5, 1.1.6, 1.1.7, 1.1.8, 1.1.9, 1.1.10, 1.1.11, 1.1.12, 1.1.13, 1.1.14, 1.1.15, 1.1.16, 1.1.17, 1.1.18, 1.1.19

A father gives away all he owns; his son asks to whom he will be given, and is sent to Death. Three nights unfed at Death’s house earn him three boons; the first two are his father’s good will and the fire that leads to heaven.

  • the third boon → The third boon — The third boon (1.1.20) is the question about the man who has died.
  • Naciketas attains Brahman → the story returns — The last mantra names Naciketas again: the teaching he asked for in 1.1.20 has been given.

Study: The three boons

The third boon · 1.1.20, 1.1.21, 1.1.22, 1.1.23, 1.1.24, 1.1.25, 1.1.26, 1.1.27, 1.1.28, 1.1.29

‘When a man has died, some say he is, others that he is not’: Naciketas asks to know this. Death offers sons, wealth, long life and pleasures instead; Naciketas refuses them all and holds to his question.

  • Naciketas and the boons → the third boon — The third boon (1.1.20) is the question about the man who has died.
  • refused, then taught → The good and the pleasant — Naciketas refuses every gift offered in place of his question (1.1.21–29); Death’s teaching opens with the good and the pleasant (1.2.1).

Study: The refusal: tests of the seeker

The good and the pleasant · 1.2.1, 1.2.2, 1.2.3, 1.2.4, 1.2.5, 1.2.6, 1.2.7, 1.2.8, 1.2.9, 1.2.10, 1.2.11, 1.2.12, 1.2.13

Death begins his teaching with a choice: the good and the pleasant both bind a person; the wise choose the good. The self is hard to hear of, not reached by reasoning, and needs a teacher.

  • The third boon → refused, then taught — Naciketas refuses every gift offered in place of his question (1.1.21–29); Death’s teaching opens with the good and the pleasant (1.2.1).
  • beyond dharma → Om, the word of all the Vedas — Naciketas asks what Death sees beyond dharma and adharma (1.2.14); the answer is Om (1.2.15).

Study: The good and the pleasant

Om, the word of all the Vedas · 1.2.14, 1.2.15, 1.2.16, 1.2.17

Asked what he sees beyond dharma and adharma, beyond what is done and undone, past and future, Death names the goal all the Vedas declare: Om. This syllable is Brahman, the best support.

  • The good and the pleasant → beyond dharma — Naciketas asks what Death sees beyond dharma and adharma (1.2.14); the answer is Om (1.2.15).
  • then → The unborn self — After Om (1.2.15–17) comes ‘the knower is not born and does not die’ (1.2.18).

Study: The hidden one and the syllable Om

The unborn self · 1.2.18, 1.2.19, 1.2.20, 1.2.21, 1.2.22, 1.2.23, 1.2.24, 1.2.25

The knower is not born and does not die; smaller than the small, greater than the great, it lies in the heart of beings. It is not gained by instruction, intellect or much learning, but by the one it chooses.

  • Om, the word of all the Vedas → then — After Om (1.2.15–17) comes ‘the knower is not born and does not die’ (1.2.18).
  • then → Two in the cave — The second vallī ends with the self that chooses (1.2.23–25); the third opens with the two in the cave (1.3.1).

Study: The unborn self and the one he chooses

Two in the cave · 1.3.1, 1.3.2

Two drink the truth in the world of good deeds, entered into the cave, in the highest beyond — shade and light, as the knowers of Brahman say.

  • The unborn self → then — The second vallī ends with the self that chooses (1.2.23–25); the third opens with the two in the cave (1.3.1).
  • then → The chariot — 1.3.3 begins the image of the chariot.

Study: Two in the cave

The chariot · 1.3.3, 1.3.4, 1.3.5, 1.3.6, 1.3.7, 1.3.8, 1.3.9

The self is the rider, the body the chariot, the intellect the charioteer, the mind the reins, the senses the horses. The one whose charioteer is understanding reaches the end of the road, Viṣṇu’s highest place.

  • Two in the cave → then — 1.3.3 begins the image of the chariot.
  • the end of the road → The ladder and the razor’s edge — The chariot reaches ‘the end of the road’ (1.3.9); the next mantras say what lies beyond, step by step (1.3.10–11).

Study: The chariot

The ladder and the razor’s edge · 1.3.10, 1.3.11, 1.3.12, 1.3.13, 1.3.14, 1.3.15

Beyond the senses the objects, beyond them the mind, the intellect, the great self, the unmanifest, the person — beyond the person nothing. Hidden in all beings, it is seen by a subtle intellect. ‘Arise, awake’: the path is a razor’s edge.

  • The chariot → the end of the road — The chariot reaches ‘the end of the road’ (1.3.9); the next mantras say what lies beyond, step by step (1.3.10–11).
  • why it is not seen → The eye turned inward — Śaṅkara (introducing 2.1): 1.3.12 said the self is seen by a subtle intellect; the second adhyāya begins by showing what blocks that intellect. Raṅgarāmānuja (on 2.1.1): even after ‘arise, awake’ people turn away from the self, and the text grieves over them.
  • the series again → The series again, and yoga — 2.3.7–8 restate the series of 1.3.10–11, with ‘the highest being’ (sattva) in place of the intellect.

Study: The ladder and the razor’s edge

The eye turned inward · 2.1.1, 2.1.2

The self-existent pierced the openings outward, so one looks outward, not at the self within. Some wise one, seeking the immortal, turned his eye inward and saw the inner self.

  • The ladder and the razor’s edge → why it is not seen — Śaṅkara (introducing 2.1): 1.3.12 said the self is seen by a subtle intellect; the second adhyāya begins by showing what blocks that intellect. Raṅgarāmānuja (on 2.1.1): even after ‘arise, awake’ people turn away from the self, and the text grieves over them.
  • then → This, truly, is that — 2.1.3 continues: that by which one knows form and taste — ‘this, truly, is that’.

Study: The openings turned outward

This, truly, is that · 2.1.3, 2.1.4, 2.1.5, 2.1.6, 2.1.7, 2.1.8, 2.1.9

That by which one knows form, taste, smell, sounds and touch; the knower of dream and waking; the honey-eater near at hand; the first-born, Aditi, the fire in the fire-sticks — each ends with the refrain ‘this, truly, is that’.

  • The eye turned inward → then — 2.1.3 continues: that by which one knows form and taste — ‘this, truly, is that’.
  • then → What is here is there — After 2.1.9, ‘what is here is there’ (2.1.10).

Study: This, truly, is that

What is here is there · 2.1.10, 2.1.11, 2.1.14, 2.1.15

What is here is there; from death to death goes the one who sees difference here. As rain on a mountain runs off down the slopes, so does one who sees things apart; as pure water poured into pure water, so the self of the sage who knows.

  • This, truly, is that → then — After 2.1.9, ‘what is here is there’ (2.1.10).
  • then → The thumb-sized person — 2.1.12–13, the thumb-sized person, stand between 2.1.10–11 and 2.1.14–15.

Study: What is here is there

The thumb-sized person · 2.1.12, 2.1.13

A person the size of a thumb stands in the middle of the body, lord of past and future, like a flame without smoke. At the close of the Upaniṣad (2.3.17) he returns as the inner self, to be drawn from the body like a reed from its sheath.

  • What is here is there → then — 2.1.12–13, the thumb-sized person, stand between 2.1.10–11 and 2.1.14–15.
  • drawn out → Naciketas attains Brahman — The thumb-sized inner self returns in 2.3.17, to be drawn from the body; 2.3.18 follows.

Study: What is here is there

The city of eleven gates · 2.2.1, 2.2.2, 2.2.3, 2.2.4, 2.2.5, 2.2.6, 2.2.7, 2.2.8

The body is the city of the unborn, with eleven gates. The one who leads the breaths, the person awake in those who sleep, shaping desire after desire — that is the bright, that is Brahman.

  • then → The light by which all shines — The second vallī moves from the city of the body (2.2.1–8) to the one fire and the one light (2.2.9–15).
  • the same words → The eternal aśvattha — ‘That is the bright, that is Brahman … on it all the worlds rest; no one goes beyond it’ closes 2.2.8 and is repeated in 2.3.1.

Study: The city of eleven gates

The light by which all shines · 2.2.9, 2.2.10, 2.2.11, 2.2.12, 2.2.13, 2.2.14, 2.2.15

As one fire takes the form of each thing it enters, so the one inner self of all beings; as the sun is not soiled by the eye’s faults, it is not soiled by the world’s pain. There the sun does not shine; all this shines by its light.

  • The city of eleven gates → then — The second vallī moves from the city of the body (2.2.1–8) to the one fire and the one light (2.2.9–15).

Study: The light by which all shines

The eternal aśvattha · 2.3.1, 2.3.2, 2.3.3

The eternal fig tree with its roots above and branches below: that is the bright, that is Brahman, on which all the worlds rest. From fear of it fire burns and the sun gives heat.

  • The city of eleven gates → the same words — ‘That is the bright, that is Brahman … on it all the worlds rest; no one goes beyond it’ closes 2.2.8 and is repeated in 2.3.1.

Study: The Kaṭha as one teaching

The series again, and yoga · 2.3.7, 2.3.8, 2.3.9, 2.3.10, 2.3.11, 2.3.12, 2.3.13

Beyond the senses the mind, beyond it the highest being, the great self, the unmanifest, the all-pervading person. When the five senses and the mind are still and the intellect does not stir: the firm holding of the senses is yoga.

  • The ladder and the razor’s edge → the series again — 2.3.7–8 restate the series of 1.3.10–11, with ‘the highest being’ (sattva) in place of the intellect.
  • then → The knots of the heart — After yoga (2.3.10–11) and ‘it is’ (2.3.12–13) come the desires and knots of the heart (2.3.14–15).
  • the rule of yoga → Naciketas attains Brahman — 2.3.18 says Naciketas received ‘the whole rule of yoga’. Raṅgarāmānuja (on 2.3.18) takes it as the yoga taught from ‘when the five…’ (2.3.10); Śaṅkara (on 2.3.18): the rule of yoga entire, with its aids and its fruit.

Study: The Kaṭha as one teaching

The knots of the heart · 2.3.14, 2.3.15, 2.3.16

When all the desires that dwell in the heart are let go, the mortal becomes immortal; when all the knots of the heart are cut, ‘this much is the teaching’.

  • The series again, and yoga → then — After yoga (2.3.10–11) and ‘it is’ (2.3.12–13) come the desires and knots of the heart (2.3.14–15).

Naciketas attains Brahman · 2.3.18

Having received from Death this knowledge and the whole rule of yoga, Naciketas reached Brahman, free of passion and of death — and so does anyone else who knows the self in this way.
