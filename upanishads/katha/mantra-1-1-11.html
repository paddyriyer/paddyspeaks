Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 11 of 29

The first boon granted: the father’s welcome

Yama grants the first boon at once: Auddālaki will know his son again, sleep in peace and be free of anger, seeing him released from the jaws of death.

5 Faithful English translation

‘As before, Auddālaki Āruṇi will be recognising [you], released by me; he will sleep the nights happily, free of anger, having seen you released from the mouth of death.’

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘Your father will know you as before. With my leave, Auddālaki Āruṇi will sleep peacefully through the nights, his anger gone, once he has seen you freed from the jaws of death.’

Key concepts मृत्यु death वर boon

7 Explain it to a beginner

Naciketas had asked, as his first boon, that his father should be at peace with him again and recognise him when he came home. Yama does not argue; he simply grants it. Your father, he says, will know you as he knew you before. He will sleep well at night, his anger gone, because he will see that you have come back from death. The scene is human before it is philosophical: a son who was given away in anger wants above all to be welcomed home. The Upaniṣad lets the first boon be about family and forgiveness before it turns to heaven and then to the self.

An analogy Like a child who has run away in a quarrel and, before asking anything else, asks only that the door be opened to him again.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does Yama grant?

The first boon exactly as asked in 1.1.10: the father will recognise the son, sleep in peace and lose his anger, seeing him back from death. Yama speaks in the definite future (bhavitā, śayitā).

Traditional Interpretation

Why does the father’s anger matter?

Śaṅkara reads the boon as a return of the father’s former affection: यथा बुद्धिः त्वयि पुरस्तात् पूर्वम् आसीत्स्नेहसमन्विता पितुस्तव. He glosses the father’s state as one of peace: सुखं प्रसन्नमनाः शयिता स्वप्ता वीतमन्युः विगतमन्युश्च. Raṅgarāmānuja says the same with ‘by my grace’: मदनुग्रहादित्यर्थः.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Madhva place the three boons?

Madhva’s commentary quotes a verse from the work he calls Gatisāra naming the three boons as the father’s good will, Hari in the Nāciketa fire, and Viṣṇu who abides in the freed soul: सौमनस्यं पितुश्चैव नाचिकेताग्निगं हरिम्. The first boon, then, is the one granted here: the father’s good will.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What forms are these?

bhavitā and śayitā are periphrastic futures (luṭ), the tense of a definite, appointed future; dadṛśivān is a perfect participle. Raṅgarāmānuja comments on śayitā: लुट्, सुखनिद्रां प्राप्स्यतीति यावत्.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.11

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

A plain reading of the narrative: Yama permits the father to be calm and affectionate again, मत्प्रसृष्टः मयानुज्ञातः सन्. Śaṅkara draws no doctrine from the first boon; it prepares the way for the second and third.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.1.11

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The same plain sense, with Yama’s grace as the cause of the father’s calm: मदनुग्रहादित्यर्थः. Raṅgarāmānuja notes the alternative reading with the son as the one sent back.

Dvaita · Madhva (Gatisāra, as he cites it), Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.1.10–11

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The first boon is the father’s good will, as the verse Madhva cites puts it: सौमनस्यं पितुश्चैव. The same verse makes the second and third boons Hari in the Nāciketa fire and Viṣṇu abiding in the freed soul: मुक्ते स्थितञ्च तं विष्णुमिति प्रादात् वरत्रयम्.

Where they disagree

There is no disagreement about this verse. The schools differ only in what the three boons together point to: Śaṅkara’s fire and heaven are steps of ritual, Madhva sees Hari behind the second and third.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • पुरस्ताद्भविता purastādbhavitā → पुरस्तात् + भविता

    consonant sandhi

    Final t before a voiced consonant becomes d: P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

  • प्रतीत औद्दालकिरारुणिर्मत्प्रसृष्टः pratīta auddālakirāruṇirmatprasṛṣṭaḥ → प्रतीतः + औद्दालकिः + आरुणिः + मत्प्रसृष्टः

    visarga sandhi

    The visarga of -aḥ before a vowel other than a is lost and the hiatus stays: P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि) with P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य). After i the visarga, which goes back to s, becomes r before a vowel or a voiced consonant: P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः), the ru kept as r.

  • वीतमन्युस्त्वाम् vītamanyustvām → वीतमन्युः + त्वाम्

    visarga before t

    Visarga before t becomes s: P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः).

  • ददृशिवान्मृत्युमुखात् dadṛśivānmṛtyumukhāt → ददृशिवान् + मृत्युमुखात्

    no change

    Final n before m stays.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • मत्प्रसृष्टः matprasṛṣṭaḥ

    tṛtīyā-tatpuruṣa · मत् + प्रसृष्ट

    Vigraha मया प्रसृष्टः

    released by me

  • वीतमन्युः vītamanyuḥ

    bahuvrīhi · वीत + मन्यु

    Vigraha वीतः मन्युः यस्य सः

    one whose anger has gone

  • मृत्युमुखात् mṛtyumukhāt

    ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa · मृत्यु + मुख

    Vigraha मृत्योः मुखम्

    the mouth of death

Morphology

भविता
The periphrastic future (luṭ) of bhū, used for a definite future: ‘he will be’.
शयिता
Periphrastic future of śī ‘lie, sleep’: ‘he will sleep’.
ददृशिवान्
Perfect active participle (kvasu) of dṛś with the connecting i. Raṅgarāmānuja’s text has दृशिवान्, which he explains as a Vedic form without reduplication: छान्दसो द्विर्वचनाभावः.
औद्दालकिः
A patronymic in -i from Uddālaka; Śaṅkara takes it as Uddālaka himself (the patronymic used of the father) and gives a second possibility, that he was the son of two families: द्व्यामुष्यायणो वा.

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

Two coordinated future statements with the same subject (Auddālaki Āruṇi): yathā purastād bhavitā pratītaḥ ‘he will be recognising, as before’, and sukhaṃ rātrīḥ śayitā vītamanyuḥ ‘he will sleep happily, free of anger’. The participle dadṛśivān with its object tvām … pramuktam gives the reason. matprasṛṣṭaḥ qualifies the father: he acts by Yama’s leave.

Ambiguities

Who is ‘released by me’ — the father or the son?
The base text reads matprasṛṣṭaḥ (nominative, with the father): ‘Auddālaki, permitted by me’; Śaṅkara: मत्प्रसृष्टः मयानुज्ञातः सन्. Raṅgarāmānuja knows a reading with the accusative, which would go with the son: मत्प्रसृष्टम् इति द्वितीयान्तपाठे मत्प्रेषितं त्वाम् इति योजना — ‘you, sent back by me’.
Auddālaki: son of Uddālaka, or Uddālaka himself?
The patronymic would normally mean ‘son of Uddālaka’, but the person meant is Naciketas’s father, called Vājaśravasa in 1.1.1. Śaṅkara takes it as Uddālaka himself; Raṅgarāmānuja gives both: उद्दालकस्य अपत्यम्, अरुणस्य गोत्रापत्यं इति वाऽर्थः.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 23

Counted as written the two lines have 22 and 23 syllables; the extra syllable is in the second line (sukhaṃ rātrīḥ śayitā vītamanyus tvāṃ dadṛśivān …), where the Vedic metre allows a hypermetric pāda.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Family and reconciliation

The first thing Naciketas asks is not knowledge or reward but that his angry father should be at peace with him. Many people know that a quarrel at home can outweigh every other success, and that reconciliation can be the most urgent wish. The Upaniṣad places that wish first, and has it granted without conditions. (A modern reflection, not a claim about the text’s purpose.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin If you could ask for one relationship to be mended, which would it be?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why might the Upaniṣad grant the human wish first, before the questions about heaven and the self?

A contemplation exercise Think of someone with whom you have a misunderstanding. Without deciding anything, wish them a night of untroubled sleep.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
