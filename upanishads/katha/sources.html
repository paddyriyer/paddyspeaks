Corrections to the base witness

  • Mantra 1.1.17: निचाय्य मां → निचाय्येमां (typo) — The base file reads निचाय्य मां (‘having perceived me’) where GRETIL, the vishvAsa mūla and Madhva’s text all read निचाय्येमां (nicāyya imāṃ, ‘having perceived this [peace]’); Śaṅkara’s gloss in the same file comments on imām: निचाय्य दृष्ट्वा चात्मभावेन इमां स्वबुद्धिप्रत्यक्षां शान्तिम्.

    Open questions of the studies

    • Naciketas: the story and its frame: Who speaks 1.1.7 — Death’s ministers or wife (Śaṅkara), the elders at the door (Raṅgarāmānuja), or Yama’s wife (Madhva)? The text does not say.
    • Naciketas: the story and its frame: Whether ‘first of many, in the middle of many’ (1.1.5) concerns the boy’s conduct among pupils (Śaṅkara) or his place among those who go to Death (Raṅgarāmānuja).
    • The three boons: What svarga means in 1.1.12–1.1.13: the heaven of the fire-rite (Śaṅkara), the place of release (Raṅgarāmānuja), or heaven as a stage on the way to release (the verses Madhva cites).
    • The three boons: The extra mantra after 1.1.18 in the text printed with Raṅgarāmānuja: its manuscript basis needs a printed critical edition.
    • The three boons: Whether ‘prete’ in 1.1.20 means a man who has died or a self that has been released.
    • The refusal: tests of the seeker: Whether the ‘subtle dharma’ of 1.1.21 is the self (Śaṅkara) or the Lord (Madhva) — the word allows both and the context does not settle it.
    • The refusal: tests of the seeker: Which reading of 1.1.28 is older: kvadhaḥsthaḥ (the base, with Śaṅkara), kva tadāsthaḥ (Raṅgarāmānuja; noted by Śaṅkara), or Madhva’s kvādhasthaḥ.
    • The refusal: tests of the seeker: Whether Death’s reluctance tests the boy’s worth, his steadiness, or both: the text gives no reason of its own.
    • The good and the pleasant: Who the ‘non-other’ of 1.2.8 is: a teacher one with what he teaches (Śaṅkara), one devoted to it alone (Raṅgarāmānuja), or one who does not see the difference between the Lord and the soul, whose teaching gives no knowledge (Madhva). The compound allows all three.
    • The good and the pleasant: Whether the boy is vidyābhīpsin, ‘longing for knowledge’ (the base and Śaṅkara), or vidyābhīpsita, ‘longed for by knowledge’ (Madhva’s text, and a reading Raṅgarāmānuja knew).
    • The good and the pleasant: What exactly the ‘chain of wealth’ (sṛṅkā) of 1.2.3 is: Śaṅkara glosses it as a road (sṛti), Madhva as a chain (śṛṅkalā).
    • The hidden one and the syllable Om: How Death can say both that the permanent is not reached by impermanent things and that he reached it by a fire built of them: a relative permanence (Śaṅkara), the knowledge that leads to the permanent (Raṅgarāmānuja), or the Lord, reached by devotion (Madhva).
    • The hidden one and the syllable Om: Who speaks 1.2.14 and what it asks: Naciketas asking again (Śaṅkara), or a question that first asks about the means of release, knowledge unlike rites (Raṅgarāmānuja). The mantra names no speaker.
    • The hidden one and the syllable Om: Whether dharmya in 1.2.13 describes the self (Śaṅkara) or names the body that is drawn away from it (Raṅgarāmānuja).
    • The unborn self and the one he chooses: Who chooses in 1.2.23: the seeker, choosing the self (Śaṅkara), or the Supreme, choosing the seeker (Raṅgarāmānuja). The pronoun eṣa allows both; Madhva’s bhāṣya here gives no comment.
    • The unborn self and the one he chooses: Whether the line of 1.2.20 read dhātuprasādāt, ‘through the clarity of the faculties’ (the base, GRETIL and Śaṅkara), or dhātuḥ prasādāt, ‘through the grace of the Creator’ (Madhva’s text) — and whether Raṅgarāmānuja’s ‘grace of the supporter’ reads the compound or the divided form.
    • The unborn self and the one he chooses: Whether 1.2.18–1.2.19 describe the self that is Brahman (Śaṅkara), the individual self (Raṅgarāmānuja), or the knower of the unborn Lord (Madhva), and whether 1.2.20 changes the subject.
    • The unborn self and the one he chooses: The relation between these mantras and Bhagavad Gītā 2.19–2.20: the shared half-verses do not show which text is earlier.
    • Two in the cave: Who the two are: the individual self and the supreme self, of whom only one drinks (Śaṅkara); the soul and the Lord, the Lord ‘drinking’ as the one who causes the action (Raṅgarāmānuja); or one Lord in two forms (Madhva).
    • Two in the cave: How the Kaṭha’s pair, where both drink, relates to the Muṇḍaka’s two birds, where one does not eat.
    • Two in the cave: Madhva’s comment on these two mantras has gaps in the digital text; it should be read against a printed edition.
    • The chariot: Whether ātman in ‘ātmendriyamanoyukta’ (1.3.4) is the self (Śaṅkara) or the body (Raṅgarāmānuja), and whether the enjoying is the self’s own or borrowed from its adjuncts.
    • The chariot: How a ‘place’ that is reached can be one from which there is no return: Śaṅkara’s answer is that the going is figurative; the other readings take the reaching more literally.
    • The chariot: Madhva’s bhāṣya, in the digital text used here, has no comment on 1.3.3–1.3.9; his comment that follows concerns 1.3.10–1.3.12.
    • The ladder and the razor’s edge: What the ‘unmanifest’ of 1.3.11 is: the seed of the world, not yet unfolded (Śaṅkara on the Kaṭha), the body pictured as the chariot (the Brahmasūtra commentaries of Śaṅkara and of the Bhāṣya Raṅgarāmānuja quotes), or Ramā (Madhva) — and in every reading, not Sāṅkhya’s independent primal matter.
    • The ladder and the razor’s edge: Whether the ‘great self’ is Hiraṇyagarbha (Śaṅkara), the individual self as the rider (the passage Raṅgarāmānuja quotes), or Brahmā (Madhva).
    • The ladder and the razor’s edge: Whether the restraint of 1.3.13 ends in the self that is Brahman (Śaṅkara), in recognising that the self belongs to the Supreme (Raṅgarāmānuja), or in meditation on the presiding deities as governed by Viṣṇu (the verse Madhva cites).
    • The ladder and the razor’s edge: Madhva’s bhāṣya has no comment on 1.3.14–1.3.17.
    • The openings turned outward: Madhva’s gloss of the root of vyatṛṇat (‘तृणु कात्करणे’) is unclear in the digital text; a printed edition is needed to read it.
    • The openings turned outward: In Madhva’s text a passage that glosses ‘इदं गुह्यं ब्रह्म प्रवक्ष्यामि … यथा मरणं प्राप्य’ — words of 2.2.6 — stands after 2.1.3; whether it belongs there or has been displaced needs a printed edition.
    • The openings turned outward: Whether ‘the noose of wide-spread death’ is Death speaking of himself, as Raṅgarāmānuja’s second reading has it, or death as the bundle of ignorance, desire and action, as Śaṅkara reads it.
    • This, truly, is that: Whether ‘etad vai tat’ asserts identity (Śaṅkara), ‘having that as its self’ (Raṅgarāmānuja, from 2.1.7), or simply refers each description to Viṣṇu (Madhva).
    • This, truly, is that: Who ‘saw’ the first-born in 2.1.6: the seeker (Śaṅkara), or the Lord who looked on Brahmā (Raṅgarāmānuja); and whether ‘was born’ is causative (Madhva).
    • This, truly, is that: The passage that stands after 2.1.3 in Madhva’s text glosses words of 2.2.6; it is not used here as his comment on 2.1.3.
    • What is here is there: Whether tādṛg eva in 2.1.15 means identity (Śaṅkara), equality in purity (Raṅgarāmānuja), or likeness excluding identity (Madhva).
    • What is here is there: Whether the ‘dharmas’ of 2.1.14 are the self’s own (Śaṅkara), the Lord’s being the inner controller of each being (Raṅgarāmānuja), or the Lord’s parts, qualities and acts (Madhva).
    • What is here is there: The works Madhva cites here by name — a Śabdanirṇaya and a Bhaviṣyat-parvan — are reported as cited by him; this edition has not verified the quotations in those works.
    • The city of eleven gates: Whether the swan verse of 2.2.2 describes the sun (as Raṅgarāmānuja takes haṃsa, and as Śaṅkara allows) or the self, and how the two readings are reconciled.
    • The city of eleven gates: How the eleven gates are to be counted: bodily openings (Śaṅkara) or the outlets of the eleven senses (Raṅgarāmānuja).
    • The city of eleven gates: The verses in Madhva’s comment on 2.2.1–2.2.2 name no source; whether they are his own or quoted needs a printed edition.
    • What becomes of the self: Whether maraṇa in 2.2.6 means death followed by further birth (Śaṅkara, Madhva) or release (Raṅgarāmānuja).
    • What becomes of the self: Whether the births of 2.2.7 describe all who die without knowledge (Śaṅkara) or those who turn away from the teaching (Raṅgarāmānuja).
    • What becomes of the self: Madhva’s gloss on 2.2.6 and 2.2.8 stands after 2.1.3 in the digital text used here; whether it belongs there needs a printed edition.
    • What becomes of the self: The line Śaṅkara cites from the Aitareya Āraṇyaka, and the Bhāṣya and Śrutaprakāśikā Raṅgarāmānuja refers to, are reported as cited by them; this edition has not verified them in those works.
    • The light by which all shines: Whether 2.2.12 reads ‘one form’ (the base and the other witnesses) or ‘one seed’ (the text printed with Raṅgarāmānuja); the reading changes what is made many.
    • The light by which all shines: Whether anubhāti in 2.2.15 implies that the sun and the rest have a light of their own derived from the Supreme (Raṅgarāmānuja) or none apart from it (Śaṅkara’s firebrand).
    • The light by which all shines: The works Madhva cites here by name — a Mahākaurma and a Mahāvarāha — and the Bhāṣya and Vyāsārya passages Raṅgarāmānuja quotes are reported as cited by them; this edition has not verified them in those works.
