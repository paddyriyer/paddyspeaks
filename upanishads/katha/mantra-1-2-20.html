Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 20 of 25

Smaller than the small, greater than the great

The self, subtler than the subtle and greater than the great, is set in the cave of every creature; one free of desire sees its greatness, and is free of sorrow — through the calm of the faculties, or through the grace of the Ordainer.

5 Faithful English translation

‘More minute than the minute, greater than the great, the self is set in the cave of this creature. One without desire sees him, the greatness of the self, through the calm of the faculties, [and is] free of sorrow.’

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

‘Smaller than the smallest, greater than the greatest, the self lies hidden in the heart of every creature. A person free of craving, whose mind and senses have grown calm, sees that greatness of the self and leaves sorrow behind.’

Key concepts प्रसाद clarity, calm; grace आत्मन् self, Self गुहा the cave क्रतु resolve, will; the sacrifice शोक grief, sorrow ईश्वर the Lord

7 Explain it to a beginner

This is one of the most quoted verses of the Upaniṣads. The self is smaller than the smallest thing and greater than the greatest, and it lives hidden in the heart of every creature. Who sees it? A person who has stopped craving. That person sees the self's greatness and is free from sorrow. Then comes a famous puzzle in a single word. In the usual text it means 'through the calm of the faculties': when mind and senses settle, the self is seen, as Śaṅkara explains. Written slightly differently, as in Madhva's text, the same letters mean 'through the grace of the Creator'. Raṅgarāmānuja reads even the usual text as grace. So the verse asks whether seeing comes from inner stillness or as a gift, and the traditions answer differently.

An analogy Like the stars at noon: they are there all along, but you see them only when the sky’s glare has gone — or when someone opens the observatory for you.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

That the self is subtler than the subtlest and greater than the greatest, set in the cave of every creature, and that one without desire sees its greatness and is freed of sorrow, ‘through dhātu-prasāda’.

Traditional Interpretation

How can one thing be both smallest and greatest?

Śaṅkara: whatever is small or great in the world is real only through the self; apart from it nothing is, so the self takes on the measure of everything: सर्वनामरूपवस्तूपाधिकत्वात्. It is set in the heart of every being from Brahmā to a blade of grass, as their very self, आत्मभूतः स्थित इत्यर्थः.

Traditional Interpretation

What are the dhātus for Śaṅkara?

The mind and the senses, which sustain the body; when the seeker withdraws from desired ends they grow calm, and through their calm he sees the self’s greatness directly, अयमहमस्मीति साक्षाद्विजानाति, and so passes beyond sorrow.

Comparative Vedānta

How does Raṅgarāmānuja read it?

The ‘self of this creature’ is the Supreme, who enters the individual self of 1.2.18–19 and rules it from within, अन्तःप्रविश्य नियन्ता इत्यर्थः. Dhātu is the supporter, the Supreme, and prasāda his grace; he cites the Śrībhāṣya on the parallel Muṇḍaka verse, where the greatness seen is the Lord’s rule over the world, and he notes the reading धातुः प्रसादान्महिमानम् ईशम्.

Traditional Interpretation

And Madhva?

His mūla reads dhātuḥ prasādāt. He explains akratuḥ as one whose resolve is fixed on Viṣṇu, विष्णौ क्रतुर्यस्य सोऽक्रतुस्तन्निश्चयः, and mahimānam ātmanaḥ as one greater than the self, आत्मनः सकाशान्महिमानं महामानम्; a verse he cites says the one greater than the individual self in qualities and measure is Viṣṇu, तस्माज्जीवात् स महिमा विष्णुरित्युच्यते श्रुतौ.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What does the difference of one letter do?

dhātuprasādāt is a single compound; dhātuḥ prasādāt makes dhātuḥ a separate genitive of dhātṛ, ‘the Ordainer’. Both are in the witnesses (corrections.json); the metre does not decide between them. The Muṇḍaka’s parallel (3.1.2) has ‘when he sees the other, the Lord, and his greatness’ without either word.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.2.20

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The self’s greatness is seen when the faculties are calm: मनआदीनि करणानि धातवः शरीरस्य धारणात्. Seeing it is knowing ‘I am this’, which ends sorrow.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.2.20

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The verse turns from the individual self to the Supreme within it, and the seeing comes through the supporter’s grace: धारकस्य परमात्मनः. The greatness seen is the Supreme, cause of the individual self’s own qualities.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.2.20

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

With the reading dhātuḥ prasādāt, the seer is one whose resolve is on Viṣṇu, विष्णौ क्रतुर्यस्य सोऽक्रतुस्तन्निश्चयः, and what he sees is Viṣṇu, greater than the self, आत्मनः सकाशान्महिमानं महामानम्.

Where they disagree

Whether vision of the self comes through the seeker’s own calm (Śaṅkara, on dhātuprasādāt) or through the grace of the Lord (Raṅgarāmānuja on the same reading; Madhva’s text, dhātuḥ prasādāt), and whether the greatness seen is the self’s own (Śaṅkara) or the Lord’s (Raṅgarāmānuja, Madhva). The difference turns partly on one letter.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • अणोरणीयान्महतो aṇoraṇīyānmahato → अणोः + अणीयान् + महतः

    visarga and n

    After o the visarga becomes r before a vowel, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः); the final n stands before m; aḥ → o before m, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

  • महीयानात्मास्य mahīyānātmāsya → महीयान् + आत्मा + अस्य

    vowel sandhi

    The final n stands before a vowel and joins it; ā + a → ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • जन्तोर्निहितो गुहायाम् jantornihito guhāyām → जन्तोः + निहितः + गुहायाम्

    visarga sandhi

    After o the visarga becomes r before a voiced sound, P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः); aḥ → o before g, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

  • तमक्रतुः tamakratuḥ → तम् + अक्रतुः

    m before a vowel

    Final m stays before a vowel.

  • वीतशोको धातुप्रसादान्महिमानमात्मनः vītaśoko dhātuprasādānmahimānamātmanaḥ → वीतशोकः + धातुप्रसादात् + महिमानम् + आत्मनः

    visarga, t before a nasal, m before a vowel

    aḥ → o before d, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च); a final t before m becomes n, P. 8.4.45 (यरोऽनुनासिकेऽनुनासिको वा); final m stays before a vowel.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • अक्रतुः akratuḥ

    nañ-bahuvrīhi · अ + क्रतु

    Vigraha अविद्यमानः क्रतुः यस्य सः

    one who has no (wilful) intent, free of desire

  • वीतशोकः vītaśokaḥ

    bahuvrīhi · वीत + शोक

    Vigraha वीतः शोकः यस्य सः

    one whose sorrow has gone

  • धातुप्रसादात् dhātuprasādāt

    ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa · धातु + प्रसाद

    Vigraha धातूनां प्रसादः

    the calm of the faculties (Śaṅkara); read by Raṅgarāmānuja as the grace of the supporter, धातोः प्रसादः

Morphology

अणीयान्
Comparative in -īyas of aṇu ‘minute’, with the ablative of comparison अणोः: ‘more minute than the minute’; महीयान् is formed the same way from mahat.
धातुप्रसादात्
As written, one compound: dhātu ‘constituent, sustainer’ + prasāda ‘clearness, calm, favour’. Śaṅkara takes dhātavaḥ as the faculties (mind and senses), so called because they sustain the body. Madhva’s text writes dhātuḥ prasādāt, two words; dhātuḥ is then the genitive of dhātṛ ‘ordainer, creator’ (see Ambiguities).
आत्मनः
Genitive with महिमानम्, ‘the greatness of the self’; Madhva takes it as an ablative of comparison, ‘greater than the self’.

Anvaya

आत्मा अणोः अणीयान् महतः महीयान् अस्य जन्तोः गुहायाम् निहितः । अक्रतुः धातुप्रसादात् तम् आत्मनः महिमानम् पश्यति [ततः] वीतशोकः [भवति] ॥

ātmā aṇoḥ aṇīyān mahataḥ mahīyān asya jantoḥ guhāyām nihitaḥ | akratuḥ dhātuprasādāt tam ātmanaḥ mahimānam paśyati tataḥ vītaśokaḥ bhavati ||

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

The first line is a nominal sentence: ātmā with four predicates, two comparatives with ablatives (aṇor aṇīyān, mahato mahīyān) and nihito guhāyām. The second has one verb, paśyati; its subject akratuḥ carries the predicate vītaśokaḥ; tam and mahimānam ātmanaḥ are both its objects; dhātuprasādāt gives the means, and can be construed with paśyati or with vītaśokaḥ, as Raṅgarāmānuja notes.

Ambiguities

dhātuprasādāt — the calm of the faculties, or the grace of the Ordainer?
Śaṅkara: the dhātus are the mind and senses, which sustain the body: मनआदीनि करणानि धातवः शरीरस्य धारणात्; when they are calm the self’s greatness is seen. Raṅgarāmānuja, with the same text, takes dhātu as the Supreme who supports all: धारकस्य परमात्मनः, and cites the line प्रसीदत्यच्युतस्तस्मिन् प्रसन्नेक्लेशसंक्षयः as smṛti. Madhva’s text reads dhātuḥ prasādāt (corrections.json, variant), ‘through the Ordainer’s grace’; his comment in our source does not gloss the phrase.
mahimānam ātmanaḥ — ‘the self’s greatness’, or ‘one greater than the self’?
Śaṅkara: the self’s own greatness, untouched by the growth and decline that action brings: कर्मनिमित्तवृद्धिक्षयरहितं. Raṅgarāmānuja: the Supreme as the cause of the individual self’s greatness, महत्त्वसंपादकं स्वसार्वज्ञ्यादिगुणाविर्भावहेतुभूतं परमात्मानं. Madhva: one greater than the self, आत्मनः सकाशान्महिमानं महामानम्, that is Viṣṇu, greater than the individual self.
akratuḥ
Śaṅkara: अकामः, free of desire for seen and unseen ends. Raṅgarāmānuja: free of rites done for desired results. Madhva divides the word so that kratu is the resolve and its first letter names the Lord: विष्णौ क्रतुर्यस्य सोऽक्रतुस्तन्निश्चयः (the digital text keys ए before विष्णौ; see Flags).
Whose self, after 1.2.18–19?
For Raṅgarāmānuja the subject changes here: having described the individual self in two verses, Yama now describes the Supreme who is its self: एवं मन्त्रद्वयेन प्रत्यगात्मस्वरूपं विशोध्य, तदात्मभूतपरमात्मस्वरूपमाह. For Śaṅkara it is the same self throughout.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 23

Counted as written, 22 and 23 syllables: the last pāda, dhātuprasādān mahimānam ātmanaḥ, has twelve, a jagatī pāda within triṣṭubh, as often in the Upaniṣads. Madhva’s dhātuḥ prasādāt has the same count.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: तमक्रतुः पश्यति वीतशोको — Madhva’s text reads dhātuḥ prasādāt, ‘by the grace of the Creator’, for dhātuprasādāt, ‘by the calm of the faculties’, as Śaṅkara explains the base (धातूनां प्रसादादात्मनो महिमानं). A reading on which the schools’ interpretations of the verse turn.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Stillness and attention

Many people know that some things are seen only when the mind stops grasping — a face, a problem, a feeling. Others know that some understanding arrives as a gift no effort could command. The two readings of this verse keep both experiences in view. (A modern reflection.)

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin When did you last understand something only after you stopped trying so hard?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Does the reading ‘calm of the faculties’ exclude grace, or the reading ‘grace’ exclude effort? What does each commentator say?

A contemplation exercise Sit for ten breaths and let the senses settle, without expecting anything to appear.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
