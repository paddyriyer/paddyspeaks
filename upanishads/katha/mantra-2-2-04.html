What remains

When the embodied one slips out of the body, what is left here? This is that.

5 Faithful English translation

Of this embodied one dwelling in the body, slipping away, being released from the body — what is left here? This truly is that.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

When the embodied one who dwells in the body slips away and is released from it, what is left here? This is that.

Key concepts आत्मन् self, Self मृत्यु death प्राण breath, life-force जीव individual self

7 Explain it to a beginner

The verse asks a quiet question about the end of life. When the one who lives in the body slips out of it, what is left here? The commentators answer differently. Śaṅkara thinks of the city of the first verse: when its ruler leaves, the whole town falls into disorder at once. So when the self goes, the body, the breath and the senses, which seemed so busy, stop working together. Since they fall apart without it, the self must be something other than they are. Raṅgarāmānuja hears a different question: for someone who has given their life to the Lord, what still remains to be done when the body falls away? Nothing; the work is complete.

An analogy Like a busy house the moment its owner moves out: the furniture is all still there, but nothing in it is lived in any more.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

When the embodied one who dwells in the body slips away and is released from it, what remains here? This is that.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the city without its lord

Nothing of the assembly of body and powers remains, प्राणादिकलापे न किञ्चन परिशिष्यते; at the self’s departure all of it at once loses its strength and falls apart, यस्यात्मनोऽपगमे क्षणमात्रात्कार्यकरणकलापरूपं सर्वमिदं हतबलं विध्वस्तं भवति विनष्टं भवति, सोऽन्यः सिद्ध आत्मा. That on which they depend is shown to be another.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: nothing left to do

He reads the verse of the meditator, for whom only the fall of the body stands in the way, शरीरपात एव अन्तरायः, citing the Chāndogya, 'तस्य तावदेव चिरं यावन्न विमोक्ष्ये'. Whether he is strong in body, weakening or dying, nothing remains to be done, कृतकृत्यत्वात् कर्तव्यं किमपि न अविशिष्यते इति भावः.

Comparative Vedānta

Two readings of ‘what remains’

For Śaṅkara the question is about the body: nothing in it survives the self’s departure, which proves the self other than body and breath. For Raṅgarāmānuja it is about the meditator: nothing remains for him to do. The same words carry an argument in the one and an assurance in the other.

Comparative Vedānta

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara reads an argument for the self as other than the body; Raṅgarāmānuja reads an assurance to the one who has meditated. Madhva’s bhāṣya comments once on 2.2.3–5, on breath upheld by Viṣṇu (quoted at 2.2.5), and says nothing particular on this verse.

Morphology

विस्रंसमानस्य
vi-√sraṃs, ‘fall apart, slip off’, present middle participle in -māna. Śaṅkara takes the next line’s dehād vimucyamānasya as its explanation: the slipping away is the release from the body.
विमुच्यमानस्य
The present passive participle of vi-√muc: ‘being released’, an event in progress.
परिशिष्यते
The passive of pari-√śiṣ, ‘leave over’: ‘is left’.

Anvaya

शरीरस्थस्य अस्य देहिनः विस्रंसमानस्य देहात् विमुच्यमानस्य [सतः] अत्र किम् परिशिष्यते । एतत् वै तत् ।

śarīrasthasya asya dehinaḥ visraṃsamānasya dehāt vimucyamānasya sataḥ atra kim pariśiṣyate | etat vai tat |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A string of genitives describes the embodied one at the moment of departure; the question kim atra pariśiṣyate, ‘what remains here?’, stands as the main clause. The refrain answers by pointing.

Ambiguities

What is the question asking?
Śaṅkara: what remains of the body and its powers when the self departs? Nothing: the whole assembly falls apart at once, as a city when its lord flees, पुरस्वामिविद्रवण इव पुरवासिनां; so the self is other than it. Raṅgarāmānuja: for the one who meditates on the Supreme, what remains to be done when the body falls? Nothing, for he has done what was to be done, citing the Chāndogya, 'तस्य तावदेव चिरं यावन्न विमोक्ष्ये'.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16 + 4

Counted as written, 16, 16 and 4 syllables: two regular half-lines; the refrain etad vai tat (4) stands outside the metre.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Aging and mortality

Anyone who has sat with a dying person knows the moment when the body is still there and someone is no longer in it. The verse asks us to take that moment seriously as a question about what a person is. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What do you think is the difference between a living body and one that is not?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Compare Śaṅkara’s and Raṅgarāmānuja’s readings of ‘what remains’. Which question does the verse seem to you to be asking?

A contemplation exercise Notice, for a minute, the many things in your body that work together without your effort.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
