Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 1 · Mantra 11 of 15

By the mind alone

By the mind alone is this to be obtained; there is no diversity here at all. From death to death goes the one who sees as if there were difference here.

5 Faithful English translation

By the mind alone is this to be obtained; here there is not anything [that is] diverse. From death to death goes he who sees here as if [there were] difference.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

It is by the mind alone that this is to be reached; here there is no diversity at all. Whoever sees anything like difference here goes from death to death.

Key concepts नाना nānā, diversity मनस् mind एकत्व oneness ब्रह्मन् Brahman मृत्यु death आचार्य ācārya, teacher

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse explains how the oneness of the last verse is to be found. Not by the eyes or by arguing, but by the mind, a mind that has been prepared. Then it repeats the warning: there is no real division in what is ultimately real, and whoever sees division there goes on from death to death. Śaṅkara says the mind must first be shaped by a teacher and by the scripture; once the knowledge of oneness dawns, nothing divided remains, not even a speck. Raṅgarāmānuja says the self is grasped by a purified mind, and that the repeated line simply drives home the last verse. Madhva reads the small word ‘anything’ as ruling out any division inside God: between his limbs, his qualities and his actions, or between these and God himself.

An analogy Like finding a word one already knows by turning the mind towards it rather than searching a room: the search is inward, but it still needs a trained attention.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

This is to be reached by the mind alone; there is no diversity here at all; whoever sees difference here, as it were, goes from death to death.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: a mind refined by teacher and scripture

Before the knowledge of oneness arises, Brahman of one nature is to be reached by the mind alone, refined by teacher and scripture, प्रागेकत्वविज्ञानादाचार्यागमसंस्कृतेन मनसैव इदं ब्रह्मैकरसम् आप्तव्यम्, as ‘the self alone exists, nothing else’, आत्मैव नान्यदस्तीति. When it is reached, ignorance, which presents diversity, has ceased, so there is not even an atom of diversity in Brahman, अणुमात्रमपि. Whoever does not give up the dim sight of ignorance goes from death to death, स्वल्पमपि भेदमध्यारोपयन्, imposing even a little difference.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: why the self of all is not perceived

He asks why the supreme Self, the self of all, is not perceived by us, सर्वात्मभूतं परमात्मतत्वं कुतो नोपलभ्यते, and answers with the first half-verse: it is grasped by a purified mind, आत्मस्वरूपं विशुद्धमनोग्राह्यम्. The rest repeats the point of 2.1.10 to make it firm, उक्तमेवार्थं दृढीकरणाय अभ्यस्यति.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: no difference in the Lord's qualities and acts

Madhva cites a verse he attributes to the Śabdanirṇaya on the senses of iva, भवेदेकत्र संयोग इवशब्दोऽविरुद्धयोः, and concludes that in nāneva the word marks even a slight difference and difference-in-identity, अतो नानेव इतीवशब्दोऽत्यल्पविषयो भेदाभेदविषयश्च. The first line of 2.1.10 denies a difference of nature in the Lord; here kiñcana extends the denial to his limbs, qualities and acts, किञ्चनशब्दादवयवानां गुणानां क्रियाणां च परस्परं तद्वतां च भेदनिषेधार्थम्.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

The Bṛhadāraṇyaka's version

The Bṛhadāraṇyaka has मनसैवानुद्रष्टव्यं नेह नानास्ति किञ्चन, ‘by the mind alone is it to be seen’, where the Kaṭha has āptavyam, ‘to be reached’; its second line is the one shared with 2.1.10 (āpnoti), not this verse's gacchati.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.1.11

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Brahman, of one nature, is reached by a mind shaped by teacher and scripture; in it there is no diversity at all, इह ब्रह्मणि नाना नास्ति किञ्चन अणुमात्रमपि.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.1.11

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The supreme Self, the self of all, is grasped by a purified mind, आत्मस्वरूपं विशुद्धमनोग्राह्यम्; the second half repeats 2.1.10 for emphasis.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.1.11

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

‘Nothing diverse here’ denies difference among the Lord's limbs, qualities and acts and between them and him, तद्वतां च भेदनिषेधार्थम् — a denial of internal difference in the Lord.

Where they disagree

‘No diversity here’ is absolute non-difference in Brahman for Śaṅkara, the one Self of all for Raṅgarāmānuja, and the Lord's freedom from internal difference for Madhva. The verse's own words do not decide between them.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • मनसैवेदमाप्तव्यं manasaivedamāptavyaṃ → मनसा + एव + इदम् + आप्तव्यम्

    ā + e; a + i; m before a vowel

    ā + e give ai, P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि); a + i give e, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः); a final m before a vowel is simply joined to it.

  • नेह neha → न + इह

    a + i

    a + i give e, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • नानास्ति nānāsti → नाना + अस्ति

    ā + a

    Like vowels fuse into the long vowel, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • य इह ya iha → यः + इह

    aḥ before a vowel other than a

    The visarga of -aḥ is lost before a vowel other than a, and the vowels then stay apart.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

आप्तव्यम्
A gerundive of √āp, ‘obtain, reach’, with -tavya: ‘to be obtained’. The Bṛhadāraṇyaka's version of the line has anudraṣṭavyam, ‘to be seen’.
किञ्चन
The neuter of the interrogative with cana: ‘anything at all’; with na, ‘nothing whatever’.

Anvaya

मनसा एव इदम् आप्तव्यम्; इह किञ्चन नाना न अस्ति । यः इह नाना इव पश्यति, सः मृत्योः मृत्युम् गच्छति ।

manasā eva idam āptavyam; iha kiñcana nānā na asti | yaḥ iha nānā iva paśyati, saḥ mṛtyoḥ mṛtyum gacchati |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A gerundive clause (idam āptavyam) with the instrumental manasā restricted by eva, then a negative existential clause (na … asti kiñcana). The second line repeats 2.1.10's second line with gacchati for āpnoti.

Ambiguities

Which mind?
Śaṅkara: the mind refined by the teacher and scripture, before the knowledge of oneness arises, प्रागेकत्वविज्ञानादाचार्यागमसंस्कृतेन मनसैव; Raṅgarāmānuja: the self is to be grasped by a purified mind, आत्मस्वरूपं विशुद्धमनोग्राह्यम्. Elsewhere the Kaṭha says the self is not reached by the mind (2.3.12), which both readings keep in view by qualifying the mind.
‘No diversity here’ — of what?
Śaṅkara: in Brahman there is no diversity at all, इह ब्रह्मणि नाना नास्ति किञ्चन अणुमात्रमपि. Madhva: the word kiñcana extends the denial to any difference among the Lord's limbs, qualities and acts, and between them and him, किञ्चनशब्दादवयवानां गुणानां क्रियाणां च परस्परं तद्वतां च भेदनिषेधार्थम्. Raṅgarāmānuja takes the line as a repetition for emphasis of 2.1.10.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 17

Counted as written, 16 and 17 syllables: the last pāda, ya iha nāneva paśyati, has nine, as in 2.1.10, because ya and iha stand apart after the lost visarga.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Psychology and inner life

Some things are found only by attention of a certain quality — a melody in a noisy room, a meaning in a difficult text. The verse places its discovery in the mind, but a prepared mind, which is a claim about training rather than effort alone. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What have you understood only after your mind was ready for it?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry The Kaṭha says the self is reached ‘by the mind alone’ here and ‘not by the mind’ in 2.3.12. How does each commentator hold both?
