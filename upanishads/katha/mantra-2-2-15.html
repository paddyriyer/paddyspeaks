Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 2 · Mantra 15 of 15

There the sun does not shine

There the sun does not shine, nor moon and stars, nor these lightnings, much less this fire; everything shines after him as he shines; by his light all this shines.

5 Faithful English translation

There the sun does not shine, nor moon-and-stars; these lightnings do not shine; whence this fire? Him alone, shining, everything shines after; by his light all this shines forth.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

There the sun does not shine, nor the moon and stars; these lightnings do not shine there, much less this fire. Everything shines only after him, as he shines; by his light all this is lit.

Key concepts ब्रह्मन् Brahman सूर्य the Sun अग्नि Agni, fire आत्मन् self, Self

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse answers the question just asked: does that highest reality shine? Yes, more than anything. There the sun does not shine, nor the moon and stars, nor lightning, let alone our little fire. Everything that shines, shines only after it, by its light. Think of the moon: it seems to glow, but its light is borrowed from the sun. In the same way, all light in the world is borrowed from this one source. Śaṅkara says the sun cannot light up Brahman, because the sun’s own light comes from it, so Brahman shines and shines forth. Raṅgarāmānuja says God has a divine form brighter than all other lights, which next to it look like fireflies in the dark, yet they still truly shine by his gift. The verse is also found word for word in the Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad.

An analogy The moon is bright at night, but its brightness is the sun’s, given back.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

There no sun, moon, star, lightning or fire shines; everything shines after him as he shines, and by his light all this shines forth.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the answer, it shines and shines forth

This is the answer to the question of 2.2.14: भाति च विभाति चेति. The sun, though it lights everything, does not illumine Brahman, nor do moon, stars, lightning, much less our fire. Everything shines after him as a firebrand burns after the fire, यथा जलोल्मुकादि अग्निसंयोगादग्निं दहन्तमनुदहति न स्वतः, तद्वत्. Since nothing can give another a light it does not have of itself, न हि स्वतोऽविद्यमानं भासनमन्यस्य कर्तुं शक्यम्, Brahman is light by its own nature: तदेव ब्रह्म भाति च विभाति च.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: a form brighter than all

The supreme Self has a divine, auspicious form, प्रमाणप्रतिपन्नशुभाश्रयदिव्यमङ्गलविग्रहोऽस्ति, and so shines above all, तद्विशिष्टः परमात्मा विभाति सर्वातिशायिदीप्तिमान् इत्याह. He quotes the Bhāṣyakāra on the sūtra ‘jyotirdarśanāt’: सर्वतेजसां छादकं सर्वतेजसां कारणभूतमनुग्राहकं च अङ्गुष्ठप्रमितस्य ज्योतिर्दृश्यते, with Vyāsārya’s explanation, both as quoted by him. He rejects the reading that nothing but the substrate shines, इति यत् परैरुच्यते, तदयुक्तम्, since anubhāti, ‘shines after’, would then have no sense. Beside him the other lights are like fireflies in the dark, अन्धकारे खद्योततुल्यानामेतेषां, and so are said ‘not to shine’.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva

Madhva’s bhāṣya has no comment of its own on this mantra: his comment after 2.2.14–15 speaks of the highest happiness of 2.2.14 only.

Comparative Vedānta

The Muṇḍaka and the Gītā

The verse stands word for word in the Muṇḍaka, न तत्र सूर्यो भाति न चन्द्रतारकं. The Gītā says of the highest abode, न तद्भासयते सूर्यो न शशाङ्को न पावकः.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 2.2.15

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Brahman is light by its own nature; every other light is borrowed from it: न हि स्वतोऽविद्यमानं भासनमन्यस्य कर्तुं शक्यम्.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 2.2.15

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The supreme Self, with his divine form, outshines all lights, which still shine after him by his favour; the reading that nothing but Brahman shines is rejected, इति यत् परैरुच्यते, तदयुक्तम्.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara takes the verse to show that Brahman is self-luminous and the source of all light. Raṅgarāmānuja reads it of the supreme Self’s divine form, and insists that the other lights are real and shine after him. Madhva’s bhāṣya has no comment on this mantra.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • सूर्यो भाति sūryo bhāti → सूर्यः + भाति

    aḥ before a voiced consonant

    -aḥ becomes -o before a voiced consonant, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

  • नेमा विद्युतो nemā vidyuto → न + इमाः + विद्युतः

    a + i; āḥ before a voiced consonant; aḥ before a voiced consonant

    a + i give e, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः). After ā the visarga is dropped before a voiced sound: P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि) and P. 8.3.22 (हलि सर्वेषाम्); -aḥ becomes -o before bh, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च).

  • कुतोऽयमग्निः kuto'yamagniḥ → कुतः + अयम् + अग्निः

    aḥ before a; m before a vowel

    The visarga of -aḥ becomes u before a, giving o, P. 6.1.114 (हशि च) with P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः), and the following a is merged, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति); the final m stays before a vowel.

  • तमेव tameva → तम् + एव

    m before a vowel

    Final m stays before a vowel and is joined to it.

  • भान्तमनुभाति bhāntamanubhāti → भान्तम् + अनुभाति

    m before a vowel

    Final m stays before a vowel and is joined to it.

  • सर्वमिदं sarvamidaṃ → सर्वम् + इदम्

    m before a vowel; anusvāra

    Final m stays before a vowel and is joined to it; before a consonant it becomes anusvāra, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • चन्द्रतारकम् candratārakam

    samāhāra-dvandva · चन्द्र + तारक

    Vigraha चन्द्रश्च तारकाश्च तेषां समाहारः

    moon and stars, taken together

Morphology

भान्तम्
Present active participle of √bhā (bhānt-), accusative: ‘him, as he shines’, P. 3.2.124 (लटः शतृशानचावप्रथमासमानाधिकरणे).
अनुभाति
anu + √bhā, ‘shine after, shine in accordance with’. Śaṅkara’s image: as a firebrand burns only after the fire burns, यथा जलोल्मुकादि अग्निसंयोगादग्निं दहन्तमनुदहति न स्वतः, तद्वत्. Raṅgarāmānuja argues from the prefix that the other lights really shine, after him (see the commentary).
कुतः
kutas, ‘whence’, here rhetorical after a series of denials: ‘how then this fire?’

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

Four negated clauses with tatra, the verb bhāti/bhānti expressed or understood, the last a rhetorical question with kutaḥ. The second line has two clauses: tam eva bhāntam (object with participle) anubhāti sarvam, and tasya bhāsā (instrumental) sarvam idaṃ vibhāti.

Ambiguities

In what sense do the sun and the rest ‘not shine’ there?
Śaṅkara: the sun, though it lights everything, does not illumine Brahman, तद्ब्रह्म न प्रकाशयतीत्यर्थः; all light is borrowed from it. Raṅgarāmānuja: before the supreme Self, endowed with a divine and auspicious form, other lights are outshone, as a minor poet is no poet beside Kālidāsa, सूर्यादितेजोन्तरं न भाति, and like fireflies in the dark beside it.
Do the other lights shine at all?
Raṅgarāmānuja rejects the reading that nothing shines except the substrate, Brahman, इति यत् परैरुच्यते, तदयुक्तम्, because then the word anubhāti, ‘shines after’, would make no sense, अनुभाति इत्यस्य शब्दस्यायोगात्: the other lights do shine, after him and by his favour. Śaṅkara: they shine only after him, as the firebrand burns only after the fire.

Metre

irregular · syllables counted as written: 25 + 22

Counted as written, 25 and 22 syllables. The second line is a regular triṣṭubh (11 + 11). The first has 13 + 12: na tatra sūryo bhāti na candratārakaṃ has thirteen, nemā vidyuto bhānti kuto ’yam agniḥ twelve. The verse stands word for word in the Muṇḍaka (2.2.11).

Textual variations between witnesses

The witnesses differ here only in spelling (listed in section 14).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Science and wonder

We now know that moonlight is sunlight returned, and that much of what we see by is borrowed light. The verse turns that pattern towards its source. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What in your life shines only because something else lights it?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Raṅgarāmānuja argues from the single prefix anu-. What does ‘shines after’ commit the verse to?

A contemplation exercise Tonight, look at the moon and remember whose light it is.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
