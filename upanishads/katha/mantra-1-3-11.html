Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 1 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 11 of 17

Beyond the Person, nothing

Beyond the great self is the unmanifest, beyond the unmanifest the Person; beyond the Person there is nothing — that is the limit, that the highest goal.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Beyond the great self is the unmanifest, and beyond the unmanifest the Person. Beyond the Person there is nothing at all: that is the end, that is the highest goal.

Key concepts अव्यक्त avyakta, the unmanifest महत् mahat, the great (self) पुरुष person प्रकृति primal matter, the unmanifest आत्मन् self, Self ब्रह्मन् Brahman

7 Explain it to a beginner

The ladder from the previous verse reaches its top. Beyond the great self is something called the unmanifest, and beyond that is the Person. Then the verse stops counting: there is nothing beyond the Person. That is the end of the line and the highest goal. The commentators fill in the steps differently. For Śaṅkara the unmanifest is the hidden seed from which the whole world unfolds, and the Person is the one reality that fills everything; to reach it is to know it as one's own deepest self. For Raṅgarāmānuja the unmanifest is simply the body, the chariot in the earlier picture, and the Person is the Lord who guides everything from within. For Madhva the unmanifest is the goddess Ramā and the Person is Viṣṇu. All agree that the series ends in the Person.

An analogy Like counting the rungs of a ladder up to the roof: at the roof the counting stops, because there is no further rung.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

It adds two steps to the ladder — the unmanifest above the great self, the Person above the unmanifest — and then closes it: nothing is higher than the Person, who is the limit and the highest goal.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the seed and the Person

The unmanifest is the seed of the whole world, in which name and form are not yet distinguished: सर्वस्य जगतो बीजभूतमव्याकृतनामरूपं. The Person is subtler and greater still, the cause of all causes, and is called puruṣa because he fills everything: पुरुषः सर्वपूरणात्. Nothing is beyond him because he is pure consciousness and nothing else exists beside him: यस्मान्नास्ति पुरुषाच्चिन्मात्रघनात्परं किञ्चिदपि वस्त्वन्तरम्. He is the highest goal of all who go, citing the Gītā: ‘यद्गत्वा न निवर्तन्ते’.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: the body and the inner controller

Quoting the Bhāṣyakāra, he finds every element of the chariot image named again in the ladder, so that the unmanifest must be the body: न रथरूपितं शरीरमिति परिशेषात् तत् अव्यक्तशब्देन उच्यते इति भाषितम्. Higher than the body is the inner controller of all, the supreme Person who is the far end of the road: सर्वान्तरात्मभूतः अन्तर्यामी अध्वनः पारभूतः परमपुरुषः. He is both the last means of control and the highest goal, वशीकार्योपासननिवृत्त्युपायकाष्ठाभूतः परमप्राप्यश्च स एव, and controlling him means taking refuge in him: तस्य च वशीकरणं तच्छरणागतिरेव.

Traditional Interpretation

Madhva: Ramā and Viṣṇu

In his comment the unmanifest is Ramā and the Person Viṣṇu, whom no one equals because he is complete: ततोऽव्यक्ताभिधा रमा । तस्यास्तु पुरुषो विष्णुः पूर्णत्वान्नैव तत्समः.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: a goal reached by knowing

Opening his comment on the next verse, Śaṅkara asks how there can be a goal (gati) from which there is no return, as 1.3.8 promised. He answers that the Person is everyone's inmost self, so ‘going’ to him is only a figure for knowing him: सर्वस्य प्रत्यगात्मत्वादवगतिरेव गतिरित्युपचर्यते.

Comparative Vedānta

The verse and the Sāṅkhya debate

Because the series mahat — avyakta — puruṣa matches Sāṅkhya terms, the Brahmasūtra (1.4.1) asks whether the verse teaches the Sāṅkhya pradhāna. Śaṅkara's Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya answers that it does not; the unmanifest is understood from the chariot image of 1.3.3. Raṅgarāmānuja reports the Bhāṣyakāra's answer to the same question in his comment on this verse.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Kaṭhopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.3.11

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The unmanifest is the undifferentiated seed of the world, resting in the supreme Self; the Person, pure consciousness, is the end of the series — सा काष्ठा निष्ठा पर्यवसानम् — and nothing exists beside him.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Raṅgarāmānuja, quoting the Bhāṣyakāra, Kaṭhopaniṣat-prakāśikā 1.3.10–11

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The unmanifest is the body; the Person is the inner controller, the end of the road and the one to be reached, and refuge in him is the last of the means: तस्य च वशीकरणं तच्छरणागतिरेव.

Dvaita · Madhva, Kāṭhakopaniṣad-bhāṣya 1.3.10–11

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The unmanifest is Ramā; the Person is Viṣṇu, whom nothing equals: पूर्णत्वान्नैव तत्समः.

Where they disagree

What the unmanifest is — the seed of the world (Śaṅkara), the body (Raṅgarāmānuja), Ramā (Madhva) — and whether the Person at the top is the one reality known as one's own Self (Śaṅkara) or the Lord distinct from the self who controls it from within (Raṅgarāmānuja, Madhva).

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • परमव्यक्तमव्यक्तात्पुरुषः paramavyaktamavyaktātpuruṣaḥ → परम् + अव्यक्तम् + अव्यक्तात् + पुरुषः

    m before a vowel; t before p

    A final m before a vowel is kept and joined to it; the final t stays before p.

  • पुरुषान्न puruṣānna → पुरुषात् + न

    t before a nasal

    A final t becomes n before the nasal n, P. 8.4.45 (यरोऽनुनासिकेऽनुनासिको वा).

  • किञ्चित्सा kiñcitsā → किञ्चित् + सा

    t before s

    The final t stays before s.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

अव्यक्तम्
a-vyakta, ‘not manifest’: the negative a- with the past participle of vi-√añj, ‘to make manifest’. The Brahmasūtra (1.4.1) discusses whether the word here means the Sāṅkhya pradhāna; Śaṅkara says it does not.
काष्ठा
Originally the post or mark at the end of a racecourse; hence ‘limit, culmination’. Feminine, so the pronoun is sā.

Anvaya

महतः अव्यक्तम् परम् [अस्ति], अव्यक्तात् पुरुषः परः; पुरुषात् परम् किञ्चित् न [अस्ति]; सा काष्ठा, सा परा गतिः ।

mahataḥ avyaktam param asti, avyaktāt puruṣaḥ paraḥ; puruṣāt param kiñcit na asti; sā kāṣṭhā, sā parā gatiḥ |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two more steps of the pattern of 1.3.10 (ablative + para- + nominative), then a negated step that closes the series, and two short nominal sentences with sā, agreeing with kāṣṭhā and gatiḥ, which name the Person as the end.

Ambiguities

What is the unmanifest?
Śaṅkara: the undifferentiated seed of the world, resting in the supreme Self as the power of the banyan in its seed — परमात्मन्योतप्रोतभावेन समाश्रितं वटकणिकायामिव वटवृक्षशक्तिः. Raṅgarāmānuja, with the Bhāṣyakāra he quotes, takes it as the body, the chariot of 1.3.3, the only element of that image the ladder has not yet named: न रथरूपितं शरीरमिति परिशेषात् तत् अव्यक्तशब्देन उच्यते. Madhva: the goddess Ramā (ततोऽव्यक्ताभिधा रमा).
gati: goal or going?
gati can mean ‘going’ as well as ‘goal’. Śaṅkara takes up the difficulty at the start of his next comment (on 1.3.12): a goal one goes to is one from which one could return.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16

Anuṣṭubh, 2 × 16 (8 + 8), regular.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Witness error GRETIL (after Olivelle 1998): पुरुषः परः । पुरुशान् न — GRETIL keys पुरुशान् with ś for ṣ.
  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: पुरुषान्न परः किञ्चित् — Madhva’s text reads paraḥ, masculine, for param, neuter, in ‘nothing is higher than the Person’.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Purpose and meaning

Many lists of priorities never end: there is always one more thing above the last. The verse closes its list on purpose — there is nothing beyond this. Whether or not one accepts its answer, asking what one's own list would end in, and whether it ends at all, is a useful question. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What would you put at the top of your own ladder — the thing beyond which there is nothing more important?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Śaṅkara and Raṅgarāmānuja read the same ‘unmanifest’ as the seed of the world and as the body. What does each reading make of the ladder as a whole?

A contemplation exercise Write down three things you want, then ask of each: for the sake of what? Follow the answers until they stop.
