Kaṭha Upaniṣad · Adhyāya 2 · Vallī 3 · Mantra 16 of 18

A hundred and one channels of the heart

A hundred and one are the channels of the heart; one of them goes out through the crown of the head. Going up by it one reaches immortality; the others, going every way, serve for departing.

5 Faithful English translation

A hundred and one [are] the channels of the heart; of them one has gone out towards the crown. Going up by it, [one] reaches immortality; the others, [going] every way, are for departing.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

The heart has a hundred and one channels; one of them rises to the crown of the head. Going up by that one, a person reaches immortality; the others, branching every way, are the paths of ordinary departure.

Key concepts नाडी channel (of the heart) अमृत immortality, the deathless मोक्ष liberation लोक world, realm ब्रह्मन् Brahman

7 Explain it to a beginner

Here the text pictures the body as a network of channels running from the heart: a hundred and one of them. One rises to the crown of the head; going up by that one at death, a person reaches immortality, while the others lead off in every direction to ordinary departures. The Chāndogya Upaniṣad has almost the same verse. Śaṅkara says this does not apply to the one who fully knows Brahman, who goes nowhere because there is nowhere to go; it describes the path of those with lesser knowledge, and the fruit of the fire-ritual Naciketas asked about, and the immortality it gives is relative, lasting until the world dissolves. Raṅgarāmānuja reads it as the final release of the meditator, and says that the Lord, pleased by meditation, guides the soul to the right channel. The text describes an image of departure; the edition reports it, not as a claim about the body.

An analogy A house with many doors has one that opens onto the hill road; the others lead back into the town.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the verse say?

There are a hundred and one channels of the heart; going up by the one that rises to the crown, one reaches immortality; the others serve for departing in every direction.

Traditional Interpretation

Śaṅkara: the course of those who have not yet fully known

The knower who has cut the knots and is Brahman while living has no going, जीवत एव ब्रह्मभूतस्य विदुषो न गतिर्विद्यते, as the Bṛhadāraṇyaka says (quoted by him), न तस्य प्राणा उत्क्रामन्ति ब्रह्मैव सन्ब्रह्माप्येति. This verse gives the course of others, to praise the highest knowledge, and the fruit of the fire-knowledge that was asked about. The one channel is the suṣumnā, एका सुषुम्ना नाम; going up by it through the sun one reaches a relative immortality, अमरणधर्मत्वमापेक्षिकम्.

Traditional Interpretation

Raṅgarāmānuja: the second release, and the Lord’s guidance

The verse states the second, highest release, पूर्वमुक्तां द्वितीयां परममुक्तिमाह. By the channel of Brahman one goes to the world of Brahman and attains release. He sets out the Brahmasūtra’s discussion: the channels are many and fine, so how could one choose? The answer, in the sūtra he quotes, is that the knower is favoured by the Lord in the heart, हार्दानुगृहीतश्शताधिकया, and so finds that channel.

Traditional Interpretation

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The verse describes the final release of the meditator, पूर्वमुक्तां द्वितीयां परममुक्तिमाह, who departs by the crown channel by the Lord’s favour.

Dvaita

No position is given for this school here: the edition does not force one.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara confines the going by the crown channel to those who have not fully known Brahman; Raṅgarāmānuja makes it the knower’s own path to final release. Madhva’s bhāṣya does not comment on this mantra.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • शतं चैका च śataṃ caikā ca → शतम् + च + एका + च

    m before a consonant; a + e

    Final m → anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः). a + e → ai, P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि).

  • नाड्यस्तासां nāḍyastāsāṃ → नाड्यः + तासाम्

    visarga before t; m before a consonant

    Visarga before t becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः). Final m → anusvāra before a consonant, P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • मूर्धानमभिनिःसृतैका mūrdhānamabhiniḥsṛtaikā → मूर्धानम् + अभिनिःसृता + एका

    m before a vowel; ā + e

    Final m stays before a vowel and is joined to it. ā + e also give ai, P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि).

  • तयोर्ध्वमायन्नमृतत्वमेति tayordhvamāyannamṛtatvameti → तया + ऊर्ध्वम् + आयन् + अमृतत्वम् + एति

    ā + ū; m before a vowel; n doubled before a vowel

    ā + ū give o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः). Final m stays before a vowel and is joined to it. A final ṅ or n after a short vowel is doubled before a vowel, P. 8.3.32 (ङमो ह्रस्वादचि ङमुण्नित्यम्).

  • विष्वङ्ङन्या उत्क्रमणे viṣvaṅṅanyā utkramaṇe → विष्वङ् + अन्याः + उत्क्रमणे

    ṅ doubled before a vowel; āḥ before a vowel

    A final ṅ or n after a short vowel is doubled before a vowel, P. 8.3.32 (ङमो ह्रस्वादचि ङमुण्नित्यम्). After ā the visarga becomes y before a vowel, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि), and the y is dropped, P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य); the hiatus stays.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

आयन्
Present active participle of ā + √i, nominative singular masculine: ‘going (upwards)’. The subject, the one who departs, is understood.
विष्वङ्
viṣvañc, ‘turned every way’, here the neuter used adverbially. Śaṅkara glosses it ‘of various courses’, नानागतयः, of the other channels; Raṅgarāmānuja, with Vyāsārya, ‘spread out’, वितताः. Madhva’s text writes viṣvag (corrections.json, orthographic).
भवन्ति
GRETIL reads bhavati, singular, against AS, the vishvAsa mūla and Madhva’s text, which agree with anyāḥ; Śaṅkara comments on the plural (corrections.json).

Anvaya

Prose already in prose order; no anvaya is given.

Syntax

Two verbless clauses in the first line (śataṃ caikā ca hṛdayasya nāḍyaḥ; tāsāṃ mūrdhānam abhiniḥsṛtaikā). The second line has a participial clause tayordhvam āyan with the main verb eti, then viṣvaṅṅ anyā utkramaṇe bhavanti, with viṣvaṅ adverbial and utkramaṇe a locative of purpose.

Ambiguities

Whom does this course concern?
Śaṅkara: not the knower of the highest Brahman, who has no going, जीवत एव ब्रह्मभूतस्य विदुषो न गतिर्विद्यते, as 2.3.14 said and the Bṛhadāraṇyaka (quoted by him); it is the course of those of lesser knowledge, to praise the highest knowledge, and the fruit of the fire-knowledge Naciketas asked about. Raṅgarāmānuja: it is the second, highest release, पूर्वमुक्तां द्वितीयां परममुक्तिमाह, after the release in this life.
What ‘immortality’ is reached?
Śaṅkara: a relative immortality, lasting until the dissolution of the elements, citing the Viṣṇu Purāṇa as he quotes it, आभूतसम्प्लवं स्थानममृतत्वं हि भाष्यते, or in time, with Brahmā, the principal one. Raṅgarāmānuja: release that consists in the manifestation of one’s own nature after reaching Brahman in a particular region, देशविशेषविशिष्ट ब्रह्मप्राप्तिपूर्वकस्वस्वरूपाविर्भावलक्षणां मुक्तिं प्राप्नोति इत्यर्थः.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Death and meaning

Many traditions picture death as a passage with more than one door. The verse offers such a picture; the commentators disagree about who needs it at all. This is a reflection, not a claim made by the text.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Why might a teaching about freedom end with a picture of departure?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Śaṅkara says the full knower ‘goes nowhere’. What would it mean for release to need no journey?

A contemplation exercise Read the verse slowly and notice what image of yourself it assumes.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
