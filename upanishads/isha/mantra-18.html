Īśāvāsya Upaniṣad · Mantra 18 of 18

Agni, lead us by a good path

The last prayer, a Ṛgvedic verse: Agni, who knows all ways, is asked to lead by a good path, to keep crooked wrong away, and to accept abundant words of homage.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Agni, you who know all the ways, lead us by a good path to what is truly rich. Keep from us the wrong that leads astray. We offer you our homage, again and again.

Key concepts अग्नि Agni, fire कर्मन् action, ritual work लोक world, realm देवता deity

7 Explain it to a beginner

The Upanishad ends with a very old prayer, older than the Upanishad itself: it is the first verse of a hymn in the Rig Veda. It speaks to Agni, the fire — the fire of the hearth, of the sacrifice, and of the funeral. ‘You know all the ways,’ it says, ‘so lead us by a good path.’ Then it asks for protection from ‘crooked’ wrongdoing, the kind that twists us off the road without our noticing. And it closes not with a demand but with a promise of thanks: ‘We offer you our words of homage, many times over.’ After all the philosophy, the last word is humility. Even the wise, the verse suggests, still need a guide who knows the road better than they do.

An analogy It is like a hiker at dusk asking a local guide who knows every trail to take them home by the safe path, and thanking the guide in advance because the hiker has nothing else to give.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the mantra say?

It addresses Agni as a god ‘knowing all the ways’ and makes two requests and one offer: lead us by a good path to wealth; keep crooked wrong away from us; we would offer you abundant words of homage. It is Ṛgveda 1.189.1, a verse to Agni, and the last of the four prayers that also close Bṛhadāraṇyaka 5.15.1. Nothing in the words mentions death; its place after mantra 17 is what makes the commentators read it as the prayer of the dying.

Traditional Interpretation

Why a Ṛgvedic verse to Agni at the end of an Upaniṣad?

Śaṅkara introduces it simply: ‘with another mantra he again asks for the path’ (पुनरन्येन मन्त्रेण मार्गं याचते). The good path is the path of the gods, chosen by one weary of the southern path of coming and going (निर्विण्णोऽहं दक्षिणेन मार्गेण गतागतलक्षणेन), and the dying person, unable now to serve Agni in ritual, offers words of homage instead. Mahīdhara, on the same verse as VS 40.16, gives the same reading and makes the wealth liberation. Deśika reads Agni as the Lord who has fire as his body, quoting the antaryāmin section of the Bṛhadāraṇyaka ('यस्याग्निः शरीरम्' इत्यन्तर्यामिब्राह्मणम्), and the path as a means untouched by anything forbidden.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What is the ‘crooked wrong’?

juhurāṇam enaḥ: enas is a fault or wrong that binds; juhurāṇa is a participle of hvṛ, ‘to be crooked, to go astray’ (Mahīdhara: 'हुर्च्छा कौटिल्ये'). Śaṅkara glosses it as crooked and deceiving (कुटिलं वञ्चनात्मकम्); Deśika as binding and inconceivably devious; Madhva as ‘making us small’ (अस्मानल्पीकुर्वत्). The petition is to keep it away, yuyodhi, from a root ‘to separate’ — not to punish it.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Śaṅkara close the Upaniṣad?

After this mantra Śaṅkara adds a discussion of mantras 11 and 14. Some, hearing that one ‘crosses death by avidyā and attains immortality by vidyā’, take vidyā as knowledge of the highest Self and conclude that it is to be combined with works. He rejects this: knowledge and works are as opposed as heat and cold in one fire, and the Kaṭha says they are ‘far apart’ (‘दूरमेते विपरीते विषूची अविद्या या च विद्येति ज्ञाता’(क. उ. १ । २ । ४) इति श्रुतेः). The ‘immortality’ of 11 is relative (‘अमृतमश्नुते’ इत्यापेक्षिकममृतम्), and if vidyā were Self-knowledge, the prayers for a door and a path in 15–18 would make no sense. So the final prayers belong to the meditator, and the combination taught is of works with meditation, ‘not with knowledge of the highest Self’ (उपासनया समुच्चयो न परमात्मविज्ञानेनेति). Mahīdhara ends his commentary on VS 40 with the same argument.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 18 and the concluding discussion

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Agni is asked to lead the dying meditator by the path of the gods, which does not return, to the fruit of his works; to destroy his crooked sin so that, purified, he may reach what he desires (ततो वयं विशुद्धाः सन्तः इष्टं प्राप्स्याम इत्यभिप्रायः); and to accept words of homage in place of ritual service. Because a path is asked for, these mantras concern meditation, not the Self-knowledge of mantra 7, which needs no path.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Vedānta Deśika, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 18

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Agni names the Lord, whose body is fire (अग्ने - अग्निशरीरक); he who knows all the means is asked to lead those who have no other refuge, to remove the wrong that obstructs the experience of him, and to accept their repeated homage — for even the liberated are described as ‘saying only namaḥ’. Deśika closes by summing up the Saṃhitā’s end as teaching the highest reality, its glory, its worship and its fruit (एवं परतत्त्व-तद्विभूतियोग-तदुपासन-तत्फलविशेषान् सङ्गृह्य संहितेयं समपूर्यत).

Dvaita · Madhva, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 18 (his 20)

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

vayuna is knowledge; juhurāṇa ‘making us small’; yuyodhi ‘separate’; and the whole is the prayer with which Manu praised Yajña (Viṣṇu) for the wealth of liberation, in a verse Madhva cites from a Skānda (नय नो मोक्षवित्तायेत्यस्तौद्यज्ञं मनुः स्वराड्). He closes with homage to Nārāyaṇa (नमो नारायणाय ते).

Where they disagree

Agni is the deity of the path of the gods (Śaṅkara, Mahīdhara) or the Lord with fire as his body (Deśika) or Viṣṇu (Madhva); the wealth is the fruit of works (Śaṅkara), liberation (Mahīdhara, Madhva) or the means of worship and unworldly wealth (Deśika). The schools agree that the Upaniṣad ends in a prayer for guidance and in homage.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • राये अस्मान्विश्वानि rāye asmānviśvāni → राये + अस्मान् + विश्वानि

    hiatus (Vedic prakṛtibhāva)

    Classical sandhi would elide the a after e (rāye ’smān, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति)). In Vedic verse e and o may stay unchanged before a within a pāda: P. 6.1.115 (प्रकृत्याऽन्तःपादमव्यपरे). The hiatus also keeps the syllable the triṣṭubh needs. See sandhi_exceptions. asmān + viśvāni: the final n stays before v and is written together with the next word.

  • युयोध्यस्मज्जुहुराणमेनो yuyodhyasmajjuhurāṇameno → युयोधि + अस्मत् + जुहुराणम् + एनः

    vowel, consonant and visarga sandhi

    yuyodhi + asmat: i before a dissimilar vowel becomes y (P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि)); asmat + juhurāṇam: t assimilates to the following j (P. 8.4.40 (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः)), after being voiced to d (P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते)); juhurāṇam keeps its m before the vowel; enaḥ before bh becomes eno.

  • नम उक्तिं nama uktiṃ → नमस् + उक्ति

    compound-internal (visarga lost before a vowel)

    namas + ukti: the s, become visarga, is dropped before a vowel after a and the hiatus stays (the same treatment as at word-ends, P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि) and P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य)). Witnesses write it as one word (नमउक्तिं, AS) or two (नम उक्तिं, G, K, VD); GRETIL’s Ṛgveda in Devanagari shows नमौक्तिं, a conversion of the transliteration namaüktim.

Junctions outside the general rules

  • राये · अस्मान् — vedic-prakṛtibhāva: राये अस्मान्: e before a left unchanged within the pāda, as P. 6.1.115 (प्रकृत्याऽन्तःपादमव्यपरे) allows in Vedic. Every witness, the Ṛgveda (1.189.1) and the Bṛhadāraṇyaka (5.15.1) have the hiatus.

    prādi-tatpuruṣa (karmadhāraya) · सु + पथिन्

    Vigraha शोभनः पन्थाः

    a good path

  • नमउक्तिम् namauktim

    ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa · नमस् + उक्ति

    Vigraha नमसः उक्तिः

    the utterance of homage

Morphology

सुपथा
Instrumental of a compound in -pathin, ‘path’, with no compound-final ending. Classical Sanskrit adds -a to such compounds — P. 5.4.74 (ऋक्पूरप्धूःपथामानक्षे) — giving supatha, instrumental supathena; the Ṛgvedic form keeps the root noun’s instrumental, like pathā.
राये
Dative of rai, ‘wealth’ (stem rāy- before vowels). What wealth is meant is disputed: the fruit of works (Śaṅkara), liberation (Mahīdhara, and Madhva’s cited verse, नय नो मोक्षवित्तायेत्यस्तौद्यज्ञं मनुः स्वराड्), or the means of sustaining the body for worship and, more properly, an ‘unworldly wealth’ (Deśika).
विद्वान्
Nominative of the perfect participle vidvas of vid ‘to know’: ‘knowing’. With viśvāni vayunāni: ‘knowing all the ways’.
युयोधि
Imperative of yu ‘to separate’, reduplicated (3rd class). Before a vowel-ending root the imperative ending -hi would normally stand; -dhi is prescribed only after hu and roots ending in an obstruent (P. 6.4.101 (हुझल्भ्यो हेर्धिः)), so Mahīdhara calls the -dhi of yuyodhi Vedic: छान्दसं हेर्धित्वम्.
जुहुराणम्
A reduplicated present participle in -āna of hvṛ/hvar ‘to be crooked’. Mahīdhara: 'हुर्च्छा कौटिल्ये' शानचि जुहोत्यादित्वेन रूपम् — the participle in śānac, reduplicated as in the juhoti class.
विधेम
Optative 1st plural of vidh ‘to worship, to honour (a god)’, the verb of the Ṛgvedic refrain ‘kasmai devāya haviṣā vidhema’. Śaṅkara and Mahīdhara gloss ‘may we serve / may we do’. Deśika glosses with vi + dhā (‘we offer’, विदध्महे) and accounts for the form by the interchange of forms allowed in Vedic (व्यत्ययो हि बहुलमनुशिष्टः; cf. P. 3.1.85 (व्यत्ययो बहुलम्)).

Anvaya

अग्ने देव विश्वानि वयुनानि विद्वान् अस्मान् राये सुपथा नय । अस्मत् जुहुराणम् एनः युयोधि । ते भूयिष्ठाम् नमउक्तिम् विधेम ।

agne deva viśvāni vayunāni vidvān asmān rāye supathā naya | asmat juhurāṇam enaḥ yuyodhi | te bhūyiṣṭhām namauktim vidhema |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Three clauses. Two imperatives addressed to Agni — ‘lead us by a good path to wealth’, ‘keep crooked wrong away from us’ — and an optative in the first person plural, ‘may we offer you abundant words of homage’. The participial phrase viśvāni deva vayunāni vidvān (‘knowing, O god, all the ways’) gives the reason for the first request: Agni knows the ways. Śaṅkara reads the last clause as an excuse — ‘we cannot serve you now; we offer only words of homage’ (किन्तु वयमिदानीं ते न शक्नुमः परिचर्यां कर्तुम्).

Ambiguities

सुपथा: which path?
Śaṅkara and Mahīdhara: the ‘good’ path is the path of the gods, which does not return, in contrast with the southern path of coming and going (गतागतलक्षणेन दक्षिणमार्गेण). Deśika: a means free of anything forbidden (प्रतिषेधस्पर्शरहितेनोपायेनेति यावत्). In its Ṛgvedic setting the verse need not refer to death at all.
वयुनानि
‘Works or knowings’ (Śaṅkara); ‘knowings’ (Madhva), Deśika citing the Nighaṇṭu, the Vedic word list, for vayuna = knowledge ('माया वयुनं ज्ञानम्' इति नैघण्टुकाः). A Vedic word whose range — ways, rules, patterns, knowledge — is wider than any single English equivalent.
The same verse elsewhere
The mantra is the first verse of Ṛgveda 1.189, a hymn to Agni; Mahīdhara says it was seen by Agastya and is a triṣṭubh to Agni (अगस्त्यदृष्टाग्नेयी त्रिष्टुप्), and notes that it was already explained at VS 5.36. In the Īśā it is placed at the end of a life; in the Ṛgveda it is a prayer of a sacrificer.

Metre

triṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 22 + 21

Counted as written: 22 + 21 syllables. A triṣṭubh has four pādas of 11. The first half has 11 + 11. The third pāda, yuyodhy asmaj juhurāṇam eno, has 10 as written and 11 if the semivowel is read as a vowel (yuyodhi asmat), as Vedic metre often requires; the fourth has 11 with the hiatus inside nama-uktim. Mahīdhara names the metre triṣṭubh.

Textual variations between witnesses

The witnesses differ here only in spelling (listed in section 14).

11 Connections with other scriptures

  • Ṛgveda 1.189.1 Direct textual parallel

    अग्ने नय सुपथा राये

    agne naya supathā rāye

    The same verse: the first of Agastya’s hymn to Agni. The Īśā takes a Ṛgvedic prayer for its last word.

  • Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 5.15.1 Direct textual parallel

    The last of the four prayers that close the Bṛhadāraṇyaka, in the same order as Īśā 15–18; there it reads नमउक्तिं as one word.

  • Bhagavad Gītā 8.24 Doctrinal comparison

    अग्निर्ज्योतिरहः शुक्लः

    agnirjyotirahaḥ śuklaḥ

    The Gītā’s path of light, beginning with fire, by which knowers of Brahman go and do not return. Śaṅkara’s reading of supathā as the non-returning path draws on this doctrine of the two paths.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Ethical leadership

The verse is a prayer to someone who ‘knows all the ways’, asked to lead others by a good path and to keep crooked wrong away. Read as a reflection on leadership, it puts guidance, honesty and gratitude in one sentence: a guide is valued because he knows the terrain, the danger named is not open evil but the crooked turn that misleads, and the led respond with thanks rather than flattery. It may also remind those who lead that they too pray to be led. This is a reflection on the teaching, not part of it.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Who in your life has ‘known the way’ when you did not, and how did you thank them?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why might a text about the Self that is everywhere end by asking to be led somewhere — and does the prayer contradict the vision of mantras 6–7, or complete it?

A contemplation exercise Before beginning something difficult today, say silently: ‘Lead me by a good path; keep me from the crooked turn.’ At the day’s end, notice one moment where you chose the straighter way.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.

Uncertainties recorded

  • uncertain The IAST of नमउक्तिम् is generated with a dot to show the hiatus inside the compound (nama·uktim); the Ṛgveda’s pada-pāṭha and the GRETIL Devanagari (नमौक्तिं) show that the junction is a known difficulty of transcription.
