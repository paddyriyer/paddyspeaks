Īśāvāsya Upaniṣad · Mantra 12 of 18

Blind darkness for those who worship non-becoming, more for those who delight in becoming

The second triad opens with the same double warning as the first, now about asambhūti and sambhūti.

5 Faithful English translation

Into blind darkness they enter who worship non-becoming (asambhūti); into darkness greater than that, as it were, [enter] they who delight in becoming (sambhūti).

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Those who worship the unmanifest enter blind darkness; those who delight in the manifest enter a darkness greater still.

Key concepts असम्भूति asambhūti, ‘non-becoming’ सम्भूति sambhūti, ‘coming into being’ तमस् darkness उपासना worship, meditation प्रकृति primal matter, the unmanifest हिरण्यगर्भ Hiraṇyagarbha, ‘golden embryo’

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse is a warning written as a riddle. It speaks of two groups of people. One group gives itself to ‘what does not come into being’; the other delights in ‘what comes into being’. Both, it says, walk into darkness — and the second into a deeper darkness than the first. That is surprising, because the second group sounds as if it has chosen the better thing. The verse is not saying that either thing is bad. It is saying that clinging to only one half of a pair leaves you in the dark, and it prepares the ground for the next two verses, which say what to do instead: hold both together. Teachers explain the two halves differently — the seed and the tree, the cause and its effect, or the clearing of obstacles and the reaching of the goal — but all agree the verse is pointing past one-sidedness.

An analogy Someone who studies only the roots of a tree, or only its fruit, misunderstands the tree; the verse says that fixing on either half alone is a kind of blindness.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the mantra itself state?

It states two outcomes for two groups: those who worship (upāsate) asambhūti enter ‘blind darkness’, and those who delight (ratāḥ) in sambhūti enter ‘a greater darkness, as it were’. It uses the same frame as mantra 9 and changes only the objects. It names no deity, defines neither term, and does not say whether the two groups are to be combined; that is said in mantras 13–14. The word iva (‘as it were’) makes the comparison of darknesses figurative.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Śaṅkara identify the two objects of worship?

Śaṅkara introduces the mantra as blame of each worship singly, in order to commend their combination: अधुना व्याकृताव्याकृतोपासनयोः समुच्चिचीषया प्रत्येकं निन्दोच्यते. asambhūti is the unmanifest cause, प्रकृतिं कारणमविद्यां कामकर्मबीजभूतामदर्शनात्मिकाम् — prakṛti, the seed of desire and action, itself a not-seeing — and its worshippers enter a darkness of the same kind. sambhūti is कार्यब्रह्मणि हिरण्यगर्भाख्ये, the ‘effect-Brahman’ called Hiraṇyagarbha. Both are objects of meditation within the world; neither is the Self of mantras 4–8. In his reading the whole triad 12–14 concerns ritual meditation and its fruits, not liberation.

Traditional Interpretation

Why does Deśika reject the reading ‘unmanifest cause’?

Deśika takes the three mantras as teaching two stages to be contemplated together as an aid to knowledge: the removal of obstacles and the experience of Brahman. sambhūti is the attainment of Brahman, as in the Upaniṣadic phrases he quotes, एतमितः प्रेत्याभिसम्भविताऽस्मि and धूत्वा शरीरमकृतं कृतात्मा ब्रह्मलोकमभिसम्भवानि. asambhūti, excluding that, refers to the destruction of the obstacles near it (तदासन्नप्रतिबन्धविनाशम्), which is why mantra 14 calls it vināśa. He explicitly refuses to take asambhūti as the non-arising or the destruction of sambhūti: a mere absence could not be named, in 14, as the means of crossing death.

Traditional Interpretation

Does Mahīdhara’s Mādhyandina commentary agree?

In the Mādhyandina recension this verse comes first of its triad (VS 40.9), before the vidyā verses. Mahīdhara gives two explanations. In the first, asambhūti is the view that the dead are not born again (मृतस्य पुनः संभवो नास्ति), which he attributes to the Bauddhas, and sambhūti is devotion to the self alone with works rejected (आत्मज्ञानमात्ररताः). His second explanation, introduced अस्या ऋचोऽर्थान्तरमुच्यते, repeats Śaṅkara’s: prakṛti and Hiraṇyagarbha. The first explanation is a polemical application by Mahīdhara and is reported here as his.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What is the relation of this mantra to mantra 9?

The two mantras are identical apart from avidyām / asambhūtim and vidyāyām / sambhūtyām, and mantras 13–14 repeat the pattern of 10–11. In the Kāṇva text the vidyā triad (9–11) comes first; in the Mādhyandina VS 40 the sambhūti triad (40.9–11) precedes the vidyā triad (40.12–14). The Bṛhadāraṇyaka (4.4.10) has the vidyā verse only. These facts show two parallel triads built on one template; they do not by themselves show which is older.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 12

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

asambhūti is the unmanifest cause (prakṛti, avyākṛta) and sambhūti the manifest effect, Hiraṇyagarbha: ये उ सम्भूत्यां कार्यब्रह्मणि हिरण्यगर्भाख्ये रताः. Each worship alone is blamed so that the two may be combined; the combination yields powers and dissolution into prakṛti, not liberation, which is by knowledge of the Self alone.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Vedānta Deśika, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 12

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

sambhūti is the attainment of Brahman (ब्रह्मप्राप्तिरूपा सम्भूतिरुक्ता); asambhūti is the removal of the obstacles that stand close to it. Contemplating either alone is blamed; both are to be contemplated together as limbs of the meditation on Brahman. The reading of the terms as creation and dissolution is rejected.

Dvaita · Madhva, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantras 12–14 (incomplete in the digital text)

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The surviving part of Madhva’s comment, a verse he cites from a text he names Kaurma, speaks of those who do not accept Hari’s creatorship and of those who do not accept his being the destroyer: एवं सृष्टिकर्तृत्वं नाङ्गीकुर्वन्ति ये हरेः । तेऽपि यान्ति तमो घोरं. On this reading the two groups of mantra 12 err about the Lord’s powers of creation and dissolution, and both go to darkness.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara (with Mahīdhara’s second explanation) reads the pair as cause and effect in the cosmos — prakṛti and Hiraṇyagarbha — whose worships are ritual meditations outside liberation. Deśika reads them as two stages of the path to Brahman and expressly rejects cosmic creation and dissolution as the sense. Madhva, as far as his comment survives, reads them as the Lord’s creating and destroying, to be acknowledged together. The disagreement is about the reference of the terms; all agree the mantra blames one-sidedness.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • अन्धं तमः andhaṃ tamaḥ → अन्धम् + तमः

    consonant sandhi (m → anusvāra)

    A word-final m becomes anusvāra before a consonant: P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः). Madhva’s and the Mādhyandina texts write the nasal as the class nasal (अन्धन्तमः).

  • येऽसम्भूतिमुपासते ye'sambhūtimupāsate → ये + असम्भूतिम् + उपासते

    vowel sandhi (pūrvarūpa) and no change

    Word-final e before a short a absorbs it, marked by the avagraha: P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति). asambhūtim + upāsate: a final m before a vowel is kept.

  • ततो भूय इव tato bhūya iva → ततः + भूयः + इव

    visarga sandhi

    -aḥ before a voiced consonant becomes -o (tataḥ + bhūyaḥ → tato bhūyaḥ); -aḥ before a vowel other than a loses its visarga, leaving a hiatus (bhūyaḥ + iva → bhūya iva).

  • तमो य उ tamo ya u → तमः + ये + उ

    visarga sandhi; vowel sandhi (ayādi, y dropped)

    tamaḥ + ye → tamo ye; ye before a vowel becomes ay (P. 6.1.78 (एचोऽयवायावः)), and the y is dropped (P. 8.3.19 (लोपः शाकल्यस्य)), leaving the hiatus ya u.

  • सम्भूत्यां रताः sambhūtyāṃ ratāḥ → सम्भूत्याम् + रताः

    consonant sandhi (m → anusvāra)

    P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः). The Saṃhitā witnesses write ँ (सम्भूत्याँ रताः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

असम्भूतिम्
sambhūti is sam + √bhū with the suffix -ti that forms feminine action nouns (P. 3.3.94 (स्त्रियां क्तिन्)): ‘coming together into being, origination’. The negative a- gives ‘non-origination’. Śaṅkara’s analysis turns the action noun into the thing that has it: सम्भवनं सम्भूतिः सा यस्य कार्यस्य सा सम्भूतिः तस्या अन्या असम्भूतिः — sambhūti is the effect that comes into being, asambhūti what is other than it, the cause. The Mādhyandina pada-pāṭha confirms the negative: अस॑म्भूति॒मित्यस॑म्ऽभूतिम्.
उपासते
upa + √ās, ‘to sit near’, hence ‘to wait upon, worship, meditate on’; 2nd class ātmanepada, present 3rd plural. The preverb is written with its verb.
भूयः
Comparative of bahu, ‘much’. With tataḥ (‘than that’) it gives ‘more than that’. Śaṅkara glosses बहुतरमिव.
रताः
Past participle of √ram, ‘to delight’, used actively: ‘[those who are] delighted in’, construed with the locative sambhūtyām.
ये
The second ye stands in hiatus before u (ya u). That it is ye (plural, with ratāḥ) and not yaḥ is shown by Mahīdhara’s gloss ये उ ये च.

Anvaya

ये असम्भूतिम् उपासते [ते] अन्धम् तमः प्रविशन्ति । ये उ सम्भूत्याम् रताः ते ततः भूयः इव तमः [प्रविशन्ति] ।

ye asambhūtim upāsate te andham tamaḥ praviśanti | ye u sambhūtyām ratāḥ te tataḥ bhūyaḥ iva tamaḥ praviśanti |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two correlative sentences. In the first the relative clause (ye asambhūtim upāsate) follows the main clause (andhaṃ tamaḥ praviśanti), whose subject ‘they’ is understood. In the second, te … ye u sambhūtyāṃ ratāḥ, the main verb praviśanti is carried over from the first line, and tato bhūya iva (‘more than that, as it were’) grades the second darkness above the first. The structure repeats mantra 9 word for word except for the two objects; mantra 9 itself is the Bṛhadāraṇyaka’s verse (BU 4.4.10).

Ambiguities

What are sambhūti and asambhūti?
The mantra gives no definition. Śaṅkara: sambhūti is the manifest effect, Hiraṇyagarbha; asambhūti the unmanifest cause, prakṛti. Deśika: sambhūti is the attainment of Brahman (he cites Upaniṣadic uses of abhisambhū), and asambhūti the removal of the obstacles close to it. Mahīdhara, on the Mādhyandina text, gives two explanations: first, those who hold that the dead are not born again (asambhūti) and those devoted only to the self, rejecting works (sambhūti); second, the explanation Śaṅkara gives. Madhva’s comment, preserved only in part, takes the pair as the Lord’s creatorship and his being the destroyer.
Why more darkness for sambhūti?
As in mantra 9, the second group, apparently devoted to the higher object, receives the worse fate. Śaṅkara explains it by the combination (samuccaya) the next two mantras recommend: each worship on its own is blamed in order to commend the two together (समुच्चिचीषया प्रत्येकं निन्दोच्यते).
The avagraha in येऽसम्भूतिम्
Without the avagraha the letters would read ye sambhūtim. Every witness marks it, the Mādhyandina pada-pāṭha gives asambhūtim, and mantra 13 has asambhavāt; the reading is secure.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16

Two half-lines of 16 syllables. Mahīdhara introduces VS 40.9–14 as six anuṣṭubh verses: अतः परमुपासनामन्त्रा उच्यन्ते षडनुष्टुभः.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Technology and digital distractions

A familiar modern pattern has two halves. One person refuses all new tools on principle; another lives for whatever has just been released. Each feels the other is lost. The mantra’s structure — darkness for the one, more darkness for the other — is a reminder that a single fixation, even on the newest and brightest thing, can be its own kind of blindness. This does not explain sambhūti and asambhūti, whose sense the commentators debate; it is a reflection prompted by the mantra’s shape, not part of its exegesis.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Is there something you have chosen ‘only one side of’ — only work or only rest, only plans or only spontaneity? What might you be missing?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Why would devotion to the higher of two objects lead into greater darkness than devotion to the lower — and what does that imply about how the object of devotion is held?

A contemplation exercise Write down one activity you pursue with enthusiasm and one you avoid. For a week, give the avoided one ten minutes a day, and note what each teaches about the other.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.

  • review Madhva’s comment on 12–14 survives in the digital text only from a point marked ‘…’ (madhva.json): its beginning, and so his own gloss of asambhūti and sambhūti, is missing. The Dvaita position is reported from what survives.
