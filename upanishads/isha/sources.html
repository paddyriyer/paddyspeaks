Open questions of the studies

  • Īśāvāsyam idaṃ sarvam: Which root of vas the composer intended cannot be settled from the text; the pada-pāṭha settles only the word division.
  • Īśāvāsyam idaṃ sarvam: Whether jagatyām means ‘on the earth’ (Śaṅkara), ‘in the three worlds’ (Mahīdhara) or ‘in prakṛti’ (Madhva) affects the scope of the vision; the mantra gives no further clue.
  • Tena tyaktena bhuñjīthāḥ: Whether the composer intended bhuj in the sense ‘protect’ cannot be settled from this one occurrence; a study of bhuj in the middle voice across the Vedic corpus would be needed.
  • Tena tyaktena bhuñjīthāḥ: Whether tena refers to the world (most commentators) or to the Lord (Madhva) is grammatically open.
  • Karma and Jñāna: Deśika quotes ‘the Bhāṣya’ on Brahmasūtra 3.4.14 and ‘the bhāṣyakāra’ on avidyā in mantra 11; these are attributed to Rāmānuja by the tradition, but the Śrībhāṣya is not among this edition’s sources and the quotations have not been checked against it.
  • Karma and Jñāna: The readings Deśika rejects in mantra 11 are not attributed by him to anyone; the resemblance of the second to Śaṅkara’s structure is the editor’s observation.
  • Karma and Jñāna: Madhva’s verses on mantras 9–11 are given without a named source in the keyed text, and the keyed passage is abbreviated (यान्ति ...); a printed text of his bhāṣya is needed.
  • Karma and Jñāna: Whether saha in mantra 11 qualifies the object or the act of knowing cannot be settled from the mantra alone.
  • Vidyā and Avidyā: Does the Kāṇva Saṃhitā print अविद्याया in mantra 10, as the digital witness keys it? A printed Kāṇva Saṃhitā is needed.
  • Vidyā and Avidyā: How does Madhva construe each word of mantras 9–11? The digital text of his comment is abridged; a printed edition of his bhāṣya (with its sub-commentaries) should be consulted.
  • Vidyā and Avidyā: Does the order of the Mādhyandina recension, with the sambhūti mantras first, reflect an older arrangement of the chapter? The materials of this edition do not decide it.
  • Vidyā and Avidyā: Is the closeness of mantra 10b to Kena 1.4 evidence of a shared source, or of a formula of transmission used in several schools? A question for textual history, not answered here.
  • Sambhūti and Asambhūti: Madhva’s own gloss of asambhūti and sambhūti in mantra 12 is missing from the digital source; a printed edition of his bhāṣya (with Rāghavendra’s khaṇḍārtha) should be checked.
  • Sambhūti and Asambhūti: Madhva’s mūla as keyed lacks the second च in mantra 14a (corrections.json, review).
  • Sambhūti and Asambhūti: The pada-pāṭha cited is the one printed with the Mādhyandina witness; a Kāṇva pada-pāṭha of 40.12–14 would bear directly on the Kāṇva text and was not available.
  • Sambhūti and Asambhūti: Whether the sambhūti triad or the vidyā triad stands first in the oldest arrangement of the chapter cannot be decided from the two recensions alone.
  • The Final Prayers: Does the Kāṇva Saṃhitā recite mantra 16 in the shorter form of witness K (beginning at tejaḥ, reading rūpe), or is that an omission of the digital text? A printed Kāṇva Saṃhitā with svara is needed.
  • The Final Prayers: What did the Mādhyandina klibe originally mean? The edition has only Mahīdhara’s derivation from klṛp.
  • The Final Prayers: Were the four prayers composed for the Īśā, or taken over from a funeral or meditation liturgy shared with Bṛhadāraṇyaka 5.15? The edition records the parallel and does not decide its direction.
  • The Final Prayers: Madhva’s digital text has gaps in its comment on mantras 15–16; a printed edition of his bhāṣya would fill them.
