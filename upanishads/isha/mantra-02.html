Īśāvāsya Upaniṣad · Mantra 2 of 18

Doing one's work here, one may wish to live a hundred years

Action within the vision of mantra 1: a full life of work, and the claim that action then does not cling.

5 Faithful English translation

Only doing works here, one should wish to live a hundred years. Thus for you — there is not [a way] otherwise than this — action does not cling to the person.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Doing your work in this world, you may wish to live a hundred years. For you there is no other way than this, and so action does not cling to you.

Key concepts कर्मन् action, ritual work ज्ञान knowledge समुच्चय combination (of knowledge and action) संन्यास renunciation जीव individual self

7 Explain it to a beginner

The first verse said: let go, and do not grab. This one answers the obvious worry — must I then stop working and withdraw from life? No. Keep doing your work, and you may even hope to live a full hundred years. The verse then makes a surprising promise: lived this way, your actions will not stick to you. Think of the difference between doing a job and being glued to it — to its rewards, its praise, the fear of failing. The work can be done completely while the gluing is absent. Teachers disagree about whom this verse is for. Some say it is for people not yet ready to renounce everything; others say it is for everyone, even the wise, for their whole lives. All agree that it treats a life of work as something that can be lived without being trapped by it.

An analogy A lotus leaf lies on the water all its life without becoming wet; the water is there, but it does not cling.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the mantra itself say?

It says that one should (or may) wish to live a hundred years only while doing works here, and that ‘thus, for you’ there is no other way than this; action does not cling to a person. The mantra does not say which works are meant, does not distinguish people by eligibility, and does not explain why action does not cling. It speaks in the singular, to ‘you’, as mantra 1 did. Its claim is modest and striking at once: a long life of action is compatible with not being stained by action.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Śaṅkara relate mantra 2 to mantra 1?

For Śaṅkara the two mantras give two ways of life for two kinds of people. Mantra 1 is for the knower of the Self, who renounces the three desires; mantra 2 is for the other, who cannot grasp the Self: अथेतरस्य अनात्मज्ञतयात्मग्रहणायाशक्तस्य इदमुपदिशति मन्त्रः. Such a person should live doing the prescribed rites, for there is no other way by which inauspicious action does not cling: कर्मणा न लिप्यस इत्यर्थः. To the objection ‘how do we know this division?’ he answers with the principle that governs his whole reading of the Upaniṣad: ज्ञानकर्मणोर्विरोधं पर्वतवदकम्प्यं यथोक्तं न स्मरसि किम्? — ‘Do you not remember the opposition of knowledge and action, unshakeable as a mountain?’ He supports the two paths with the Mokṣadharma verse द्वाविमावथ पन्थानौ यत्र वेदाः प्रतिष्ठिताः and with the Taittirīya saying न्यास एवात्यरेचयत्, ‘renunciation alone surpassed [the rest]’.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Deśika read the same words?

Deśika reads mantra 2 as addressed to the knower: works done without attachment to results or the sense of being the doer, as an auxiliary of knowledge, are to be done for life: एवं विदुषः फलसङ्गकर्तृत्वादित्यागयुक्तं नित्यनैमित्तिकरूपं (विद्याङ्गभूतं) कर्म यावज्जीवमनुष्ठेयमित्याह, and न कदाचिदपि विद्याङ्गं कर्म परित्यजेदित्यर्थः. The desiderative ‘wish to live’ shows that even a knower of Brahman wants life until knowledge is complete. Action ‘does not cling’ in the sense that it does not become the cause of further results such as heaven. He rejects outright the view that the knower, his eligibility ‘burnt by the fire of knowledge’, stands beyond injunction and prohibition: ज्ञानाग्निदग्धाधिकारो विधिनिषेधानधिकारीति पक्षस्तु न वेदवित्सम्मतः.

Traditional Interpretation

Does action cling to the one who does not act?

Madhva reads the second half as a warning in the other direction: अकुर्वतः कर्म न लिप्यत इति नास्ति — it is not the case that action fails to cling to one who does not act. He supports this with verses he cites from a text he calls the Nāradīya: action clings to the ignorant who do not worship Kṛṣṇa, and even for the knower there is a certain loss of bliss, so worship must always be done: अतोऽलेपेऽपि लेपः स्यादतः कार्यैव सा सदा. The verse is reported here as cited by Madhva; this edition has not verified it in a text of that name.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

How do the person and case fit?

jijīviṣet is third person (‘one should wish to live’), but the next half-line speaks to ‘you’ (tvayi). Mahīdhara treats both as Vedic licences — a shift of person (पुरुषव्यत्ययः । त्वं जिजीविषेः) and of case (त्वयीति विभक्तिव्यत्ययः), so that the whole is addressed to the hearer. Śaṅkara keeps the forms and builds a single phrase, tvayi … nare, ‘for you, a person’, with the participle supplied: त्वयि जिजीविषति नरे. Mahīdhara then gives Śaṅkara’s reading too, as an alternative (उत्तरार्धस्वार्थान्तरं वा). The meaning is not much affected; the grammar shows why commentators had to supply words.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 2; Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya 3.4.13–14

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The mantra is for one who cannot hold to knowledge: अनात्मज्ञतयात्मग्रहणायाशक्तस्य. Knowledge and action are opposed like a mountain that cannot be moved, so the two mantras describe two separate paths. In his Brahmasūtra commentary he adds a second explanation of the same mantra: even if it is taken of the knower, the permission to act is there to praise knowledge — ‘विद्यास्तुतये कर्मानुज्ञानम्’ (Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya 3.4.14, cited under Parallels) — since one who knows is not touched by action even while acting.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Vedānta Deśika, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 2

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The mantra is for the knower: prescribed daily and occasional works, done as auxiliaries of knowledge and without attachment, are to be done for life (यावज्जीवमनुष्ठेयम्). Deśika cites the same Brahmasūtras as Śaṅkara (‘नाविशेषात्’, ‘स्तुतयेऽनुमतिर्वा’) and quotes ‘the Bhāṣya’ on 3.4.14 — in his tradition, Rāmānuja’s — to show that the permission praises knowledge without making works optional.

Dvaita · Madhva, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 2

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Action does not fail to cling to one who does not act: अकुर्वतः कर्म न लिप्यत इति नास्ति. Worship of the Lord is always to be done, by the ignorant and by the knower, since omitting it diminishes even the knower’s bliss (ज्ञानिनोऽपि यतो ह्रास आनन्दस्य भवेद् ध्रुवम्).

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara restricts the mantra to those not ready for Self-knowledge and holds knowledge and action to be incompatible; Deśika and Madhva apply it to the knower as well and hold that action rightly done supports knowledge. Both readings use the same Brahmasūtras (3.4.13–14). On the plain words — a life of work in which action does not cling — all agree; they disagree on whose life it is.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • कुर्वन्नेवेह kurvanneveha → कुर्वन् + एव + इह

    consonant doubling, then vowel sandhi (guṇa)

    A word-final n after a short vowel is doubled before a vowel: P. 8.3.32 (ङमो ह्रस्वादचि ङमुण्नित्यम्) — kurvan + eva → kurvann eva. Then a + i → e: P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः) — eva + iha → eveha.

  • जिजीविषेच्छतं jijīviṣecchataṃ → जिजीविषेत् + शतम्

    consonant sandhi (t + ś)

    Before ś the t becomes c, P. 8.4.40 (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः), and the ś after it may become ch, P. 8.4.63 (शश्छोऽटि): jijīviṣet + śatam → jijīviṣec chatam.

  • च्छतं समाः cchataṃ samāḥ → शतम् + समाः

    consonant sandhi (m → anusvāra)

    P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः). The Saṃhitā witness writes शतँ, Madhva’s text शतग्ं; the same nasal.

  • नान्यथेतोऽस्ति nānyatheto'sti → न + अन्यथा + इतः + अस्ति

    vowel sandhi (lengthening, guṇa) and visarga sandhi with elision

    na + anyathā → nānyathā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः). anyathā + itaḥ → anyathetaḥ, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः). itaḥ + asti: the final of itas becomes u before a, P. 6.1.113 (अतो रोरप्लुतादप्लुते), joins the a before it as o, and the a of asti is absorbed, P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति), written with avagraha: ito ’sti.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

जिजीविषेत्
Desiderative of jīv ‘to live’: reduplication and the suffix -sa (san), ‘to wish to live’, formed when the wisher and the one who lives are the same person, P. 3.1.7 (धातोः कर्मणः समानकर्तृकादिच्छायां वा). The optative (vidhiliṅ, P. 3.3.161 (विधिनिमन्त्रणामन्त्रणाधीष्टसंप्रश्नप्रार्थनेषु लिङ्)) expresses what one should or may do. The form is third person, but the second half addresses ‘you’; Mahīdhara explains the mismatch as a Vedic shift of person (पुरुषव्यत्ययः).
कुर्वन्
Present participle (śatṛ) of kṛ, ‘doing’: P. 3.2.124 (लटः शतृशानचावप्रथमासमानाधिकरणे). It describes the subject of jijīviṣet; with eva, the life one may wish for is a life of doing.
लिप्यते
Passive present of lip ‘to smear’. ‘Action is not smeared on the man’ is the image: action does not stick to the agent, as dirt or paint would. Śaṅkara turns the construction around (कर्मणा न लिप्यस इत्यर्थः).
इतः
The adverb ito (from idam with -tas), here with anyathā, ‘otherwise than this’. What ‘this’ refers to is the question the commentators answer differently (see Ambiguities).
त्वयि
Locative of tvam. Śaṅkara reads tvayi … nare together, ‘for you, a man’ (त्वयि जिजीविषति नरे नरमात्राभिमानिनि); Mahīdhara takes the locative as standing for the genitive tava (विभक्तिव्यत्ययः).

Anvaya

इह कर्माणि कुर्वन् एव शतम् समाः जिजीविषेत् । एवम् [जिजीविषति] त्वयि नरे इतः अन्यथा न अस्ति [येन] कर्म न लिप्यते ।

iha karmāṇi kurvan eva śatam samāḥ jijīviṣet | evam jijīviṣati tvayi nare itaḥ anyathā na asti yena karma na lipyate |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

The first half is a single sentence: an optative of wish or permission (jijīviṣet, ‘one should / may wish to live’) with an accusative of duration (śataṃ samāḥ) and a participle phrase (kurvann eveha karmāṇi) that states the condition. The second half has two short clauses whose relation is not marked: evaṃ tvayi nānyatheto ’sti (‘thus, for you, it is not otherwise than this’) and na karma lipyate nare (‘action does not cling to a person’). Śaṅkara joins them as main clause and purpose: there is no way other than this by which action does not cling. Mahīdhara’s first reading supplies ‘liberation’ to the first clause: इतः प्रकारादन्यथा प्रकारान्तरेण मुक्तिर्नास्ति. The words support both; what ‘this’ is — a life of works — is clear from the first half.

Ambiguities

Who is addressed?
The mantra does not say. Śaṅkara (and Mahīdhara after him) restricts it to one who cannot hold to Self-knowledge: अथेतरस्य अनात्मज्ञतयात्मग्रहणायाशक्तस्य इदमुपदिशति मन्त्रः. Deśika applies it to the knower himself: works are to be done for life as auxiliary to knowledge. The restriction is an interpretation drawn from the relation the commentator sees between mantras 1 and 2.
नान्यथेतोऽस्ति: ‘no other way’ to what?
Śaṅkara: no other way by which bad action does not cling. Mahīdhara’s first reading: no other way to liberation than desireless works. Deśika: for the one eligible for knowledge, what must be done is only this, ‘stated negatively for emphasis’ (व्यतिरेकेणोक्तं दृढीकारार्थम्).
न कर्म लिप्यते नरे: which action does not cling?
Śaṅkara: inauspicious action (येन प्रकारेणाशुभं कर्म न लिप्यते); Deśika: prescribed action does not become the cause of results such as heaven (कर्म न स्वर्गादिफललेपहेतुर्भवति); Mahīdhara: action done for liberation does not bind. The mantra says only ‘action’.
शतं समाः
Literally a hundred years; Śaṅkara takes it as the full human life span. Deśika: शतमिति च प्रायिक(प्रायशत)विषयम्, ‘a hundred’ refers to the usual span.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 17

The first half-line has 16 syllables; the second, as counted, 17: its first pāda, evaṃ tvayi nānyatheto ’sti, has 9 syllables for the usual 8. The witnesses agree on the words, so the extra syllable belongs to the text (a hypermetric pāda), not to a slip of one copy.

11 Connections with other scriptures

  • Bhagavad Gītā 5.10 Doctrinal comparison

    लिप्यते न स पापेन पद्मपत्रमिवाम्भसा

    lipyate na sa pāpena padmapatramivāmbhasā

    ‘He is not stained by evil, as a lotus leaf by water.’ The Gītā uses the same verb lip for the one who acts having placed his actions in Brahman and given up attachment. A doctrinal comparison with a shared image of not being smeared; the Gītā names the condition (giving up attachment) that the Īśā leaves implicit.

  • Bhagavad Gītā 3.8 Doctrinal comparison

    नियतं कुरु कर्म त्वं कर्म ज्यायो ह्यकर्मणः

    niyataṃ kuru karma tvaṃ karma jyāyo hyakarmaṇaḥ

    ‘Do your prescribed work; action is better than inaction.’ The Gītā’s defence of action; closest to the theistic readings of mantra 2.

  • Bhagavad Gītā 3.4 Doctrinal comparison

    न कर्मणामनारम्भान्नैष्कर्म्यं पुरुषोऽश्नुते

    na karmaṇāmanārambhānnaiṣkarmyaṃ puruṣo'śnute

    ‘Not by abstaining from works does a person reach freedom from action.’ A doctrinal parallel to Madhva’s ‘action does not fail to cling to one who does not act’.

  • Śaṅkara on Brahmasūtra 3.4.14 Doctrinal comparison

    स्तुतयेऽनुमतिर्वा

    stutaye'numatirvā

    Brahmasūtra 3.4.14, ‘or it is a permission, for praise’. Śaṅkara’s commentary on this sūtra quotes Īśā 2 and reads it as permission to act given to praise knowledge; under 3.4.7 and 3.4.13 the same mantra is the opponent’s proof-text and its answer. A direct use of this mantra in the Brahmasūtra tradition.

  • Brahmasūtra 3.4.13 Doctrinal comparison

    नाविशेषात्

    nāviśeṣāt

    ‘No, because there is no specification.’ The sūtra Śaṅkara and Deśika both connect with mantra 2: the injunction to live doing works does not specify the knower.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Corporate life

Many people who take their work seriously know two kinds of exhaustion: the tiredness of having done a great deal, and the corrosion of having been consumed by it — by the next promotion, by blame, by the need to be indispensable. The mantra does not advise working less. It says a full working life is possible in which the work ‘does not cling’. A modern reader might hear in this a distinction between commitment to the task and capture by its results. That is an application of the teaching, offered as a reflection; the commentators’ debate is about ritual action and liberation, not careers.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Is there a task you do well that stays with you long after it is finished? What exactly is it that clings?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry If action does not cling, is that because of something in the action, something in the agent, or the vision of mantra 1 that precedes it?

A contemplation exercise Choose one routine task tomorrow and do it with full care. When it is done, notice whether your mind keeps returning to how it will be judged. Simply note what returns, without trying to stop it.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.

Further notes

  • Deśika on this mantra quotes ‘the Bhāṣya’ on Brahmasūtra 3.4.14 (वाशब्दोऽवधारणार्थः । 'ईशा वास्यमिदं सर्वम' इति विद्याप्रकरणात् विद्यास्तुतये सर्वदा कर्मानुष्ठानानुमतिरियम्); in the Śrīvaiṣṇava tradition ‘the Bhāṣya’ is Rāmānuja’s Śrībhāṣya. The quotation is reported as Deśika gives it; the Śrībhāṣya itself is not among this edition’s sources.
  • Mahīdhara (on VS 40.2) gives two readings of the second half: desireless works lead to liberation by purifying the mind, or (Śaṅkara’s) there is no other way by which bad action does not cling.

Uncertainties recorded

  • interpretation The restriction of this mantra to those unfit for renunciation is Śaṅkara’s (and Mahīdhara’s) interpretation; Deśika and Madhva reject it. The page presents both.
