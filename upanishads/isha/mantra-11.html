Īśāvāsya Upaniṣad · Mantra 11 of 18

Knowing both together: crossing death, reaching the deathless

The resolution of mantras 9–10: one who knows vidyā and avidyā together crosses death by the one and attains immortality by the other.

5 Faithful English translation

Vidyā and avidyā — whoever knows that pair together, having crossed death by avidyā, by vidyā attains immortality.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Whoever knows vidyā and avidyā together crosses death by avidyā and reaches immortality by vidyā.

Key concepts विद्या vidyā अविद्या avidyā समुच्चय combination (of knowledge and action) मृत्यु death अमृत immortality, the deathless कर्मन् action, ritual work उपासना worship, meditation

7 Explain it to a beginner

After two warnings, here is the answer. Do not choose one path and despise the other: know the two together. Then the verse gives them a sequence. The first, the one it calls ‘not-knowledge’ (the old teachers say this means work and duty), carries you across ‘death’. The second, ‘knowledge’, brings you to what does not die. Notice the order: first crossing, then arriving. It is like a river crossing that has two parts, the boat and the far shore. You cannot reach the shore without the boat, and the boat is not the shore. Teachers have disagreed about exactly what ‘death’ and ‘the deathless’ mean here, and whether this is the final goal or a step on the way, but every reading keeps the two together.

An analogy A ferry gets you across the river, but the ferry is not your destination; you need both the boat and the shore, and you need them in that order.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the mantra itself say?

It names one person: ‘whoever knows vidyā and avidyā, that pair, together’. It then describes what that person does, in two steps joined by an absolutive: avidyā is the means of crossing death; vidyā the means of attaining immortality (amṛta). So the mantra resolves the double warning of mantra 9: the error there was devotion to either one alone; here each has a function, and the knower holds both. The mantra does not say what vidyā and avidyā are, or whether ‘immortality’ is final liberation.

Traditional Interpretation

What do vidyā and avidyā mean for Śaṅkara here?

Śaṅkara keeps the meanings he gave in mantra 9: विद्यां च अविद्यां च देवताज्ञानं कर्म चेत्यर्थः — vidyā is knowledge of deities, avidyā is ritual. ‘Knowing both together’ means knowing that one person is to practise both, and for such a person the results come in order: अविद्यया कर्मणा अग्निहोत्रादिना मृत्युम् … तीर्त्वा अतिक्रम्य विद्यया देवताज्ञानेन अमृतं देवतात्मभावम् अश्नुते. Death is natural action and knowledge — action and knowing as they arise of themselves, not as scripture directs — and the immortality reached is identity with the deity meditated on. For Śaṅkara this is a combination that belongs to the life of works; it is not the knowledge of the Self of mantras 6–8, which cannot be combined with anything.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Deśika read the same words?

For Deśika vidyā is meditation on the Supreme Self and avidyā the works that are subordinate to it: विद्याम् - परमात्मोपासनरूपाम्, अविद्याम् - तदङ्गभूतकर्मात्मिकां. Works prescribed as auxiliaries of knowledge overcome ‘death’, the past karma that contracts knowledge; meditation then attains the Supreme Self, who is the ‘immortal’. He argues that the word avidyā, though it negates vidyā, refers to what is closest to it, as a-brāhmaṇa refers to a kṣatriya rather than to a stone (क्षत्रियादिविषयाब्राह्मणशब्दादिवत् आसन्नतदन्यवृत्तिरन्तरङ्गकर्मविषय इति भावः), and he quotes the Bhāṣyakāra, as his tradition calls Rāmānuja: अत्राविद्याशब्दाभिहितं वर्णाश्रमविहितं कर्म. He also cites a Purāṇic verse, which he locates in the Viṣṇu Purāṇa (वैष्णवे पुराणे): तर्तुं मृत्युमविद्यया.

Comparative Vedānta

Is the ‘immortality’ of this mantra liberation?

Here the schools part clearly. Śaṅkara says no: तद्ध्यमृतमुच्यते, यद्देवतात्मगमनम् — what is called immortality here is going to the self of a deity, a high but finite state; liberation is by Self-knowledge, which the Upaniṣad taught in mantras 6–8 and which does not need works. Deśika says yes: the immortal is the Supreme Self, described elsewhere in the Upaniṣads as एतदमृतमभयमेतद्ब्रह्म, and attaining him is release. Mahīdhara follows Śaṅkara (on VS 40.14: देवतात्मभावमश्नुते). The disagreement is not about the words of the mantra, which all read alike, but about what kind of knowledge the Upaniṣad allows to be combined with action.

Traditional Interpretation

What about Madhva?

Madhva’s comment covers mantras 9–11 together, and in the digital text it is abridged. In it, those who know the Lord as he is together with the censure of what is untrue (अयथार्थस्य निन्दां च ये विदुः सह सज्जनाः) cross sorrow and ignorance and attain a nature of happiness and knowledge यथार्थस्य परिज्ञानात्, ‘through full knowledge of what is true’. On this reading the pair known ‘together’ is right knowledge of Nārāyaṇa and the rejection of wrong views of him. Because the text breaks off, how he assigns each word of the mantra cannot be stated with certainty.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 11

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

vidyā is knowledge of deities, avidyā ritual; one person practises both, crossing ‘death’ (natural action and knowledge) by ritual and reaching identity with a deity by meditation: अमृतं देवतात्मभावम् अश्नुते प्राप्नोति. This combination (samuccaya) is legitimate because each has its own result, but it is not liberation; Self-knowledge cannot be combined with action.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Vedānta Deśika, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 11

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

vidyā is meditation on the Supreme Self and avidyā the works auxiliary to it; works remove past karma, meditation attains the Supreme Self. ‘Together’ means both are to be undertaken, as principal and subsidiary: अङ्गाङ्गिनोरप्यनुष्ठेयत्वसाम्यादुभयं सह. The ‘immortal’ is the Lord himself, and attaining him is liberation.

Dvaita · Madhva, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantras 9–11 (one comment, abridged in the digital text)

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The pair known together is true knowledge of Nārāyaṇa as he is and the censure of what is untrue: तस्माद्यथास्वरूपं तु नारायणमनामयम्. By that censure one crosses sorrow and ignorance; by full knowledge of the true one attains a nature of happiness and knowledge.

Where they disagree

All three accept a combination of some kind; they differ on its terms and its goal. For Śaṅkara the combination is ritual with meditation on deities, and its ‘immortality’ is a finite divine state; for Deśika it is works with meditation on the Lord, and its goal is liberation; for Madhva it is right knowledge of the Lord with the rejection of wrong views. Deśika expressly argues against an interpretation with two means and two goals.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • विद्यां चाविद्यां च vidyāṃ cāvidyāṃ ca → विद्याम् + च + अविद्याम् + च

    anusvāra and vowel sandhi (savarṇa-dīrgha)

    Final m becomes anusvāra before c: P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः). ca + avidyām: a + a → ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

  • यस्तद्वेदोभयं yastadvedobhayaṃ → यः + तत् + वेद + उभयम्

    visarga, consonant and vowel sandhi (guṇa)

    yaḥ + tat: visarga before t becomes s, P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः). tat + veda: t is voiced to d before v (P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते)). veda + ubhayam: a + u → o, P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः).

  • विद्ययामृतमश्नुते vidyayāmṛtamaśnute → विद्यया + अमृतम् + अश्नुते

    vowel sandhi (savarṇa-dīrgha); m before a vowel

    vidyayā + amṛtam: ā + a → ā (P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः)); Madhva’s text writes the avagraha, विद्ययाऽमृतम्. amṛtam + aśnute: m before a vowel is written with it.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

वेद
Perfect form of vid ‘to know’ used with present meaning, ‘he knows’; P. 3.4.83 (विदो लटो वा) allows these forms as substitutes for the present of vid. The object is the whole pair, vidyā and avidyā, gathered up in tad ubhayam.
सह
‘Together’. Grammatically it qualifies the knowing: ‘who knows that pair together’. Whether ‘knowing together’ means practising both (Śaṅkara: एकेन पुरुषेण अनुष्ठेयं वेद, ‘knows that both are to be practised by one person’) or knowing both as equally to be undertaken though one is subordinate (Deśika: अङ्गाङ्गिनोरप्यनुष्ठेयत्वसाम्यादुभयं सह) is interpretation.
तीर्त्वा
Absolutive in -tvā of tṝ ‘to cross’: the action precedes that of the main verb and has the same subject (P. 3.4.21 (समानकर्तृकयोः पूर्वकाले)). The sequence is explicit: first death is crossed, then immortality is reached.
अश्नुते
aś (5th class, ‘to reach, attain’), middle present. Not to be confused with aś (9th class), ‘to eat’.
उभयम्
The neuter singular ubhaya, ‘the pair’, sums up two feminine nouns; Śaṅkara repeats it: तत् एतदुभयं.

Anvaya

यः विद्याम् च अविद्याम् च तत् उभयम् सह वेद [सः] अविद्यया मृत्युम् तीर्त्वा विद्यया अमृतम् अश्नुते ।

yaḥ vidyām ca avidyām ca tat ubhayam saha veda saḥ avidyayā mṛtyum tīrtvā vidyayā amṛtam aśnute |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A relative–correlative sentence. The relative clause (‘whoever knows vidyā and avidyā, that pair, together’) names the subject; the main clause, with the correlative ‘he’ understood, describes two steps in order, joined by the absolutive: ‘having crossed death by avidyā, by vidyā he attains immortality’. The second line is built as a chiasmus with the first: vidyā … avidyā in the first line, avidyā … vidyā in the second, each now given its own work.

Ambiguities

What is ‘death’, and what is ‘immortality’?
Śaṅkara: death is natural (unregulated) action and knowledge (स्वाभाविकं कर्म ज्ञानं च मृत्युशब्दवाच्यम्), and immortality is becoming one with a deity (तद्ध्यमृतमुच्यते, यद्देवतात्मगमनम्) — a relative immortality, not liberation. Deśika: death is past karma, which contracts knowledge (ज्ञानसङ्कोचरूपमृतिहेतुं प्राक्तनं कर्म), and immortality is the Supreme Self himself, so liberation proper. Mahīdhara follows Śaṅkara.
‘Together’ (saha)
Equal partners, or a main thing and its auxiliary? Śaṅkara: both to be practised by one person, each with its own result. Deśika: works are auxiliary to meditation, and both are to be undertaken; he rejects explicitly the reading that knowledge and works are two means with two separate goals (ये पुनरिह विद्याकर्माख्यसाधनद्वयसमुच्चयं मृत्युतरणामृतत्वप्राप्तिरूपसाध्यद्वित्वं च वर्णयन्ति).
Why is the order reversed?
In the first line vidyā comes first; in the second avidyā does its work first. The commentators take the second order as the order of practice: avidyā (works) first removes an obstacle, vidyā then gives the goal.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 14 + 16

Counted as written the half-lines have 14 and 16 syllables. The first is two short because two junctions are written contracted: ca + avidyām (चाविद्यां) and veda + ubhayam (वेदोभयं). Read with both vowels kept (ca avidyāṃ ca … veda ubhayam), it has 16. Such restoration of contracted vowels is common in Vedic metre; it is the counting, not a different text.

11 Connections with other scriptures

  • Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 1.1.4 Doctrinal comparison

    द्वे विद्ये वेदितव्ये

    dve vidye veditavye

    ‘Two kinds of knowledge are to be known, the higher and the lower.’ The Muṇḍaka also asks for two knowledges to be known, and also ranks them. A doctrinal comparison: the Muṇḍaka’s two are not the Īśā’s vidyā and avidyā in any commentator’s reading, but both texts refuse to treat knowledge as one undifferentiated thing.

  • Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.4.10 Modern thematic association

    य उ विद्यायां रताः

    ya u vidyāyāṃ ratāḥ

    The Bṛhadāraṇyaka has the warning of Īśā 9 but not its resolution. The Īśā’s mantras 10–11, which reconcile the two, have no counterpart in that passage.

  • Chāndogya Upaniṣad 7.1.3 Modern thematic association

    तरति शोक

    tarati śoka

    ‘The knower of the Self crosses sorrow.’ The same image of crossing (√tṝ) applied to knowledge; in the Īśā it is avidyā that crosses death and vidyā that reaches the deathless. A thematic association.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Aging and mortality

The mantra describes a crossing and an arrival, and gives each its own means. People facing the end of a long working life often find themselves in exactly that place: the duties that carried them — raising a family, keeping a job, serving a community — have brought them across a great deal, and they sense that something else is needed for what comes next. The mantra does not ask them to disown the duties, or to pretend that duties alone are enough. It asks that the two be held together and in their order. This is a reflection on the teaching, not part of it.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What in your life is like the ferry — necessary for getting across — and what is like the far shore?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry If ‘death’ is crossed by avidyā and ‘the deathless’ reached by vidyā, can the deathless be the result of anything at all? How do the commentators answer?

A contemplation exercise At the end of each day for a week, note one thing you did out of duty and one thing you came to understand. On the last day, read the list and ask how the two were connected.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
