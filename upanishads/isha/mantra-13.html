Īśāvāsya Upaniṣad · Mantra 13 of 18

One thing from becoming, another from non-becoming

The two worships have different results, and the teaching is handed down from the wise.

5 Faithful English translation

Quite other, they say, [comes] from becoming (sambhava); other, they say, from non-becoming (asambhava). Thus we have heard from the wise who explained that to us.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

One result, they say, comes from becoming, and another from non-becoming: so we have heard from the wise who taught us this.

Key concepts सम्भूति sambhūti, ‘coming into being’ असम्भूति asambhūti, ‘non-becoming’ धीर the wise श्रुति śruti, ‘what is heard’

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse sounds like a student reporting a lesson. ‘They say one thing comes from becoming, and another from not-becoming — that is what we heard from the wise who explained it to us.’ It does two things. First, it keeps apart the two paths that the previous verse warned about: each one, followed on its own, leads somewhere different. Second, it tells us where the teaching came from: not from the speaker’s own cleverness, but from teachers who passed it on. That second point matters for how the whole text should be read. The Upaniṣad presents itself as received wisdom, handed from teacher to student, and invites the reader to join that line of listening before arguing with it.

An analogy A medical student who says ‘this drug does one thing and that drug another — so our professors taught us’ is reporting tested knowledge, not offering a personal opinion; the verse speaks in that voice.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the mantra state?

That ‘they’ — the wise of the second line — declare something different from sambhava and something different from asambhava; and that this is what ‘we’ have heard from the wise who explained it to ‘us’. The mantra states that the two differ, not what either result is. It repeats the frame of mantra 10 exactly, replacing vidyayā and avidyayā with the ablatives sambhavāt and asambhavāt.

Traditional Interpretation

What results does Śaṅkara assign?

Śaṅkara introduces the mantra as giving the reason for combining the two worships: their results are parts of one whole (उभयोरुपासनयोः समुच्चयकारणमवयवफलभेदमाह). From sambhava, the worship of the effect-Brahman, comes power such as aṇimā, the ability to become minute (अणिमाद्यैश्वर्यलक्षणम्); from asambhava, the worship of the unmanifest, comes what mantra 12 called blind darkness and what the Purāṇic writers call dissolution into prakṛti (प्रकृतिलय इति च पौराणिकैरुच्यते). Mahīdhara, on VS 40.10, gives the same explanation in nearly the same words.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Deśika read the verse?

Deśika comments only briefly: the word tat refers to the pair, stated next in mantra 14, that is to be contemplated together (अत्र तच्छब्दः समुच्चित्यानुसन्धेयतया वक्ष्यमाणद्वितयं वक्ति). The mantra thus prepares the instruction of 14 rather than assigning cosmic fruits to two worships.

Direct Textual Meaning

Why the appeal to ‘the wise’?

The second line — ‘thus we have heard from the wise who explained it to us’ — appears twice in the Īśā (10, 13) and, with vyācacakṣire, in the Kena (KeU 1.4). It presents the teaching as received from a line of teachers. Śaṅkara, commenting on the identical line in mantra 10, says that this teaching of the ācāryas has come down in succession from teacher to teacher.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 13

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The two worships have separate results, which is why they are to be combined: from worship of the effect-Brahman, powers (अणिमाद्यैश्वर्यलक्षणम्); from worship of the unmanifest, dissolution into prakṛti. Both results lie within the world; the mantra concerns meditation, not knowledge of the Self.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Vedānta Deśika, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 13

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The mantra’s tat points forward to the pair to be contemplated together (समुच्चित्यानुसन्धेयतया वक्ष्यमाणद्वितयं): the removal of obstacles and the attainment of Brahman, two stages of one path.

Dvaita · Madhva (as far as his comment survives), Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantras 12–14

A modern comparative inference

Madhva’s surviving comment treats 12–14 together and does not gloss this mantra separately. On its reading of the pair as the Lord’s creating and destroying, the ‘different results’ would be those of acknowledging each power — an inference from the comment on 12–14, not his own gloss of this line.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara assigns two cosmic results (powers; dissolution into prakṛti) to two worships; Deśika reads the verse as pointing forward to a single combined contemplation on the path to Brahman. Madhva’s own gloss of this mantra is not preserved in the edition’s source.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • अन्यदेवाहुः anyadevāhuḥ → अन्यत् + एव + आहुः

    consonant sandhi (voicing); vowel sandhi (savarṇa-dīrgha)

    A word-final t becomes d before a vowel (P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते)); eva + āhuḥ: a + ā → ā (P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः)).

  • सम्भवादन्यदाहुरसम्भवात् sambhavādanyadāhurasambhavāt → सम्भवात् + अन्यत् + आहुः + असम्भवात्

    consonant and visarga sandhi

    sambhavāt and anyat voice their t to d before a vowel; āhuḥ before a vowel: a visarga after u becomes r (āhur asambhavāt).

  • धीराणां ये dhīrāṇāṃ ye → धीराणाम् + ये

    consonant sandhi (m → anusvāra)

    P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • नस्तद्विचचक्षिरे nastadvicacakṣire → नः + तत् + विचचक्षिरे

    visarga sandhi; consonant sandhi

    A visarga before t becomes s: P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः). tat + vicacakṣire: t is voiced to d before v.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

सम्भवात्
sambhava, ‘coming into being’, from sam + √bhū, is a masculine action noun of the same root as sambhūti (mantras 12, 14); asambhava is its negative. The commentators treat the two pairs as one: Śaṅkara glosses सम्भवात् सम्भूतेः and असम्भवात् असम्भूतेः. The Mādhyandina pada-pāṭha divides स॒म्भ॒वादिति॑ सम्ऽभ॒वात् and अस॑म्भवा॒दित्यस॑म्ऽभवात्.
अन्यत्
With anya, ‘other’, the ablative can express that from which something differs (P. 2.3.29 (अन्यारादितरर्त्तेदिक्‌शब्दाञ्चूत्तरपदाजाहियुक्ते)): ‘[a result] other than sambhava’. Śaṅkara instead takes the ablatives as the source of the result: ‘from sambhava they declare a different [fruit]’. Both constructions give the same teaching — the two results differ.
आहुः
From the defective root ah, ‘to say’, which has only a few perfect forms; āhuḥ, ‘they say’, is used with present meaning.
शुश्रुम
Reduplicated perfect of √śru, 1st person plural parasmaipada: ‘we have heard’. The construction with the genitive dhīrāṇām (‘heard from the wise’) is the usual one for hearing from a person.
विचचक्षिरे
Perfect 3rd plural ātmanepada of √cakṣ, ‘to see, to declare’, with vi: ‘they explained’. The Kena Upaniṣad uses the same formula with vyācacakṣire (KeU 1.4), and Vedānta Deśika’s mūla is keyed so (corrections.json: a slip of the digital text).

Anvaya

सम्भवात् अन्यत् एव [फलम्] आहुः । असम्भवात् अन्यत् [फलम्] आहुः । इति ये नः तत् विचचक्षिरे [तेषाम्] धीराणाम् [वचनम्] शुश्रुम ।

sambhavāt anyat eva phalam āhuḥ | asambhavāt anyat phalam āhuḥ | iti ye naḥ tat vicacakṣire teṣām dhīrāṇām vacanam śuśruma |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

The first line is two parallel statements, anyad eva āhuḥ sambhavāt / anyad āhur asambhavāt, with an unexpressed subject ‘they’ (the wise of the second line). The second line is a formula of transmission: iti closes the reported statement; śuśruma dhīrāṇām, ‘we have heard from the wise’, is completed by the relative clause ye nas tad vicacakṣire, ‘who explained that to us’. The line repeats mantra 10 exactly.

Ambiguities

Ablative of source or of comparison
sambhavāt and asambhavāt can be the source of a result (‘from becoming, a different [result]’ — Śaṅkara, Mahīdhara) or the standard from which something differs (‘other than becoming’). In the second construction the line would say that what the wise teach is different from both, the sense the parallel formula of the Kena (KeU 1.4: ‘other than the known, and beyond the unknown’) has. The commentators take the first.
sambhava and sambhūti
The mantra uses sambhava where 12 and 14 use sambhūti. The commentators equate them; the change may be metrical, since sambhūteḥ would add a syllable.
What does tat refer to?
For Śaṅkara, the fruits of the two worships (व्याकृताव्याकृतोपासनफलं व्याख्यातवन्त इत्यर्थः); for Deśika, the pair of contemplations to be combined, which mantra 14 states.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16

Two half-lines of 16 syllables.

11 Connections with other scriptures

  • Kena Upaniṣad 1.4 Direct textual parallel

    इति शुश्रुम पूर्वेषां ये नस्तद्व्याचचक्षिरे

    iti śuśruma pūrveṣāṃ ye nastadvyācacakṣire

    The Kena’s line differs only in pūrveṣām (‘from the ancients’) for dhīrāṇām and vyācacakṣire for vicacakṣire. There the teaching heard is that Brahman is ‘other than the known and beyond the unknown’ — a use of anyat that supports reading the ablative as one of comparison.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Ethical leadership

Leaders are often tempted to present inherited wisdom as their own insight. The mantra’s second line does the opposite: ‘so we have heard from the wise who explained it to us.’ Naming where an idea came from is not only modest; it lets others test it against its source. This is a reflection on the formula of transmission, not an interpretation of sambhava and asambhava.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Who taught you something important? Do you remember who it was when you pass it on?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry If two paths lead to two different results, what makes a combination of them better than either — and is the combination itself a third path or simply both?

A contemplation exercise Next time you explain an idea to someone, name the person or book you learned it from, and notice how that changes the conversation.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
