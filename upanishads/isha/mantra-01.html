Īśāvāsya Upaniṣad · Mantra 1 of 18

Everything here is to be dwelt in by the Lord

The opening vision and the two commands that follow from it: let go, and do not covet.

5 Faithful English translation

By the Lord [is] to be covered (or: dwelt in) all this — whatever moves in the moving [world]. By that [which is] given up, you should enjoy. Do not covet: whose, indeed, [is] wealth?

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

All this, whatever moves in this moving world, is to be seen as dwelt in by the Lord. Let go of it, and so enjoy it. Do not covet — for whose is wealth?

Key concepts ईश् the Lord, ‘ruler’ जगत् world, ‘the moving’ त्याग giving up, relinquishment भोग enjoyment, use एषणा desire, ‘the three desires’

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse makes one big claim and gives two pieces of advice. The claim: everything in the world, every moving and changing thing, is filled by something greater — the text calls it ‘the Lord’ — the way a house is filled by the life of the family who lives in it. The advice follows from the claim. First, let go of the idea that things are yours, and then enjoy them; the letting go comes first, and the enjoying grows out of it. Second, do not grab at what others have. The verse ends with a question that is meant to stop you: whose is wealth, really? Teachers have argued for centuries about the exact sense of ‘let go’ and ‘enjoy’, and you will meet their views below, but the direction is plain: live fully, hold lightly.

An analogy A guest in a friend’s home can enjoy the garden, the books and the meals completely — precisely because the guest is not trying to take any of it home.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the mantra itself say, before any interpretation?

The Sanskrit makes a statement and gives two commands. The statement is that ‘all this’ (idaṃ sarvam) — specified as ‘whatever moves in the moving world’ — is ‘to be covered’ or ‘to be dwelt in’ (vāsyam) ‘by the Lord’ (īśā). The commands are addressed to ‘you’: ‘you should enjoy’ (or ‘use’, bhuñjīthāḥ) by means of, or together with, ‘that which is given up’ (tena tyaktena); and ‘do not covet’ (mā gṛdhaḥ). The final words, kasya svid dhanam, either complete the prohibition (‘anyone’s wealth’) or ask a question (‘whose is wealth?’). The mantra does not name the Lord, does not say what ‘covering’ consists in, and does not say what is to be given up. Those are the places where the commentaries begin.

Traditional Interpretation

How does Śaṅkara read the opening vision?

Śaṅkara takes īśā as the Lord who is the inner Self of every creature, and vāsyam as ‘to be covered’ (आच्छादनीयम्): the whole moving world is to be covered over by the vision of the Self — अहमेवेदं सर्वमिति, ‘I alone am all this’ — so that its appearance as a world of separate owners and things is hidden by what is real. His image is sandalwood: a bad smell that sandalwood acquires from damp is removed when the wood is rubbed and its own fragrance comes out; so the world superimposed on the Self, with its doing and enjoying, is ‘covered’ by the Self’s own nature. For him this vision is the condition for renunciation: एवमीश्वरात्मभावनया युक्तस्य पुत्राद्येषणात्रयसंन्यासे एवाधिकारः, न कर्मसु — one who holds it is fit for renouncing the three desires (for sons, wealth and worlds), not for ritual works.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Can ‘bhuñjīthāḥ’ mean ‘protect’?

Śaṅkara glosses the verb भुञ्जीथाः पालयेथाः, ‘you should protect [yourself]’, and argues from tena tyaktena: an abandoned or dead son or servant does not protect anyone, so tyaktena must mean ‘by renunciation’ (तेन त्यक्तेन त्यागेनेत्यर्थः). The grammatical difficulty is that bhuñjīthāḥ is an ātmanepada form, and Pāṇini prescribes the ātmanepada of bhuj precisely when it does not mean ‘protect’ — P. 1.3.66 (भुजोऽनवने). On the form alone, the verb means ‘enjoy, eat, use’, which is how Mahīdhara takes it (भुञ्जीथाः भोगमनुभवेः). Śaṅkara’s gloss is therefore an interpretation that works against the usual sense of the form; it is not a mistake that can be dismissed, because Vedic usage does not always follow the later rule, but it is not the plain sense either.

Traditional Interpretation

What does renunciation actually mean here?

The commentators do not agree on what is ‘given up’. For Śaṅkara it is the three desires, renounced by a monk (saṃnyāsa). For Mahīdhara, on the same mantra in the Mādhyandina recension, it is the sense of ownership: ममेदमिति धियं त्यजेत्यर्थः — ‘give up the thought “this is mine”’ — and he adds that because all goods pass from hand to hand, the idea that something is mine is ignorance (अतो ममेदमिति बुद्धिरविद्या). For Deśika it is the world itself, given up because one sees its many defects, while one uses what the body needs. For Madhva the word does not mean renunciation at all but a gift: what the Lord has given. The mantra’s own words support the common ground: whatever ‘giving up’ is, it is the opposite of ‘coveting’ (gṛdh), the attitude the second half forbids.

Traditional Interpretation

Who is ‘the Lord’ of this mantra?

The Upaniṣad says only īś, ‘ruler’. Śaṅkara identifies the ruler with the inner Self (pratyagātman) of all beings: ईशिता परमेश्वरः परमात्मा सर्वस्य. Deśika identifies him with the controller of all, distinct from the individual self (जीवादत्यन्तविलक्षणतया प्रख्यातेन सर्वनियन्त्रा पुरुषोत्तमेन), and argues at length that the word does not here mean Rudra. Madhva reads the whole as a hymn to Hari, citing a story that Svāyambhuva Manu praised Viṣṇu with these mantras. These identifications belong to the commentators’ systems; the mantra itself names no deity.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 1

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The Lord is one’s own Self, and the world is ‘to be covered’ by the knowledge अहमेवेदं सर्वमिति; what is renounced is everything other than the Self, परमार्थसत्यात्मभावनया त्यक्तं स्यात्. tena tyaktena means ‘by renunciation’, bhuñjīthāḥ means ‘protect [yourself]’, and the mantra is addressed to one fit for renouncing the three desires (पुत्राद्येषणात्रयसंन्यासे एवाधिकारः, न कर्मसु). Mantra 2 is then for the one who cannot.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Vedānta Deśika, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 1

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

‘This’ is everything other than the Lord, conscious and non-conscious (तत्तत्प्रमाणसिद्धमीश्वरव्यतिरिक्तं चिदचिदात्मकम्); it is pervaded and supported by the Lord, the Supreme Person, who is distinct from the individual self. tena tyaktena: the world, seen as full of defects, is given up (दोषभूयस्त्वदर्शनात् परित्यक्तेन), and one enjoys only what sustains the body — or, in his alternative, one enjoys the Lord, the highest object of enjoyment (निरतिशयभोग्यं).

Dvaita · Madhva, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 1

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

One word, īśāvāsyam: the world is fit to be the dwelling of the Lord (ईशस्यावासयोग्यमीशावास्यम्), because nothing in it can act by itself and all its activity depends on him. tena tyaktena means ‘with what has been given by him’ (तेन ईशेन । त्यक्तेन दत्तेन): enjoy what the Lord gives, and so do not beg from another. The Lord and the world remain distinct.

Where they disagree

Three questions divide the schools: whether ‘the Lord’ is one’s own Self (Śaṅkara) or a Person distinct from the self who pervades and supports the world (Deśika, Madhva); whether tyakta means renunciation (Śaṅkara), the world given up (Deśika) or a gift (Madhva); and whether the mantra addresses a renouncer (Śaṅkara) or every seeker. All three agree that the second half forbids greed.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • इदं सर्वं idaṃ sarvaṃ → इदम् + सर्वम्

    consonant sandhi (m → anusvāra)

    A word-final m becomes anusvāra before a consonant: P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः). The Saṃhitā witnesses write the nasal before s as ँ (इदँ सर्वं), the Upaniṣad witnesses as ं; the sound is the same.

  • वास्यमिदं vāsyamidaṃ → वास्यम् + इदम्

    no change (m before a vowel)

    Before a vowel a final m stays m and is simply written with the following vowel.

  • यत्किञ्च yatkiñca → यत् + किम् + च

    consonant sandhi

    t stays before the voiceless k. kim + ca: the m becomes anusvāra (P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः)), which at a word-end may be written as the nasal of the following class, ñ before c (P. 8.4.59 (वा पदान्तस्य), after P. 8.4.58 (अनुस्वारस्य ययि परसवर्णः)). AS writes यत्किं च.

  • भुञ्जीथा मा bhuñjīthā mā → भुञ्जीथाः + मा

    visarga sandhi (āḥ before a voiced sound)

    After ā, a visarga before a voiced sound becomes y (P. 8.3.17 (भोभगोअघोअपूर्वस्य योऽशि)), and that y is dropped before a consonant (P. 8.3.22 (हलि सर्वेषाम्)): bhuñjīthāḥ + mā → bhuñjīthā mā.

  • स्विद्धनम् sviddhanam → स्वित् + धनम्

    consonant sandhi (voicing)

    A word-final t is voiced to d before a voiced sound (P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते) sets the final as d; before dh it stays d): svit + dhanam → sviddhanam.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

ईशा
Instrumental of the root noun īś (a root used as a noun with no suffix, as Śaṅkara’s ईष्टे इति ईट् shows). The instrumental marks the agent of the gerundive vāsyam: ‘to be covered by the Lord’. Madhva and Deśika’s mūla print one word, ईशावास्यम्; see ‘Ambiguities’.
वास्यम्
A gerundive (‘to be …’) in -ya. Mahīdhara derives it from vas ‘to cover’ with the suffix ṇyat, citing P. 3.1.124 (ऋहलोर्ण्यत्), the rule that gives ṇyat after a root ending in ṛ or a consonant. Deśika allows both senses: वास्यम् - व्याप्यमित्यर्थः; सर्वाधारे स्वस्मिन् स्वेन वासनीयं वा — ‘to be pervaded’, or ‘to be made to dwell [in him]’.
भुञ्जीथाः
Optative (vidhiliṅ) of bhuj, 7th class, in the ātmanepada. P. 1.3.66 (भुजोऽनवने) prescribes the ātmanepada for bhuj when it does not mean ‘to protect’; the ātmanepada form therefore points to ‘enjoy, eat’, which is how Mahīdhara reads it (भोगमनुभवेः). Śaṅkara glosses it ‘you should protect’ (पालयेथाः), the sense the rule associates with the parasmaipada. The tension is real and is discussed in the study on tena tyaktena bhuñjīthāḥ.
गृधः
An augmentless aorist form used as an injunctive after mā: the prohibition ‘do not covet’. With mā the aorist is used (P. 3.3.175 (माङि लुङ्)) and the augment a- is not added (P. 6.4.74 (न माङ्योगे)): agṛdhaḥ → mā gṛdhaḥ.
जगत्
Formally a present participle of gam ‘to go’ (reduplicated: ‘going, moving’), used as a noun for the world as what is always moving. jagatyām is the feminine form of the same word, ‘the moving one’, used for the earth.

Anvaya

जगत्याम् यत् किम् च जगत् [तत्] इदम् सर्वम् ईशा वास्यम् । तेन त्यक्तेन भुञ्जीथाः । मा गृधः । कस्य स्वित् धनम् ।

jagatyām yat kim ca jagat tat idam sarvam īśā vāsyam | tena tyaktena bhuñjīthāḥ | mā gṛdhaḥ | kasya svit dhanam |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two halves. The first is a statement with a gerundive predicate: ‘all this, whatever moves in the moving world, [is] to be covered (or: dwelt in) by the Lord’; the relative clause yat kiṃ ca jagatyāṃ jagat specifies idaṃ sarvam. The second gives two injunctions in the second person — an optative (bhuñjīthāḥ, ‘you should enjoy’) and a prohibition (mā gṛdhaḥ, ‘do not covet’) — and ends with kasya svid dhanam, which can be the object of the prohibition (‘do not covet anyone’s wealth’) or a separate question (‘whose, after all, is wealth?’). Śaṅkara gives both constructions.

Ambiguities

ईशा वास्यम् or ईशावास्यम्
GRETIL, Advaita Sharada and the Mādhyandina pada-pāṭha (ई॒शा। वा॒स्य᳖म्।) divide two words: īśā, ‘by the Lord’, and vāsyam, ‘to be covered/dwelt in’. Madhva reads one compound, īśa + āvāsya, ‘fit to be the dwelling of the Lord’ (ईशस्यावासयोग्यमीशावास्यम्). The letters are identical; only the division differs. The name of the Upaniṣad, Īśāvāsya, is taken from these opening words and does not decide the question.
वास्यम्: ‘covered’ or ‘dwelt in’
vas ‘to clothe, cover’ (Śaṅkara, Mahīdhara) and vas ‘to dwell’ (Deśika’s second option, Madhva’s āvāsa) are different roots with the same form. ‘Covered by the Lord’ suggests seeing the world through the Lord, so that its separateness is covered over; ‘dwelt in by the Lord’ suggests the Lord inhabiting the world.
तेन त्यक्तेन
Instrumental of means (‘by that renunciation’, Śaṅkara), instrumental of accompaniment (‘with that [world] given up’, Deśika), or ‘with what has been given [by him]’ (Madhva, taking tyakta in the sense of a gift). The words allow each.
भुञ्जीथाः
‘Enjoy, use, eat’ (the sense the ātmanepada form normally has) or ‘protect’ (Śaṅkara). See the grammar note.
कस्य स्विद्धनम्
Either the object (‘do not covet the wealth of anyone’) or a rhetorical question (‘whose is wealth?’ — that is, no one’s). Śaṅkara gives both: कस्यचित् परस्य स्वस्य वा धनं मा काङ्क्षीः, and न कस्यचिद्धनमस्ति, यद्गृध्येत.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16

Two half-lines of 16 syllables (8 + 8 + 8 + 8). Mahīdhara names the metre of the whole adhyāya as anuṣṭubh (अनुष्टुप्छन्दस्को).

Textual variations between witnesses

The witnesses differ here only in spelling (listed in section 14).

11 Connections with other scriptures

  • Chāndogya Upaniṣad 3.14.1 Doctrinal comparison

    सर्वं खल्विदं ब्रह्म

    sarvaṃ khalvidaṃ brahma

    ‘All this is Brahman.’ The Chāndogya states the identity outright; the Īśā speaks of a Lord by whom all this is to be covered or dwelt in. A doctrinal comparison, not a verbal parallel: the Advaita reading brings the two together, the theistic readings keep the Lord and ‘all this’ distinct.

  • Bhagavad Gītā 9.4 Doctrinal comparison

    मया ततमिदं सर्वं

    mayā tatamidaṃ sarvaṃ

    Kṛṣṇa: ‘all this is pervaded by me in my unmanifest form’. The same idaṃ sarvam pervaded by the divine; the Gītā adds at once that the beings are in him and he is not in them, a paradox close to Īśā 4–5.

  • Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 3.5.1 Doctrinal comparison

    पुत्रैषणायाश्च वित्तैषणायाश्च लोकैषणायाश्च

    putraiṣaṇāyāśca vittaiṣaṇāyāśca lokaiṣaṇāyāśca

    Śaṅkara’s ‘three desires’ (eṣaṇā) for sons, wealth and worlds come from this Bṛhadāraṇyaka passage, where knowers of the Self rise above them and live as mendicants. His reading of tena tyaktena depends on it.

  • Bhagavad Gītā 2.71 Modern thematic association

    निर्ममो निरहङ्कारः स शान्तिमधिगच्छति

    nirmamo nirahaṅkāraḥ sa śāntimadhigacchati

    The person who gives up all desires and lives ‘without “mine”, without “I”’ finds peace. A thematic association with Mahīdhara’s reading of tyakta as giving up the thought ‘this is mine’.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Wealth and consumption

A modern reader meets this mantra in a world organised around acquiring: more storage, more subscriptions, more upgrades, each promising to complete us. The mantra does not ask anyone to stop using things — even Śaṅkara’s reading, the most renunciant, is about an inner relation to them. It asks a prior question: whose is this? People who have lost a home, or cleared out a parent’s house, often discover that the objects they guarded so carefully were held for a while and then passed on. The mantra’s order — let go, and so enjoy — suggests that the pleasure of a thing is not increased by clutching it. This is a reflection on the teaching, not part of it.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Think of something you own and love. Would you enjoy it more or less if you thought of it as lent to you?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry If all this is ‘to be covered by the Lord’, what remains that can be called ‘mine’ — and who is the ‘I’ that wants to own?

A contemplation exercise For one day, each time you pick up something you value — a phone, a pen, a cup — pause for one breath and say silently, ‘lent, not owned’. In the evening, note whether anything about how you held those things changed.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.

Further notes

  • Śaṅkara’s introduction to the Upaniṣad (Advaita Sharada unit IS_C01_I01) explains why these mantras are not used in ritual: they reveal the Self, which no action produces or changes.
  • Mahīdhara (on VS 40.1) records the tradition that the adhyāya was seen by the seer Dadhyañc Ātharvaṇa and has the Self as its deity: आत्मदेवत्य अनुष्टुप्छन्दस्कोऽध्यायो दधीचाथर्वणेन दृष्टः.
