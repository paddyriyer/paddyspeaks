Īśāvāsya Upaniṣad

Concept map

The eighteen mantras as a web of teachings. Solid lines follow the text’s own sequence and wording; dashed lines are interpretive links, each attributed.

Select a teaching to see what it connects to. The connections marked as interpretive are the commentators’ or the editor’s, not statements of the text; each says whose it is.

The Lord in all this · 1

All this, whatever moves in the moving world, is to be covered or dwelt in by the Lord. The opening vision, from which the rest unfolds.

  • therefore → Let go, and enjoy — The two halves of mantra 1.
  • described as → Unmoving, swifter than mind — The commentators read 4–5 as describing the reality named in mantra 1.

Study: Īśāvāsyam idaṃ sarvam

Let go, and enjoy · 1

With that given up, enjoy; do not covet — whose is wealth? The first instruction, read differently by each commentator.

  • The Lord in all this → therefore — The two halves of mantra 1.
  • or else → Act without clinging — Śaṅkara: mantra 1 is for the renouncer, mantra 2 for the one who cannot renounce.

Study: Tena tyaktena bhuñjīthāḥ

Act without clinging · 2

Doing works here, one may wish to live a hundred years; action does not cling to such a person.

  • Let go, and enjoy → or else — Śaṅkara: mantra 1 is for the renouncer, mantra 2 for the one who cannot renounce.
  • contrast → Worlds of blind darkness — Mantra 3 follows as the fate of those who obscure the self.
  • works and knowledge → Vidyā and avidyā — Śaṅkara and Deśika both read avidyā in 9–11 as action (karma).

Study: Karma and Jñāna

Worlds of blind darkness · 3

Those who slay the self go after death to worlds covered in blind darkness.

  • Act without clinging → contrast — Mantra 3 follows as the fate of those who obscure the self.

Unmoving, swifter than mind · 4, 5

The one that does not move is faster than the mind; it moves and does not move; it is far and near, within all this and outside it.

  • The Lord in all this → described as — The commentators read 4–5 as describing the reality named in mantra 1.
  • and as → The bright, bodiless one — Mantra 8 describes the same ‘he’ positively and by negation.

Study: Īśāvāsyam idaṃ sarvam

All beings in the self · 6

One who sees all beings in the self, and the self in all beings, does not shrink from anything.

  • The bright, bodiless one → known as — The knower of 6–7 sees what 4–5 and 8 describe.
  • matures into → No delusion, no grief — Mantra 7 continues mantra 6.

No delusion, no grief · 7

For the knower in whom all beings have become the self, what delusion, what grief, seeing oneness?

  • All beings in the self → matures into — Mantra 7 continues mantra 6.
  • echo → That person I am — Editor’s note: ‘so ’ham asmi’ (16) recalls the self seen in all (6–7); the commentators differ on what the identification means.

The bright, bodiless one · 8

He has gone round — bright, bodiless, without wound or sinew, pure, untouched by evil; the seer, wise, all-encompassing, self-existent, who ordered things for endless years.

  • Unmoving, swifter than mind → and as — Mantra 8 describes the same ‘he’ positively and by negation.
  • known as → All beings in the self — The knower of 6–7 sees what 4–5 and 8 describe.

Vidyā and avidyā · 9, 10, 11

Both alone lead into darkness; each has its own result; knowing both together, one crosses death and attains immortality.

  • Act without clinging → works and knowledge — Śaṅkara and Deśika both read avidyā in 9–11 as action (karma).
  • same pattern → Sambhūti and asambhūti — Mantras 12–14 repeat the form of 9–11 word for word apart from the paired terms.

Study: Vidyā and Avidyā

Sambhūti and asambhūti · 12, 13, 14

The same pattern again with becoming and non-becoming: each alone leads to darkness, both together cross death.

  • Vidyā and avidyā → same pattern — Mantras 12–14 repeat the form of 9–11 word for word apart from the paired terms.
  • the way of the gods → The golden lid — Śaṅkara: the one who has combined works and meditation asks at death for the path of light.

Study: Sambhūti and Asambhūti

The golden lid · 15

The face of the real is covered by a golden vessel; uncover it, Pūṣan, for one whose dharma is truth, to see.

  • uncovered → That person I am — The lid removed (15), the form is seen (16).
  • Sambhūti and asambhūti → the way of the gods — Śaṅkara: the one who has combined works and meditation asks at death for the path of light.

Study: The Final Prayers

That person I am · 16

Draw back your rays; I see your fairest form. That person yonder — I am he.

  • No delusion, no grief → echo — Editor’s note: ‘so ’ham asmi’ (16) recalls the self seen in all (6–7); the commentators differ on what the identification means.
  • The golden lid → uncovered — The lid removed (15), the form is seen (16).
  • then → Breath to air, body to ash — The dying person’s prayers in sequence; BU 5.15.1 has the same order.

Study: The Final Prayers

Breath to air, body to ash · 17

Breath to the immortal air, this body ending in ash. Om, mind, remember what was done.

  • That person I am → then — The dying person’s prayers in sequence; BU 5.15.1 has the same order.
  • then → Lead by a good path — The final prayer, also Ṛgveda 1.189.1.

Study: The Final Prayers

Lead by a good path · 18

Agni, lead us by a good path to wealth; you know all ways; keep crooked fault from us; we offer you our fullest homage.

  • Breath to air, body to ash → then — The final prayer, also Ṛgveda 1.189.1.
