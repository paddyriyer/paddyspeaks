Īśāvāsya Upaniṣad · Mantra 8 of 18

He went round: the bright, the bodiless, untouched by evil

The nature of the Self (or of the one who reaches it), described by negations and by titles, and its ordering of things for all time.

5 Faithful English translation

He went round (or: reached) the bright, the bodiless, the woundless, the sinewless, the pure, the unpierced by evil. Seer, wise, encompassing, self-existent, he distributed things in accordance with [their] truth, for the everlasting years.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

He has gone everywhere — bright, bodiless, without wound or sinew, pure, untouched by evil. The seer, the wise, the all-encompassing, the self-existent, he has ordered all things rightly for years without end.

Key concepts कवि seer, poet स्वयम्भू self-existent आत्मन् self, Self ईश् the Lord, ‘ruler’

7 Explain it to a beginner

The earlier verses talked about seeing the self in all things. This verse describes that self, partly by saying what it is not. It has no body, so it cannot be wounded; it has no sinews or flesh; it is pure, and no wrongdoing sticks to it. Then the verse gives it four titles: the seer who sees everything, the wise one, the one who is above and around all things, the one who exists by itself, needing nothing else. Finally it says this being has set everything in its proper order for all time. Notice how the verse speaks: it cannot point to a picture, so it removes, one by one, the things we would wrongly add — body, injury, stain. Teachers disagree about whether ‘he’ is the self itself or the person who comes to know it, but the description is the same.

An analogy When you describe sunlight to someone, you can say it has no weight, no smell, no edges, and cannot be cut — and they understand it better by what it is not than by any drawing.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the mantra itself say?

A subject, ‘he’ (saḥ), is said to have ‘gone round’ (paryagāt), followed by six neuter words: bright, bodiless, woundless, sinewless, pure, untouched by evil. Then four masculine titles — seer, wise, encompassing, self-existent — and a second verb: he ‘distributed things in accordance with their truth for the everlasting years’. The mantra does not say who ‘he’ is, and its genders do not agree. Everything the commentators conclude about this verse begins with how they resolve those two facts.

Traditional Interpretation

What is the Self, according to Śaṅkara?

Śaṅkara introduces the verse as the description of the Self taught in the previous mantras, ‘in its own form’ (स स्वेन रूपेण किंलक्षण इत्याह अयं मन्त्रः). ‘He went round’ means he pervades like space. The negations exclude the bodies: अव्रणमस्नाविरमित्येताभ्यां स्थूलशरीरप्रतिषेधः, ‘woundless and sinewless exclude the gross body’; शुद्धं निर्मलमविद्यामलरहितमिति कारणशरीरप्रतिषेधः, ‘pure, free of the stain of ignorance, excludes the causal body’; and bodiless excludes the subtle body. The titles make him the omniscient Lord: kavi is the all-seer, supported by ‘नान्योऽतोऽस्ति द्रष्टा’ (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 3.7.23); manīṣī the ruler of the mind. This eternally free Lord ‘distributed the things to be done, according to the results of action, to the Prajāpatis called years’.

Traditional Interpretation

Can the verse describe the knower rather than the known?

Deśika’s first reading takes saḥ as the one who sees Brahman as the inner self of all, mentioned in mantras 6–7: he ‘reaches’ (paryagāt) Brahman, which the neuter words describe as free of everything to be rejected — एवमशेषहेयप्रत्यनीकः परमात्मा मुमुक्षोः प्राप्यः प्रापक उपास्यश्चेत्युक्तं भवति. The masculine titles then describe the knower: a seer, master of his mind through practice and detachment, overcoming desire, anger and greed (कामक्रोधलोभादीन् दुर्जयान् अरातीन् अभिभवतीति वा), who has discerned and holds in his heart the highest goal and its means. Mahīdhara gives the same reading first (य एवमात्मानं पश्यति स ईदृशं ब्रह्म पर्यगात्) and Śaṅkara’s as an alternative (एतस्या ऋचोऽर्थान्तरं यथा).

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

How do the genders decide the reading?

The grammar offers two constructions. If śukram … apāpaviddham are accusatives, they are the object of paryagāt and refer to Brahman (neuter), while saḥ, kaviḥ, manīṣī, paribhūḥ, svayambhūḥ (masculine) refer to someone else, naturally the knower. If they describe saḥ, they are in the ‘wrong’ gender, and Śaṅkara must convert them: शुक्रमित्यादीनि वचांसि पुंलिङ्गत्वेन परिणेयानि. His justification is the frame of the verse — it begins with ‘he’ and ends with masculine titles. Neither construction is impossible; the first keeps the grammar as it stands, the second keeps one subject throughout. The verse’s irregular metre (see Metre) gives no help either way.

Traditional Interpretation

What is ‘arranging things truly for the everlasting years’?

Śaṅkara: the Lord, being omniscient, ‘distributed the things to be done’ as befits the results of action, to the Prajāpatis called years (संवत्सराख्येभ्यः प्रजापतिभ्य इत्यर्थः). Deśika, on his first reading, takes the knower to have discerned and held in his heart all things — the goal, its means and its obstacles — until he reaches Brahman; on his second, the Lord created real things to last until dissolution, ‘not like a conjurer who merely displays them’ (न पुनरैन्द्रजालिकवत्केवलं प्रकाशितवान्). Madhva, through verses he cites from a ‘Vārāha’, reads it as the eternal creation of a real world in an unbroken flow (अनाद्यनन्तकालेषु प्रवाहैकप्रकारतः).

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 8

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

‘He’ is the Self of mantras 4–7, all-pervading like space; the neuter words, read as masculine, deny it every kind of body; the titles make it the omniscient, eternally free Lord (स नित्यमुक्त ईश्वरः), who allotted to the Prajāpatis their tasks according to the results of action.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Vedānta Deśika, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 8

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

First reading: the knower of mantras 6–7 reaches Brahman, which is bright and untouched by all that is to be rejected; the titles describe the disciplined knower. Second reading: the purified individual self is described by the neuter words, and the Supreme Self pervades it and creates real things for all time — not as a conjurer’s display (न पुनरैन्द्रजालिकवत्केवलं प्रकाशितवान्).

Dvaita · Madhva, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 8

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Madhva explains every word of the Lord, through verses he cites from a ‘Vārāha’: śukram because free from grief (शुक्रं तच्छोकराहित्यादव्रणं नित्यपूर्णतः), kavi because omniscient, svayambhū because he depends on nothing; and the world he creates is real (एवम्भूतो महाविष्णुर्यथार्थं जगदीदृशम्). The cited verses are reported as cited by Madhva, not verified.

Where they disagree

Whether ‘he’ is the Self/Lord (Śaṅkara, Madhva, Deśika’s second reading) or the knower who reaches it (Deśika’s first reading, Mahīdhara’s first); whether the neuter adjectives describe ‘he’ (converted to the masculine) or are the object he reaches; and whether ‘truly arranging things’ affirms the reality of the created world (Deśika, Madhva) or speaks of the allotment of duties (Śaṅkara).

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • स पर्यगाच्छुक्रम् sa paryagācchukram → सः + पर्यगात् + शुक्रम्

    visarga sandhi (saḥ) and consonant sandhi

    The pronoun saḥ loses its visarga before a consonant (the special rule for saḥ and eṣaḥ). pari + agāt → paryagāt by P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि). paryagāt + śukram: t becomes c before ś (P. 8.4.40 (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः)), and ś after it may become ch (P. 8.4.63 (शश्छोऽटि)): paryagāc chukram.

  • अस्नाविरं शुद्धम् asnāviraṃ śuddham → अस्नाविरम् + शुद्धम्

    consonant sandhi (m → anusvāra)

    P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः). The Saṃhitā witness writes अस्नाविरँ.

  • कविर्मनीषी kavirmanīṣī → कविः + मनीषी

    visarga sandhi (-iḥ before a voiced sound)

    The final s of kavis becomes ru (P. 8.2.66 (ससजुषो रुँः)); it becomes visarga only before a voiceless sound or a pause (P. 8.3.15 (खरवसानयोर्विसर्जनीयः)), so before m it stays r: kavir manīṣī.

  • स्वयम्भूर्याथातथ्यतो svayambhūryāthātathyato → स्वयम्भूः + याथातथ्यतः

    visarga sandhi (-ūḥ before a voiced sound)

    svayambhūḥ before the voiced y keeps r, as kavir manīṣī.

  • याथातथ्यतोऽर्थान्व्यदधाच्छाश्वतीभ्यः yāthātathyato'rthānvyadadhācchāśvatībhyaḥ → याथातथ्यतः + अर्थान् + व्यदधात् + शाश्वतीभ्यः

    visarga sandhi with elision of a; consonant sandhi (t + ś → cch)

    yāthātathyataḥ + arthān: -aḥ before a gives -o, and the initial a is elided (marked by avagraha): P. 6.1.113 (अतो रोरप्लुतादप्लुते), P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः), P. 6.1.109 (एङः पदान्तादति). arthān stays before v. vyadadhāt + śāśvatībhyaḥ: as paryagāc chukram, P. 8.4.40 (स्तोः श्चुना श्चुः) and P. 8.4.63 (शश्छोऽटि).

Morphology

शुक्रम्
śukram and the five adjectives after it are neuter, while saḥ, kaviḥ, manīṣī, paribhūḥ and svayambhūḥ are masculine. Read as accusatives they are the object of paryagāt: ‘he reached the bright, the bodiless …’ (Deśika’s first reading; Mahīdhara’s first: य एवमात्मानं पश्यति स ईदृशं ब्रह्म पर्यगात्). Read as descriptions of saḥ they must be changed to the masculine, which Śaṅkara does explicitly: शुक्रमित्यादीनि वचांसि पुंलिङ्गत्वेन परिणेयानि, स पर्यगात् इत्युपक्रम्य कविर्मनीषी इत्यादिना पुंलिङ्गत्वेनोपसंहारात् — ‘the words śukram etc. are to be converted into the masculine, since the verse begins with “he went round” and ends with the masculine “seer, wise”’. Mahīdhara’s second reading does the same (लिङ्गव्यत्ययेन पुंलिङ्गे नेतव्यानि).
पर्यगात्
pari + the root aorist agāt (the aorist of ‘to go’ uses the stem gā-). ‘Went round’ can mean ‘pervaded’ (Śaṅkara) or ‘reached’ (Deśika: पर्यगच्छत्; प्राप्नुयात् इत्यर्थः). The pada-pāṭha of VS 40.8 separates the preverb: सः। परि॑। अ॒गा॒त्।.
व्यदधात्
Imperfect of dhā (3rd class, reduplicated: a-da-dhā-t) with vi: ‘set apart, distributed, arranged’. The pada-pāṭha separates वि। अ॒द॒धा॒त्।.
याथातथ्यतः
From the phrase yathā-tathā (‘as … so’) with vṛddhi of the first vowel, yāthātathya ‘the being so as it is’, and the suffix -tas, ‘according to’. The pada-pāṭha marks the parts: या॒था॒त॒थ्य॒त इति॑ याथाऽत॒थ्य॒तः.
अपापविद्धम्
The pada-pāṭha divides the compound अपा॑पऽविद्धम्, separating the last member viddham. The commentators all read it as ‘not touched by evil’ (a- negating pāpaviddha).
समाभ्यः
samā ‘year’ is feminine; samābhyaḥ is dative or ablative plural. Śaṅkara takes it as a dative (‘to the eternal years’, which he explains as the Prajāpatis presiding over them); Deśika as duration (‘for all time until dissolution’).

Anvaya

सः पर्यगात् शुक्रम् अकायम् अव्रणम् अस्नाविरम् शुद्धम् अपापविद्धम् । [सः] कविः मनीषी परिभूः स्वयम्भूः शाश्वतीभ्यः समाभ्यः याथातथ्यतः अर्थान् व्यदधात् ।

saḥ paryagāt śukram akāyam avraṇam asnāviram śuddham apāpaviddham | saḥ kaviḥ manīṣī paribhūḥ svayambhūḥ śāśvatībhyaḥ samābhyaḥ yāthātathyataḥ arthān vyadadhāt |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two sentences with one subject, saḥ. The first has the verb paryagāt and six neuter adjectives; the second has four masculine titles in apposition to saḥ and the verb vyadadhāt with its object arthān, its manner yāthātathyataḥ and its indirect object (or time) śāśvatībhyaḥ samābhyaḥ. The clash of genders is the knot of the verse: either the neuters are objects of paryagāt (‘he reached the bright …’), and saḥ may be the knower; or they describe saḥ and must be read as masculine, and saḥ is the Self or the Lord.

Ambiguities

Who is ‘he’?
The Self described in the preceding mantras (Śaṅkara: सः यथोक्त आत्मा), or the knower who sees Brahman in all (Deśika’s first reading: सर्वभूतान्तरात्मभूतब्रह्मदर्शी; Mahīdhara’s first reading). Deśika also gives a second reading in which the neuter words describe the purified individual self and saḥ is the Supreme Self that pervades it (तदा शुक्रम् इत्यादिकं सर्वोपाधिविनिर्मुक्तपरिशुद्धजीवपरम्). Madhva takes the whole as the Lord, Viṣṇu.
पर्यगात्: pervaded or reached
‘Went round’ as pervading everything like space (Śaṅkara) or as reaching, attaining (Deśika, Mahīdhara). The choice follows from who ‘he’ is.
The three bodies
Śaṅkara finds in the negations a denial of the three bodies of later Vedānta: akāyam the subtle body, avraṇam asnāviram the gross body, śuddham the causal body. The mantra itself lists bodilessness, woundlessness and sinewlessness, purity and freedom from evil; the threefold scheme is his systematisation.
याथातथ्यतोऽर्थान् व्यदधात्: how real is what is arranged?
Deśika insists that ‘truly’ means the Lord created real things, not like a conjurer who only displays them (न पुनरैन्द्रजालिकवत्केवलं प्रकाशितवान्); Madhva’s cited verses say the world so made is real (स सत्यं जगदेतादृङ्). Śaṅkara reads ‘he distributed the things to be done, according to the results of action’, which does not raise the question of the world’s reality.
Witness slip in Deśika’s mūla
Deśika’s mūla as keyed reads अस्राविरँ; every other witness, and Deśika’s own bhāṣya (अव्रणम् अस्नाविरञ्च), read asnāviram (corrections.json).

Metre

irregular · syllables counted as written: 23 + 27

Counted as written, the half-lines have 23 and 27 syllables, 50 in all. Divided at word boundaries the verse falls into units of 12 (sa paryagāc chukram akāyam avraṇam), 11 (asnāviraṃ śuddham apāpaviddham), 11 (kavir manīṣī paribhūḥ svayambhūḥ) and 16 (yāthātathyato ’rthān vyadadhāc chāśvatībhyaḥ samābhyaḥ). The first three are of the length of triṣṭubh and jagatī pādas; the last is too long for any regular pāda. Mahīdhara describes the adhyāya as anuṣṭubh as a whole, which this verse plainly is not. The edition records it as irregular and does not force a scansion.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Witness error Vedānta Deśika’s mūla: अस्राविरँ — Deśika’s mūla as keyed reads अस्राविरँ (asrāviram); every other witness reads अस्नाविरम् (‘without sinews’). Probably a slip of the digital text (स्न / स्र); Deśika’s bhāṣya would settle it.
11 Connections with other scriptures

  • Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 3.7.23 Doctrinal comparison

    nānyo'to'sti draṣṭā

    ‘There is no other seer than he.’ Śaṅkara cites this sentence of the Bṛhadāraṇyaka’s inner-controller passage to explain kavi as the all-seer.

    The Kaṭha’s ‘bodiless among bodies, stable among the unstable’ expresses the bodilessness of akāyam in another form.

    kaviṃ purāṇamanuśāsitāram

    The Gītā’s ‘the ancient seer, the ruler’ applies the same title kavi to the supreme. A thematic association: the word, not the verse, is shared.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Ego and self-image

Much of what we call ‘myself’ is a list of injuries and achievements: what was done to me, what I did, what others think. The mantra describes the self by subtracting exactly these — no body to be measured, no wound to be carried, no stain that sticks. A person recovering from a public failure or a long illness can find this list startling: it names as unreal the things that feel most defining. It does not deny that wounds hurt; it denies that they reach what one is. Whether ‘he’ is the self or the one who comes to know it, the verse invites a reader to ask what remains of identity when the record is set aside. This is a reflection on the teaching, not part of it.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Make a list of three things that describe you. Which of them could be taken away while you would still be you?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry If the self is ‘unwounded’ and ‘untouched by evil’, who is it that is hurt and who is it that does wrong — and what follows for responsibility?

A contemplation exercise Sit for a few minutes and, each time a thought about yourself comes (‘I am tired’, ‘I failed at…’), silently add ‘— and that which knows this is not tired / did not fail’. Notice what that does to the thought.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.
