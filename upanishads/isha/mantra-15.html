Īśāvāsya Upaniṣad · Mantra 15 of 18

The face of the real is covered by a golden vessel

The first of the four closing prayers: the seeker asks Pūṣan to uncover the face of the real so that it may be seen.

5 Faithful English translation

By a golden vessel the face of the real [is] covered. That, you, O Pūṣan, uncover, for [one] whose dharma is truth (or: for the dharma of the true), for seeing.

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

The face of the real is hidden by a golden vessel. Uncover it, Pūṣan, so that I, who hold to the truth, may see.

Key concepts सत्य truth, the real पूषन् Pūṣan सूर्य the Sun धर्म dharma

7 Explain it to a beginner

Here the Upanishad changes its voice. Until now it has taught; now it prays. The speaker imagines the truth as a face, and that face is hidden — not by something ugly, but by something golden and dazzling. That is the striking point: what hides the truth is beautiful. The prayer is addressed to Pūṣan, an old name for the sun as the one who nourishes and guides. ‘Open it,’ the speaker asks, ‘so that I can see.’ Teachers have read the golden cover in different ways: as the blazing disk of the sun itself, as the attractive surface of the world, or as our own desires, which glitter and keep our attention on the surface. What they share is the idea that seeing truly is not something we simply do; something has to be opened.

An analogy Think of sunlight on a window so bright that you cannot see into the room behind it: the glare is beautiful, and it is exactly what hides what is inside.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the mantra say, and to whom?

It states that ‘the face of the real’ (satyasya mukham) is ‘covered’ (apihitam) by ‘a golden vessel’ (hiraṇmayena pātreṇa), and asks Pūṣan to uncover it ‘for one whose dharma is truth’ or ‘for the true one’s dharma’, ‘for seeing’. It is the first of four mantras in the voice of a petitioner; the teaching voice of mantras 1–14 does not return. The same four mantras, in the same order, close Bṛhadāraṇyaka 5.15.1, where they belong to the rites of the dying. Nothing in the words says who the speaker is, what ‘the real’ is, or what the golden vessel stands for.

Traditional Interpretation

Why does a prayer for a path come after the teaching on knowledge?

Śaṅkara’s introduction to this mantra joins it to mantras 11 and 14. The whole Veda has two aims, activity and withdrawal (द्विप्रकारः प्रवृत्तिनिवृत्तिलक्षणो वेदार्थोऽत्र प्रकाशितः); the highest result of the path of works with meditation is still within saṃsāra, ending at dissolution into prakṛti, while knowledge of the Self is the result of renunciation. The person who has lived by works together with meditation on the lower Brahman, and meditated on the ‘true’ as the person in the sun and in the right eye (he quotes Bṛhadāraṇyaka 5.5.2), asks at the time of death for the door of entry: सोऽन्तकाले प्राप्ते सत्यात्मानमात्मनः प्राप्तिद्वारं याचते. The prayer is therefore not, for Śaṅkara, the prayer of the knower of mantra 7, who needs no path, but of the meditator.

Traditional Interpretation

What covers the face of the real?

For Śaṅkara the vessel is ‘golden as if golden, that is, luminous’ (हिरण्मयमिव हिरण्मयम् , ज्योतिर्मयमित्येतत्), the radiance of the sun’s disk which hides the door to Brahman within it. Mahīdhara, on the Mādhyandina form, gives the same picture and adds an etymology: the disk is a pātra because there the rays ‘drink’ the waters. Deśika moves the image inward: the ‘face’ of the true self is the mind, and the golden lid is passion (rajas), golden because it attracts (रागात्मकतया हिरण्मयसदृशेन रजोमयेन पात्रेण), which blocks the mind’s turning towards the Lord who is near in the heart. Madhva returns to the sun: the golden vessel is the solar disk, by which the face of Viṣṇu, the Real, is always covered, and which he himself, as Pūṣan, removes for his devotee.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Who is Pūṣan, and who is the satyadharman?

Pūṣan is a Vedic deity of nourishment, roads and safe passage; in this mantra and the next he is addressed together with the sun, and Śaṅkara, commenting on the next mantra, identifies him with it: the sun, named Pūṣan because it nourishes the world. The petitioner describes himself, or his purpose, with satyadharmāya. Śaṅkara’s first reading makes it a bahuvrīhi, ‘for me, whose dharma is truth’, because the speaker has worshipped the true; his second, ‘for the one who performs dharma as it is’. Deśika reads it with dṛṣṭaye, ‘for the sight that is the dharma (attribute) of the true one’, the self. The a-stem form satyadharmāya sits more easily with Deśika’s tatpuruṣa than with a classical bahuvrīhi (see the morphology note); Vedic usage permits both.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 15 (with its introduction)

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The speaker is the meditator who combined works with meditation on the true (the person in the sun); at death he asks Pūṣan to remove the luminous covering of the door to Brahman in the solar disk, दृष्टये तव सत्यात्मन उपलब्धये. This is the path of the meditator, not of the knower: for the knower of the Self ‘there is no going’ — Śaṅkara argues in his conclusion (after mantra 18) that if vidyā in mantra 11 meant Self-knowledge, the request for a door and a path in this mantra would make no sense.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Vedānta Deśika, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 15

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Mantras 15–18 are to be meditated on by one established in the knowledge of Brahman with its auxiliaries; Pūṣan and the other names denote the Supreme Self, directly or through the deity (पूषादिशब्दाः सर्वशब्दवाच्यं परमात्मानमनुसन्दधताम्). ‘The true’ is here the individual self (सत्यशब्दोऽत्र जीवपरः), its ‘face’ the mind, and the golden lid passion; the prayer asks the Lord, ‘whose nature is to nourish those who depend on him’, to remove the obstacle to meditation.

Dvaita · Madhva, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 15

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The golden vessel is the sun’s disk, by which the face of Viṣṇu, the Real, is always covered (पात्रं हिरण्मयं सूर्यमण्डलं समुदाहृतम् । विष्णोः सत्यस्य तेनैव सर्वदाऽपिहितं मुखम्); Viṣṇu himself, called Pūṣan because he is full (pūrṇa), reveals it to his devotee. Madhva gives this in verses he does not attribute in the surviving digital text, which has a gap at the end of the passage.

Where they disagree

The schools divide on almost every noun: the real is Brahman in the sun (Śaṅkara), the individual self (Deśika) or Viṣṇu (Madhva); the golden vessel is the sun’s radiance (Śaṅkara, Mahīdhara, Madhva) or passion (Deśika); the speaker is a dying meditator (Śaṅkara) or a living devotee in meditation (Deśika, Madhva). They agree that the uncovering is a gift asked for, not an achievement.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • सत्यस्यापिहितं satyasyāpihitaṃ → सत्यस्य + अपिहितम्

    vowel sandhi (savarṇa-dīrgha)

    a + a → ā: P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः). The final m of apihitam becomes anusvāra before the consonant of mukham: P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः).

  • तत्त्वं पूषन् tattvaṃ pūṣan → तत् + त्वम् + पूषन्

    consonant sandhi

    t before t stays (tat tvam, written तत्त्वं); the m of tvam becomes anusvāra before p (P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः)). Written together, तत्त्वं can look like the abstract noun tattva, ‘thatness’; it is not.

  • पूषन्नपावृणु pūṣannapāvṛṇu → पूषन् + अपावृणु

    consonant sandhi (doubling of n)

    A word-final n after a short vowel is doubled before a vowel: P. 8.3.32 (ङमो ह्रस्वादचि ङमुण्नित्यम्) — pūṣan + apāvṛṇu → pūṣann apāvṛṇu.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • सत्यधर्माय satyadharmāya

    bahuvrīhi (Śaṅkara); or ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa (Deśika) · सत्य + धर्म

    Vigraha सत्यं धर्मः यस्य सः — or — सत्यस्य धर्मः

    one whose dharma is truth (the seeker); or: the dharma (attribute) of the true one

Morphology

हिरण्मयेन
hiraṇmaya, ‘golden’, is one of the forms Pāṇini gives ready-made rather than by rule, in the list of P. 6.4.174 (दाण्डिनायनहास्तिनायनाथर्वणिकजैह्माशिनेयवाशिनायनिभ्रौणहत्यधैवत्यसारवैक्ष्वाकमैत्रेयहिरण्मयानि): from hiraṇya + -maya with the syllable ya lost.
अपिहितम्
api + √dhā ‘to put’: the participle of dhā is hita (P. 7.4.42 (दधातेर्हिः)); api-hita, ‘put over, covered’.
अपावृणु
Imperative of vṛ ‘to cover’ (5th class, stem vṛṇu-) with apa ‘away’ and ā: ‘uncover’. The ending -hi is dropped after the u of the stem: P. 6.4.106 (उतश्च प्रत्ययादसंयोगपूर्वात्).
सत्यधर्माय
An a-stem dative. If the compound is a bahuvrīhi, as Śaṅkara takes it (he calls the person satyadharmā), classical grammar would turn dharma into dharman at the end of it — P. 5.4.124 (धर्मादनिच् केवलात्) — and give the dative satyadharmaṇe. The a-stem form fits a tatpuruṣa more naturally, as Deśika reads it; as a bahuvrīhi it is a Vedic form. Both readings are possible; the grammar alone does not decide.
दृष्टये
Dative of dṛṣṭi, an action noun in -ti from dṛś (P. 3.3.94 (स्त्रियां क्तिन्)): the dative expresses purpose, ‘for seeing’.

Anvaya

सत्यस्य मुखम् हिरण्मयेन पात्रेण अपिहितम् । पूषन् त्वम् सत्यधर्माय दृष्टये तत् अपावृणु ।

satyasya mukham hiraṇmayena pātreṇa apihitam | pūṣan tvam satyadharmāya dṛṣṭaye tat apāvṛṇu |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two sentences. The first is a statement with a participle as its predicate: ‘the face of the real [is] covered by a golden vessel’. The second is a request in the imperative addressed to Pūṣan: ‘that, you, Pūṣan, uncover’, with two datives saying for whom and for what — satyadharmāya (for the one whose dharma is truth, or for the true one’s dharma) and dṛṣṭaye (for seeing). tat refers back to the face, or to what covers it; Śaṅkara takes it as the covering to be moved aside.

Ambiguities

सत्यस्य: whose face?
Śaṅkara: Brahman dwelling in the sun’s disk (सत्यस्य आदित्यमण्डलस्थस्य ब्रह्मणः). Deśika: the individual self, whose ‘face’ — the mind — is covered by the golden lid of passion (rajas). Madhva: Viṣṇu, the Real, whose face is covered by the sun’s disk (पात्रं हिरण्मयं सूर्यमण्डलं समुदाहृतम्). The mantra names no one.
पात्र: vessel, lid or disk
pātra is a drinking vessel or bowl. Śaṅkara reads it as a vessel used as a lid; Mahīdhara (on VS 40.17) derives it from ‘drinking’ and identifies it with the sun’s disk, where the rays drink the waters (पिबन्ति यत्र स्थिता रश्मयो रसानिति पात्रं मण्डलम्); Deśika as the ‘golden’, that is, attractive, covering of passion.
सत्यधर्माय
‘For me, whose dharma is truth’ (Śaṅkara’s first gloss); ‘for one who practises dharma as it truly is’ (his second, यथाभूतस्य धर्मस्यानुष्ठात्रे); ‘for the sight that is the attribute of the true self’ (Deśika); ‘for the devotee’ (Madhva’s verse: सत्यधर्माय भक्ताय). See the morphology note on the form.
The Mādhyandina form of this mantra
VS 40.17 keeps only the first line and continues ‘yo ’sāv āditye puruṣaḥ so ’sāv aham | om khaṃ brahma’ — ‘the person who is in the sun, that I am. Om, Brahman is space’. The request to Pūṣan is absent there (corrections.json, class recension).

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16

Two half-lines of 16 syllables. Mahīdhara classes the Mādhyandina form of the mantra (VS 40.17, which adds prose) as an uṣṇik ending in two yajus (उष्णिक् यजुर्द्वयान्ता).

11 Connections with other scriptures

  • Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 5.15.1 Direct textual parallel

    हिरण्मयेन पात्रेण सत्यस्यापिहितं मुखम्

    hiraṇmayena pātreṇa satyasyāpihitaṃ mukham

    The same mantra, word for word, opens the closing prayer of the Bṛhadāraṇyaka (5.15.1, Kāṇva), which goes on with mantras 16, 17 and 18. There it stands among the rites at the end of life.

  • Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 5.5.2 Doctrinal comparison

    य एष एतस्मिन्मण्डले पुरुषो

    ya eṣa etasminmaṇḍale puruṣo

    The person in the sun’s disk, and in the right eye, is meditated on as ‘the true’ (satya). Śaṅkara quotes this passage in his introduction to mantra 15 to identify the ‘true’ whose face is covered.

  • Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 2.2.10 Modern thematic association

    हिरण्मये परे कोशे विरजं ब्रह्म निष्कलम्

    hiraṇmaye pare kośe virajaṃ brahma niṣkalam

    ‘In the highest golden sheath is Brahman, stainless, without parts.’ The same golden imagery, but in the Muṇḍaka the gold is the dwelling of Brahman rather than what hides it.

  • Bhagavad Gītā 8.9 Modern thematic association

    रूपमादित्यवर्णं तमसः परस्तात्

    rūpamādityavarṇaṃ tamasaḥ parastāt

    The Gītā’s ‘sun-coloured, beyond darkness’, in a verse on remembering the supreme at the time of death — the context of Īśā 15–18. Deśika uses the phrase ‘sun-coloured’ of the form the seeker asks to see in mantra 16.

Every quoted phrase is checked against the text of the passage cited (sources in section 14).

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Technology and digital distractions

The golden lid is an image many readers recognise from their own screens: what keeps attention from what matters is rarely ugly; it is bright, rewarding and well designed. Deśika’s reading — the covering is the mind’s attraction itself — fits this most closely, but even Śaṅkara’s solar reading makes the same point: the radiance is the obstacle. The mantra does not ask the seeker to smash the vessel; it asks for it to be opened. This is a reflection on the teaching, not part of the commentarial tradition.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What is something bright and pleasant in your life that sometimes stops you from noticing something more important?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry If what hides the real is itself golden, can the real be found by rejecting the beautiful — or only by seeing through it?

A contemplation exercise Before opening an app or a screen today, pause once and ask: ‘What am I hoping to see?’ Write down three of your answers in the evening, without judging them.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.

Further notes

  • Śaṅkara’s concluding discussion (Advaita Sharada unit IS_C01_I02) uses this mantra’s request for a door as evidence that mantras 9–11 do not speak of Self-knowledge.
  • Mahīdhara reads the Mādhyandina VS 40.17, which keeps only this mantra’s first line, as a meditation on the person in the sun: सूर्यमण्डलस्थः पुरुषोऽहमेवेत्यभेदेन चिन्तयेत्.
