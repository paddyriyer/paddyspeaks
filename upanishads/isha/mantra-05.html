Īśāvāsya Upaniṣad · Mantra 5 of 18

It moves and it does not move; it is far and it is near

Mantra 4’s paradox restated in four pairs of opposites: moving and unmoving, far and near, within and without.

5 Faithful English translation

That moves; that does not move. That [is] far, and that [is] near. That [is] within all this, and that [is] outside all this.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

It moves, and it does not move. It is far away, and it is near. It is inside all of this, and it is outside all of this.

Key concepts आत्मन् self, Self ब्रह्मन् Brahman ईश् the Lord, ‘ruler’

7 Explain it to a beginner

This short verse repeats the puzzle of the last one, on purpose, so that it sinks in. It moves and it does not move. It is far and it is near. It is inside everything and outside everything. The teachers explain that the reality the verse describes is like space in a room full of jars: the space is inside every jar and outside every jar; it seems to travel when you carry a jar across the room, yet the space itself goes nowhere. Why far and near? Because it depends on who is looking. To someone who never looks inward it seems impossibly distant; to someone who knows it, nothing is nearer, because it is their own being. The verse uses opposites because no single ordinary word can hold something that is everywhere.

An analogy Space fills every jar in a room and surrounds every jar; carry a jar across the room and the space inside seems to move, though space itself goes nowhere.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the mantra itself say?

Six clauses predicate three pairs of opposites of one subject, ‘that’: it moves and does not move; it is far and near; it is within all this and outside all this. The mantra does not say how the opposites are compatible, or for whom it is far and near. It continues the unnamed subject of mantra 4 and uses the same root, ej, that mantra 4 used in anejat.

Traditional Interpretation

Why does the Upaniṣad repeat itself?

Śaṅkara opens with a principle about Vedic mantras: न मन्त्राणां जामितास्तीति पूर्वमन्त्रोक्तमप्यर्थं पुनराह — there is no fault of repetition in mantras, so the meaning already stated is stated again. Deśika says the same in his own words: what was said before is taught again ‘out of regard, in another form’ (आदरान्मुखान्तरेणानुशास्ति). Mahīdhara gives the teacher’s reason: रहस्यं सकृदुक्तं चित्ते नायातीति — a secret teaching said once does not enter the mind.

Traditional Interpretation

How can it be both far and near?

Śaṅkara: far for the ignorant, who would not reach it in hundreds of millions of years; near — indeed nearest — for the wise, because it is their Self. Deśika makes the same distinction between the deluded and the awakened (मूढप्रतिबुद्धपुरुषभेदापेक्षया) and cites verses he attributes to Śaunaka: to those turned away from Govinda the highest Brahman stands ‘farther than the far’; to those whose minds are set on him it is near. Mahīdhara, in his second reading, makes the pairs refer to the world itself, all of which is Brahman: far as the sun and stars, near as the earth.

Traditional Interpretation

What does ‘within and without all this’ add?

Śaṅkara: within, as the Self is ‘the inner self of all’ (‘य आत्मा सर्वान्तरः’ इति श्रुतेः), being infinitely subtle; without, because it pervades like space; and without any gap, being a mass of consciousness only (he cites ‘प्रज्ञानघन एव’). Deśika notes that ordinary things are either inside something or outside it; this is both, and is present even where finite things are absent (परिमितानां पदार्थानां भावदेश इवाभावदेशेऽपि वर्तत इति यावत्). He cites the Taittirīya Nārāyaṇa verse अन्तर्बहिश्च तत्सर्वं व्याप्य नारायणः स्थितः, ‘pervading all that, within and without, Nārāyaṇa stands’.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What do the witnesses and the metre show?

Two traditions transmit small differences in the second clause: Madhva’s tan nejati and Deśika’s tad u naijati, each confirmed by its commentator’s gloss. Madhva’s text also lacks asya in the last pāda. The counted syllables (15 and 17) depart from the anuṣṭubh in two places, and in both the departure can be described: a short pāda that the particle u restores if it is read as a syllable, and a long pāda that Madhva’s reading happens to shorten. Metre is evidence about readings, not proof; the edition reports the observation and keeps the reading of the base witness and the majority of witnesses.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 5

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The Self does not move of itself and seems to move; it is far for the ignorant and nearest for the wise, being their Self; it is within all as its inner self and outside all as its pervader, without a gap anywhere (‘प्रज्ञानघन एव’(बृ. उ. ४ । ५ । १३) इति शासनान्निरन्तरं च).

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Vedānta Deśika, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 5

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Reading ‘tad u naijati’: the Lord seems to move and in fact does not; his distance and nearness depend on the hearer’s turning away from or toward him; he is within and without all, as the Nārāyaṇa verse he cites says (अन्तर्बहिश्च तत्सर्वं व्याप्य नारायणः स्थितः).

Dvaita · Madhva, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 5

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

‘It moves’ means that all else moves from it; it itself does not move. Taking ej as ‘to tremble’, he cites a verse from a text he calls the Tattvasaṃhitā: all things fear him, Hari fears nothing; being everywhere, he is far and near, outside and within (ततो बिभेति सर्वोऽपि न बिभेति हरिः स्वयम्).

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara and Deśika resolve the moving/unmoving pair in a similar way (it seems to move and does not), but for Śaṅkara the subject is the Self one is, for Deśika the Lord who pervades selves and things. Madhva shifts the sense of ‘moves’ to causation and fear. All three take far and near as relative to the knower.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • तदेजति tadejati → तत् + एजति

    consonant sandhi (voicing before a vowel)

    t → d before a vowel, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

  • तन्नैजति tannaijati → तत् + न + एजति

    consonant sandhi (t before a nasal); vowel sandhi (vṛddhi)

    tat + na → tan na: before a nasal a stop may become the nasal of its class, P. 8.4.45 (यरोऽनुनासिकेऽनुनासिको वा). na + ejati → naijati: a + e → ai, P. 6.1.88 (वृद्धिरेचि).

  • तद्दूरे taddūre → तत् + दूरे

    consonant voicing

    t → d before the voiced d, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते).

  • तद्वन्तिके tadvantike → तत् + उ + अन्तिके

    consonant voicing; vowel sandhi (u → v)

    tat + u → tad u, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते); u + antike → v antike, the vowel u becoming v before a dissimilar vowel, P. 6.1.77 (इको यणचि). The particle u thus disappears into the junction; GRETIL spaces it तद् व् अन्तिके.

  • तदन्तरस्य tadantarasya → तत् + अन्तः + अस्य

    consonant voicing; r of antar before a vowel

    tat + antaḥ → tad antaḥ, P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते). antar keeps its r before a vowel; it appears as visarga only at a pause, P. 8.3.15 (खरवसानयोर्विसर्जनीयः): antar + asya → antar asya.

  • सर्वस्यास्य sarvasyāsya → सर्वस्य + अस्य

    vowel sandhi (lengthening)

    a + a → ā, P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

एजति
Present of ej, ‘to stir, tremble’, the same root as anejat in mantra 4 (Śaṅkara cites the root as ‘एजृ कम्पने’ there). Mantra 5 states as two finite clauses what mantra 4 gave as a participle: ejati … na ejati.
अन्तः
The adverb antar, ‘within’, construed with a genitive (asya sarvasya). Written together, tadantarasya could also be read as tad antarasya, ‘of the interior’; GRETIL’s spacing (तद् अन्तर् अस्य), Śaṅkara’s gloss तत् अन्तः अभ्यन्तरे अस्य सर्वस्य and the parallel with bāhyataḥ all favour antar + asya.
बाह्यतः
Adverb in -tas from bāhya, ‘outer’: ‘on the outside, from without’. It pairs with antaḥ, ‘within’, both governing ‘of all this’.
उ
The particle u, ‘and, yet’, appears twice (tad u antike; tad u sarvasyāsya). In the first it vanishes into the sandhi tadvantike; in the second it stands written (tadu). Deśika’s text has it a third time, tad u naijati.

Anvaya

तत् एजति । तत् न एजति । तत् दूरे [वर्तते] । तत् उ अन्तिके [वर्तते] । तत् अस्य सर्वस्य अन्तः [वर्तते] । तत् उ अस्य सर्वस्य बाह्यतः [वर्तते] ।

tat ejati | tat na ejati | tat dūre vartate | tat u antike vartate | tat asya sarvasya antaḥ vartate | tat u asya sarvasya bāhyataḥ vartate |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Six short clauses, all with the same subject tat, arranged as three pairs of opposites: ejati / na ejati, dūre / antike, antar asya sarvasya / sarvasyāsya bāhyataḥ. Only the first pair has a verb; the others are nominal, with ‘is’ understood. The particle u marks the second member of the second and third pairs as the surprising one: ‘and yet near’, ‘and yet outside’.

Ambiguities

तन्नैजति, तन्नेजति, तदु नैजति
The edition reads tan naijati (tat na ejati). Madhva’s text reads tan nejati, and his bhāṣya repeats तत् स्वयं नेजति, ‘it itself does not move’; the sense is the same. Deśika’s text reads tad u naijati, ‘and yet it does not move’, and his bhāṣya quotes it so (तदु नैजति - तदेव वस्तुवृत्त्या न कम्पते). Both are recorded in corrections.json and not adopted.
‘It moves’: in what sense?
Śaṅkara: it does not move of itself, yet seems to move (स्वतः अचलमेव सत् चलतीवेत्यर्थः). Deśika: it seems to move, through its swiftness described in mantra 4 (कम्पत इवेत्यर्थः). Madhva: ‘it moves’ means that everything else moves from it (तत एव एजत्यन्यत्), reading ej also as ‘to tremble’: all fear it, Hari fears nothing. Mahīdhara adds an alternative: it moves as the moving creatures and does not move as the stationary, being all things.
Far and near: for whom?
Śaṅkara, Deśika and Mahīdhara (first reading) agree that the distance belongs to the knower, not to the reality: far for the ignorant, near for the wise. Mahīdhara’s second reading takes it of the world: far as the sun and stars, near as the earth, since all is Brahman ('सर्वं खल्विदं ब्रह्म' इति श्रुतेः).
सर्वस्यास्य or सर्वस्य
Madhva’s text lacks अस्य in the last pāda: tad u sarvasya bāhyataḥ. Every other witness has it. See Metre.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 15 + 17

Mahīdhara: तिस्रोऽनुष्टुभः — mantras 5–7 are three anuṣṭubhs. As counted, the half-lines have 15 and 17 syllables. In the first, taddūre tadvantike has 7: read with the particle restored as a syllable (tad u antike), it has 8. In the second, tadu sarvasyāsya bāhyataḥ has 9; Madhva’s text, without asya, would have 8. The edition records Madhva’s reading as a variant and draws no conclusion from the metre alone.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: तन्नेजति — Madhva’s text reads तन्नेजति (tan nejati) for तन्नैजति, and his bhāṣya repeats it: तत् स्वयं नेजति. The sense is the same (‘it does not move’); na + ejati regularly gives naijati, so nejati is recorded as the reading of Madhva’s text, not adopted.
  • Variant reading Madhva’s mūla: तदु सर्वस्य बाह्यतः — Madhva’s text omits अस्य: ‘tad u sarvasya bāhyataḥ’ (‘it is outside of all’). Every other witness, Kāṇva and Mādhyandina, has सर्वस्यास्य (‘of all this’). Not verified against a printed text of Madhva’s bhāṣya.
  • Variant reading Vedānta Deśika’s mūla: तदेजति तदु नैजति — Vedānta Deśika’s text reads ‘tad u naijati’ (‘and yet it does not move’) for ‘tan naijati’, and his bhāṣya quotes it so: तदु नैजति - तदेव वस्तुवृत्त्या न कम्पते. A reading of the Śrīvaiṣṇava tradition, recorded and not adopted.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Emotional attachments

What is nearest is often what we see least: a person we have lived with for years can become, without anyone noticing, very far away, while someone we have lost can feel closer than when they were present. The mantra’s ‘far and near’ is about the supreme reality, and the commentators say the distance lies in the one who looks. A modern reader may recognise the same structure in ordinary attachment: distance and nearness are not only measured in space. This is a reflection prompted by the mantra, not a claim about its meaning.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin What is so close to you that you almost never notice it?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry If the same reality is ‘far’ and ‘near’, is the distance a fact about it or about the knower — and what would it mean to close a distance that is not in space?

A contemplation exercise Sit quietly and notice, one after another, something far (a sound in the distance), something near (your breath), something outside (the floor beneath you) and something inside (a feeling). Then ask what all four have in common: that they are known.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.

Further notes

  • Mahīdhara (on VS 40.5) closes his second reading with a remark on the result: for one who meditates thus there is no journey by the path of light, since Brahman is attained here (एतदुपासितुरर्चिरादिमार्गेण गमनं नास्ति इहैव ब्रह्मप्राप्तेः).
