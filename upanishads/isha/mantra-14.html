Īśāvāsya Upaniṣad · Mantra 14 of 18

Knowing becoming and destruction together

Whoever knows sambhūti and vināśa together crosses death by the one and attains the deathless by the other.

5 Faithful English translation

Whoever knows becoming (sambhūti) and destruction (vināśa) — that, both together — having crossed death by destruction, attains the deathless by becoming.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

Whoever knows both together — becoming and perishing — crosses death through the one that perishes and reaches the deathless through becoming.

Key concepts सम्भूति sambhūti, ‘coming into being’ असम्भूति asambhūti, ‘non-becoming’ विनाश destruction, perishing समुच्चय combination (of knowledge and action) मृत्यु death अमृत immortality, the deathless हिरण्यगर्भ Hiraṇyagarbha, ‘golden embryo’ प्रकृति primal matter, the unmanifest

7 Explain it to a beginner

This verse resolves the riddle of the last two. There, two one-sided paths each led into darkness. Here, someone who knows both together — what comes into being and what passes away — crosses over death by means of the one and reaches the deathless by means of the other. Notice that the verse does not ask you to choose. Coming into being and passing away are not rivals; they belong to the same life, the way breathing in and breathing out belong to one breath. Teachers disagree, sometimes sharply, about what exactly the two words point to — parts of the universe, stages of a spiritual path, or powers of God — and one great teacher even reads a hidden ‘not’ into the verse. But on its surface the lesson is about wholeness: understanding comes from holding together what we usually separate.

An analogy A gardener who understands both how seeds sprout and how plants die back works with the whole cycle of the year, instead of being surprised by each season.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the mantra itself state?

It describes one who ‘knows both together’ — sambhūti and vināśa — and says what that person achieves: by vināśa, the crossing of death; by sambhūti, the attainment of the deathless. The pair here is sambhūti and vināśa, not sambhūti and asambhūti as in mantra 12. The form copies mantra 11 (vidyā and avidyā) exactly. The text does not say whether the deathless here is final liberation or a lesser immortality.

Traditional Interpretation

Why does Śaṅkara read a hidden ‘a-’?

In Śaṅkara’s reading mantra 12 opposed asambhūti (prakṛti) and sambhūti (Hiraṇyagarbha), and mantra 13 gave dissolution into prakṛti as the fruit of asambhava. Mantra 14 names vināśa, which he takes as Hiraṇyagarbha — what has destruction as its property. If vināśa is Hiraṇyagarbha, the other member of the pair must be the unmanifest; hence his note: ‘सम्भूतिं च विनाशं च’ इत्यत्रावर्णलोपेन निर्देशो द्रष्टव्यः, प्रकृतिलयफलश्रुत्यनुरोधात्. By the worship of Hiraṇyagarbha one gains powers and crosses ‘death’ — lack of power and the faults of desire — and by the worship of the unmanifest one attains an ‘immortality’ that is dissolution into prakṛti (असम्भूत्या अव्याकृतोपासनया अमृतं प्रकृतिलयलक्षणम् अश्नुते). The combination of the two worships is fitting because both serve one human aim (समुच्चयः सम्भूत्यसम्भूत्युपासनयोर्युक्तः).

Traditional Interpretation

How do Deśika and Mahīdhara read the pair without Śaṅkara’s restoration?

Deśika: by vināśa, contemplated, one removes the obstacle; by sambhūti, contemplated, one attains Brahman (अनुसन्धीयमानेन विनाशेन प्रतिबन्धमपोह्य, सम्भूत्याऽनुसन्धीयमानया ब्रह्म प्राप्नोति). He offers a second sense of vināśa: the destruction of pride and hypocrisy, of violence and theft, and of the outward-turned senses. He holds that reading the words as creation and dissolution gives not ‘even a trace of fitness’ for the division of results (सृष्टिप्रलयादिविवक्षया मृत्युतरणामृतप्राप्तिरूपफलविभागवचनस्यौचित्यलवोऽपि नोपलभ्यते). Mahīdhara’s first explanation takes sambhūti as the supreme Brahman, the cause of the world’s origin (सर्वजगत्संभवैकहेतुं परं ब्रह्म), and vināśa as the perishable body: knowing embodied self and body together, one does desireless works with the body, purifies the mind, and by self-knowledge attains liberation.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What does the received text allow?

In the second half-line the restoration costs nothing: tīrtvā + asambhūtyā and tīrtvā + sambhūtyā are written with the same letters, since ā + a gives ā. In the first half-line sambhūtiṃ opens the verse and no sandhi could have swallowed an a-; there the restoration is an interpretive device, not a choice between readings of the same letters. The Mādhyandina pada-pāṭha (VS 40.11) analyses the words as sam-bhūtim, vi-nāśam, sam-bhūtyā, without a negative. Mahīdhara’s alternative — the a- in vināśa — shows that the tradition itself was not agreed on where the elision lies.

Comparative Vedānta

How is this mantra related to mantra 11?

Mantras 11 and 14 have one template: ‘whoever knows X and Y together, crossing death by Y, attains the deathless by X’. In 11 the pair is vidyā and avidyā; in 14 sambhūti and vināśa. Śaṅkara reads both as combinations within the realm of meditation and ritual, short of liberation; Deśika reads both as stages of the one path to Brahman. The parallelism is the strongest evidence that the two triads were meant to be read together, whatever their terms denote.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 14

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

sambhūti is to be read asambhūti, the unmanifest cause, and vināśa is Hiraṇyagarbha. Worship of Hiraṇyagarbha yields powers (हिरण्यगर्भोपासनेन ह्यणिमादिप्राप्तिः फलम्) and crosses ‘death’; worship of the unmanifest yields dissolution into prakṛti. The ‘immortality’ is relative; liberation is by knowledge of the Self alone, as mantras 6–8 teach.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Vedānta Deśika, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 14

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The two contemplations are limbs of the knowledge of Brahman: destruction of the obstacles (or of pride, violence and outward-turned senses) and the experience of Brahman. By the first one crosses death, by the second one attains Brahman (सम्भूत्याऽनुसन्धीयमानया ब्रह्म प्राप्नोति). No a- is restored and the cosmic reading is rejected.

Dvaita · Madhva, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantras 12–14 (incomplete in the digital text)

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

In the verse Madhva cites, one who knows the Lord as both the creator and the destroyer is freed from the bondage of the body by the knowledge of his destroying (यो वेद संहृतिज्ञानाद्देहबन्धाद्विमुच्यते), and by knowing him as the agent of bliss, knowledge and the rest reaches their manifestation. Knowing the Lord’s supremacy, distinct even from the released, one is liberated (ततो विष्णोः परोत्कर्षं सम्यग्ज्ञात्वा विमुच्यते).

Where they disagree

The schools divide on three points: whether the text is to be read as it stands (Deśika, Madhva, Mahīdhara’s first explanation) or with a restored a- (Śaṅkara in sambhūti, Mahīdhara’s second explanation in vināśa); whether the terms denote cosmic principles (Śaṅkara), stages of the path (Deśika) or the Lord’s powers (Madhva); and whether the ‘immortality’ attained is relative (Śaṅkara) or liberation (Deśika, Madhva, Mahīdhara’s first explanation).

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • सम्भूतिं च विनाशं च sambhūtiṃ ca vināśaṃ ca → सम्भूतिम् + च + विनाशम् + च

    consonant sandhi (m → anusvāra)

    P. 8.3.23 (मोऽनुस्वारः), written ं before c (or as the class nasal ñ).

  • यस्तद्वेदोभयं yastadvedobhayaṃ → यः + तत् + वेद + उभयम्

    visarga, consonant and vowel sandhi

    yaḥ + tat: visarga before t becomes s (P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः)); tat + veda: t voiced to d; veda + ubhayam: a + u → o (P. 6.1.87 (आद्गुणः)).

  • सम्भूत्यामृतमश्नुते sambhūtyāmṛtamaśnute → सम्भूत्या + अमृतम् + अश्नुते

    vowel sandhi (savarṇa-dīrgha); no change

    ā + a → ā (P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः)); a final m before a vowel is kept. Madhva’s text marks the elided a with an avagraha (सम्भूत्याऽमृतम्).

  • तीर्त्वा सम्भूत्या tīrtvā sambhūtyā → तीर्त्वा + सम्भूत्या

    a hidden alternative

    Written as it is, the junction is tīrtvā + sambhūtyā. But tīrtvā + asambhūtyā would give the same letters, ā + a → ā (P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः)), distinguished only by an avagraha that the witnesses do not write. Śaṅkara’s reading asambhūtyā is therefore possible in the second line without changing a letter; in the first line, where sambhūtiṃ begins the verse, it is not.

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

सम्भूतिम्
sam + √bhū with the action-noun suffix -ti (P. 3.3.94 (स्त्रियां क्तिन्)). The Mādhyandina pada-pāṭha divides सम्भू॑ति॒मिति॒ सम्ऽभू॑तिम्, not asambhūtim; Śaṅkara’s a- is an interpretive restoration, which he justifies by the result the Upaniṣad assigns: ‘सम्भूतिं च विनाशं च’ इत्यत्रावर्णलोपेन निर्देशो द्रष्टव्यः, प्रकृतिलयफलश्रुत्यनुरोधात्.
विनाशम्
vi + √naś, ‘to perish’, with the suffix -a: ‘perishing’. Śaṅkara takes it of the thing that has perishing as its property, by identifying the property with its bearer: विनाशो धर्मो यस्य कार्यस्य स तेन धर्मिणा अभेदेनोच्यते ‘विनाशः’ इति. Mahīdhara, in his first explanation, derives vināśa ‘having destruction’ with the possessive suffix ac of the arśa-ādi group (अर्शआदित्वादच्प्रत्ययः, cf. P. 5.2.127 (अर्शआदिभ्योऽच्)) and applies it to the perishable body.
विनाशेन
In Mahīdhara’s second explanation the a- is restored here instead: अत्र विनाशशब्दद्वये अवर्णलोपो द्रष्टव्यः पृषोदरादित्वात् — ‘in both occurrences of vināśa an elided a is to be understood, as an irregular form’. He appeals to the class of forms accepted as taught, P. 6.3.109 (पृषोदरादीनि यथोपदिष्टम्). The pada-pāṭha gives विना॒शेनेति॑ विना॒शेन॑ and वि॒ना॒शमिति॑ विऽना॒शम्.
वेद
Perfect of √vid used with present meaning (‘knows’), a regular feature of this root.
तीर्त्वा
Absolutive of √tṝ, ‘to cross’: an action prior to the main verb with the same agent (P. 3.4.21 (समानकर्तृकयोः पूर्वकाले)). Deśika, commenting on mantra 12, rejects taking tṝ in these verses in the sense of ‘attaining’, as he had for the parallel mantra 11.
अश्नुते
√aś, 5th class ātmanepada, ‘to reach, attain’ (distinct from aś, 9th class, ‘to eat’).

Anvaya

यः सम्भूतिम् च विनाशम् च तत् उभयम् सह वेद [सः] विनाशेन मृत्युम् तीर्त्वा सम्भूत्या अमृतम् अश्नुते ।

yaḥ sambhūtim ca vināśam ca tat ubhayam saha veda saḥ vināśena mṛtyum tīrtvā sambhūtyā amṛtam aśnute |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

A relative sentence (yaḥ … veda) and its main clause (the subject sa is understood). The objects of veda are sambhūtim and vināśam, linked by ca … ca and summed up by tad ubhayam, ‘that, both’; saha, ‘together’, says how they are known. The main clause takes the two objects up again in the instrumental and gives each a result: by vināśa one crosses death (absolutive tīrtvā), by sambhūti one attains the deathless (aśnute). The order of the pair is reversed in the second line (sambhūti first, then vināśa; then vināśa first, then sambhūti), a chiasmus that mantra 11 has in the same way with vidyā and avidyā.

Ambiguities

sambhūti or asambhūti in this mantra
The text has sambhūtim and sambhūtyā. Śaṅkara reads both as asambhūti (the unmanifest), because vināśa already stands for the manifest Hiraṇyagarbha and because mantra 13, as he reads it, assigns dissolution into prakṛti to asambhava. Mahīdhara, in his second explanation, keeps sambhūti and restores the a- in vināśa instead (avināśa, the unmanifest). Deśika, Madhva and Mahīdhara’s first explanation restore nothing. The pada-pāṭha supports the text as it stands.
What is ‘death’ and what is ‘the deathless’?
For Śaṅkara, death is lack of power and the faults of desire, and ‘immortality’ only dissolution into prakṛti, relative and not liberation. For Mahīdhara’s first explanation, death is crossed by desireless works done with the perishable body, and immortality is liberation (मुक्तिं प्राप्नोति). For Deśika, crossing death is removing the obstacles, and immortality the attainment of Brahman.
Madhva’s text
Madhva’s mūla as keyed lacks the second ca (सम्भूतिं च विनाशं यस्तद्), which leaves the half-line one syllable short; corrections.json classifies it for review against a printed text.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 16 + 16

Two half-lines of 16 syllables. Madhva’s keyed text, without the second च, would have 15 in the first.

Textual variations between witnesses

  • Verification required Madhva’s mūla: सम्भूतिं च विनाशं यस्तद् — Madhva’s text as keyed lacks the second च (‘sambhūtiṃ ca vināśaṃ yas tad’), which leaves the line a syllable short of the anuṣṭubh. Probably a slip of the digital text; not verified against a printed Madhva bhāṣya.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Aging and mortality

Most people live as though growth were the whole story and decline an interruption. The mantra puts coming-into-being and perishing side by side and says that knowing them together is what carries one past death. Without claiming that this is what the commentators meant, a modern reader may hear in it something about aging: that a life understood only as accumulation is incomplete, and that coming to terms with what passes may be part of reaching what does not. This is a reflection, separate from the exegesis above.

13 Reflect and contemplate

A question to begin Think of something in your life that began and something that ended this year. How are the two connected?

Go deeper

A philosophical inquiry Can perishing itself be a means — something by which one ‘crosses death’ — and if so, what is it that perishes?

A contemplation exercise At the end of each day for a week, note one thing that came into being and one that came to an end. On the last day, read the list and look for the pattern.

An invitation to attend, not a treatment; no medical or psychological claim is made.

Further notes

  • The pada-pāṭha printed with the Mādhyandina witness (VS 40.11) analyses सम्भू॑ति॒मिति॒ सम्ऽभू॑तिम्, वि॒ना॒शमिति॑ विऽना॒शम् and सम्भू॒त्येति॒ सम्ऽभू॑त्या.

Uncertainties recorded

  • interpretation Śaṅkara’s and Mahīdhara’s restored a- are reported, not adopted: the literal translation follows the letters and the pada-pāṭha.
  • review Madhva’s mūla as keyed lacks the second च (corrections.json, class review), and his comment on 12–14 survives only in part in the digital source.
