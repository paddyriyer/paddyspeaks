Īśāvāsya Upaniṣad · Mantra 10 of 18

One thing, they say, by vidyā; another by avidyā

The two have different results — and the teaching is received from the wise who explained it.

5 Faithful English translation

Quite another [result], they say, by vidyā; another, they say, by avidyā. Thus we have heard [the word] of the wise, who explained that to us.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

6 Readable English an interpretive rendering, not a literal translation

One thing, they say, comes from vidyā, and another from avidyā: so we have heard from the wise who explained it to us.

Key concepts विद्या vidyā अविद्या avidyā धीर the wise समुच्चय combination (of knowledge and action) श्रुति śruti, ‘what is heard’

7 Explain it to a beginner

The previous verse warned against two lopsided paths. This one explains why both matter: each of the two leads somewhere different. You do not get the result of one by doing the other. Then the speaker does something modest and important. Instead of claiming the idea as his own, he says, ‘This is what we have heard from wise people who explained it to us.’ The teaching is presented as something received, tested and handed on, not invented. Later teachers argued about the first line: some read it as ‘each of the two gives its own result’, others as ‘the goal is something other than either one alone’. Both readings agree that neither half, by itself, is the whole.

An analogy Exercise and study each do something for a student that the other cannot; a coach who learned this from her own teachers passes it on as she received it.

8 Traditional philosophical commentary
Direct Textual Meaning

What does the mantra itself say?

The first line repeats one pattern twice: anyad … āhuḥ, ‘something other, they say’, with vidyā and with avidyā in the instrumental. The second line says that this has been heard from ‘the wise’ (dhīrāḥ) ‘who explained it to us’. The mantra therefore does two things: it distinguishes vidyā from avidyā by what each yields (or by what lies beyond each), and it places the teaching in a line of transmission. It does not say what the ‘other’ is; mantra 11 does.

Traditional Interpretation

Why does this mantra follow the warning of mantra 9?

Śaṅkara introduces it as the reason for combining the two: तत्रावान्तरफलभेदं विद्याकर्मणोः समुच्चयकारणमाह — the difference of their intermediate results is the ground for practising vidyā and ritual together; otherwise, if one had a result and the other none, putting them together would be pointless. The results are those of Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.5.16, which he quotes: कर्मणा पितृलोकः, विद्यया देवलोकः — ‘by works the world of the fathers, by knowledge the world of the gods’. Mahīdhara, on the same mantra as VS 40.13, gives the same scheme with a different first term: the fruit of vidyā, which he here calls Self-knowledge, is immortality, and the fruit of works the world of the fathers.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

How does Deśika read the instrumental?

Deśika asks what then is the means to liberation, and answers from this mantra: the instrumental is used in the sense of the ablative, ‘other than’, because otherwise the words would not construe, and because the parallel mantra 13 has the ablative sambhavāt: अन्यदेवाहुस्सम्भवात् इति वक्ष्यमाणसारूप्याच्च. On this reading the line says that the means to liberation is other than mere works and other than knowledge that dispenses with works. His grammatical point is well founded: anya ordinarily takes the ablative (P. 2.3.29 (अन्यारादितरर्त्तेदिक्शब्दाञ्चूत्तरपदाजाहियुक्ते)), as in Kena 1.4, anyad eva tad viditād, ‘other than the known’. Śaṅkara’s reading does not need the substitution, because he does not take anyat with the instrumental as a comparison: he takes it as ‘a separate result, produced by vidyā’.

Traditional Interpretation

Who are ‘the wise’, and why does the Upaniṣad cite them?

Śaṅkara takes the line as a claim of tradition: ये आचार्या नः अस्मभ्यं तत् कर्म च ज्ञानं च विचचक्षिरे व्याख्यातवन्तः, तेषामयमागमः पारम्पर्यागत इत्यर्थः — the teachers who explained both work and knowledge to us; this is their teaching, come down in succession. Deśika takes the wise as those devoted to meditation on the Supreme Self, and adds that the teaching has been established by a line of earlier teachers (पूर्वपूर्व(सत्)सम्प्रदायसिद्धोऽस्माकमयमर्थ इत्याह). Both treat the line as the Upaniṣad’s own warrant: what follows is not speculation but received instruction.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

What do the witnesses show about this line?

The Mādhyandina recension (VS 40.13, coming after the sambhūti mantras) reads the ablative/genitive forms, विद्याया॑ऽअ॒न्यदा॑हु॒रवि॑द्यायाः, and its pada-pāṭha divides वि॒द्यायाः॑ and अवि॑द्यायाः. The Kāṇva Saṃhitā witness has विद्यया … अविद्याया, which mixes the two and is marked for verification. All Upaniṣad witnesses read the instrumental. The second line recurs almost exactly in Kena 1.4, with pūrveṣām, ‘of the ancients’, for dhīrāṇām and vyācacakṣire for vicacakṣire — the reading that Deśika’s mūla, as keyed, also shows. Whether that keying reflects a recitation influenced by the Kena line cannot be decided from the materials; it is a modern inference.

9 Comparative Vedānta
Comparative Vedānta

Advaita · Śaṅkara, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya, introduction to mantra 10 and bhāṣya

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Each has its own result: अन्यत् पृथगेव विद्यया क्रियते फलमिति आहुः — by vidyā, knowledge of deities, the world of the gods; by avidyā, ritual, the world of the fathers. That difference of intermediate results is why the two are to be combined by the person committed to works; it says nothing about liberation, which for Śaṅkara comes by Self-knowledge alone.

Viśiṣṭādvaita · Vedānta Deśika, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantra 10

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

The instrumentals stand for ablatives (पञ्चम्यर्थे तृतीया): the means to liberation is other than works alone and other than knowledge without works. The ‘wise’ are those devoted to meditation on the Supreme Self, and the first-person perfect shuśruma shows how hard this knowledge is to grasp entire.

Dvaita · Madhva, Īśāvāsyopaniṣad-bhāṣya on mantras 9–11 (one comment)

From the school’s commentary on this mantra

Madhva comments on mantras 9–11 together and the digital text gives no separate gloss of this mantra. His joint comment contrasts those who worship the Lord otherwise than as he is with those who know him as he is: यथास्वरूपं तु नारायणमनामयम्. How he construes the two instrumentals here is not stated in the text available.

Where they disagree

Śaṅkara (with Mahīdhara) reads two results, each produced by one of the two; Deśika reads a means of liberation other than either taken alone. The disagreement turns on a point of grammar — whether anyat with the instrumental can mean ‘other than’ — and on whether the passage concerns intermediate results or liberation itself.

10 Advanced Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi and sandhi-viccheda

  • अन्यदेवाहुर्विद्यया anyadevāhurvidyayā → अन्यत् + एव + आहुः + विद्यया

    consonant, vowel and visarga sandhi

    anyat + eva: t is voiced to d before a vowel (P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते)). eva + āhuḥ: a + ā → ā (P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः)). āhuḥ + vidyayā: a visarga after u, before a voiced sound, becomes r.

  • विद्ययान्यदाहुरविद्यया vidyayānyadāhuravidyayā → विद्यया + अन्यत् + आहुः + अविद्यया

    vowel sandhi (savarṇa-dīrgha), consonant and visarga sandhi

    vidyayā + anyat: ā + a → ā (P. 6.1.101 (अकः सवर्णे दीर्घः)); Advaita Sharada, Madhva’s and Deśika’s texts print the junction open (विद्यया अन्यत्). anyat + āhuḥ → anyad āhuḥ; āhuḥ + avidyayā → āhur avidyayā.

  • नस्तद्विचचक्षिरे nastadvicacakṣire → नः + तत् + विचचक्षिरे

    visarga and consonant sandhi

    A visarga before t becomes s: P. 8.3.34 (विसर्जनीयस्य सः). tat + vicacakṣire: t is voiced to d before the voiced v (P. 8.2.39 (झलां जशोऽन्ते)).

Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

No compound in this mantra.

Morphology

विद्यया
Instrumental singular. With anya, ‘other’, Sanskrit normally uses the ablative of the thing compared: P. 2.3.29 (अन्यारादितरर्त्तेदिक्शब्दाञ्चूत्तरपदाजाहियुक्ते). Hence two readings. Śaṅkara takes the instrumental at face value, ‘a different result is produced by vidyā’. Deśika says the instrumental stands for the ablative by the licence of interchange (अत्र व्यत्ययानुशासनात् पञ्चम्यर्थे तृतीया, अन्यथा अनन्वयात्) — ‘other than vidyā’ — and points to the ablative of mantra 13, sambhavāt. The Mādhyandina recension (VS 40.13) has the ablative form, विद्यायाः … अविद्यायाः. Pāṇini’s general rule of Vedic interchange is P. 3.1.85 (व्यत्ययो बहुलम्); that Deśika’s ‘vyatyaya’ refers to it is an inference, not his statement.
आहुः
From the defective root ah ‘to say’, which has only a few forms, perfect in shape but present in meaning; P. 3.4.84 (ब्रुवः पञ्चानामादित आहो ब्रुवः) gives āha, āhuḥ etc. as substitutes for forms of brū ‘say’. ‘They say’, with the subject unexpressed.
शुश्रुम
Perfect (liṭ) of śru, 1st plural: ‘we have heard’. The perfect is the tense of what one has not witnessed (P. 3.2.115 (परोक्षे लिट्)), so a first person perfect is unusual. Deśika remarks on it: ब्रह्मविद्याया दुरवगाहत्वेन निश्शेषग्रहणाशक्यत्वाभिप्रायोऽत्र लिडुत्तमः — the first-person perfect conveys that this knowledge is so deep that it cannot be grasped completely.
विचचक्षिरे
vi + √cakṣ, perfect, middle, 3rd plural: ‘they explained’. In classical Sanskrit cakṣ is replaced by khyā outside the present system (P. 2.4.54 (चक्षिङः ख्याञ्)), so the classical perfect would be vyācakhyuḥ, which is how Mahīdhara glosses the word (व्याचख्युः); the Upaniṣad keeps the Vedic perfect of cakṣ. Deśika’s mūla as keyed reads व्याचचक्षिरे, but his bhāṣya quotes विचचक्षिरे (corrections.json); Kena 1.4 has व्याचचक्षिरे in the same line.
नः
The enclitic form of the 1st person plural pronoun in the dative, genitive or accusative (P. 8.1.21 (बहुवचने वस्नसौ)); here dative, ‘explained to us’ (Śaṅkara: अस्मभ्यम्).

Anvaya

विद्यया अन्यत् एव [फलम्] आहुः । अविद्यया अन्यत् [फलम्] आहुः । इति धीराणाम् [वचनम्] शुश्रुम ये नः तत् विचचक्षिरे ।

vidyayā anyat eva phalam āhuḥ | avidyayā anyat phalam āhuḥ | iti dhīrāṇām vacanam śuśruma ye naḥ tat vicacakṣire |

Prose order; words in [brackets] are supplied.

Syntax

Two short parallel clauses with an unexpressed subject (‘they say’) and the object anyat, followed by a statement of transmission: ‘thus we have heard [the words] of the wise, who explained it to us’. The genitive dhīrāṇām depends on an understood ‘word’ (Śaṅkara’s वचनम्, which Deśika also says must be supplied: वचनमित्यध्याहार्यम्), or is a genitive of the source with a verb of hearing. The relative ye … vicacakṣire refers back to the wise. Whether the instrumentals mean ‘by’ or ‘other than’ (see morphology) decides whether the first line is about two results or about a third thing other than both.

Ambiguities

‘By vidyā’ or ‘other than vidyā’?
On Śaṅkara’s reading the first line says that vidyā and avidyā each lead to a different result (for him, the world of the gods and the world of the fathers, citing Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.5.16). On Deśika’s reading the instrumentals have the sense of ablatives: the means to liberation is ‘other than’ mere works and ‘other than’ knowledge without works (केवलकर्मणः कर्मनिरपेक्षविद्यातश्चान्यत् मोक्षसाधनमित्युक्तं भवति). The Mādhyandina text has the ablative/genitive form विद्यायाः … अविद्यायाः, but Mahīdhara reads it as a genitive, ‘the fruit of vidyā is other’ (विद्याया आत्मज्ञानस्य फलमन्यदेवाहुरमृतरूपम्), which brings it back to Śaṅkara’s sense.
What is the ‘other’ thing?
The Sanskrit says only anyat, ‘something other’. Śaṅkara and Mahīdhara supply ‘result’ (phala); Deśika takes the line as being about the means of liberation.
The Kāṇva Saṃhitā’s अविद्याया
The Kāṇva Saṃhitā witness ends the line अविद्याया but begins it with विद्यया; this inconsistency is marked for verification against a printed Kāṇva Saṃhitā (corrections.json). The edition keeps the reading of every Upaniṣad witness, अविद्यया.

Metre

anuṣṭubh · syllables counted as written: 15 + 16

Counted as written the half-lines have 15 and 16 syllables. The first is one short because vidyayā + anyat is written contracted (विद्ययान्यत्); with the hiatus vidyayā anyat, as Advaita Sharada and Madhva’s and Deśika’s texts print it, it has 16. The Mādhyandina reading with the ablative (विद्याया॑ऽअ॒न्यत्) keeps the hiatus and so the count.

11 Connections with other scriptures

  • Kena Upaniṣad 1.4 Direct textual parallel

    इति शुश्रुम पूर्वेषां ये नस्तद्व्याचचक्षिरे

    iti śuśruma pūrveṣāṃ ye nastadvyācacakṣire

    The Kena’s second line is the same formula of transmission, with pūrveṣām (‘of the ancients’) for dhīrāṇām and vyācacakṣire for vicacakṣire. Its first line, ‘it is other than the known and beyond the unknown’, uses anya with the ablative — the construction Deśika finds in the Īśā’s instrumental.

  • Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.5.16 Doctrinal comparison

    कर्मणा पितृलोको विद्यया देवलोको

    karmaṇā pitṛloko vidyayā devaloko

    The passage Śaṅkara quotes for the two different results: the world of the fathers by works, the world of the gods by knowledge. Its use here is his interpretation.

12 Contemporary relevance
Contemporary Reflection

Theme Ethical leadership

The second line is a habit worth noticing: the speaker does not claim the idea. ‘We have heard it from the wise who explained it to us.’ In organisations that prize originality, people often hide where their best ideas came from, and teams lose the memory of why a practice exists. The mantra models another stance — to hold a teaching as something received, to name its line, and to pass it on with its explanation. A leader who can say ‘this is what I was taught, and by whom’ invites others to test it rather than merely obey it. This is a reflection on the teaching, not part of it.

Further notes

  • Mahīdhara comments on this mantra as VS 40.13, where the Mādhyandina text reads विद्यायाः … अविद्यायाः.
  • Deśika also explains the genitive dhīrāṇām as possibly having the sense of the ablative with a verb of hearing (नटस्य शृणोतीतिवद्वा कथञ्चित् पञ्चम्यर्थे षष्ठी).

Uncertainties recorded

  • variant Kāṇva Saṃhitā witness: अविद्याया (review); Mādhyandina VS 40.13: विद्यायाः … अविद्यायाः (recension). See corrections.json.
  • interpretation The literal translation keeps ‘by’ for the instrumentals; Deśika’s ‘other than’ is reported, not adopted.
