Texts and their parts

आरण्यक āraṇyaka Āraṇyaka

‘Forest’ texts, dealing with ritual of special power and its inner meaning.

Several Upaniṣads are embedded in Āraṇyakas (the Aitareya, the Taittirīya). The Bṛhadāraṇyaka calls itself both an Āraṇyaka and an Upaniṣad.

See also Brāhmaṇa · Upaniṣad

भाष्य bhāṣya commentary

A commentary that explains a text word by word and argues for an interpretation.

The edition draws on four: Śaṅkara’s (Advaita), Vedānta Deśika’s (Viśiṣṭādvaita), Madhva’s (Dvaita) and Mahīdhara’s Vedadīpa on the Mādhyandina Saṃhitā.

See also Advaita, ‘non-duality’ · Viśiṣṭādvaita, ‘non-duality of the qualified’ · Dvaita, ‘duality’

ब्राह्मण brāhmaṇa Brāhmaṇa

Prose texts that explain the ritual and its meaning.

The Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa belongs to the Śukla Yajurveda; its final book contains the Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad. Not to be confused with brahman (the absolute) or brāhmaṇa (a member of the priestly class).

See also Veda · Āraṇyaka

काण्व kāṇva Kāṇva

One of the two recensions of the Śukla Yajurveda; the source of the eighteen-mantra Īśā.

The Kāṇva Saṃhitā’s fortieth chapter is the Īśā in eighteen mantras. The recension is followed today in parts of Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh and Odisha.

See also branch, recension · Mādhyandina

माध्यन्दिन mādhyandina Mādhyandina

The other recension of the Śukla Yajurveda; its VS 40 has seventeen mantras in another order.

In the Mādhyandina chapter the sambhūti mantras come before the vidyā mantras, the prayer to Pūṣan is shorter, and it ends ‘om khaṃ brahma’. Mahīdhara’s commentary, used in this edition, follows this recension.

See also branch, recension · Kāṇva

मन्त्र mantra mantra

A verse or formula of the Saṃhitā; here, each of the Upaniṣad's eighteen units.

Because the Īśā belongs to a Saṃhitā, its units are called mantras, not ślokas. Śaṅkara begins by saying that these mantras are not used in ritual (कर्मस्वविनियुक्ताः): they reveal the Self.

See also Saṃhitā · seer

प्रस्थानत्रय prasthānatraya the three foundations

The Upaniṣads, the Bhagavad Gītā and the Brahmasūtra, the three sources every Vedānta school comments on.

The Upaniṣads are śruti; the Gītā is smṛti that the tradition treats as their essence; the Brahmasūtra systematises their teaching. A Vedānta ācārya establishes a school by commenting on all three.

See also Vedānta · commentary

ऋषि ṛṣi seer

A seer to whom a Vedic mantra was revealed.

Mahīdhara records that the fortieth adhyāya was seen by Dadhyañc Ātharvaṇa. The word also occurs in the Īśā itself, in ekarṣi, ‘sole seer’ (mantra 16).

In the Taittirīya 1.7

See also śruti, ‘what is heard’ · mantra

शाखा śākhā branch, recension

A school that preserves its own version of a Veda.

The Śukla Yajurveda survives in two recensions, Kāṇva and Mādhyandina. Their fortieth chapters both contain the Īśā, with differences of order and wording; the eighteen-mantra text commented on by Śaṅkara, Madhva and Deśika is the Kāṇva.

See also Saṃhitā · Kāṇva · Mādhyandina

संहिता saṃhitā Saṃhitā

The collection of a Veda's mantras, recited with sandhi and svara.

The word also names the continuous, sandhi-joined way of reciting, as against the pada-pāṭha (word by word). The Īśāvāsya is unusual among the principal Upaniṣads in being part of a Saṃhitā: chapter 40 of the Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā of the Śukla Yajurveda.

See also pada-pāṭha · svara, Vedic accent · branch, recension

स्मृति smṛti smṛti, ‘what is remembered’

Authoritative tradition that depends on śruti: law books, epics, Purāṇas, the Gītā.

Smṛti is held to be authoritative because it rests on śruti. The Bhagavad Gītā is smṛti in this classification, though it is treated as one of the three foundations of Vedānta.

See also śruti, ‘what is heard’ · the three foundations

श्रुति śruti śruti, ‘what is heard’

Revealed scripture: the Vedas, including the Upaniṣads.

In the tradition the Vedas are not composed by any person but ‘heard’ by seers (ṛṣi) and transmitted orally. Śruti is authoritative in itself; everything else is judged by agreement with it. Historians date the Vedic texts by language and content; that is a different kind of claim and does not settle the traditional one.

See also smṛti, ‘what is remembered’ · Saṃhitā · seer

उपनिषद् upaniṣad Upaniṣad

The concluding, philosophical portion of a Vedic school's literature; a text of that kind.

Etymologically upa + ni + √sad, ‘to sit down near’. Śaṅkara, introducing his Kaṭha commentary, derives it from √sad in the senses of destroying, reaching and loosening (सदेर्धातोर्विशरणगत्यवसादनार्थस्योपनिपूर्वस्य क्विप्प्रत्ययान्तस्य रूपमिदम् ‘उपनिषत्’ इति): the word names first the knowledge that destroys ignorance and leads to Brahman, and the book only because it serves that knowledge. Modern scholars read it as ‘connection, equivalence’ — the hidden correspondences the texts teach — or ‘secret teaching’. The accounts answer different questions — what the word is made of, what it means to the tradition, how it was used — and are best kept apart.

See also śruti, ‘what is heard’ · Vedānta · Āraṇyaka

वेद veda Veda

‘Knowledge’; the four collections Ṛg, Yajus, Sāman and Atharvan with their layers of text.

Each Veda is preserved by schools (śākhā) and comprises Saṃhitā (mantras), Brāhmaṇa (ritual exegesis), Āraṇyaka (‘forest’ texts) and Upaniṣad. The layers overlap: the Īśā is itself the fortieth chapter of a Saṃhitā.

See also Saṃhitā · Brāhmaṇa · Āraṇyaka · Upaniṣad · branch, recension

वेदाङ्ग vedāṅga vedāṅga, auxiliary science of the Veda

The six disciplines that serve the reading of the Veda: śikṣā, kalpa, vyākaraṇa, nirukta, chandas, jyotiṣa.

The Muṇḍaka lists them with the four Vedas as ‘lower’ knowledge. Kūranārāyaṇa calls śikṣā ‘the fifth limb’ of the Veda in his comment on Taittirīya 1.2.

See also śikṣā, the science of pronunciation · Veda

वेदान्त vedānta Vedānta

‘The end of the Veda’: the Upaniṣads, and the schools of thought that interpret them.

‘End’ in two senses: the last part of the Vedic corpus, and its culmination. As a school of philosophy Vedānta interprets the Upaniṣads, the Gītā and the Brahmasūtra; its principal schools differ on how Brahman, the self and the world are related.

See also the three foundations · Advaita, ‘non-duality’ · Viśiṣṭādvaita, ‘non-duality of the qualified’ · Dvaita, ‘duality’

Schools of Vedānta

अद्वैत advaita Advaita, ‘non-duality’

The school of Śaṅkara: the Self is Brahman, and the world of many is not finally real apart from it.

For Advaita the self (ātman) and Brahman are identical; individuality and the world of distinctions are due to ignorance (avidyā) and are removed by knowledge. Liberation is knowing what one already is.

See also Viśiṣṭādvaita, ‘non-duality of the qualified’ · Dvaita, ‘duality’ · avidyā · Brahman · self, Self

द्वैत dvaita Dvaita, ‘duality’

The school of Madhva: the Lord (Viṣṇu), selves and matter are eternally distinct.

Madhva teaches five real differences (Lord and self, Lord and matter, self and self, self and matter, matter and matter) and the complete dependence of everything on the Lord. His commentary on the Īśā reads it as a hymn to Hari.

See also Advaita, ‘non-duality’ · Viśiṣṭādvaita, ‘non-duality of the qualified’ · the Lord

विशिष्टाद्वैत viśiṣṭādvaita Viśiṣṭādvaita, ‘non-duality of the qualified’

The school of Rāmānuja and Vedānta Deśika: Brahman is one, qualified by souls and matter, which are real and form his body.

The Lord (Nārāyaṇa) is the inner controller of all; selves and the world are real, distinct from him and inseparable from him, as a body is from its self. Rāmānuja wrote no commentary on the Īśā; the school’s commentary on it is Vedānta Deśika’s.

See also Advaita, ‘non-duality’ · Dvaita, ‘duality’ · the Lord

Concepts

आचार्य ācārya ācārya, teacher

The teacher who imparts the Veda and its meaning.

In the great joining of learning (Taittirīya 1.3) the teacher is the prior form, the pupil the latter, knowledge the junction and teaching the connector.

In the Taittirīya 1.3 · 1.11

See also brahmacārin, Vedic student · vidyā

अग्नि agni Agni, fire

Fire as deity; addressed in mantra 18 (= Ṛgveda 1.189.1).

Agni is the messenger between humans and gods and the fire of the cremation. The last mantra asks him to lead ‘by a good path’.

In the Īśā 18

In the Taittirīya 1.3 · 1.5 · 1.6 · 1.7 · 1.9

See also Pūṣan · resolve, will; the sacrifice

अमृत amṛta immortality, the deathless

Freedom from death; what the person who knows ‘both together’ attains (mantras 11, 14).

Śaṅkara distinguishes relative immortality — identity with a deity, or dissolution into prakṛti — from liberation. The word recurs in mantra 17 (‘breath to the immortal air’).

In the Īśā 11 · 14 · 17

In the Taittirīya 1.4 · 1.6 · 1.10

See also death · liberation

असम्भूति asambhūti asambhūti, ‘non-becoming’

In mantras 12–14, ‘the unmanifest’ or ‘not coming into being’.

For Śaṅkara the unmanifest cause, prakṛti; its worshippers enter darkness (mantra 12). See the study.

In the Īśā 12 · 13

See also sambhūti, ‘coming into being’ · primal matter, the unmanifest

असुर्य asurya sunless, demonic

Mantra 3's asuryāḥ lokāḥ: worlds ‘of the asuras’.

All four commentators derive the word from asura: Śaṅkara takes even gods as ‘asuras’ when measured against the non-dual Self, Madhva reads ‘reached by the asuras’. The rendering ‘sunless’, common in modern translations, would require a-sūrya with long ū; every witness has short u.

In the Īśā 3

See also world, realm · slayer of the self

आत्महन् ātmahan slayer of the self

Mantra 3's ātmahanaḥ: those who ‘kill the self’.

Not suicide in the commentaries. Śaṅkara: those who, through ignorance, hide the Self that is always present (अविद्यादोषेण विद्यमानस्यात्मनस्तिरस्करणात्). Madhva: those turned away from Hari (विमुखा हरौ).

In the Īśā 3

See also self, Self · world, realm

आत्मन् ātman self, Self

One’s own self; in Vedānta, the conscious principle that is one’s real nature.

In the Īśā the word appears in mantras 6–7 (‘sees all beings in the self’) and in ‘slayers of the self’ (mantra 3). Whether that self is identical with the Lord (Advaita) or is the self that the Lord indwells (Viśiṣṭādvaita, Dvaita) is the central disagreement of the commentaries.

In the Īśā 6 · 7

In the Taittirīya 1.4 · 1.5 · 1.7

See also Brahman · individual self · slayer of the self · oneness

अविद्या avidyā avidyā

Literally ‘not-knowledge’; in mantras 9–11, read by the commentators as works or ritual action.

In later Vedānta avidyā means the ignorance that hides the Self. In the Īśā’s mantras 9–11 Śaṅkara takes it as ritual action (karma) and vidyā as knowledge of deities; Deśika as works done as a means to knowledge. The everyday translation ‘ignorance’ is not adequate to these mantras; see the study on vidyā and avidyā.

In the Īśā 9 · 10 · 11

See also vidyā · action, ritual work · combination (of knowledge and action)

भोग bhoga enjoyment, use

Enjoying or using; mantra 1's bhuñjīthāḥ.

The verb bhuj means ‘to enjoy, eat’ and, in another use, ‘to protect’. Pāṇini’s rule on its middle forms (P. 1.3.66 (भुजोऽनवने)) bears on how mantra 1 is read.

In the Īśā 1

See also giving up, relinquishment

ब्रह्मचारिन् brahmacārin brahmacārin, Vedic student

A student living in the discipline of Vedic study with a teacher.

The teacher’s prayer in Taittirīya 1.4 asks that students come ‘as water runs downhill’; the convocation of 1.11 is addressed to the student leaving.

In the Taittirīya 1.4

See also ācārya, teacher

ब्रह्मन् brahman Brahman

The absolute, the ground of all that is.

The Upaniṣads’ name for the ultimate reality. Advaita holds it to be without qualities and identical with the Self; Viśiṣṭādvaita and Dvaita identify it with the Lord, possessed of infinite auspicious qualities. The Īśā does not use the word in its eighteen mantras; the Mādhyandina ending (‘om khaṃ brahma’) does.

In the Taittirīya 1.1 · 1.4 · 1.5 · 1.6 · 1.8 · 1.12

See also self, Self · the Lord · Advaita, ‘non-duality’

देवता devatā deity

A deity; the divinity addressed or meditated on.

Śaṅkara’s vidyā in mantras 9–11 is devatājñāna, knowledge of a deity. In mantra 4 the devāḥ are, for him, the senses (‘the shining ones’).

In the Īśā 4 · 18

In the Taittirīya 1.4 · 1.5

See also worship, meditation · vidyā

धर्म dharma dharma

Right order, duty, nature.

Occurs in mantra 15 in satyadharmāya. A word whose sense depends heavily on context: order, law, duty, quality. In the Taittirīya’s convocation (1.11): धर्मं चर, ‘practise dharma’.

See also truth, the real

धीर dhīra the wise

The steadfast, the wise, from whom the teaching was heard (mantras 10, 13).

‘Thus we have heard from the wise who explained it to us’: the Upaniṣad presents its teaching as received through a line of teachers.

In the Īśā 10 · 13

See also śruti, ‘what is heard’

एकत्व ekatva oneness

Mantra 7's ekatvam: the oneness that the knower sees.

‘What delusion, what sorrow, for one who sees oneness?’ Advaita reads oneness as identity of all with the Self; the theistic schools as the one Lord present in all, or unity of kind.

In the Īśā 7

See also self, Self · delusion · grief, sorrow

एषणा eṣaṇā desire, ‘the three desires’

Desire; in Śaṅkara, the three desires for sons, wealth and worlds.

From Bṛhadāraṇyaka 3.5.1, where knowers of the Self rise above the desire for sons, for wealth and for worlds. Śaṅkara’s reading of mantra 1 depends on it.

See also giving up, relinquishment · renunciation

हिरण्यगर्भ hiraṇyagarbha Hiraṇyagarbha, ‘golden embryo’

The first-born cosmic being, the manifest totality.

Śaṅkara identifies sambhūti and vināśa in mantras 12–14 with Hiraṇyagarbha, whose worship brings powers such as aṇimā.

See also sambhūti, ‘coming into being’

ईश् īś the Lord, ‘ruler’

The ruler; the word that opens the Upaniṣad and gives it its name.

A root noun from √īś, ‘to rule’: ईष्टे इति ईट् (Śaṅkara). Śaṅkara identifies the ruler with the inner Self of all; Deśika with the Supreme Person, distinct from the individual self; Madhva with Hari.

In the Īśā 1

See also the Lord · self, Self

ईश्वर īśvara the Lord

God as ruler and cause of the world.

For Advaita, Brahman as it appears in relation to the world; for the theistic schools, the supreme Person himself. The Īśā’s own word is īś (mantra 1).

See also the Lord, ‘ruler’ · Brahman

जगत् jagat world, ‘the moving’

The world as what is always moving and changing.

Formally a participle of √gam, ‘going’. Mantra 1 uses it twice: ‘whatever moves (jagat) in the moving one (jagatyām)’. Śaṅkara takes jagatyām as ‘on the earth’, Madhva as ‘in prakṛti’.

In the Īśā 1

See also the Lord, ‘ruler’ · primal matter, the unmanifest

जीव jīva individual self

The living, embodied self that acts and experiences.

Advaita treats the jīva as the Self appearing as limited; the theistic schools treat it as a real self, distinct from the Lord and dependent on him. The verb ‘to live’ from the same root occurs in mantra 2 (jijīviṣet, ‘should wish to live’).

In the Īśā 2

See also self, Self · the Lord

ज्ञान jñāna knowledge

Knowledge; in Vedānta, knowledge of the Self or of Brahman that liberates.

Advaita holds that knowledge alone liberates and that it is incompatible with the sense of being an agent that action requires. The theistic schools hold that knowledge (as devotion and meditation) is supported by action.

See also action, ritual work · vidyā · liberation

कर्मन् karman action, ritual work

Action, especially the action prescribed by the Veda; also its results.

Mantra 2: ‘doing works here, one should wish to live a hundred years… action does not cling to a person’. Whether action and knowledge can be combined is the question the commentators answer differently.

In the Īśā 2

In the Taittirīya 1.11

See also knowledge · combination (of knowledge and action) · renunciation

कवि kavi seer, poet

Mantra 8's kaviḥ: the all-seeing.

Śaṅkara: क्रान्तदर्शी सर्वदृक्, ‘one whose sight goes beyond, who sees all’. The Gītā’s kaviṃ purāṇam (8.9) uses the same word of the supreme.

In the Īśā 8

See also self-existent

क्रतु kratu resolve, will; the sacrifice

Mantra 17's krato: ‘O resolve’ — addressed at death, with Om.

Śaṅkara addresses Om, the symbol of Brahman called Agni, and glosses krato as सङ्कल्पात्मक, ‘you whose nature is resolve’; Chāndogya 3.14.1 says a person is ‘made of resolve’ (kratumaya). See the study on the final prayers.

In the Īśā 17

See also Agni, fire · person

लोक loka world, realm

A world or realm of experience, here or after death.

Mantra 3 speaks of ‘worlds covered in blind darkness’. Śaṅkara reads the worlds as births in which the fruits of action are experienced (जन्मानि); Madhva as the worlds reached by those turned away from Hari.

In the Īśā 3

In the Taittirīya 1.3 · 1.5

See also sunless, demonic · slayer of the self

महासंहिता mahāsaṃhitā great joining

The Taittirīya’s five meditations on the joining of sounds, applied to the worlds, lights, learning, offspring and the person.

Each has four terms — prior form, latter form, junction and connector — modelled on a sandhi such as a + u → o (Taittirīya 1.3). Śaṅkara treats them as upāsanā.

In the Taittirīya 1.3

See also Saṃhitā · sandhi, junction · worship, meditation

मेधा medhā medhā, retentive intelligence

Intelligence that grasps and holds what is taught.

The first gift asked of Om in Taittirīya 1.4. Śaṅkara glosses it prajñā and calls the request one for ‘strength of understanding’; in ‘covered by medhā’ he takes it as worldly understanding, Kūranārāyaṇa as divine knowledge.

In the Taittirīya 1.4 · 1.10

See also Om · vidyā

मोह moha delusion

Confusion, delusion; with śoka, what the knower of oneness is free from (mantra 7).

The pair moha and śoka, delusion and grief, names the whole condition of saṃsāra in Śaṅkara’s introduction.

In the Īśā 7

See also grief, sorrow · oneness

मोक्ष mokṣa liberation

Release from the cycle of birth and death.

The Īśā speaks of ‘immortality’ (amṛta) and of the person who ‘crosses death’ (mantras 11, 14). The schools differ on what liberation is: knowing oneself as Brahman (Advaita), or attaining the Lord and serving him (theistic schools).

See also immortality, the deathless · death · knowledge

मृत्यु mṛtyu death

Death; in mantras 11 and 14, what is ‘crossed’.

Śaṅkara glosses mṛtyu in 11 as natural action and knowledge, and in 14 as lack of power, unrighteousness and desire.

In the Īśā 11 · 14

See also immortality, the deathless

ओम् om Om

The sacred syllable; in mantra 17, the call that opens ‘O mind, remember’.

Śaṅkara takes Om as the symbol (pratīka) through which Brahman, here called Agni, is approached. The Kāṇva Saṃhitā witness writes it with the pluti figure (ओम्३).

In the Īśā 17

In the Taittirīya 1.1 · 1.8

See also resolve, will; the sacrifice

पाङ्क्त pāṅkta fivefold

‘Belonging to the paṅkti’ (the five-footed metre): arranged in fives.

Śīkṣāvallī 7 sets three groups of five outside (worlds, lights, beings) beside three within (breaths, faculties, tissues) and concludes ‘all this is fivefold’. Śaṅkara connects the word with the sacrifice, which the Veda calls pāṅkta.

In the Taittirīya 1.7

See also worship, meditation

प्रकृति prakṛti primal matter, the unmanifest

The unmanifest material cause of the world (also avyakta, avyākṛta).

Śaṅkara identifies asambhūti with it (अव्याकृतोपासनया); its worship ends in ‘dissolution into prakṛti’. Madhva glosses jagatyām in mantra 1 as ‘in prakṛti’.

See also asambhūti, ‘non-becoming’ · world, ‘the moving’

पूर्ण pūrṇa full, whole

Full; the word of the śānti verse: ‘that is full, this is full…’.

The verse says that from the full the full arises, and when the full is taken from the full, the full remains. It is Bṛhadāraṇyaka 5.1.1.

See also peace

पुरुष puruṣa person

Person; in mantra 16, ‘that person yonder’ in the sun, whom the speaker says he is.

Śaṅkara gives two derivations: ‘the one who fills (pūrṇa) the world’ and ‘the one who lies in the city (pur, the body)’.

In the Īśā 16

In the Taittirīya 1.6

See also the Sun · self, Self

पूषन् pūṣan Pūṣan

The nourishing deity, addressed in mantras 15–16 as the Sun.

In the Ṛgveda Pūṣan is a guide of paths and of the dead. Śaṅkara: ‘the nourisher of the world, the sun’ (जगतः पोषणात्पूषा रविः).

In the Īśā 15 · 16

See also the Sun · Agni, fire

ऋत ṛta the right; cosmic and moral order

What is right and fitting; in the commentaries, the truth rightly settled in the mind.

An old Vedic word for order, the right. Paired with satya in the Taittirīya (1.1, 1.9, 1.12), it is distinguished by Śaṅkara as the meaning rightly settled in the mind (satya being the same carried out in speech and act), by Madhva as right knowledge.

In the Taittirīya 1.1 · 1.9 · 1.12

See also truth, the real · dharma

सम्भूति sambhūti sambhūti, ‘coming into being’

In mantras 12–14, ‘the manifest’ or ‘becoming’; its meaning is disputed.

Śaṅkara takes sambhūti as Hiraṇyagarbha, the manifest first-born, and asambhūti as the unmanifest cause (prakṛti), and reads 14 with an elided a-. Deśika and Madhva read the terms differently. See the study on sambhūti and asambhūti.

In the Īśā 12 · 13 · 14

See also asambhūti, ‘non-becoming’ · destruction, perishing · Hiraṇyagarbha, ‘golden embryo’

संन्यास saṃnyāsa renunciation

Formal renunciation of the household and of ritual life.

Śaṅkara reads mantra 1 as addressed to those fit for renouncing the three desires; mantra 2 then addresses those who are not. The Upaniṣad itself does not use the word.

See also giving up, relinquishment · desire, ‘the three desires’ · action, ritual work

समुच्चय samuccaya combination (of knowledge and action)

The doctrine that knowledge and ritual action should be practised together.

Mantras 11 and 14 seem to recommend knowing ‘both together’. Śaṅkara accepts a combination only of ritual and meditation on deities, which leads to the world of the gods, never of action with Self-knowledge; the theistic commentators accept works as auxiliary to knowledge of the Lord.

See also action, ritual work · vidyā · avidyā

शान्ति śānti peace

The peace invocation recited before and after the Upaniṣad.

‘Om pūrṇam adaḥ…’, from Bṛhadāraṇyaka 5.1.1, closes with ‘Om, peace, peace, peace’. It is not one of the eighteen mantras.

See also full, whole

सत्य satya truth, the real

The real, the true; ‘the face of the real’ hidden by a golden vessel (mantra 15).

satyadharman in mantra 15: Śaṅkara takes it of the speaker — ‘for me, whose dharma is truth’ (सत्यं धर्मो यस्य मम सोऽहं सत्यधर्मा) — or ‘for one who practises dharma as it truly is’. Madhva: सत्यं ब्रह्म हृदये धारयतीति सत्यधर्मा, ‘who holds the true Brahman in the heart’. In the Taittirīya satya is paired with ṛta (1.1, 1.9, 1.12), and the departing student is told सत्यं वद (1.11).

शोक śoka grief, sorrow

Grief; see moha.

‘The knower of the Self crosses grief’ (Chāndogya 7.1.3) is the closest parallel to mantra 7.

श्रद्धा śraddhā faith, trust

Faith or trust; in giving, the conviction that it is right to give.

‘Give with faith; without faith do not give’ (Taittirīya 1.11). The Gītā (17.28) calls what is given without śraddhā asat. Rāmānanda glosses it as āstikya-buddhi, the conviction that the teaching is true.

In the Taittirīya 1.11

See also dharma

श्री śrī śrī, prosperity

Fortune, prosperity, the means of life.

Asked for in Taittirīya 1.4 only after intelligence: ‘for one without intelligence, prosperity only does harm’ (Śaṅkara). For the Viśiṣṭādvaita commentators also the name of the Lord’s consort.

In the Taittirīya 1.4 · 1.11

सूर्य sūrya the Sun

The Sun, invoked in mantra 16.

In mantra 16 the Sun is addressed by several names — Pūṣan, sole seer, Yama, Sūrya, son of Prajāpati — and asked to withdraw its rays.

स्वाध्याय svādhyāya recitation; study of one’s own Veda

The recitation and study of one’s own Veda, repeated daily.

Literally ‘one’s own reading’: the learning and regular recitation of the Veda of one’s own branch. In the Taittirīya (1.9, 1.11) it is paired with pravacana, teaching, and joined to every other duty; Śaṅkara glosses it simply as adhyayana, study.

In the Taittirīya 1.9 · 1.11

See also Veda · branch, recension

स्वाराज्य svārājya self-rule, sovereignty

The state of a svarāj, one who rules himself; the fruit of the meditation in Śīkṣāvallī 6.

For Śaṅkara, becoming sovereign over the deities who are the limbs of Brahman, as Brahman is; for Kūranārāyaṇa, freedom from the power of karma and the service of the Lord.

In the Taittirīya 1.6

स्वयम्भू svayambhū self-existent

Mantra 8's svayambhūḥ: ‘who exists by himself’.

Śaṅkara explains it as ‘he who is himself everything, both over which he is and who is over it’.

तमस् tamas darkness

Darkness; ‘blind darkness’ in mantras 3, 9 and 12.

The same image recurs: worlds covered in blind darkness (3), and the blind darkness entered by those who worship avidyā (9) or asambhūti (12).

In the Īśā 3 · 9 · 12

See also world, realm

तपस् tapas austerity; disciplined effort

Austerity: the heat of disciplined effort, from fasting to sustained study.

From tap, ‘to heat’. In the Taittirīya Pauruśiṣṭi holds tapas to be what matters most, and Nāka says study and teaching are themselves tapas (1.9); in the Bhṛguvallī Varuṇa tells Bhṛgu to seek Brahman by tapas (3.2). Śaṅkara glosses it as kṛcchra and other penances.

त्याग tyāga giving up, relinquishment

Letting go; mantra 1's tena tyaktena, ‘by what is given up’.

Read by Śaṅkara as renunciation, by Mahīdhara as giving up the thought ‘this is mine’, by Deśika as giving up the world seen as full of defects, by Madhva as ‘what has been given’ by the Lord. See the study on tena tyaktena bhuñjīthāḥ.

उपासना upāsanā worship, meditation

Sustained meditation on, or worship of, a deity or principle.

The verb upāsate, ‘they worship’, opens mantras 9 and 12. Upāsanā is a practice of the mind, distinct from knowledge that simply sees what is.

विद्या vidyā vidyā

Knowledge; in mantras 9–11, read by the commentators as meditative knowledge of a deity.

Śaṅkara: in mantras 9–11 vidyā is knowledge of deities (devatājñāna), not knowledge of the Self, and the Upaniṣad criticises those devoted to it alone. The theistic commentators read it as knowledge of the Lord. See the study.

विनाश vināśa destruction, perishing

In mantra 14, a name for what perishes, set beside sambhūti.

Śaṅkara: vināśa stands for what has destruction as its property, the manifest product, Hiraṇyagarbha.

व्याहृति vyāhṛti the sacred calls

The calls bhūḥ, bhuvaḥ, suvar (and, in the Taittirīya, a fourth, mahaḥ), spoken in Vedic ritual.

An action noun from vi-ā-hṛ, ‘to utter’. The three calls bhūḥ, bhuvaḥ, suvar name earth, the mid-region and heaven. Śīkṣāvallī 5 adds a fourth, mahaḥ, ‘greatness’, made known by Māhācamasya, and makes it the whole of which the others are limbs; each call is matched with a world, a light, a Veda and a breath.

Method of this edition

अन्वय anvaya anvaya, prose order

The words of a verse rearranged in the order of a prose sentence.

Supplied words are shown in brackets; the checker requires every other anvaya word to be a word of the padaccheda.

See also padaccheda, word division

पदच्छेद padaccheda padaccheda, word division

Separating the words of a verse joined by sandhi.

In this edition every padaccheda is proven: the checker rejoins the words by the rules of external sandhi and requires that they spell the verse.

See also sandhi, junction · pada-pāṭha

पदपाठ padapāṭha pada-pāṭha

The word-by-word recitation of a Saṃhitā, with sandhi undone.

An ancient analysis of the text into words, preserved by the reciters. The Mādhyandina witness gives the pada-pāṭha of VS 40, which supports, for example, ईशा। वास्यम्। as two words.

See also Saṃhitā · padaccheda, word division

समास samāsa compound

A compound word, analysed by its vigraha (the phrase it stands for).

Types: tatpuruṣa (dependent), karmadhāraya (descriptive), bahuvrīhi (possessive), dvandva (copulative), avyayībhāva (adverbial).

See also sandhi, junction · anvaya, prose order

सन्धि sandhi sandhi, junction

The sound changes where words (or parts of words) meet.

Vowel sandhi (a + i → e), visarga sandhi (-aḥ + voiced → -o), consonant sandhi (t + dh → ddh). Vedic texts allow some junctions classical Sanskrit does not (prakṛtibhāva, mantra 18).

See also padaccheda, word division · compound

शिक्षा śikṣā śikṣā, the science of pronunciation

The auxiliary science of correct recitation: sounds, accent, length, effort, evenness and joining.

First of the six auxiliary sciences of the Veda (Muṇḍaka 1.1.5). The Taittirīya’s second anuvāka names its six topics — varṇa, svara, mātrā, bala, sāman, santāna — and spells the word with a Vedic long ī (śīkṣā).

In the Taittirīya 1.2

See also svara, Vedic accent · sandhi, junction · Saṃhitā · vedāṅga, auxiliary science of the Veda

स्वर svara svara, Vedic accent

The pitch accents of Vedic recitation, marked in the Saṃhitā.

Udātta (raised), anudātta (low, marked ॒) and svarita (falling, marked ॑). The edition shows the accented text of the Kāṇva Saṃhitā witness as keyed, and does not reconstruct or correct it.

See also Saṃhitā · pada-pāṭha
