1 The one clothed in white Verse of the prayoga

शुक्लाम्बरधरं विष्णुं शशिवर्णं चतुर्भुजम् प्रसन्नवदनं ध्यायेत् सर्वविघ्नोपशान्तये

śuklāmbaradharaṃ viṣṇuṃ śaśivarṇaṃ caturbhujam prasannavadanaṃ dhyāyet sarvavighnopaśāntaye

Think of the one dressed in white, bright as the moon, four-armed and smiling, so that every obstacle may be stilled.

Faithful English translation

One should meditate on the white-clothed, all-pervading, moon-coloured, four-armed one with a serene face, for the quieting of all obstacles.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

A short meditation said at the very beginning, before any mantra of the rite. Its words picture a figure dressed in white, pale as the moon, with four arms and a calm, kindly face, and give the reason for remembering him: that every obstacle in what follows be quieted. The epithets do not name the deity directly — viṣṇu here can be read as a name or as ‘the one who pervades’ — and the prayoga uses the verse for Vighneśvara, the remover of obstacles: the stotrasamhita text heads the step विघ्नेश्वर-ध्यानम्. The verse is said again before the saṅkalpa for the Gāyatrī japa.

In the rite Opening of the rite (step vighneśvara-dhyāna), in all three services, and again at the start of the japa (step japa-saṅkalpa). The stotrasamhita text adds the gesture: the temples are struck gently five times with the backs of the fingers.

2 Ojo ’si and the gāyatrī with its head: the invitation completed and the breath-control mantra Vedic · Mahānārāyaṇa 10.35.53 (TA 10)

ओं भूःओं भुवःओं सुवःओं महःओं जनःओं तपःओं सत्यम्ओं तत्सवितुर्वरेण्यं भर्गो देवस्य धीमहिधियो यो नः प्रचोदयात्ओमापो ज्योती रसोऽमृतं ब्रह्म भूर्भुवस्सुवरोम्

om bhūḥom bhuvaḥom suvaḥom mahaḥom janaḥom tapaḥom satyamom tatsaviturvareṇyaṃ bhargo devasya dhīmahidhiyo yo naḥ pracodayātomāpo jyotī raso'mṛtaṃ brahma bhūrbhuvassuvarom

The rite recites part of this unit; the translation is of the whole unit.

You are vigour and conquering power, strength and radiance; the splendour of the gods is your very name; you are the universe and its life, all things and the life of all, the one who prevails. Om — I call here the gāyatrī, the sāvitrī, Sarasvatī, the metres and the seers, and Śrī. The gāyatrī: its metre gāyatrī, its seer Viśvāmitra, its deity Savitṛ; Agni its mouth, Brahmā its head, Viṣṇu its heart, Rudra its crest, the earth its womb; bearing the five breaths, white, of the lineage of Sāṅkhyāyana; twenty-four syllables in three feet; used at the upanayana. Om earth, Om mid-space, Om heaven, Om the great, Om janaḥ, Om tapaḥ, Om the true. Om — let us contemplate the excellent radiance of the god Savitṛ, who impels our minds. Om — the waters, light, essence, the immortal: Brahman; earth, mid-space, heaven — Om.

This is a mantra of the Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad (Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10), where its text is proven and analysed in full: padaccheda, word by word, translation, the three commentaries. Mahānārāyaṇa 10.35.53 in the Upaniṣad edition.

What it says and does

The saṅkalpa, the statement of intent, for the Gāyatrī japa. It names the means — the removal of all the faults one has incurred — and the purpose, the pleasure of the Supreme Lord, and then the act itself. The verbs are first person: the worshipper speaks for himself, and the formula is the same for every performer.

In the rite Step japa-sankalpa, after śuklāmbaradharam and a prāṇāyāma.

Notes
Traditional Interpretation

A saṅkalpa is said with the breath held after the prāṇāyāma, or immediately after it; it names the act, the time, and the purpose. The Smārta form offers the act for the pleasure of the Supreme Lord (Parameśvara); Vaiṣṇava prayogas use other wording, which is outside this edition.

Source and witnesses

Base witness: S (stotrasamhita); collated with M.

The other witness

  • Variant reading M: M prints only the closing form; S writes the saṅkalpa in full.
