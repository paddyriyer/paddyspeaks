Faithful English translation

Of the mantra “dadhikrāvṇaḥ”: Vāmadeva the seer, anuṣṭubh the metre, the All-gods (Dadhikrāvan) the deity. Of the mantra “āpo hi ṣṭha”: Sindhudvīpa the seer, divine gāyatrī the metre, the Waters the deity, the use in sprinkling.

What it says and does

A viniyoga is the statement said before a mantra of its seer (ṛṣi), its metre (chandas), its deity (devatā) and the use (viniyoga) it is put to. Here two viniyogas together, for the second sprinkling: dadhikrāvṇo akāriṣam (TS 1.5.11.4; RV 4.39.6), whose seer is Vāmadeva and whose deity is given as the All-gods or Dadhikrāvan, the divine horse it names; and again āpo hi ṣṭhā. The commas of the witness mark the pauses between the parts; a word before a comma keeps its pausal form.

In the rite Step punar-marjana, before dadhikrāvṇo and āpo hi ṣṭhā, in all three services (M only).

Faithful English translation

I have made (praise) of Dadhikrāvan, the victorious, swift horse; may he make our mouths fragrant, may he prolong our lives.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

A single anuṣṭubh to Dadhikrāvan, the divine horse of the Ṛgveda, who stands for the swift morning power that sets the world going; Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara reads him as Agni in the form of a horse. The worshipper says that he has praised him, and asks two things: that the mouth be made fragrant — fit to speak holy words — and that life be lengthened. In the Taittirīya Saṃhitā (1.5.11.4) it is the invitatory verse of an offering of rice to Dadhikrāvan (Bhāskara’s viniyoga); in the Ṛgveda it is 4.39.6. It opens the second sprinkling of the Sandhyāvandanam, before the three ṛks to the Waters are said again.

In the rite Punar-mārjana (the second sprinkling), in all three services: water is sprinkled with dadhikrāvṇo and then with āpo hi ṣṭhā and the vyāhṛtis. The Mylapore prayoga gives the viniyoga: Vāmadeva the seer, anuṣṭubh the metre, the All-gods (or Dadhikrāvan) the deity (vin-dadhikravnah).

The commentators
Traditional Interpretation

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara explains the name: the offerings that hold (dadhi) are what he goes to, तानि क्रामति गच्छतीति दधिक्रावा अग्निः — Dadhikrāvan is Agni, who took the form of a horse. ‘I have made’ means ‘I am his worker in the rite’: कर्मकरस् स्यामिति यावत्. The second line asks for fragrance won by good deeds — अस्मत्सुकृतसमृद्ध्या समासादित-सौरभ्याणि — and long life, आयूंषि च प्रतारिषत् प्रवर्धयतु.

Traditional Interpretation

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara’s viniyoga

In the Saṃhitā the verse is the invitatory (puronuvākyā) of the offering to Dadhikrāvan in a three-offering rite: दधिक्राव्ण्णे चरुम्, दधिकाव्ण्ण इत्यनुष्टुप्.

Notes
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Anuṣṭubh: four pādas of eight syllables. The Ṛgveda (4.39.6) has the same words; the Taittirīya writes the doubled ṇ of its tradition (दधिक्राव्ण्णो).

Academic History

Dadhikrāvan is praised in four hymns of the fourth maṇḍala of the Ṛgveda (4.38–40) as a divine racehorse; this verse closes the second of them.

3 To the Waters: you are the bringers of well-being Vedic · TS 4.1.5.1

आपो॒ हि ष्ठा म॑यो॒भुव॒स्ता न॑ ऊ॒र्जे द॑धातनम॒हे रणा॑य॒ चक्ष॑से

apo-hi-stha.1 Waters, you are the source of well-being: give us strength, and the joy of seeing clearly.

apo-hi-stha.2 Give us here a share of your most kindly essence, as loving mothers give to their children.

apo-hi-stha.3 May we come fully to that essence of yours, in whose abode you delight; and, Waters, give us life and offspring.

Faithful English translation

apo-hi-stha.1 O Waters, you are indeed bringers of well-being; such, set us towards nourishment, towards great delightful seeing.

apo-hi-stha.2 Which sap of yours is most kindly, of that make us share here, like loving mothers.

apo-hi-stha.3 To that may we go fully, to you, for whose dwelling you are pleased; and, O Waters, generate us.

What it says and does

Three short ṛks addressed to the Waters, the most recited verses of the Sandhyāvandanam. The Waters are asked for three things: strength and clear sight, a share of their kindly essence, and new life. The second verse compares them to loving mothers who give of themselves to their children. In the Taittirīya Saṃhitā (4.1.5.1) the verses are said while water is poured on the clay of the fire-pan, to cool the fire’s heat — Sāyaṇa cites the Brāhmaṇa’s instruction तिसृभिरुप सृजति. The same three ṛks open hymn 10.9 of the Ṛgveda. In the rite they accompany the sprinkling of water on the head: what cooled the fire is now asked to cool and purify the worshipper.

In the rite Mārjana: water is sprinkled on the head, verse by verse, and then round the self with the vyāhṛtis; recited again in the second mārjana after dadhikrāvṇo, in all three services. The Mylapore prayoga gives the viniyoga: Sindhudvīpa the seer, gāyatrī the metre, the Waters the deity, used for sprinkling (vin-apo-hi-stha).

The commentators
Traditional Interpretation

Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa, on TS 4.1.5, places the three ṛks in the making of the fire-pan: water is mixed in to calm the fire’s heat, as the Brāhmaṇa says, तिसृभिरुप सृजति त्रिवृद्वा अग्निर्यावानेवाग्निस्तस्य शुचँ शमयति. On the second verse he explains the simile: कामयमानाः प्रीतियुक्ता मातरो यथा वत्सान्स्वकीयस्तन्यरसं प्रापयन्ति तद्वत् — as loving mothers give their calves their own milk.

Traditional Interpretation

Sāyaṇa

On the third verse Sāyaṇa reads tasmai as ‘to that sap’ and janayatha as a prayer for offspring: तस्मै रसाय वौ युष्मानरं गमामालं भृशं प्राप्नुमः, हे आपो यूयं नोऽस्माञ्जनयथ प्रजोत्पादकन्कुरुथ.

Traditional Interpretation

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara glosses the first verse: आपः व्यापितास् स्थ मयोभुवः सुखस्य भावयित्र्यः — the Waters pervade, and they bring well-being; ūrje is ‘for sap’ (or ‘give us strength’), and the great delightful seeing is knowledge: चक्षसे ज्ञानाय.

Traditional Interpretation

Sāyaṇa on the Ṛgveda

Sāyaṇa on the Ṛgveda (10.9.1) takes hi as ‘because’ and draws the same reading of the last line towards insight: अस्मान् सम्यग्ज्ञानं प्रति यौग्यान् कुरुतेत्यर्थः.

Notes
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Gāyatrī metre: three pādas of eight syllables per ṛk. The Ṛgveda (10.9.1–3) has the same words; the Taittirīya differs only in spelling (वश्शिवतमः for वः शिवतमः, अरङ्गमाम for अरं गमाम).

Traditional Interpretation

In the Saṃhitā these verses calm the fire; in the rite they are said with water on the head. The prayoga’s viniyoga names Sindhudvīpa as the seer.

What it says and does

The three ‘utterances’ (vyāhṛtis) — bhūḥ, bhuvaḥ, suvaḥ — name the three worlds, earth, the mid-region and heaven, and are said with the praṇava Om before them. In the Taittirīya tradition the third is suvaḥ, where other schools say svaḥ. In the Sandhyāvandanam they close the two mārjanas (the water is then sprinkled round oneself) and they open the Gāyatrī at the arghya and at the japa.

In the rite Said after āpo hi ṣṭhā at the mārjana and after the second mārjana, while water is sprinkled round oneself; and before the Gāyatrī at the arghya, at the arghya of atonement and at the japa (steps marjana, punar-marjana, arghya, prayascitta-arghya, japa).
