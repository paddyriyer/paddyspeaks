Āghrāṇa (aghamarṣaṇa) आघ्राणम्

Water held to the nose with drupadād iva and the three ṛks of creation, then cast away (in the Mylapore prayoga).

Faithful English translation

Of the mantra “drupadād iva”: so-and-so the seer, anuṣṭubh the metre, Drupadā the deity, the use in smelling.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

A viniyoga is the statement said before a mantra of its seer (ṛṣi), its metre (chandas), its deity (devatā) and the use (viniyoga) it is put to. Here: drupadād iva muñcatu, recited while water held in the hand is brought to the nose and then thrown away (āghrāṇa). The witness leaves the seer blank with amuka, ‘so-and-so’, written in parentheses: it does not know or does not give his name. The commas of the witness mark the pauses between the parts; a word before a comma keeps its pausal form.

In the rite Step aghrana, before drupadād iva (M only).

2 As from a wooden stock Vedic (composite)

द्रु॒प॒दादि॑व मुञ्चतुद्रु॒प॒दादि॒वेन्मु॑मुचा॒नःस्वि॒न्नः स्वा॒त्वी मला॑दिवपू॒तं प॒वित्रेणे॒वाज्य॑म्आपः॑ शुन्धन्तु॒ मैन॑सः

drupadādiva muñcatudrupadādivenmumucānaḥsvinnaḥ svātvī malādivapūtaṃ pavitreṇevājyamāpaḥ śundhantu mainasaḥ

As a man is freed from the stocks, as one who sweats is freed from dirt by bathing, as butter is made pure by the strainer, so may the Waters cleanse me of sin.

Faithful English translation

As from a wooden stock may it release; like one released from the stock, like a sweating man (who has) bathed from dirt, like butter purified by the strainer — may the Waters cleanse me from sin.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

Three images of release and cleansing build to one prayer. The drupada is a wooden stock in which, Sāyaṇa explains, a thief’s foot was fastened. A man freed from it, a sweating man after his bath, butter strained clean: so may the Waters cleanse the worshipper from sin. In the rite water is held to the nose while it is recited, before the three ṛks of creation (ṛtaṃ ca satyaṃ ca) and the casting away of the water.

In the rite Step āghrāṇa, all three services, in the Mylapore prayoga only (S lacks the step); its viniyoga is vin-drupadad-iva.

The commentators
Traditional Interpretation

Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa, on TB 2.6.6, explains the stock: राजानो हि अपराधिनस्तस्करस्य पादं सच्छिद्रे काष्ठे कीलयन्ति तत् द्रुपदमित्युच्यते — kings fasten an offending thief’s foot in a pierced block of wood, and that is called the drupada. The man released from it is treated as clean; so is the sweating man who has bathed (यथा स्विन्नः पुरुषः स्नात्वी स्नानं कृत्वा मलान्मुमुचानः शुद्ध इत्येव व्यवह्रियते), and the butter purified by the strainer; तथेमा आपो मामेनसः पापात् शुन्धन्तु शुद्धं कुर्वन्तु, so may these Waters cleanse me from sin.

Notes
Direct Textual Meaning

A composite. The first line, द्रुपदादिव मुञ्चतु, and the next three are TB 2.4.4.9, which ends विश्वे मुञ्चन्तु मैनसः (‘may all [the gods] release me from sin’); the close, आपः शुन्धन्तु मैनसः, is that of TB 2.6.6, where the verse begins द्रुपदादिवेन्मुमुचानः. Both passages are in text.json (drupadad-iva.1, .2). The Sandhyā joins the opening of the one to the close of the other and so addresses the prayer to the Waters.

Verification required M prints स्वा॒त्वी; both TB sources (स्वि॒न्नस्स्ना॒त्वी) and Sāyaṇa read स्नात्वी. A correction स्वा → स्ना is proposed; the padaccheda word स्वात्वी must then become स्नात्वी.

3 Ambhasy apāre: the one beyond the waters, the gāyatrīs of the gods, and the mantras of the bath Vedic · Mahānārāyaṇa 10.1.13 (TA 10)

यदपां क्रूरं यदमेध्यं यदशान्तं तदपगच्छतात्अत्याशनादतीपानाद्यच्च उग्रात्प्रतिग्रहात्तन्नो वरुणो राजा पाणिना ह्यवमर्शतुसोऽहमपापो विरजो निर्मुक्तो मुक्तकिल्बिषःनाकस्य पृष्ठमारुह्य गच्छेद्ब्रह्म सलोकताम्यश्चाप्सु वरुणस्स पुनात्वघमर्षणःइमं मे गङ्गे यमुने सरस्वति शुतुद्रि स्तोमं सचता परुष्णियाअसिक्निया मरुद्वृधे वितस्तयाऽऽर्जीकीये शृणुह्या सुषोमयाऋतं च सत्यं चाभीद्धात्तपसोऽध्यजायतततो रात्रिरजायत ततः समुद्रो अर्णवः

yadapāṃ krūraṃ yadamedhyaṃ yadaśāntaṃ tadapagacchatātatyāśanādatīpānādyacca ugrātpratigrahāttanno varuṇo rājā pāṇinā hyavamarśatuso'hamapāpo virajo nirmukto muktakilbiṣaḥnākasya pṛṣṭhamāruhya gacchedbrahma salokatāmyaścāpsu varuṇassa punātvaghamarṣaṇaḥimaṃ me gaṅge yamune sarasvati śutudri stomaṃ sacatā paruṣṇiyāasikniyā marudvṛdhe vitastayā''rjīkīye śṛṇuhyā suṣomayāṛtaṃ ca satyaṃ cābhīddhāttapaso'dhyajāyatatato rātrirajāyata tataḥ samudro arṇavaḥ

Let whatever in the waters is harmful, unclean or disturbing go away. Whatever wrong comes of eating or drinking too much, or of taking gifts from dangerous people, may King Varuṇa wipe it away with his own hand. Then, sinless, stainless, set free and rid of guilt, I would climb to the summit of heaven and reach the world of Brahman. May Varuṇa, who dwells in the waters, the remover of sin, purify me. [The rivers invoked:] Gaṅgā, Yamunā, Sarasvatī, Śutudrī, Paruṣṇī; Asiknī, Marudvṛdhā, Vitastā, Ārjīkīyā, Suṣomā — come and hear my praise. [The aghamarṣaṇa, said under the water:] From the blazing heat of reflection right order and truth were born; from it the night, from it the billowing ocean.

This is a mantra of the Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad (Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10), where its text is proven and analysed in full: padaccheda, word by word, translation, the three commentaries. Mahānārāyaṇa 10.1.13 in the Upaniṣad edition.
