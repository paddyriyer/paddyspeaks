Faithful English translation

With body, speech, mind or senses, with intellect or self, or from the disposition of nature — whatever I do, all of it I offer to the Supreme, thinking “to Nārāyaṇa”.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

The rite ends by giving itself away. Every agency by which anything is done is named — body, speech, mind, the senses, intellect, the self, and finally the inborn tendencies of one’s nature — and whatever has been done through any of them, the rite just completed included, is offered to the Supreme, Nārāyaṇa. M follows it with ॐ तत्सत् ब्रह्मार्पणमस्तु (tat-sat).

In the rite Step samarpaṇa, all three services, the last verse of the rite before ācamana.

Notes
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Upajāti metre (mixed indravajrā and upendravajrā): four pādas of eleven syllables. S writes बुद्‌ध्याऽऽत्मना with a double avagraha, marking the ā of ātmanā; M writes बुद्ध्याऽत्मना.

Source and witnesses

Base witness: S (stotrasamhita); collated with M.

Faithful English translation

That, the real: let (this) be an offering to Brahman.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

The closing words of the samarpaṇa in the Mylapore prayoga: after kāyena vācā offers everything done to Nārāyaṇa, tat sat names the absolute, and the act is declared an offering to Brahman. Om tat sat as a designation of Brahman is the formula discussed in the Bhagavad Gītā (17.23); the witness itself names no source for this line.

In the rite Step samarpana, after kāyena vācā (M only; S ends with kāyena vācā).
