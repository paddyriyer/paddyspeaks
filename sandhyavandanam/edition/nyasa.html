What it says and does

A viniyoga is the statement said before a mantra of its seer (ṛṣi), its metre (chandas), its deity (devatā) and the use (viniyoga) it is put to. Here: the Gāyatrī itself, called Sāvitrī after its deity, before the japa. The metre is given as nicṛd-gāyatrī, ‘a gāyatrī wanting one syllable’: as written, the verse has twenty-three syllables, one short of the twenty-four of the gāyatrī (the first pāda is counted full by reading vareṇiyam).

In the rite Step nyasa, before the nyāsas and the meditation, in all three services.

2 Kara-nyāsa: placing the Gāyatrī on the hands Nyāsa

तत्स॑वि॒तुः ब्रह्मात्मने अङ्गुष्ठाभ्यां नमःवरे॑ण्यं विष्ण्वात्मने तर्जनीभ्यां नमःभर्गो॑ दे॒वस्य॑ रुद्रात्मने मध्यमाभ्यां नमःधी॒म॒हि॒ ईश्वरात्मने अनामिकाभ्यां नमःधियो॒ यो नः॑ सदाशिवात्मने कनिष्ठिकाभ्यां नमःप्र॒चो॒दया॑त् सर्वात्मने करतलकरपृष्ठाभ्यां नमः

tatsavituḥ brahmātmane aṅguṣṭhābhyāṃ namaḥvareṇyaṃ viṣṇvātmane tarjanībhyāṃ namaḥbhargo devasya rudrātmane madhyamābhyāṃ namaḥdhīmahi īśvarātmane anāmikābhyāṃ namaḥdhiyo yo naḥ sadāśivātmane kaniṣṭhikābhyāṃ namaḥpracodayāt sarvātmane karatalakarapṛṣṭhābhyāṃ namaḥ

The six parts of the Gāyatrī are placed on the hands, each with its deity: tat savituḥ (Brahmā) on the thumbs, vareṇyam (Viṣṇu) on the forefingers, bhargo devasya (Rudra) on the middle fingers, dhīmahi (Īśvara) on the ring fingers, dhiyo yo naḥ (Sadāśiva) on the little fingers, pracodayāt (the Self of all) on the palms and backs of the hands.

What it says and does

A nyāsa (‘placing’) of the Gāyatrī on the hands before the japa. The mantra is cut into six parts; each part is joined to a deity — Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Rudra, Īśvara, Sadāśiva, and finally the Self of all — and touched to a finger in order, from the thumbs to the little fingers, and last the palms and backs of the hands. The formula is the same in each line: the words of the Gāyatrī, the deity in the dative with -ātmane, the fingers in the dative dual, and namaḥ.

In the rite Step nyāsa, all three services, after the viniyoga of the Gāyatrī japa (vin-savitri), in the Mylapore prayoga only; it is followed by the aṅga-nyāsa.

3 Aṅga-nyāsa: placing the Gāyatrī on the body Nyāsa

तत्स॑वि॒तुः ब्रह्मात्मने हृदयाय नमःवरे॑ण्यं विष्ण्वात्मने शिरसे स्वाहाभर्गो॑ दे॒वस्य॑ रुद्रात्मने शिखायै वषट्धी॒म॒हि॒ ईश्वरात्मने कवचाय हुम्धियो॒ यो नः॑ सदाशिवात्मने नेत्रत्रयाय वौषट्प्र॒चो॒दया॑त् सर्वात्मने अस्त्राय फट्भूर्भुवः॒सुव॒रोम् इति दिग्बन्धः

tatsavituḥ brahmātmane hṛdayāya namaḥvareṇyaṃ viṣṇvātmane śirase svāhābhargo devasya rudrātmane śikhāyai vaṣaṭdhīmahi īśvarātmane kavacāya humdhiyo yo naḥ sadāśivātmane netratrayāya vauṣaṭpracodayāt sarvātmane astrāya phaṭbhūrbhuvaḥsuvarom iti digbandhaḥ

The six parts of the Gāyatrī, each with its deity, are placed on the body: heart, head, tuft, the ‘armour’ of the crossed arms, the three eyes, and the ‘weapon’, each with its own ritual call (namaḥ, svāhā, vaṣaṭ, hum, vauṣaṭ, phaṭ); then bhūr bhuvaḥ suvar om binds the directions.

Faithful English translation

“Tat savituḥ”: to the Brahmā-self, to the heart, namaḥ. “Vareṇyam”: to the Viṣṇu-self, to the head, svāhā. “Bhargo devasya”: to the Rudra-self, to the tuft, vaṣaṭ. “Dhīmahi”: to the Īśvara-self, to the armour, hum. “Dhiyo yo naḥ”: to the Sadāśiva-self, to the three eyes, vauṣaṭ. “Pracodayāt”: to the Self of all, to the weapon, phaṭ. “Bhūr bhuvaḥ suvar om” — thus the binding of the directions.

What it says and does

The second nyāsa: the same six parts of the Gāyatrī and the same six deities as the kara-nyāsa, now placed on the body — the heart, the head, the tuft of hair, the ‘armour’, the three eyes and the ‘weapon’ — each closed with its own ritual call. The last line, with the three vyāhṛtis and om, ‘binds the directions’ around the worshipper for the japa; it is undone after the japa (dig-vimoka).

In the rite Step nyāsa, all three services, after the kara-nyāsa, in the Mylapore prayoga only.

    Vigraha दिशां बन्धः

    the binding of the directions

Notes
Traditional Interpretation

The places and calls (hṛdaya namaḥ, śiras svāhā, śikhā vaṣaṭ, kavaca hum, netra vauṣaṭ, astra phaṭ) are the six members of the common aṅga-nyāsa; M gives them for the Gāyatrī. S has no nyāsa.

Verification required As for kara-nyāsa: M writes तत्स॑वि॒तुः ब्रह्मात्मने unjoined (proposed तत्स॑वि॒तुर्ब्रह्मात्मने), and the svara marks in the text must be stripped before the word-division proof. With both, the padaccheda rejoins (declared pauses: धीमहि·ईश्वरात्मने, सर्वात्मने·अस्त्राय).

Source and witnesses

Base witness: M (the Mylapore prayoga); the other witness does not have this text.
