Faithful English translation

mitrasya.1 Of Mitra, supporter of the peoples, of the god, the winnable fame, true, most brilliantly famed.

mitrasya.2 Mitra, knowing, sets people in order; Mitra has held up the earth and the sky; Mitra watches the peoples unblinking; to the true one let us offer an oblation with ghee.

mitrasya.3 Let that mortal stand forth rich in offerings, O Mitra, who strives for you, O Āditya, by your ordinance. He, helped by you, is not slain, not overpowered; distress does not reach him from near or from afar.

What it says and does

Three ṛks to Mitra, the god of the morning and of agreements between men. The first praises his glory; the second says what he does — he sets each person to his work, holds up earth and sky, and watches over everyone without blinking; the third promises that the one who serves him by his ordinance is safe from harm near or far. In the Taittirīya Saṃhitā the first verse is used in making the fire-pan (Sāyaṇa: Mitra is chosen to calm it, वारुण्युखाऽसीद्धा मैत्रियोपैति शान्त्यै), and the three together stand among the offering verses of TS 3.4.11; in the Ṛgveda they are 3.59.6, 3.59.1 and 3.59.2. In the morning Sandhyā the worshipper stands before the rising Sun and greets him as Mitra.

In the rite Morning sūryopasthāna: the three ṛks are recited standing, facing the rising Sun, after the Gāyatrī has been given leave. The Mylapore prayoga gives the viniyoga: the All-gods as seers, gāyatrī and triṣṭubh the metres, Mitra the deity, used in the upasthāna of the morning Sandhyā (vin-mitrasya).

The commentators
Traditional Interpretation

Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa, on the first verse at TS 4.1.6, supplies the verb ‘is’: मित्रस्य देवस्य श्रवः श्रोतुं योग्यं यशो महदस्तीति शेषः, and explains the choice of Mitra: the blazing fire-pan is fierce, Varuṇa’s, and Mitra’s verse calms it — अभिद्धा प्रज्वलिता येयमुखा सेयमुग्रत्वाद्वारुणी, तस्या मैत्र्यच शान्तिर्भ-वति.

Traditional Interpretation

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara, on TS 3.4.11: Mitra moves people to act knowing what each is fit for — जनान् सर्वान् यातयति चेष्टयति प्रजानन् तत्तदधिकारानुरूपविधानज्ञः — and ‘watching’ means helping: आभिमुख्येन पश्यति उपकरोतीत्यर्थः.

Forms

  • mitrasya.1 श्रवः — no verb is stated; Sāyaṇa supplies अस्ति: ‘great is the fame of Mitra’.
  • mitrasya.2 दाधार — the perfect of √धृ with the reduplicating syllable lengthened (दा- for द-), a Vedic form; the perfect here states a lasting fact.
  • mitrasya.2 अनिमिषा — instrumental of the root noun अनिमिष् ‘not-blinking’; Bhāskara also allows it as a plural, ‘the unwinking gods’.
  • mitrasya.3 शिक्षति — the desiderative of √शक् ‘to be able’, used in the Veda for ‘to try to help, to serve’.
  • mitrasya.3 जीयते — the passive of √ज्या (जिनाति) ‘to overpower’.
Notes
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

The first ṛk is a gāyatrī, the other two triṣṭubhs — hence the viniyoga’s ‘gāyatrī and triṣṭubh’. The Ṛgveda (3.59.6) reads the first verse differently: मित्रस्य चर्षणीधृतो ऽवो देवस्य सानसि द्युम्नं चित्रश्रवस्तमम् — avaḥ ‘help’ for śravaḥ ‘fame’, and dyumnam ‘splendour’ for satyam. The Taittirīya and the prayogas have श्रवः and सत्यम्.

Academic History

Ṛgveda 3.59 is the one hymn of the Ṛgveda addressed to Mitra alone; the Sandhyā recites its sixth, first and second verses in that order.

What it says and does

The first verse of the midday upasthāna, recited standing and facing the Sun. It pictures the Sun as Savitṛ, the god who sets everything going: he travels the sky on a golden chariot, and as he goes he puts gods and mortals each in their place and looks over all the worlds. On the second: the face is the disc, the gathering of the rays, अनीकं सङ्घातरूपम्मण्डलम्; it is the eye even of Mitra and the other gods, for they too see by its light — मित्रादीनां देवानामपि चक्षुस्स्थानं , तेपि हि तेन प्रकाशितं पश्यन्ति; and the Lord within the disc fills the worlds — तदा तन्मण्डलान्तर्गतो भगवान् सूर्यः जगतो जङ्गमस्य तस्थुषः स्थावरस्य च विश्वस्यात्मा.

Sandhi

  • ud-u-tyam.1 उदु udu → उत् + उ

    consonant

    t before a vowel becomes d

  • ud-u-tyam.1 त्यञ्जातवेदसन्देवं tyañjātavedasandevaṃ → त्यम् + जातवेदसम् + देवम्

    consonant

    final m before a stop becomes the nasal of its class (ñ before j, n before d)

  • ud-u-tyam.2 उदगादनीकञ्चक्षुर्मित्रस्य udagādanīkañcakṣurmitrasya → उत् + अगात् + अनीकम् + चक्षुः + मित्रस्य

    consonant and visarga

    t before a vowel becomes d; m before c becomes ñ; -uḥ before a voiced consonant becomes -ur

  • ud-u-tyam.2 वरुणस्याग्नेः varuṇasyāgneḥ → वरुणस्य + अग्नेः

    vowel

    a + a becomes ā

  • ud-u-tyam.2 सूर्य आत्मा sūrya ātmā → सूर्यः + आत्मा

    visarga

    -aḥ before a vowel other than a loses its visarga, leaving a hiatus

Compounds

    bahuvrīhi · जात + वेदस्

    Vigraha जातानि वेद इति, or जातं वेदः यस्य

    who knows what is born; whose knowledge is born

Forms

  • ud-u-tyam.1 दृशे — a Vedic infinitive, the dative of the root noun दृश्; Bhāskara notes it as a listed form ('दृशे विख्ये च' इति निपात्यते).
  • ud-u-tyam.1 विश्वाय — the dative of the pronominal विश्व without the pronominal ending -smai (Bhāskara: स्मैभावाभावश्छान्दंसः).
  • ud-u-tyam.2 द्यावापृथिवी — in a dvandva of deities दिव् takes the form द्यावा-; the dual ending -ī is the Vedic option for -yau (Bhāskara: 'वा छन्दसि' इति पूर्वसवर्णदीर्घः).
  • ud-u-tyam.2 तस्थुषः — the weak stem of the perfect participle of √स्था, ‘having stood’, i.e. what is stationary.
Notes
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

The first verse is a gāyatrī, the second a triṣṭubh. The Ṛgveda (1.50.1, 1.115.1) has the same words; the Taittirīya spelling joins them (त्यञ्जातवेदसन्देवंवहन्ति).

Academic History

Ṛgveda 1.50 and 1.115 are the two great hymns to Sūrya in the first maṇḍala; ud vayam (1.50.10), recited just before in the rite, comes from the first of them.
