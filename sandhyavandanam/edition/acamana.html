To Acyuta, salutation. To Ananta, salutation. To Govinda, salutation.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

Three names of Viṣṇu, each with namaḥ, ‘salutation’, said as water is sipped from the right palm three times. The ācamana purifies the performer before the rite and is repeated at its joints. Acyuta is ‘the one who does not fall away’, Ananta ‘the endless’, and Govinda a name of Kṛṣṇa as the finder or keeper of cows.

In the rite The opening ācamana (step acamana): water is sipped once with each name; the face and body are then touched with the twelve names (anga-vandana).

Faithful English translation

O Keśava, O Nārāyaṇa, O Mādhava, O Govinda, O Viṣṇu, O Madhusūdana, O Trivikrama, O Vāmana, O Śrīdhara, O Hṛṣīkeśa, O Padmanābha, O Dāmodara.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

The twelve names of Viṣṇu, called in the vocative, one for each part of the face and body touched after the sipping: the two cheeks, the eyes, the nostrils, the ears, the shoulders, the navel and the head. The same twelve names receive water at the tarpaṇa later in the rite (tarpana-kesava), there in the accusative.

In the rite Ācamana (step acamana), after the three sips: each name is said while touching one place — the stotrasamhita text gives the order cheeks, eyes, nostrils, ears, shoulders, navel, head, with the finger for each.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis
