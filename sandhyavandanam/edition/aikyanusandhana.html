Aikyānusandhāna ऐक्यानुसन्धानम्

Meditating on the identity of the Sun and Brahman, and of Brahman and oneself.

1 Aikyānusandhāna: the Sun is Brahman Formula

असावादित्यो ब्रह्मब्रह्मैवाहमस्मि

asāvādityo brahmabrahmaivāhamasmi

That Sun is Brahman. I am Brahman itself.

What it says and does

The ‘meditation on oneness’: after the arghya the performer, holding the hands as in salutation, thinks of the Sun as Brahman and of Brahman as his own self. The first sentence states the identity of the deity worshipped with the absolute; the second takes the worshipper into the same identity. The two short sentences carry the purpose of the whole rite as the tradition reads it: worship of the Sun that ends in knowledge of the self.

In the rite After the arghyas (step aikyanusandhana), in all three services; then the ācamana and the tarpaṇa.

Notes
Traditional Interpretation

The first sentence, asāv ādityo brahma, occurs in the Sandhyā brāhmaṇa of the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka (TA 2.2, the passage that enjoins the rite; text.json mantra sandhya-brahmana), where the knower who meditates on the rising and setting sun as Brahman is said to become Brahman. The second sentence is the prayoga’s.
