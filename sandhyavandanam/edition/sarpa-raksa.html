1 Salutation to the Narmadā Verse of the prayoga

नर्मदायै नमः प्रातर्नर्मदायै नमो निशि नमोऽस्तु नर्मदे तुभ्यं त्राहि मां विषसर्पतः

narmadāyai namaḥ prātarnarmadāyai namo niśi namo'stu narmade tubhyaṃ trāhi māṃ viṣasarpataḥ

Homage to the Narmadā at dawn and at night; homage to you, Narmadā: guard me from poisonous snakes.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

A prayer to the river Narmadā for protection from snakes, the first of three verses of protection against serpents recited together near the end of the rite. The river is greeted morning and night, then asked directly to guard the worshipper from venomous snakes.

In the rite Step sarpa-rakṣā, all three services, facing north (S marks the direction), before jaratkāroḥ and sarpāpasarpa.

2 Āstīka, son of Jaratkāru Verse of the prayoga

जरत्कारोर्जरत्कार्वां समुत्पन्नो महायशाः अस्तीकः सत्यसन्धो मां पन्नगेभ्योऽभिरक्षतु पन्नगेभ्योऽभिरक्षत्वोन्नम इति

jaratkārorjaratkārvāṃ samutpanno mahāyaśāḥ astīkaḥ satyasandho māṃ pannagebhyo'bhirakṣatu pannagebhyo'bhirakṣatvonnama iti

May Āstīka, the famous son of the sage Jaratkāru and his wife Jaratkāru, faithful to his word, protect me from serpents. From serpents may he protect me; om, homage.

Faithful English translation

Born of Jaratkāru in Jaratkāru, of great fame, Astīka, true to his word, may he protect me from serpents. “May he protect from serpents — om, homage.”

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

The second of the three verses against serpents. It calls on Āstīka, son of the sage Jaratkāru and of his wife, also named Jaratkāru: in the traditional story he is the one who brought King Janamejaya’s sacrifice of the serpents to an end, and so serpents keep their word to spare whoever remembers him (the theme of the next verse). The last words are repeated with om namaḥ, the refrain-form that closes several of these salutations.

In the rite Step sarpa-rakṣā, all three services, after narmadāyai namaḥ.

3 Serpent, go away Verse of the prayoga

सर्पापसर्प भद्रं ते गच्छ सर्प महाविष जनमेजयस्य यज्ञान्ते आस्तीकवचनं स्मरन्

sarpāpasarpa bhadraṃ te gaccha sarpa mahāviṣa janamejayasya yajñānte āstīkavacanaṃ smaran

Go away, serpent, and good luck to you; go, venomous one — remember what Āstīka asked at the end of Janamejaya’s sacrifice.

Faithful English translation

Serpent, creep away; good luck to you; go, serpent of great venom, remembering Āstīka’s word at the end of Janamejaya’s sacrifice.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

The third verse against serpents speaks to the snake itself, politely: it wishes it well and sends it away, reminding it of the word given to Āstīka when he ended King Janamejaya’s serpent sacrifice. The verse assumes the story behind jaratkāroḥ: the serpents, saved by Āstīka, promised to spare those who remember him.

In the rite Step sarpa-rakṣā, all three services, the last of the three verses.

Notes
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Anuṣṭubh (śloka). S’s third pāda, जनमेजयस्य यज्ञान्ते, has nine syllables; M’s जनमेजय-यज्ञान्ते has the regular eight.

Direct Textual Meaning

M reads the first line differently: अपसर्प सर्प भद्रं ते दूरं गच्छ महायशाः (‘go far away, O famous one’); the variants are listed in corrections.json.
