Salutation to all the deities समष्ट्यभिवादनम्

1 Samaṣṭy-abhivādana: salutation to all the deities Formula

सन्ध्यायै नमःसावित्र्यै नमःगायत्र्यै नमःसरस्वत्यै नमःसर्वाभ्यो देवताभ्यो नमो नमः

sandhyāyai namaḥsāvitryai namaḥgāyatryai namaḥsarasvatyai namaḥsarvābhyo devatābhyo namo namaḥ

Salutation to Sandhyā, to Sāvitrī, to Gāyatrī and to Sarasvatī; to all the deities, salutation again and again.

Faithful English translation

To Sandhyā salutation. To Sāvitrī salutation. To Gāyatrī salutation. To Sarasvatī salutation. To all the deities salutation, salutation.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

After the upasthāna the worshipper turns to the four quarters and salutes four goddesses, one in each, and then all the deities together. Sandhyā is the twilight as a goddess; Gāyatrī, Sāvitrī and Sarasvatī are the forms of the goddess of the Gāyatrī mantra (the evening dhyāna calls her Sarasvatī). The stotrasamhita text marks the directions: Sandhyā east, Sāvitrī south, Gāyatrī west, Sarasvatī north, and all the deities again to the east.

In the rite Step samasti-abhivadana, after the sūryopasthāna, in all three services; followed by kāmo ’kārṣīt and the abhivādana.

2 Kāmo ’kārṣīt, manyur akārṣīt Formula

कामोऽकार्षी᳚न्मन्युरकार्षी᳚न्नमो॒ नमः

kāmo'kārṣīnmanyurakārṣīnnamo namaḥ

It was desire that acted, it was passion that acted — salutation, salutation.

Faithful English translation

Desire did (it); anger did (it). Salutation, salutation.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

A one-line confession said with a bow: whatever wrong has been done was done by desire and by anger, not by the self. It abbreviates two expiatory formulas of the Mahānārāyaṇa (10.61 kāmo ’kārṣīt … and 10.62 manyur akārṣīt …), where each is spelled out at length (‘desire acts, not I; desire is the doer, not I’). The rite keeps only their first words and the salutation.

In the rite Step samasti-abhivadana, after the salutation to all the deities and before the abhivādana, in all three services.

3 Abhivādana: the formal greeting Formula

अभिवादये (त्रयार्षेय) प्रवरान्वित गोत्रः (आपस्तम्ब) सूत्रः यजुःशाखा अध्यायी () शर्मा नामाहम् अस्मि भोः

abhivādaye (trayārṣeya) pravarānvita gotraḥ (āpastamba) sūtraḥ yajuḥśākhā adhyāyī () śarmā nāmāham asmi bhoḥ

I greet you: I am …, of the … gotra, with a pravara of three seers …, following the Āpastamba sūtra, a student of the Yajurveda — … Śarmā by name.

Faithful English translation

I salute: (I am) of the gotra (…), endowed with the pravara of three seers (…), of the (Āpastamba) sūtra, a student of the Yajus branch; (…) Śarmā by name I am, O sirs!

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

The abhivādana is the formal greeting in which one announces oneself by lineage: the seers of one’s pravara, the gotra, the sūtra of one’s rites, the Veda one studies, and one’s name. S prints it as a formula with blanks: the parentheses and the empty brackets mark the places where each performer puts his own seers, gotra and name (M fills them with a worked example for the Bhāradvāja gotra). The words in parentheses — trayārṣeya, āpastamba — are the common case and are replaced when they do not apply (pañcārṣeya for five seers, another sūtra or Veda).

In the rite Said with a bow, touching the ears, after kāmo ’kārṣīt (step samasti-abhivadana) and again at the end of the salutation to Sūrya-Nārāyaṇa, in all three services.
