1 The Gāyatrī with five faces and ten hands Verse of the prayoga

मुक्ता-विद्रुम-हेम-नील-धवळच्छायैर्मुखैस्त्रीक्षणैः युक्तामिन्दु-निबद्ध-रत्न-मकुटां तत्त्वार्थ-वर्णात्मिकाम् गायत्रीं वरदाभयाङ्कुशकशाः शुभ्रं कपालं गदाम् शङ्खं चक्रमथारविन्दयुगलं हस्तैर्वहन्तीं भजे

muktā-vidruma-hema-nīla-dhavaḻacchāyairmukhaistrīkṣaṇaiḥ yuktāmindu-nibaddha-ratna-makuṭāṃ tattvārtha-varṇātmikām gāyatrīṃ varadābhayāṅkuśakaśāḥ śubhraṃ kapālaṃ gadām śaṅkhaṃ cakramathāravindayugalaṃ hastairvahantīṃ bhaje

I worship the Gāyatrī: five faces, pearl, coral, gold, blue and white, each with three eyes; the moon and jewels in her crown; her very self the syllables that carry the truth; in her ten hands the gestures of boon and fearlessness, the goad, the whip, a white skull-bowl, a mace, the conch, the discus and two lotuses.

Faithful English translation

With faces of pearl, coral, gold, blue and white hue, three-eyed, endowed; whose crown has the moon and jewels set in it; whose self is the syllables of the meaning of reality — the Gāyatrī, bearing in her hands the boon and fearlessness gestures, the goad and the whip, a white skull-bowl, a mace, a conch, a discus and a pair of lotuses, I worship.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

The general meditation on the goddess of the Gāyatrī before the japa, in both witnesses. She has five faces, each a different colour — pearl, coral, gold, blue and white — and each three-eyed; the moon and jewels are set in her crown; and she is described as made of the syllables that carry the meaning of reality, that is, as the mantra itself. Her ten hands hold the attributes listed in the second half.

In the rite Step dhyāna of the Gāyatrī japa, all three services, before the time-specific verse (M) or yo devaḥ savitā (S).

Notes
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Śārdūlavikrīḍita metre: four pādas of nineteen syllables.

Direct Textual Meaning

M reads the second pāda युक्तां इन्दु-कला-निबद्ध-मकुटां, the digit of the moon bound in the crown (with इदु for इन्दु); see corrections.json.

Verification required S has no danda between the pādas, so the text is one line and त्रीक्षणैः युक्ताम् (the end of the first pāda and the start of the second) stands unjoined within it; the padaccheda rejoins into त्रीक्षणैर्युक्तामिन्दु… The fix is either the joined spelling त्रीक्षणैर्युक्तामिन्दु… or dandas at the pāda ends (the padaccheda would then be four lines).

Source and witnesses

Base witness: S (stotrasamhita); collated with M.

The other witness

  • Spelling only M: S writes the Vedic ळ for intervocalic ḍ in धवळ and joins छायैः with c-c (धवळच्छायैः); M writes धवल-छायैः.

  • Variant reading M: M: इन्दु-कला-निबद्ध (the digit of the moon bound in the crown); S: इन्दु-निबद्ध-रत्न (the moon and jewels bound in the crown).

2 The Gāyatrī at dawn: in Brahmā’s form Verse of the prayoga Morning

बालार्कारुण-शोण-पाटल-मुखैर्युक्तां कुमारीं गिरम् प्रातर्लोहित-विग्रहां भुवि-गतां सौख्यात्मिकां रासजीम्बिभ्राणामभयं कमण्डलुमथाम्भोजाक्षमालां ब्रुवम् गायत्रीं हृदि हंसगां भवकरीं श्रीब्रह्मरूपां भजे

bālārkāruṇa-śoṇa-pāṭala-mukhairyuktāṃ kumārīṃ giram prātarlohita-vigrahāṃ bhuvi-gatāṃ saukhyātmikāṃ rāsajīmbibhrāṇāmabhayaṃ kamaṇḍalumathāmbhojākṣamālāṃ bruvam gāyatrīṃ hṛdi haṃsagāṃ bhavakarīṃ śrībrahmarūpāṃ bhaje

In the morning I worship in my heart the Gāyatrī as a girl, red-bodied, her faces glowing like the rising sun: she is Speech, she dwells on the earth, her nature is happiness; she shows the gesture of fearlessness and holds a water-pot, a lotus, a rosary and the ladle; she rides the swan, brings things into being, and has the form of Brahmā.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

The morning form of the goddess of the Gāyatrī in the Mylapore prayoga. At dawn she is a young girl, red like the rising sun, Speech herself, with the attributes of Brahmā the creator: the water-pot, the rosary, the ladle of sacrifice, the swan as her mount. She is the first of the three: Brahmā’s form and creation at dawn, Rudra’s form and dissolution at midday, Viṣṇu’s form and preservation at dusk.

In the rite Step dhyāna of the Gāyatrī japa, morning service only (the Mylapore prayoga), after the general verse muktā-vidruma.

3 The Gāyatrī at midday: Sāvitrī in Rudra’s form Verse of the prayoga Midday

मुक्ता-पाण्डुर-हेम-धूसर-सितास्यां त्रिक्षणां व्योमगां मध्याह्ने सितविग्रहां सुयुवतीं सत्यात्मिकां तामसीम्खट्वाङ्गाभय-शूलकानि दधतीं रुद्राक्षमालामुमाम् सावित्रीं वृषवाहनां क्षयकरीं श्रीरुद्ररूपां भजे

At midday I worship the goddess as Sāvitrī: white-bodied, a young woman, three-eyed, moving in the sky, her faces pale as pearl, golden, grey and white; she holds the skull-staff, the trident and a rudrākṣa rosary and shows the gesture of fearlessness; she is Umā, rides the bull, brings things to their end, and has the form of Rudra.

    Vigraha सर्वैः उपचारैः पूजाः

    worship with all attendances

Notes
Traditional Interpretation

The matching of seed syllables, elements and offerings (laṃ earth, haṃ space, yaṃ air, raṃ fire, vaṃ nectar) is as M gives it. S has no pañcopacāra.

Verification required M writes धूपं आघ्रापयामि with the anusvāra before a vowel; the padaccheda (धूपम् आघ्रापयामि) rejoins into धूपमाघ्रापयामि. Proposed text: यं वाय्वात्मने धूपमाघ्रापयामि.

Source and witnesses

Base witness: M (the Mylapore prayoga); the other witness does not have this text.

Corrections to the base witness

  • ंआ → मा: M writes धूपं आघ्रापयामि unjoined inside one formula; joined, धूपमाघ्रापयामि, like the other lines of the pūjā.
