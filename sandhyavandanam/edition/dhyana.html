3 The Gāyatrī at midday: Sāvitrī in Rudra’s form Verse of the prayoga Midday

मुक्ता-पाण्डुर-हेम-धूसर-सितास्यां त्रिक्षणां व्योमगां मध्याह्ने सितविग्रहां सुयुवतीं सत्यात्मिकां तामसीम्खट्वाङ्गाभय-शूलकानि दधतीं रुद्राक्षमालामुमाम् सावित्रीं वृषवाहनां क्षयकरीं श्रीरुद्ररूपां भजे

muktā-pāṇḍura-hema-dhūsara-sitāsyāṃ trikṣaṇāṃ vyomagāṃ madhyāhne sitavigrahāṃ suyuvatīṃ satyātmikāṃ tāmasīmkhaṭvāṅgābhaya-śūlakāni dadhatīṃ rudrākṣamālāmumām sāvitrīṃ vṛṣavāhanāṃ kṣayakarīṃ śrīrudrarūpāṃ bhaje

At midday I worship the goddess as Sāvitrī: white-bodied, a young woman, three-eyed, moving in the sky, her faces pale as pearl, golden, grey and white; she holds the skull-staff, the trident and a rudrākṣa rosary and shows the gesture of fearlessness; she is Umā, rides the bull, brings things to their end, and has the form of Rudra.

What it says and does

The midday form of the goddess of the Gāyatrī, one of three dhyāna verses in the Mylapore prayoga, one for each service. At dawn she is a girl with Brahmā’s attributes, at midday a young woman with Rudra’s, at dusk an old woman with Viṣṇu’s: the three times of day are mapped onto creation, dissolution and preservation, and onto the three guṇas. Here she is Sāvitrī and Umā, white, three-eyed, riding Rudra’s bull, holding the khaṭvāṅga (a staff topped with a skull), the trident and a rudrākṣa rosary.

In the rite Step dhyāna of the Gāyatrī japa, midday service only (the Mylapore prayoga), after the general verse muktā-vidruma. S does not have the three time-specific verses.

What it says and does

The evening form of the goddess of the Gāyatrī in the Mylapore prayoga. 5 The Gāyatrī as a verse Verse of the prayoga

What it says and does

A verse that retells the Gāyatrī (tat savitur vareṇyam …) in plain classical Sanskrit, as a meditation before the japa. Each element of the mantra reappears: the god Savitṛ (savituḥ devasya), our minds (dhiyaḥ … naḥ), impelling them (pracodayāt, here prerayet), his radiance (bhargaḥ), its excellence (vareṇyam), and our contemplation of it (dhīmahi, here upāsmahe). It adds what the minds are impelled towards: dharma and the other aims of life.

In the rite Step dhyāna of the Gāyatrī japa, all three services, in the stotrasamhita text (S), after the general dhyāna verse; M does not have it.
