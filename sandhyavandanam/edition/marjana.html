Mārjana मार्जनम्

Sprinkling water on the head with the three ṛks to the Waters, then round the self with the vyāhṛtis.

2 To the Waters: you are the bringers of well-being Vedic · TS 4.1.5.1

आपो॒ हि ष्ठा म॑यो॒भुव॒स्ता न॑ ऊ॒र्जे द॑धातनम॒हे रणा॑य॒ चक्ष॑से

यो व॑श्शि॒वत॑मो॒ रस॒स्तस्य॑ भाजयते॒ह नः॑उ॒श॒तीरि॑व मा॒तरः॑

तस्मा॒ अर॑ङ्गमाम वो॒ यस्य॒ क्षया॑य॒ जिन्व॑थआपो॑ ज॒नय॑था च नः

āpo hi ṣṭhā mayobhuvastā na ūrje dadhātanamahe raṇāya cakṣase

yo vaḥśivatamo rasastasya bhājayateha naḥuśatīriva mātaraḥ

tasmā araṅgamāma vo yasya kṣayāya jinvathaāpo janayathā ca naḥ

apo-hi-stha.1 Waters, you are the source of well-being: give us strength, and the joy of seeing clearly.

apo-hi-stha.2 Give us here a share of your most kindly essence, as loving mothers give to their children.

apo-hi-stha.3 May we come fully to that essence of yours, in whose abode you delight; and, Waters, give us life and offspring.

Faithful English translation

apo-hi-stha.1 O Waters, you are indeed bringers of well-being; such, set us towards nourishment, towards great delightful seeing.

apo-hi-stha.2 Which sap of yours is most kindly, of that make us share here, like loving mothers.

apo-hi-stha.3 To that may we go fully, to you, for whose dwelling you are pleased; and, O Waters, generate us.

What it says and does

Three short ṛks addressed to the Waters, the most recited verses of the Sandhyāvandanam. The Waters are asked for three things: strength and clear sight, a share of their kindly essence, and new life. The second verse compares them to loving mothers who give of themselves to their children. In the Taittirīya Saṃhitā (4.1.5.1) the verses are said while water is poured on the clay of the fire-pan, to cool the fire’s heat — Sāyaṇa cites the Brāhmaṇa’s instruction तिसृभिरुप सृजति. The same three ṛks open hymn 10.9 of the Ṛgveda. In the rite they accompany the sprinkling of water on the head: what cooled the fire is now asked to cool and purify the worshipper.

In the rite Mārjana: water is sprinkled on the head, verse by verse, and then round the self with the vyāhṛtis; recited again in the second mārjana after dadhikrāvṇo, in all three services. The Mylapore prayoga gives the viniyoga: Sindhudvīpa the seer, gāyatrī the metre, the Waters the deity, used for sprinkling (vin-apo-hi-stha).

The commentators
Traditional Interpretation

Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa, on TS 4.1.5, places the three ṛks in the making of the fire-pan: water is mixed in to calm the fire’s heat, as the Brāhmaṇa says, तिसृभिरुप सृजति त्रिवृद्वा अग्निर्यावानेवाग्निस्तस्य शुचँ शमयति. On the second verse he explains the simile: कामयमानाः प्रीतियुक्ता मातरो यथा वत्सान्स्वकीयस्तन्यरसं प्रापयन्ति तद्वत् — as loving mothers give their calves their own milk.

Traditional Interpretation

Sāyaṇa

On the third verse Sāyaṇa reads tasmai as ‘to that sap’ and janayatha as a prayer for offspring: तस्मै रसाय वौ युष्मानरं गमामालं भृशं प्राप्नुमः, हे आपो यूयं नोऽस्माञ्जनयथ प्रजोत्पादकन्कुरुथ.

Traditional Interpretation

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara glosses the first verse: आपः व्यापितास् स्थ मयोभुवः सुखस्य भावयित्र्यः — the Waters pervade, and they bring well-being; ūrje is ‘for sap’ (or ‘give us strength’), and the great delightful seeing is knowledge: चक्षसे ज्ञानाय.

Traditional Interpretation

Sāyaṇa on the Ṛgveda

Sāyaṇa on the Ṛgveda (10.9.1) takes hi as ‘because’ and draws the same reading of the last line towards insight: अस्मान् सम्यग्ज्ञानं प्रति यौग्यान् कुरुतेत्यर्थः.

Forms

  • apo-hi-stha.1 ष्ठा — the verb स्थ, ‘you are’, as the Saṃhitā recites it after हि: the s is retroflexed and the final vowel lengthened. The word division keeps the recited form.
  • apo-hi-stha.1 दधातन — the old Vedic imperative ending -tana for -ta (classical दधात).
  • apo-hi-stha.2 शिवतमः — शिव with the superlative suffix -tama.
  • apo-hi-stha.2 उशतीः — the Vedic feminine plural in -īḥ of the present participle of √वश् ‘to desire’.
  • apo-hi-stha.3 जनयथा — the final a of जनयथ is lengthened in the Saṃhitā of a ṛk (P. 6.3.133 (ऋचि तुनुघमक्षुतङ्कुत्रोरुष्याणाम्) lists such lengthenings); the word division keeps the recited form.
  • apo-hi-stha.3 गमाम — a root aorist subjunctive of √गम्: ‘let us go’.
Notes
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Gāyatrī metre: three pādas of eight syllables per ṛk. The Ṛgveda (10.9.1–3) has the same words; the Taittirīya differs only in spelling (वश्शिवतमः for वः शिवतमः, अरङ्गमाम for अरं गमाम).

Traditional Interpretation

In the Saṃhitā these verses calm the fire; in the rite they are said with water on the head. The prayoga’s viniyoga names Sindhudvīpa as the seer.
