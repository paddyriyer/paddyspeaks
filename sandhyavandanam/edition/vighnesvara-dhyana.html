Meditation on the remover of obstacles विघ्नेश्वरध्यानम्

1 The one clothed in white Verse of the prayoga

शुक्लाम्बरधरं विष्णुं शशिवर्णं चतुर्भुजम् प्रसन्नवदनं ध्यायेत् सर्वविघ्नोपशान्तये

śuklāmbaradharaṃ viṣṇuṃ śaśivarṇaṃ caturbhujam prasannavadanaṃ dhyāyet sarvavighnopaśāntaye

Think of the one dressed in white, bright as the moon, four-armed and smiling, so that every obstacle may be stilled.

Faithful English translation

One should meditate on the white-clothed, all-pervading, moon-coloured, four-armed one with a serene face, for the quieting of all obstacles.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

A short meditation said at the very beginning, before any mantra of the rite. Its words picture a figure dressed in white, pale as the moon, with four arms and a calm, kindly face, and give the reason for remembering him: that every obstacle in what follows be quieted. The epithets do not name the deity directly — viṣṇu here can be read as a name or as ‘the one who pervades’ — and the prayoga uses the verse for Vighneśvara, the remover of obstacles: the stotrasamhita text heads the step विघ्नेश्वर-ध्यानम्. The verse is said again before the saṅkalpa for the Gāyatrī japa.

In the rite Opening of the rite (step vighneśvara-dhyāna), in all three services, and again at the start of the japa (step japa-saṅkalpa). The stotrasamhita text adds the gesture: the temples are struck gently five times with the backs of the fingers.

Notes
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

An anuṣṭubh (śloka) verse: two half-lines of sixteen syllables. The witnesses M and S agree letter for letter.

Source and witnesses

Base witness: S (stotrasamhita); collated with M.
