Health Intelligence Lab

Your data. Your history. Your health questions. One understandable view.

◆Every person and every number here is synthetic. Five fictional people, generated from documented seeds, imported through the same adapters a real file would use. There is no upload: the lab reads only its own synthetic files and never asks for health data. Like every PaddySpeaks page it counts an anonymous page view (privacy policy). Nothing here is medical advice, a diagnosis or a prediction.

What this is, and what it is not

What it does. It answers six questions: what do we know, am I improving, is my exercise working, are there changes worth attention, how do I compare, and what should I do next. Each answer shows the measurement behind it, where that measurement came from, and how much to trust it.

How it works. A Python pipeline reads Apple Health–style exports, a blood-pressure cuff, a scale, a glucose sensor and FHIR clinical records, then checks units, time zones, duplicates and conflicts. Every result is computed once and shipped as data; this page only draws it. The system design explains every decision.

What it never does. It never invents a single health score, never infers a condition from exercise, never compares you with a made-up population without saying so, never advises on medicines, and never says exercise is safe for you. A website cannot read your iPhone’s Health database, and this one does not try.

Before real data. A real version would need authentication, consent and revocation, encryption with managed keys, audit logs, contracts, a HIPAA and consumer-privacy analysis, and clinical validation. The production readiness gate lists them.
