## 6. Unresolved — marked uncertain for scholarly review (129)

- **1 श्रीमाता** (śrī-mātā) — Two compound analyses are current: karmadhāraya ('the auspicious Mother') and ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa ('Mother of Śrī/Lakṣmī'). The meaning follows the first; the second is given in the samāsa note.
- **4 चिदग्निकुण्डसम्भूता** (cid-agni-kuṇḍa-sambhūtā) — Ablative ('arisen from') and locative ('born in') analyses of the last member are both current.
- **25 शुद्धविद्याङ्कुराकारद्विजपङ्क्तिद्वयोज्ज्वला** (śuddha-vidyāṅkurākāra-dvija-paṅkti-dvayojjvalā) — The identification of śuddhavidyā with the sixteen-syllabled mantra (and of the 32 teeth with it) is widely reported from the commentarial tradition; its exact source wording was not confirmed here.
- **26 कर्पूरवीटिकामोदसमाकर्षिदिगन्तरा** (karpūra-vīṭikāmoda-samākarṣi-dig-antarā) — The relation of samākarṣin to digantara admits two readings: the quarters draw in (are pervaded by) the fragrance, as analysed here, or the fragrance draws the beings of every quarter to Her, the usual paraphrase, which takes digantara by metonymy for those who dwell there.
- **32 रत्नग्रैवेयचिन्ताकलोलमुक्ताफलान्विता** (ratna-graiveya-cintāka-lola-muktāphalānvitā) — The exact form of the cintāka is not certain: it is taken as a gold collar-necklace or as the pendant (locket) of the jewelled necklace. On the second reading the analysis is 'the jewelled necklace with its pendant, and swaying pearls'.
- **37 अरुणारुणकौसुम्भवस्त्रभास्वत्कटीतटी** (aruṇāruṇa-kausumbha-vastra-bhāsvat-kaṭī-taṭī) — aruṇāruṇa may be read as 'red as the dawn' (upamāna, as analysed) or as the color word doubled for emphasis, 'deep red, red upon red'.
- **41 इन्द्रगोपपरिक्षिप्तस्मरतूणाभजङ्घिका** (indragopa-parikṣipta-smara-tūṇābha-jaṅghikā) — Translators take indragopa here either as the scarlet insects themselves, scattered over the quiver, or as red gems of their color set around it; the comparison of the calves to Kāma's quiver is not in doubt.
- **59 महापद्माटवीसंस्था** (mahā-padmāṭavī-saṃsthā) — mahā may qualify the forest ('the great lotus forest') or the lotuses ('the forest of great lotuses'); both readings are current and the sense is the same.
- **62 कामाक्षी** (kāmākṣī) — Several analyses are current: kāma as 'lovely' (the meaning given), as 'love, desire' dwelling in Her eyes, and the traditional kā + mā + akṣī (Sarasvatī and Lakṣmī as Her eyes).
- **68 चक्रराजरथारूढसर्वायुधपरिष्कृता** (cakra-rāja-rathārūḍha-sarvāyudha-pariṣkṛtā) — The compound can also be read with ārūḍha qualifying āyudha, 'equipped with all the weapons placed on the Cakrarāja chariot', parallel to names 69–70 where ārūḍha qualifies the following member. The meaning given follows the usual reading.
- **86 कण्ठाधःकटिपर्यन्तमध्यकूटस्वरूपिणी** (kaṇṭhādhaḥ-kaṭi-paryanta-madhya-kūṭa-svarūpiṇī) — The first part, kaṇṭhādhaḥ-kaṭiparyanta, can be analysed adverbially or as a bahuvrīhi qualifying an understood 'part of the body'; the relation to the head is therefore stated in sense (locative) rather than as one fixed samāsa type.
- **90 कुलामृतैकरसिका** (kulāmṛtaika-rasikā) — eka can be taken with kulāmṛta ('the kula-nectar alone') or with rasikā (eka-rasikā 'having one delight'); the sense is the same.
- **93 कुलान्तस्था** (kulānta-sthā) — The division kula-anta-sthā 'abiding at the end (or the inner part) of kula' is also possible; the reading antar + sthā is followed here.
- **97 समयान्तस्था** (samayānta-sthā) — As with name 93, the division samaya-anta-sthā ('abiding at the end of samaya') is also possible.
- **106 सुधासाराभिवर्षिणी** (sudhāsārābhivarṣiṇī) — sudhā-sāra 'the essence of nectar' is also a possible division; 'downpour' suits abhivarṣiṇī 'raining down' and is followed here.
- **109 महासक्तिः** (mahāsaktiḥ) — The division mahā + sakti 'great attachment' is also possible; maha 'festival' + āsakti 'attachment' is followed here because it explains the festive union of the traditional gloss.
- **113 भावनागम्या** (bhāvanā-gamyā) — Both divisions are traditional: bhāvanā-gamyā 'attainable through contemplation' and bhāvanā-agamyā 'unattainable through (mere) imagination'. The Devanagari is the same either way.
- **207 मनोन्मनी** (manonmanī) — The junction mano + unmanī is not regular sandhi and the analysis of this technical term varies (some read manas + unmanī as a karmadhāraya, 'the mind that has become unmanī'); the meaning follows the common yogic sense.
- **237 महाचतुःषष्टिकोटियोगिनीगणसेविता** (mahā-catuḥ-ṣaṣṭi-koṭi-yoginī-gaṇa-sevitā) — Whether mahā- qualifies the number, the yoginīs or their hosts is not decided by the grammar; the sense hardly changes.
- **258 स्वपन्ती** (svapantī) — By Pāṇini (7.1.80–81) the feminine participle of the class-2 root svap (svapiti) would be svapatī; svapantī follows the thematic inflection (svapati) found in epic usage. The text reads svapantī and the metre is the same either way.
- **264 सृष्टिकर्त्री** (sṛṣṭi-kartrī) — Pāṇini 2.2.15 restricts compounding a genitive with an agent noun in -tṛ; compounds like sṛṣṭi-kartṛ are nevertheless standard in usage and are usually explained as a general genitive (śeṣa-ṣaṣṭhī).
- **281 उन्मेषनिमिषोत्पन्नविपन्नभुवनावली** (unmeṣa-nimiṣotpanna-vipanna-bhuvanāvalī) — A bahuvrīhi ending in an ī-stem like āvalī would by Pāṇini 5.4.153 normally take the suffix -ka (…āvalīkā); the name keeps -āvalī, which is common in such poetic compounds.
- **303 हृद्या** (hṛdyā) — Two derivations are current: 'dear to the heart' (4.4.95) and 'being in the heart'; the meaning gives both.
- **309 रञ्जनी** (rañjanī) — The root allows two senses, 'one who colors (reddens)' and 'one who delights'; the meaning gives the second, the significance the first.
- **313 रमा** (ramā) — The earlier rendering added Sarasvatī; that rests on a commentarial derivation I could not confirm, so it is left out.
- **316 रतिप्रिया** (rati-priyā) — Bahuvrīhi ('to whom Rati is dear') and ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa ('dear to Rati') are both possible; rati may also be the common noun 'delight'.
- **320 रमणलम्पटा** (ramaṇa-lampaṭā) — ramaṇa can be the beloved (Śiva) or the act of love-play itself ('eager for love-play'); the meaning follows the first.
- **322 कामकलारूपा** (kāma-kalā-rūpā) — kāmakalā is analysed both as 'the digit of Kāma' (ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa) and as 'Kāma and Kalā' (dvandva).
- **328 कलालापा** (kalālāpā) — The fused ā allows kalā 'art' + ālāpa as well: 'She whose talk is of the arts'. The usual reading takes kala 'sweet'.
- **332 वामनयना** (vāma-nayanā) — vāma means both 'beautiful' and 'left'; the meaning takes the first.
- **342 क्षेत्रेशी** (kṣetreśī) — The name may mean 'ruler of the field' (ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa) or 'wife of Kṣetreśa, Śiva' (puṃyoga feminine); both are current.
- **345 क्षेत्रपालसमर्चिता** (kṣetra-pāla-samarcitā) — The story of the infant Kṣetrapāla is traditionally told on this name; I could not confirm its Purāṇic source, so it is not attributed.
- **351 वामकेशी** (vāma-keśī) — Read as a bahuvrīhi ('beautiful-haired') or as the consort of Vāmakeśa, Śiva; the meaning takes the first.
- **367 प्रत्यक्चितीरूपा** (pratyak-citī-rūpā) — The long ī of citī is not the regular form of citi; it is analysed here as a lengthening of citi (some take it as metrical).
- **368 पश्यन्ती** (paśyantī) — The bodily seat of each level of speech is placed differently in different texts (paśyantī at the svādhiṣṭhāna in some, at the navel in others); the sequence parā–paśyantī–madhyamā–vaikharī itself is undisputed.
- **370 मध्यमा** (madhyamā) — The bodily seat of each level of speech is placed differently in different texts (paśyantī at the svādhiṣṭhāna in some, at the navel in others); the sequence parā–paśyantī–madhyamā–vaikharī itself is undisputed.
- **374 कृतज्ञा** (kṛta-jñā) — kṛtajñā means both 'knowing what is done' and 'grateful'; both are given.
- **375 कामपूजिता** (kāma-pūjitā) — The allusion to the Kāmagiri pīṭha (reading 'worshipped at Kāma[giri]') is traditional but not in the grammar of the name.
- **392 श्रीकण्ठार्धशरीरिणी** (śrīkaṇṭhārdha-śarīriṇī) — For an exact half Pāṇini's rule (2.2.2 ardhaṃ napuṃsakam) puts ardha first; here, as often in later usage, it follows in a genitive compound.
- **405 शिवदूती** (śiva-dūtī) — The bahuvrīhi ('whose messenger is Śiva', as in the Devī Māhātmya story) and the ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa ('messenger of Śiva') are both current.
- **407 शिवमूर्तिः** (śiva-mūrtiḥ) — 'whose form is Śiva' (bahuvrīhi) and 'auspicious form' (karmadhāraya) are both possible.
- **410 शिवपरा** (śiva-parā) — 'devoted to Śiva' (bahuvrīhi) and 'beyond Śiva' (pañcamī-tatpuruṣa) are both current; the meaning follows the first.
- **411 शिष्टेष्टा** (śiṣṭeṣṭā) — iṣṭā may be from √iṣ ('dear to the righteous') or √yaj ('worshipped by the righteous'); a bahuvrīhi 'to whom the righteous are dear' is also given by some.
- **415 मनोवाचामगोचरा** (mano-vācām-agocarā) — Whether manovācām agocarā is one compound with the genitive retained or two words read as a single name is not settled; the meaning is the same.
- **422 सन्ध्या** (sandhyā) — The word is derived from sam + √dhyai ('meditation') or from sandhi 'junction' (sam + √dhā); both are traditional.
- **424 तत्त्वासना** (tattvāsanā) — A second analysis takes āsana as an agent noun from √ās 'to sit': tattveṣu āste 'She who abides in the tattvas'. The bahuvrīhi ('whose seat is the tattvas') is the more natural reading of the compound.
- **427 अयी** (ayī) — The sense of ayī is not settled. The common interjection of address is ayi with a short i; the long ī here is read either as that particle lengthened or as a feminine form, and the commentarial derivation could not be confirmed here. The rendering follows the usual reading as a call of address.
- **438 कुरुकुल्ला** (kurukullā) — The derivation of the name is obscure, and the place Kurukullā holds in Śrīnagara is described differently in the sources; the old note that she resides in the kuruvinda ruby could not be confirmed.
- **448 स्वस्तिमती** (svasti-matī) — The reading of svasti as 'imperishable being' is traditional; the exact source in the commentaries could not be confirmed here.
- **452 तेजोवती** (tejovatī) — The usual Pāṇinian derivation keeps the s of tejas before a possessive suffix (Pāṇini 1.4.19 tasau matvarthe), giving tejasvatī; tejovatī, with the s treated as a word-end, is the attested form of the name.
- **454 लोलाक्षीकामरूपिणी** (lolākṣī-kāma-rūpiṇī) — lolākṣī can also be taken as an epithet of the Goddess herself ('the restless-eyed one, whose form is Kāma'); the rendering follows the reading of lolākṣī as 'women'.
- **456 हंसिनी** (haṃsinī) — The possessive suffix allows several senses — 'having the haṃsas (great yogins) as Her own', 'having the haṃsa (swan or breath-mantra)' — and the tradition gives more than one.
- **460 नलिनी** (nalinī) — nalinī also means a lotus pond, and is the name of one of the streams of the Gaṅgā; which sense is primary is a matter of interpretation.
- **464 कालकण्ठी** (kāla-kaṇṭhī) — The name can also be read directly as a bahuvrīhi of Her own ('She whose throat is dark'), or with kālakaṇṭha in the sense 'whose throat is sweet-voiced'; the reading as Śiva's consort is the usual one.
- **466 क्षोभिणी** (kṣobhiṇī) — What She stirs is not named; the creative agitation of prakṛti is the usual reading, and agitation of the mind of Śiva or of devotees is also given.
- **469 वामदेवी** (vāma-devī) — Two analyses are current: the consort of Vāmadeva, followed here, and 'the lovely goddess' (vāmā devī).
- **472 सिद्धविद्या** (siddha-vidyā) — The reason the pañcadaśī is called siddha is given traditionally as above; the exact commentarial source could not be confirmed here.
- **476 आरक्तवर्णा** (ārakta-varṇā) — ā- can also intensify ('deep red'); the rendering 'rosy, reddish' is the usual one.
- **479 वदनैकसमन्विता** (vadanaika-samanvitā) — The order face-one is unusual. It is read here as a karmadhāraya with the numeral placed second (compare 487 and 496, where dvaya and traya are nouns, 'a pair', 'a triad'); the sense is not in doubt.
- **484 डाकिनीश्वरी** (ḍākinīśvarī) — Karmadhāraya ('She who is Ḍākinī, the sovereign') and ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa ('sovereign of the ḍākinīs') are both possible; the parallel names 494, 503, 520 and 534 (…ambāsvarūpiṇī) favor the first.
- **491 कालरात्र्यादिशक्त्यौघवृता** (kālarātryādi-śaktyaugha-vṛtā) — śaktyaugha requires the member augha, a by-form of ogha 'flood, host' (śakti + ogha would give śaktyogha); the sense is not affected.
- **526 हरिद्रान्नैकरसिका** (haridrānnaika-rasikā) — eka-rasikā may also be read as 'having a single taste, absorbed in one relish'; the sense is the same.
- **545 पुलोमजार्चिता** (pulomajārcitā) — That Śacī's worship secures Indra's rule is a traditional reading; no single source for it was confirmed here.
- **547 बन्धुरालका** (bandhurālakā) — The reading itself: this edition follows the verse text (bandhurālakā) where the old name list read barbarālakā; which of the two Bhāskararāya's text adopts could not be confirmed here. The sense is the same either way.
- **556 कात्यायनी** (kātyāyanī) — Purāṇic accounts differ on whether She was born in Kātyāyana's hermitage or first worshipped by him; the derivation from Kata is standard grammar.
- **572 पराशक्तिः** (parā-śaktiḥ) — Phrase or compound: the retained feminine parā and the verse's spacing point to two words in agreement (no samāsa); in later usage Parāśakti is also treated as a fixed compound title.
- **573 परानिष्ठा** (parā-niṣṭhā) — Phrase or compound, as with name 572: the feminine parā is kept, so the name is read as two words in agreement.
- **587 श्रीषोडशाक्षरीविद्या** (śrī-ṣoḍaśākṣarī-vidyā) — The composition of the Ṣoḍaśī (which syllable completes the Pañcadaśī, and where it is placed) differs between lineages; the significance gives the commonest account.
- **589 कामकोटिका** (kāma-koṭikā) — koṭi has several senses (portion, extremity, ten million), and the name is also read as 'She who dwells at Kāmakoṭi', the pīṭha at Kāñcī. The meaning follows the reading of Kāma as a part of Her.
- **590 कटाक्षकिङ्करीभूतकमलाकोटिसेविता** (kaṭākṣa-kiṅkarī-bhūta-kamalā-koṭi-sevitā) — kaṭākṣa may be taken in the genitive ('handmaids of Her glance') or the instrumental ('made handmaids by Her glance'); the image is the same.
- **603 गुरुमूर्तिः** (guru-mūrtiḥ) — Two compound analyses: bahuvrīhi ('whose form is the guru') and karmadhāraya ('the weighty, awesome form'). The meaning follows the first.
- **610 प्रतिपन्मुख्यराकान्ततिथिमण्डलपूजिता** (pratipan-mukhya-rākānta-tithi-maṇḍala-pūjitā) — tithimaṇḍala may be taken as the agent (instrumental, 'by the circle of days', i.e. their deities) or as the time (locative, 'on each of the days'); both readings are current.
- **612 कलानाथा** (kalā-nāthā) — Read as a ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa ('mistress of the kalās'); the sense 'She who is the moon' (kalānātha) is also current.
- **622 क्लीङ्कारी** (klīṅkārī) — Two analyses: 'maker of klīṃ' (upapada-tatpuruṣa) and 'She who is the syllable klīṃ' (klīṅkāra + feminine -ī); traditionally She is both its source and its form.
- **624 गुह्या** (guhyā) — Derived from √guh 'to hide' (gerundive) or from guhā 'cave' ('dwelling in the cave of the heart'); both are traditional.
- **626 त्रिपुरा** (tripurā) — Two derivations: tri + pura 'three cities' (bahuvrīhi) and tri + purā 'before the three' (the traditional reading). The meaning gives both.
- **630 त्र्यक्षरी** (try-akṣarī) — Which three syllables are meant is explained in more than one way (Oṃ, the Bālā mantra, the three kūṭas).
- **638 स्वर्णगर्भा** (svarṇa-garbhā) — The old meaning gave an interpretation ('the cause of the universe'); the literal bahuvrīhi and its echo of Hiraṇyagarbha are given here. Bhāskararāya's own gloss of this name was not confirmed.
- **639 अवरदा** (avaradā) — The derivation from √do 'to cut' is the traditional one; -dā could also be read from √dā 'to give' ('She who gives even to the lowly'). The name is avaradā, not varadā: the avagraha in the verse marks the elided a.
- **655 योग्या** (yogyā) — yogya means both 'fit, worthy' and 'to be attained by yoga'; the meaning follows the second, the significance mentions the first.
- **666 भूमरूपा** (bhūma-rūpā) — bhūman can mean 'the Infinite' (Chāndogya 7.23–24) or 'manyness, the aggregate'; both are current for this name.
- **674 ब्राह्मणी** (brāhmaṇī) — Several readings are current: 'of the nature of Brahman', 'predominantly sāttvic' (the brāhmaṇa as sattva), and the Śakti of Brahmā; the source of the sāttvic gloss could not be confirmed.
- **677 बलिप्रिया** (bali-priyā) — bali may also be read from balin 'the strong': 'She who loves the strong' (those strong in devotion and self-mastery).
- **683 शोभनासुलभागतिः** (śobhanā-sulabhā-gatiḥ) — Not a compound: the adjectives keep their feminine -ā, which they would lose inside a karmadhāraya (śobhana-sulabha-gati); the name is three nominatives in apposition, counted as one name. gati can be 'goal' or 'path'.
- **684 राजराजेश्वरी** (rāja-rājeśvarī) — rājarāja may be 'kings of kings' (emperors) or Kubera, the king of the yakṣas; both readings are current.
- **686 राज्यवल्लभा** (rājya-vallabhā) — vallabha also means 'lord, overseer' in some lexicons, which would support 'protector of kingdoms'; the bahuvrīhi 'to whom sovereignty is dear' and the tatpuruṣa 'beloved of the realm' are the plain readings.
- **695 दीक्षिता** (dīkṣitā) — The participle is passive ('consecrated'), but the name is commonly explained as 'She who initiates', taking dīkṣitā as 'possessed of dīkṣā'; both are current.
- **706 गुहाम्बा** (guhāmbā) — The fused ā allows guha 'Skanda' or guhā 'cave'; both readings are traditional.
- **711 साधु** (sādhu) — Bhāskararāya's own gloss of sādhu could not be checked against a printed Saubhāgyabhāskara; 'equanimity' (the earlier meaning) may reflect it.
- **712 ई** (ī) — The kāmakalā reading is the Śrīvidyā one; single-syllable lexicons also give ī as a name of Lakṣmī. Bhāskararāya's exact gloss could not be checked against a printed text.
- **714 कुलोत्तीर्णा** (kulottīrṇā) — kula has several traditional senses here — the senses, the Kaula path, the family of the knower–knowing–known; the reading as 'the senses' follows the earlier meaning.
- **715 भगाराध्या** (bhagārādhyā) — bhaga can be taken as the sun's disc or as the trikoṇa of Śrīcakra; the meaning follows the first.
- **725 दक्षिणामूर्तिरूपिणी** (dakṣiṇāmūrti-rūpiṇī) — The sense of dakṣiṇā in the proper name is explained variously ('facing south', 'gracious', 'skilled'); 'facing south' is the usual one.
- **728 चित्कला** (cit-kalā) — ṣaṣṭhī ('a digit of consciousness') and karmadhāraya ('consciousness as subtle power') readings are both possible.
- **733 नन्दिविद्या** (nandi-vidyā) — Which form of the mantra is meant by Nandividyā, and its place among the worshippers' vidyās, could not be confirmed against a printed commentary.
- **754 अपर्णा** (aparṇā) — The name has two analyses: apa + ṛṇa 'free of debt' (the meaning given) and a + parṇa 'without (even) a leaf', the classical name of Pārvatī.
- **785 मार्ताण्डभैरवाराध्या** (mārtāṇḍa-bhairavārādhyā) — The place of Mārtāṇḍa Bhairava in Her city follows the usual account; the exact enclosure could not be confirmed against a printed text.
- **786 मन्त्रिणीन्यस्तराज्यधूः** (mantriṇī-nyasta-rājya-dhūḥ) — Pāṇini 5.4.74 adds the compound-final -a to a compound ending in dhur (…-dhura); the name keeps the bare dhūḥ, a usage also found in epic and Purāṇic Sanskrit. Noted, not a text concern: the verse and the metre support dhūḥ.
- **790 परापरा** (parāparā) — Read as 'both supreme and non-supreme' (karmadhāraya) or as 'the intermediate, Parāparā' (the middle of the Tantric triad).
- **793 कपर्दिनी** (kapardinī) — The feminine of kapardin can mean 'wife of Kapardin' (the usual reading) or 'She who herself wears the kaparda braid'.
- **794 कलामाला** (kalā-mālā) — Ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa ('the garland of the arts') and bahuvrīhi ('whose garland is the arts') readings are both current.
- **796 कामरूपिणी** (kāma-rūpiṇī) — Three readings are current: 'taking form at will', 'having the form of Kāma/Kāmeśvara', and 'Goddess of Kāmarūpa'.
- **804 पुष्करा** (puṣkarā) — puṣkara has many senses (lotus, water, sky, the tīrtha Puṣkara); the gloss 'nourishing' rests on a traditional etymology from √puṣ rather than on regular grammar.
- **809 परात्परा** (parāt-parā) — Whether parātparā is an aluk compound or a two-word phrase written together is a matter of analysis; the meaning is the same.
- **815 अनित्यतृप्ता** (anitya-tṛptā) — The śloka's continuous text mūrtāmūrtānitya… could also be divided amūrtā + nityatṛptā 'ever satisfied'; the edited text marks the a of anitya with an avagraha, following the traditional division.
- **821 ब्रह्माणी** (brahmāṇī) — The traditional interpretation through 'brahma pucchaṃ pratiṣṭhā' rests on a derivation that could not be confirmed; the plain grammatical sense is given as the meaning.
- **838 मुकुन्दा** (mukundā) — The derivation muku 'liberation' + dā 'giving' is a traditional etymology (nirukti), not regular grammar.
- **839 मुक्तिनिलया** (mukti-nilayā) — The feminine ending admits two analyses: an adjectival ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa ('the abode of liberation') and a bahuvrīhi ('whose abode is liberation').
- **847 तलोदरी** (talodarī) — Two readings are current: 'whose waist is flat (slender)' and 'in whose belly are the talas, the lower worlds'.
- **855 गगनान्तस्था** (gaganānta-sthā) — anta can be read as 'limit' (beyond the sky) or 'interior' (within space); both readings are current.
- **860 अकान्ता** (akāntā) — aka 'pain, sin' is a rare word and the analysis aka + antā is the traditional one; the śloka's sandhi kāṣṭhā'kāntā fixes the initial a, so a reading kāntā 'beloved' is not available here.
- **875 त्रिपुरमालिनी** (tripura-mālinī) — As a proper name its internal analysis is not fixed; the vigraha given is grammatical, not a traditional gloss.
- **895 योनिनिलया** (yoni-nilayā) — Read as 'the abode of all sources' or as 'She who dwells in the yoni (the central triangle)'.
- **903 कल्या** (kalyā) — kalya has several senses (able, healthy, auspicious; also 'dawn'), and traditional glosses differ; the rendering combines the main ones.
- **912 सव्यापसव्यमार्गस्था** (savyāpasavya-mārga-sthā) — savya is mostly 'left' but is occasionally used for 'right' in ritual contexts; the name covers both paths either way.
- **916 धीरा** (dhīrā) — The sense 'giver of wisdom' depends on the traditional analysis dhī + √rā; the ordinary meanings are 'wise' and 'steadfast'.
- **923 दक्षिणादक्षिणाराध्या** (dakṣiṇādakṣiṇārādhyā) — Besides right-hand / left-hand ways, the pair is also read as the skillful and the unskillful (learned and unlearned); both readings rest on the same dvandva.
- **925 कौलिनीकेवला** (kaulinī-kevalā) — The internal relation (apposition, or a genitive 'the Absolute for the Kaulas') is not settled; both are traditional and the meaning combines them.
- **940 परामोदा** (parāmodā) — The usual division is para + āmodā; parā + modā is also possible, with the same sense.
- **945 वामकेश्वरी** (vāmakeśvarī) — The sense of vāmaka (the left-hand way, or 'beauty') is variously explained.
- **946 पञ्चयज्ञप्रिया** (pañca-yajña-priyā) — Which five sacrifices are meant (śrauta rites, the five mahāyajñas, or Āgamic five) differs between explanations.
- **971 सुवासिन्यर्चनप्रीता** (suvāsiny-arcana-prītā) — suvāsinī-arcana may mean worship OF suvāsinīs (the Śrīvidyā rite of suvāsinī-pūjā) or worship BY them; the first is followed.
- **972 आशोभना** (āśobhanā) — The verse string can be cut prītā + āśobhanā or prītā + śobhanā with identical sound; Bhāskararāya's division (āśobhanā) is followed.
- **976 त्रिपुराम्बिका** (tripurāmbikā) — Genitive ('Mother of the three cities') and appositional ('Tripurā the Mother') analyses are both current.
- **978 त्रिपुराश्रीवशङ्करी** (tripurāśrī-vaśaṅkarī) — Tripurāśrī as the fifth-enclosure deity and as 'the fortune of the three worlds' are both traditional readings.
- **983 त्रिखण्डेशी** (tri-khaṇḍeśī) — Trikhaṇḍā as the tenth mudrā, or the three kūṭas of the Pañcadaśī mantra: both are traditional.
- **985 अम्बा** (ambā) — triguṇāmbā can also be read as one word, 'the Mother of the three guṇas'; the 1,000-name count follows Bhāskararāya's two names.
- **994 आबालगोपविदिता** (ābāla-gopa-viditā) — The outer link of the avyayībhāva to the participle viditā is loose; it may also be read as an instrumental, 'known by (all) down to children and cowherds'.
- **996 श्रीचक्रराजनिलया** (śrī-cakra-rāja-nilayā) — The compound can also be read śrī + cakrarāja, 'the glorious king of cakras'; both name the Śrīcakra.
