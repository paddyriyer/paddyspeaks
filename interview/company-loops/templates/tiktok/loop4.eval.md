# TikTok · Loop 4 — evaluator notes (hidden from candidates)

These are simulated practice scenarios. The reference designs below are one
defensible answer each, not a description of how any company builds its systems.
Moderation is judged as a process only: never ask for, offer or accept examples of
content, and never reward a design that infers protected or sensitive traits about
people from videos, sounds, comments or behaviour.

## TTK-L4-01

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Records**: `report (report_id, comment_id, reporter_id, reason_code, reported_at)` — many per comment, never shown to the reported person; `case (case_id, comment_id, language, opened_at, sla_due_at, priority, score_version, state)` — one per comment per open period; `assignment (case_id, reviewer_id, offered_at, accepted_at, released_at, reason)`; `decision (decision_id, case_id, outcome, guidelines_version, score_version, reviewer_id, decided_at, supersedes)` — append-only; `appeal (appeal_id, decision_id, filed_by_role, filed_at, state)`.
- **Intake**: reports land on a partitioned log keyed by `comment_id`, so all reports for one comment are processed in order by one consumer. The consumer opens a case if none is open (conditional insert on `(comment_id, open)`), otherwise attaches the report and updates counts. Reports on a comment with a recent "keep" decision attach to the closed case unless a stated rule reopens it (for example, N new reports after the decision, or a new score version above a bar). The score arrives as its own event; a missing score sets a default priority and a flag, never blocks the case.
- **Queues and ordering**: one logical queue per language (and an undetermined-language triage queue with its own SLA and staffed by multilingual reviewers who re-route). Cases are offered by an effective priority that combines score band and SLA age (for example, priority boosted as `sla_due_at` approaches, with a hard rule that any case within X% of its deadline jumps ahead). Overflow: when a language's projected wait exceeds the SLA, cases are offered to qualified reviewers in other regions and shifts; staffing alerts fire on projected breaches, not on actual ones.
- **Assignment and well-being**: a reviewer is offered one case at a time by the assignment service, which counts high-severity cases per shift against the cap, inserts breaks on a schedule and after a run of high-severity cases, and honours a "step away" action that returns the case to the queue without penalty. Text is collapsed until the reviewer opens it. Well-being and quality are measured in aggregate (per queue, per language, per shift pattern), never as individual leaderboards; handle time is not a target.
- **Decisions to surfaces**: decisions are appended to the ledger and published on a decision stream; the comment service, LIVE overlays, notification sender and search index consume it and apply the outcome idempotently by `decision_id`. A reconciler compares recent decisions with each surface's state and re-applies any missing ones; freshness (decision to applied, per surface) is monitored against the one-minute target.
- **Appeals**: an appeal creates a new case linked to the decision, routed with a constraint that excludes the original reviewer (and, for stricter rules, their shift group); the outcome is a new decision that supersedes the old one, never an edit.
- **Versions**: each decision records guidelines and score versions. When guidelines change on a date, open cases are decided under the version in force at decision time (or a stated rule), and appeals of older decisions are judged against the version the decision used unless the rule says otherwise.

**Competing designs to accept.** A single global priority queue with language as a routing constraint instead of per-language queues. Automatic actions for a narrow band of cases, if the candidate adds a human review sample, a per-language kill switch, and a reversal path. Pull-based reviewer tooling, if the cap and breaks are still enforced server-side.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Report storms on one comment creating duplicate cases; one language flooding while others idle; starvation of low-priority cases until they breach; score version shifts that move the mix overnight; decisions that do not reach every surface; appeals routed back to the same reviewer; well-being limits that exist only in a policy document.

**Incident guidance.**
- *One language's reports rise forty times during a LIVE*: dedup already collapses many reports per comment; check that it holds. Raise that language's overflow to qualified reviewers in other shifts, triage by effective priority, and tell staffing early from the projected breach. Prevention: per-language surge alerts on report rate, and a pre-agreed overflow roster for events.
- *New score version doubles the high-severity share*: the cap works as designed, so high-severity cases stop being assigned; do not raise the cap. Roll back or recalibrate the score version for routing, compare the new version's band shares against the old on a shadow sample before switching, and add capacity planning per band. The version on each decision makes the affected cases findable.
- *Removals not reaching surfaces for three hours*: the reconciler (or a replay of the decision ledger from the deploy time) re-applies the missing decisions idempotently. Prevention: write the ledger and the stream in one transaction (outbox), and alert on decision-to-surface freshness per surface.
- *4% of appeals decided by the original reviewer*: re-route those appeals to a different reviewer and redo them; the exclusion must be a hard constraint in assignment, tested, not an effect of grouping. Report the error and its fix in the audit record.

**Follow-up guidance.** Push on the ordering rule (can the candidate show a low-priority case never breaches unless capacity is genuinely short?), on what makes the cap a system property, and on quality measures that do not rank people. If a candidate starts describing content, steer back to the process.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: one queue, reports treated as cases, no SLA logic, well-being mentioned as a policy only. 3: reports deduplicated into cases, per-language queues with SLA-aware ordering, decisions as an append-only log consumed by surfaces, appeals to a different reviewer, a cap enforced by assignment. 4: also overflow and projected-breach staffing, score and guidelines versions with a change procedure, freshness reconciliation per surface, aggregate quality and well-being measures, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Asks what a case is before designing queues; treats reviewer well-being as a requirement of the assignment step; keeps every surface downstream of decisions. **Red flags.** Asking for or giving content examples; individual reviewer leaderboards or handle-time targets; a design where a report storm creates thousands of cases; automatic removal with no reversal path.

## TTK-L4-02

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Ledger**: append-only entries with ids: `coin_purchase (purchase_id from the channel, viewer_id, coins, amount_minor, currency)`, `gift (gift_id generated by the client, viewer_id, session_id, creator_id, gift_type, coins, sent_at)`, `reward_accrual (gift_id, creator_id, reward_units, state pending)`, `reward_release` after {{hold_days}} days, `reversal (purchase_id, reason, received_at)`, `reversal_allocation (reversal_id, gift_id, coins, reward_units)`, `creator_adjustment` for already-paid rewards. Duplicate channel events are dropped by `(channel, event_id)`; duplicate gifts by `gift_id`.
- **Exact path (per viewer)**: the coin balance lives in a store keyed by `viewer_id` that supports conditional updates. Sending a gift is one conditional write: debit if `balance ≥ coins`, insert the gift row keyed by `gift_id` (in one single-partition transaction, since both are keyed by the viewer). Contention is limited to one person's devices, so this scales with viewers, not sessions. Retries with the same `gift_id` return the original result.
- **Creator side (asynchronous)**: gift rows stream (outbox from the viewer partition) into per-session aggregation. On-screen totals come from pre-aggregation: gifts for a busy session are counted in K shard counters or in short micro-batches, and the total is pushed to viewers at a fixed cadence (for example every 500 ms), so on-screen updates do not scale with gift rate. Gift animations are fanned out via the LIVE message channel with sampling or batching for a very busy session. The hot session is isolated by hashing `(session_id, shard)` across partitions.
- **Rewards and statements**: reward accruals are written from the same stream, keyed by `gift_id` (idempotent). After the session ends, the statement per session and day is computed from the ledger, and the on-screen final total is reconciled with it within a minute.
- **Reversals**: a refund or chargeback for `purchase_id` is matched to the gifts it funded by a stated rule (for example, spending consumes coins first-in-first-out by purchase, recorded at gift time as `gift_funding (gift_id, purchase_id, coins)`, so the reversal is a lookup, not a guess). Pending rewards from those gifts are reversed; paid rewards become a negative `creator_adjustment` on the next statement with the reason. Viewer balance: unspent coins from the purchase are removed; spent coins may leave the viewer's balance negative or the account restricted, by stated rule.
- **Money**: coins and reward units are integers; conversion to money happens once, per statement line, in integer minor units with a stated rounding rule.
- **Reconciliation**: daily and month-end, `coins sold − coins reversed = coins spent + coins outstanding`, and `rewards accrued − reversed = released + pending`, each checked from the ledger.

**Competing designs to accept.** A distributed transaction across viewer and creator records, if the candidate shows the throughput cost and why it is unnecessary. Reserving coins per session (a viewer moves coins into a session wallet, spent locally). A single-writer actor per busy session for totals. Allocating reversals by most recent purchase, if stated and applied consistently.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** One session's totals as a hot key; fan-out of gift animations to many viewers; double-spend across devices; duplicate channel events; late reversals after payout; on-screen totals that disagree with statements.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Busiest session's total partition four minutes behind*: shard the session's counters across partitions (or switch it to micro-batch aggregation) and push totals at a fixed cadence; the animation path is independent and keeps working. Prevention: automatic hot-session detection that splits counters before the lag builds, and a lag alert per session tier.
- *Refund events resent, rewards reversed twice*: reversals must be idempotent on `(channel, event_id)` and on `reversal_allocation (reversal_id, gift_id)`; repair by removing duplicate reversal entries (new compensating entries, not deletes) and notifying affected creators. Prevention: a uniqueness constraint and a daily check that reversed coins never exceed purchased coins per purchase.
- *Two devices both pass the balance check*: the check and debit were not one conditional write. Fix the write to be conditional (or serialise per viewer), find negative balances, and apply the stated rule (recover from future purchases, restrict sending). Prevention: a test that races two devices against the store.
- *Chargebacks arriving {{reversal_days}} days late in a batch*: process them like any reversal; paid rewards become negative adjustments with reasons; spread the batch's processing so statements are not delayed. Prevention: monitor reversal age per channel and size the hold period from observed reversal timing.

**Follow-up guidance.** Look for the insight that the per-viewer balance is the only exact contended record and that creator totals can lag. Push on what makes a retried gift harmless and on how a reversal finds the gifts it funded without guessing.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: one transaction updating viewer and creator for every gift; a single counter for the busy session; no reversal story. 3: conditional per-viewer debits with client idempotency keys, asynchronous creator aggregation, a ledger with holds and reversals. 4: also isolated hot-session aggregation with a fixed push cadence, a stated coin-to-purchase matching rule recorded at gift time, negative adjustments shown clearly, integer units with one rounding step, month-end reconciliation, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Finds the consistency boundary before choosing stores; designs reversals at gift time. **Red flags.** Floating-point money; a global lock per session; reversals that "subtract from the creator's total" with no allocation; showing creators information about viewers beyond the gifts sent in their session.

## TTK-L4-03

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Settings model**: `setting_value (account_id, setting_key, value, source ∈ {default, holder, parent_link}, locked, version, set_by, set_at)`. The effective value is resolved by precedence: a locked value from a parent-linked account beats a holder value, which beats a default; an unlocked parent-linked value is a suggestion the holder can change if this exercise's rules allow. Versions are server-assigned and monotonic per (account, key); devices submit changes with the version they last saw, and the server rejects or merges stale writes (compare-and-set), never resolving by device clock.
- **Defaults by declared age band**: a table `defaults (age_band, setting_key, value, effective_from)`; applied at account creation and when the declared age band changes. Band transitions are scheduled per account on the local date (a daily job per time zone, spread across the day, writing default changes only for keys whose source is still `default` or whose current value the new band forbids). No inference of age; the declared age on the account is the only input.
- **Sync**: devices hold the effective settings with versions. Changes are pushed when online (a notification that tells the device to fetch) and pulled on app start, reconnection and every {{sync_seconds}} seconds. On reconnection the device fetches settings before reporting usage or playing, so locked changes apply first.
- **Usage pipeline**: devices record intervals of foreground use `(device_id, interval_id, start, end)` locally and upload them, idempotent by `interval_id`. The server stores intervals per account and day (home time zone), computes the daily total as the length of the union of intervals clipped to the day, and serves it to devices. Late intervals update the union. After this exercise's retention period, intervals are reduced to daily totals; nothing records which videos were watched.
- **Enforcement on device**: the device computes `server_total + local unsent union` and enforces the limit and reminders locally. Offline for up to {{offline_hours}} hours, it uses the last server total plus its own time; the stated policy is to err towards stopping slightly early for under-18 defaults (or late, if argued). When the passcode screen appears, the passcode is checked against the parent-linked account's or holder's setting as this exercise's rules say.
- **Day boundary**: the account's home time zone with IANA rules; a 23- or 25-hour day on daylight-saving transitions is handled by computing the day's start and end instants from the zone, on both server and device using the same zone version. Home time zone changes take effect from the next local day.
- **Parent-linked view and audit**: the parent-linked account reads settings and daily totals only. Every change is an append-only audit entry `(account_id, key, old, new, source, actor, at)`, from which the settings in force on any day can be reconstructed.

**Competing designs to accept.** Server-side enforcement with a device fallback only when offline. CRDT-style settings with explicit precedence rather than server compare-and-set. Usage counted as minute buckets with a "any device active in this minute" bitmap per account, which approximates the union at minute grain.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Last write wins by device clock; summed rather than unioned usage; time zone and daylight-saving mismatches between device and server; band transitions at midnight creating a burst; offline devices that never learn of a locked change; usage data that grows into a viewing history.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Newest write by device clock overrides a lock*: switch resolution to server versions with precedence; restore the locked values from the audit log on all {{devices}} devices and notify the parent-linked account. Prevention: reject writes from a source that cannot override a lock, server-side, and a test with skewed clocks.
- *Usage backlog delays the server total by three hours*: devices should already enforce with their own local time, so the damage shows that enforcement depended on the server; make device-side enforcement the primary path, alert on ingest lag, and fire the evening reminder from the device's own schedule.
- *Sum instead of union*: recompute affected days from intervals with the union; restore limits that were hit early; tell affected accounts if their passcode was demanded wrongly. Prevention: a test case with overlapping devices in the pipeline's suite.
- *Daylight-saving day boundary disagreement*: both sides must derive the day from the same zone rules and version; ship the zone data with the app and log the zone version on each total. Recompute that day's totals from intervals.

**Follow-up guidance.** Push on the precedence rule under an offline stale write; on the union calculation with late and overlapping reports; and on what a parent-linked account can see. If a candidate drifts towards verifying or estimating ages, steer back: the declared age on the account is the input, and that is out of scope.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: settings as a key-value blob with last write wins; usage summed across devices; server-only enforcement. 3: versioned settings with precedence and locks, defaults by declared age band, usage as intervals unioned per account day, device-side enforcement with sync on reconnection. 4: also scheduled band transitions that keep explicit choices, consistent daylight-saving handling on both sides, minimal retention with no viewing history, parent-linked visibility limited to totals, an audit that reconstructs any day's settings, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Asks who may change what before designing storage; treats usage as intervals; names the data that must never be collected. **Red flags.** Device clocks deciding conflicts; inferring or estimating age from behaviour; giving parent-linked accounts viewing history; storing per-video usage for this feature.

## TTK-L4-04

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Eligibility**: `creator_eligibility (creator_id, valid_from, valid_to, eligible, reasons, recorded_at)` built from dated facts (declared age, follower counts per day, standing changes including backdated appeal outcomes, region). It is bitemporal: valid time for when the creator was eligible, recorded time for when the system knew, so a statement can be explained as of when it was produced. A video's eligibility is fixed at posting: `video (video_id, creator_id, posted_at, duration_s, eligible_at_post)`.
- **Qualified views**: view events `(view_id, video_id, watch_seconds, viewer_region, event_ts, received_ts)` deduplicated on `view_id`; a view qualifies if `watch_seconds ≥ {{min_seconds}}`, the video is at least 60 seconds and eligible at posting. Counts aggregate to `view_counts (video_id, view_date, viewer_region, qualified, invalid)`, with invalid views subtracted when the invalid-activity list arrives (as a separate column, so provisional and final can both be shown). The date is the view's event date in UTC (or the creator's zone, stated), and late views land on their event date in an open period or as an adjustment in a closed one.
- **Estimates**: per creator time zone, an incremental job processes new events and computes yesterday's estimate as `qualified × provisional rate`, ready by 09:00 local; time zones are processed as their day closes, not when UTC's does. Labelled "estimate".
- **Statements**: monthly, after the {{review_days}}-day review, a final snapshot `statement_line (creator_id, month, region, qualified_views, rate, amount_minor)` rounded once per line by a stated rule; immutable. Corrections after finalisation (late invalid activity, rate errors, backdated eligibility) produce `adjustment (creator_id, period_affected, reason, amount_minor)` on the next statement.
- **Traceability**: each line references the counts it came from; a creator can drill into views by video and day (counts only).
- **Viewer privacy**: viewer region is used only for the rate and aggregated; creators see counts by video and day and, where shown, broad regions above a minimum size; nothing identifies a viewer or infers viewers' traits.

**Competing designs to accept.** Recomputing open months fully each day if the candidate sizes it. Counting by the creator's local date instead of UTC, if consistent. Holding the whole month as provisional until review ends rather than showing daily estimates, if the candidate addresses the 09:00 requirement differently.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Joining {{daily_views}} views to eligibility intervals; late invalid-activity lists after finalisation; backdated standing changes; time zones whose 09:00 comes before the UTC day closes; estimates systematically higher than finals; rounding at several steps.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Invalid-activity list {{review_days}} days late, after finalisation*: do not edit final statements; compute per-creator adjustments for the affected month and place them on the next statement with a clear reason; communicate before creators see the negative line. Prevention: monitor the list's arrival against the review deadline and delay finalisation for a region when it is late.
- *Views under {{min_seconds}} seconds counted for two days*: estimates were wrong, statements not yet final; fix the rule, recompute the open days, and show creators a note explaining the drop. Prevention: a data test on the qualification rule and an alert on a jump in qualified-per-view ratio.
- *Eligibility computed before a backdated standing reversal*: bitemporal eligibility lets you recompute; restore the affected dates and credit the views in the open period or as adjustments. Prevention: eligibility recomputed on standing-change events, not only on a schedule.
- *Estimates late in far-east time zones*: process each time zone's day as it closes; the job is partitioned by creator time zone, not UTC day.

**Follow-up guidance.** Push on the final-versus-adjustment rule, on bitemporal eligibility, and on exactly what a creator can see about viewers.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: one daily job recomputing totals from all views; statements edited in place; no eligibility history. 3: eligibility intervals, deduplicated qualified counts at a clear grain, estimates labelled as estimates, immutable final statements with adjustments. 4: also bitemporal eligibility with backdated corrections, per-time-zone incremental estimates, one rounding step per line, full traceability, privacy-preserving viewer aggregates, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Separates provisional and final from the start; asks how corrections are shown to creators. **Red flags.** Editing paid statements; showing creators individual viewers or inferred audience traits; designing or speculating about how invalid activity is detected.

## TTK-L4-05

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Ledger**: `decision (decision_id, object_type, policy_area_id, taxonomy_version, region_at_decision, method ∈ {automated, human}, decided_at, recorded_at)`; `reversal (reversal_id, decision_id, cause ∈ {appeal, later_review}, decided_at, recorded_at)`; `appeal (appeal_id, decision_id, filed_at, outcome, decided_at)`. Append-only; `region_at_decision` captured when the decision is made and never backfilled from current data.
- **Definitions**: actions in quarter Q = decisions with `decided_at` in Q; appealed in Q = appeals filed in Q (or appeals of Q's actions, stated); restored in Q = reversals with `decided_at` in Q, counted once per decision (a decision restored by an appeal and later confirmed by a review is still one restoration). Each definition is written down and implemented once.
- **Freeze and publish**: the report for Q is a query over the ledger with `recorded_at ≤ cutoff`, stored as a versioned snapshot with its query, cut-off and taxonomy version. Appeals for Q's actions keep arriving for {{appeal_days}} days; the next report restates Q (old value, new value, reason) rather than editing the published file.
- **Taxonomy changes**: `policy_area_map (from_version, from_id, to_version, to_id, rule)`; one-to-one and merges map counts directly; splits use decision-level data (the decisions were re-labelled at review) or are shown as a combined series with a break note. Never guess an allocation.
- **Suppression**: compute the full table, mark primary cells below {{min_cell}}, then choose complementary cells (at least two suppressed cells in any row, column or published total that contains one, chosen by a heuristic minimising lost information), and verify with an audit that solves for each suppressed cell's feasible range from the published values; the range must be wider than a stated minimum. Alternatives: publish coarser regions, or ranges, for small cells.
- **One computation**: the files, tables and internal dashboards read the same snapshot; dashboards that show live numbers label them as live and differ by definition, with a reconciliation report.
- **Review**: a pre-publication check compares the quarter with the previous one per cell, flags large shifts, and records sign-off.

**Competing designs to accept.** Noise-based protection (differential privacy) with explicitly reconciled totals, if the candidate handles consistency. Restating in place with a change log, if downloads are versioned and old versions remain available. Counting restorations against the original quarter, if stated and shown consistently.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Double counting restorations with two causes; taxonomy splits mid-quarter; complementary suppression missed; backfilled regions changing history; dashboards and files disagreeing; restatements that are silent.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Restorations double counted by cause*: count distinct decisions restored; publish a correction in the next report with old and new values for that region, and an erratum note now. Prevention: a uniqueness test (one restoration per decision per quarter) in the pipeline.
- *Policy area split mid-quarter shown as three rows*: present the quarter under one taxonomy version: map pre-split decisions to the new areas using their review labels where they exist, otherwise show the combined area with a break note; explain the series change in the report. Prevention: taxonomy changes effective at quarter boundaries for reporting, whatever the operational date.
- *Suppressed cells recoverable*: withdraw and republish the affected table with complementary suppression, and run the feasibility audit on every table before publishing. Prevention: suppression as a solved problem across the table, never per cell.
- *Region backfilled from current account data*: restore `region_at_decision` from the original decision events (or the snapshot), rerun, and publish the restatement. Prevention: forbid backfilling time-of-event attributes from current state, enforced by schema (write-once columns).

**Follow-up guidance.** Push on precise definitions, on how a frozen quarter coexists with late appeals, and on whether suppressed values can be recovered. Keep the discussion on counts; never discuss examples of content or individual cases.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: a dashboard query run at publication time; per-cell suppression; restatements in place. 3: as-of snapshots from an append-only ledger, written definitions, restatements in the next report, a taxonomy mapping, a minimum cell size. 4: also complementary suppression with a recoverability audit, region and taxonomy captured at decision time, one computation feeding every output, a pre-publication review, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Defines each count before building; treats a published number as immutable. **Red flags.** Silent edits to published files; suppression that ignores totals; anything that publishes or exposes individual decisions or content.
