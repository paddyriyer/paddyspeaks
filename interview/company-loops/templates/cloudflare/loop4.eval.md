# Cloudflare · Loop 4 — evaluator notes (hidden from candidates)

These are simulated design scenarios. The reference architectures are one
defensible design each, not a description of how any company builds or operates
its network. Product rules quoted in the templates are the exercise's rules;
accept a candidate's sound alternative and check current docs before relying on
any real product behaviour.

## CFX-L4-01

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Sizing first**: {{edge_rps}} requests per second at a few hundred bytes per compressed log line is tens of GB per second before sampling. A strong candidate does this arithmetic and concludes that storing every line for every zone in the query store is the expensive choice, and that most of the volume comes from a few thousand zones.
- **Two products at the edge**: (1) *exact counters*: each location aggregates requests and bytes per (zone, hour) — and, if wanted, per (zone, minute, status class, cache status) — in memory and flushes them with an idempotent key `(location, zone, window, flush_seq)`; the central job sums them per zone and hour, and the hourly total becomes final after a lateness bound (3 hours), with late flushes after that recorded as restatements. (2) *sampled rows*: per (zone, location, short interval), the location picks a sample interval N from the zone's current rate (N = 1 below a threshold, so small zones stay exact), keeps 1 in N lines at random, and writes `sample_interval = N` on each kept row.
- **Transport**: locations buffer to local disk and ship batches to regional collectors over a durable log (Kafka-like), keyed by zone so a zone's rows land on predictable partitions; each batch carries `(location, batch_seq)` so retries are deduplicated.
- **Storage**: a columnar store (ClickHouse-like, Druid or Pinot are fine) with the raw sampled table ordered by `(zone, timestamp)` and partitioned by day, kept {{raw_days}} days. Rollups (materialised views or a stream job) per (zone, minute) and per (zone, hour) over the common dimensions (status, cache status, country, method, security action), each storing `sum(sample_interval)` as the request estimate and `sum(bytes × sample_interval)`, plus mergeable sketches: an HLL per (zone, day) for distinct client addresses and a top-K sketch (space-saving) per (zone, hour) for paths. Rollups kept 13 months.
- **Query planning**: the dashboard API picks the coarsest grain that answers the filter (hour rollups for 30 days, minute rollups for 24 hours, raw sampled rows only for rare filter combinations), and returns an `estimated` flag and an error bar whenever any contributing row had N > 1. For a count estimate from K sampled rows at interval N, a rough relative error is 1/√K; with K small the page should say "estimated, low confidence" rather than print a precise-looking number.
- **Top-N**: from the top-K sketch or from the minute rollup; the page shows ranks and approximate counts and never lists a path the sketch cannot vouch for (show only items whose lower bound exceeds the next item's upper bound, or label the list approximate).
- **Late data**: rows are placed by event time. A location that was cut off sends its backlog; minute rollups for those minutes are recomputed (or incrementally updated) and the dashboard shows that recent minutes are provisional until the lateness bound passes. The exact hourly totals carry a "final" state.
- **Isolation**: very large zones get their own shards or partitions, adaptive sampling raises N as their rate climbs, and the query layer enforces per-tenant concurrency and memory limits; ingestion back-pressure on one zone raises its sample interval rather than delaying everyone.
- **Reconciliation monitor**: per zone and hour, compare `sum(sample_interval)` from sampled rows with the exact count; alert when the ratio leaves a band that depends on K. This catches broken weights, dropped batches and duplication.

**Competing designs to accept.** Sampling at read time only (store everything for a short raw window, sample when querying) if the candidate costs it. Fixed sampling per zone tier instead of adaptive. A stream processor producing all rollups instead of store-side materialised views. Keeping small zones fully raw and only rolling up large ones.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Part explosion and merge pressure in the columnar store when one zone surges; unweighted aggregates after sampling; average-of-averages and averaged percentiles; top-N lists that are not monotone across grains; late backlogs that silently rewrite history; cardinality blow-up from paths and query strings.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Attack on one zone, other dashboards slow*: raise that zone's sample interval at the edge (adaptive sampling should have done this), move it to dedicated shards, apply per-tenant query limits, and let ingestion catch up. Prevention: back-pressure that degrades the surging zone's resolution, not everyone's freshness; isolation tests with a synthetic surge.
- *Location backlog dip then spike*: the dip was missing data, not missing traffic. Recompute the affected minutes from the backlog; show provisional markers for recent minutes and a "data delayed from some locations" banner driven by per-location lag; tell the customer the corrected chart is right and why. Prevention: per-location lag monitoring and lag-aware rendering.
- *Sample interval written as 1*: the reconciliation monitor (sampled estimate ÷ exact count ≈ 0.1) should have fired within an hour. Roll back the logger, then repair: for the affected window, rows from the bad release can be re-weighted if the true interval can be recovered (from the logger's config history per location and zone), or the window's rollups rescaled to the exact hourly totals with a recorded restatement. Prevention: the logger writes the interval it actually used, a canary location compared with exact counts before rollout, and alerts on the ratio.

**Follow-up guidance.** Make the candidate state what a sampled row multiplies (counts and sums by N, not distinct counts). On the 40-request path at 1 in 100, a strong answer says the estimate is 0 or 100 with huge uncertainty and the page should say so, or that small-rate filters should be answered from exact minute rollups when the dimension is kept. On adding a dimension, accept "new rollups from the change date, raw rows for the retained window, older history labelled unavailable for that dimension".

**Rubric anchors.** 2: one big table of every log line, averages and percentiles stored per location, no sampling story or an unweighted one. 3: weighted sampling with the interval on every row, exact totals separated, columnar rollups at several grains, late data by event time, some isolation for large zones. 4: also adaptive per-zone sampling that keeps small zones exact, uncertainty shown, mergeable sketches for top-N and distinct counts, a reconciliation monitor between sampled and exact, back-pressure that degrades only the surging zone, and precise handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Does the volume arithmetic before choosing storage; separates "exact because billed" from "estimated because explored". **Red flags.** Sampling without recording the rate; computing global percentiles by averaging; letting one zone's surge delay all ingestion; no notion of finality.

## CFX-L4-02

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Signals at the location**: each location keeps per-zone counters over short windows (for example 1-second buckets) of request rate and a few coarse distributions (status class, share of requests matching the traffic signatures it has been given, share from client networks new to the zone in the last week, cache status). It ships these per-zone aggregates every second to a regional aggregation tier.
- **Global per-zone view**: the aggregation tier sums location aggregates per zone over sliding windows (for example 10 s and 60 s) and publishes them on a stream keyed by zone. Late or missing location updates are marked; the decision uses what has arrived plus a completeness fraction, so one late location cannot block a decision.
- **Baselines**: per zone, an expected rate by time of week (for example a seasonal profile over the last four weeks at 5-minute grain, robustly estimated with medians) plus a spread (median absolute deviation). Periods with active mitigations, and periods the customer marked as known events, are excluded from the baseline so attacks do not inflate it. New zones or zones with low counts use a prior from similar zones and a minimum absolute threshold (with Poisson-style allowance for small counts) instead of a z-score that is unstable at low volume.
- **Detection separated from action**: an anomaly is "rate well above the baseline band". Action selection uses the shape of the traffic within the anomaly: whether one or a few signatures (given by upstream) explain most of the excess, how that traffic responds to softer actions, and the zone's sensitivity setting and allow rules. Actions are graded: log only → rate limit the matching signature → challenge → block, each with an expiry (for example 5–15 minutes, renewed while the signal persists). A surge that is broad across many signatures and normal in its response mix is more likely legitimate and gets the softest action or none.
- **Decision record**: `mitigation_id, zone, created_at, signal snapshot (rates, baseline, spread, completeness), signature, rule and threshold version, action, sensitivity setting in force, state transitions (proposed → active → renewed → expired | overridden by customer | overridden by responder) with actor and time`. Written once per decision and per transition to a durable, replicated store, retained {{audit_retention}}. Every mitigated request's log line carries `mitigation_id`, so "why was this request challenged?" is a join from the customer's own log line to the record. Per-request volume stays in the ordinary (sampled) request logs.
- **Customer view**: a timeline of mitigations per zone with the signal snapshot, the matched share, the action and the counts of requests by outcome (challenged, passed, blocked), plus an override button within their settings.
- **False positives**: no single label. Combine (a) challenge pass rates by mitigation (a high pass rate suggests humans were challenged), (b) customer overrides and support reports per mitigation, (c) downstream signals the customer exposes (for example error or conversion drops in their analytics, if offered), and (d) a sampled review of mitigations by responders. Each is biased (customers rarely report; pass rates depend on the action), so report them side by side and track them per threshold version.
- **Changing thresholds**: run new thresholds in shadow (decide and record, act nowhere) across all zones, compare would-have-mitigated sets with current ones and with known surge events, then roll out gradually by zone cohort with automatic rollback on override or report rates.

**Competing designs to accept.** A learned model for anomaly scoring if labels and bias are discussed. Per-location decisions combined with a global decision for very large floods, if consistency is addressed. Baselines per zone and path for large zones only.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Per-request audit writes that collapse under the flood; baselines poisoned by past attacks; unstable statistics at low counts; legitimate surges mitigated; late location data making the global sum low; customers unable to explain a mitigation.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Flash sale challenged for nine minutes*: give the customer the decision records, the signal snapshot and the counts of affected requests (from `mitigation_id` on their log lines), and the challenge pass rate, which was likely high. Fix: allow customers to declare scheduled events, weigh breadth across signatures and a high challenge pass rate as evidence of legitimacy, and shorten expiry or de-escalate automatically when pass rates are high. Record it as a false positive for threshold tuning.
- *Decision store dropping writes during a {{attack_rps}} flood*: the store was sized per request or shared with request logs. Recover what can be rebuilt (mitigated log lines carry `mitigation_id`, and the aggregation stream holds signal snapshots), mark the gap honestly in the report, and tell the compliance reviewer what is reconstructed and what is lost. Prevention: one record per decision and transition, a separate durable store with its own capacity, local buffering at the decision service, and an alert on write failures.
- *Baselines inflated by last week's attack*: exclude mitigated periods from baseline computation, recompute affected zones' baselines, and add a monitor that compares each zone's baseline with its pre-attack value. Prevention: robust estimators and an absolute ceiling on week-over-week baseline growth without customer confirmation.

**Follow-up guidance.** Keep the discussion at aggregates, baselines and decisions; if a candidate drifts into how floods are generated or how detection could be avoided, steer back — that is out of scope and should not be scored. Push on per-zone summation across locations, baseline hygiene, and an honest false-positive estimate.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: a fixed global threshold, block on volume, no audit trail. 3: per-zone baselines with seasonality, aggregation across locations, graded actions with expiry, a decision record. 4: also baseline exclusion of attack periods and low-count handling, `mitigation_id` on log lines with per-decision (not per-request) audit writes, false positives estimated from several biased signals, shadow rollout of threshold changes, and precise handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Treats a legitimate surge as the hard case from the start; designs the customer's "why" question into the data model. **Red flags.** Volume alone as the decision; per-request audit writes during floods; baselines that learn from attacks; discussion of evasion techniques.

## CFX-L4-03

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **API**: authenticate, validate (URLs belong to the zone, tags well-formed, prefix depth sane), apply per-account rate limits with a burst allowance, accept bulk requests (many URLs or tags per call), assign a purge id and a position in an ordered log, return `202` with the id. Idempotency on a client key so a retried call does not create a second purge. Status endpoint: per purge, locations acknowledged out of total and a completion time.
- **Purge log**: a durable, replicated log ordered per zone (or globally with per-zone sequence numbers). Each entry: `purge_id, zone, type (url | tag | prefix | host | everything), values, created_at, seq`.
- **Fan-out**: a distribution tree (core → regional relays → locations) with push for speed and pull for repair: every location knows the last `seq` it applied and asks for anything newer on reconnect or when it sees a gap. Locations acknowledge `(location, zone, seq)`. Upper tiers apply and acknowledge before (or no later than) lower tiers; alternatively lower tiers revalidate against upper tiers carrying the purge sequence.
- **Applying at a location**: *URL*: compute the cache keys for the URL under the zone's cache key rules and delete or mark every variant (keep a small index URL → variant keys). *Tag*: record `tag_purged_at[zone, tag] = t`; on a cache hit, compare the object's `cached_at` with the latest purge time of each of its (≤ {{max_tags}}) tags; stale ones are treated as a miss. *Prefix*: a per-zone trie of purged prefixes with times, checked on hit. *Host / everything*: bump a zone (or host) generation number that is part of the cache key or compared on hit. Lazy checks avoid scans; records can be dropped once older than the zone's maximum TTL, which bounds growth.
- **Unreachable locations**: a location that has not received purges up to the log head within a bound (for example a few seconds behind the last heartbeat) must not serve that zone's cached content as fresh: it either catches up before serving, or it revalidates with the upper tier/origin until caught up. A heartbeat carries the current head `seq` so a location knows it is behind.
- **Origin protection**: after large purges (everything, wide prefixes) use request coalescing per object at each tier and serve-while-revalidating where the customer allows it, so the origin sees one fetch per object per upper tier rather than one per location.
- **Completion measurement**: per purge, completion time = when the last location acknowledged its `seq`; the SLI is the p99 of that over all purges, plus the share of purges with every location acknowledged within {{target_seconds}} s. Synthetic probes request canary objects that are purged on a schedule, from every location, and measure when the new version appears — that checks the data path, not just acknowledgements.
- **Fairness**: queues per account with weighted fair scheduling; bulk API encouraged; a flood from one account fills only its own queue; customers see their queue position.

**Competing designs to accept.** Eager deletion with a local tag → object index instead of lazy comparison (with its memory cost). A global log instead of per zone. Short TTLs with revalidation as a complement for highly dynamic content.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Head-of-line blocking in a shared queue; tier order races; locations behind without knowing it; unbounded purge records; thundering herd at the origin after purge-everything; acknowledgements that do not prove the data path.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Location back after 20 minutes serves purged pages*: the location served cached content while behind. Immediately make it revalidate or catch up; fix the rule so a location that is behind the log head for a zone does not serve that zone's content as fresh. Monitor per-location lag and the age of the last applied `seq`.
- *Upper tier refills stale content*: purges must be applied at upper tiers first (or lower-tier refetches must carry the purge `seq` and upper tiers must refuse to serve objects cached before it). Purge again for the affected tag; add probes that fetch through lower tiers after a purge.
- *400,000 single-URL purges*: per-account fair queues so that account waits on its own backlog; rate limits with clear errors and guidance toward tag, prefix or bulk purges; capacity alerts on queue age. Communicate with affected customers whose purges were slow.

**Follow-up guidance.** Make the candidate explain how a far location applies a tag purge without a scan, and what bounds the purge records. On reconnection, insist on a rule that prevents stale-as-fresh serving. On measurement, look for acknowledgements plus independent probes.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: "send the purge to every server and delete", no ordering, no acknowledgements. 3: ordered durable log, fan-out with acknowledgements, lazy tag and prefix checks, a completion metric. 4: also catch-up rules for lagging locations, tier ordering, origin protection, bounded purge state, probes beyond acknowledgements, per-account fairness, and precise handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Asks how the cache key affects URL purges; spots the upper-tier refill race unprompted. **Red flags.** Scanning caches on every tag purge; counting a purge complete when it is sent rather than applied; no plan for unreachable locations.

## CFX-L4-04

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Inputs**: each request's log line carries zone, time, path (normalised to a route template or top-level path segment), country, verified-bot flag and category, bot score, model version, coarse decision label, and the action the customer's rules took (with rule id and version).
- **Aggregates**: per (zone, hour, model version) a 99-bucket score histogram, broken down by a fixed set of dimensions kept for every zone (verified-bot category, action taken, country, and top-N paths per zone with an "other" bucket). Stored in a columnar store keyed by (zone, time), kept for at least {{history_days}} days at hourly grain and longer at daily grain. Sizes are small: buckets × kept dimensions per zone-hour. Sampled raw rows (with sample interval) kept for a shorter window answer rarer questions.
- **What-if**: for a draft rule "score < X on path P (and other conditions)", if every condition is a kept dimension, sum histogram buckets below X over the matching cells for {{history_days}} days — exact over the aggregates. Otherwise replay sampled rows and report the estimate with an interval and the sample size. The result shows: matched requests per day, share of the zone's traffic, how much of it is verified-bot traffic (by category) and traffic from paths the customer marked as their own apps or partners, and the match rate by hour so quiet periods are visible. It states the period and method.
- **Rollout**: new rules start in log-only mode by default; a live panel shows match rate, challenge pass rate and requests by score band for the rule; the customer promotes it to challenge or block when satisfied. Rule versions are recorded so reports can attribute changes.
- **Model versions**: annotated on every chart; a version change publishes, for each zone with score rules, the before/after match rate of each rule computed on the same traffic scored by both versions (from a shadow scoring window) and notifies customers whose rules change materially before the new version takes effect for them. Rollout of a new version is gradual by zone cohort with a watch on rule match rates and support signals.
- **False positives for customers**: a "who scores low on my zone" view by path, user-agent family and client network type at aggregate level; an alert when a rule's match rate jumps on a path that was previously quiet; suggestions to exempt known API paths or authenticated traffic.
- **Global measurement**: across zones, track challenge pass rates by score band, overrides and exceptions added after a rule goes live, and sampled review — all biased labels, reported together, never as ground truth.
- **Privacy**: aggregates only in reports; no per-visitor profile; client addresses appear only in the customer's own logs under their retention.

**Competing designs to accept.** Coarser score bands (for example 10 buckets) to cut storage, if the customer's thresholds are aligned to them. Full replay over raw logs for the largest customers with a cost discussion. Automatic threshold adjustment on model updates, if opt-in and visible.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** High-cardinality paths in aggregates; what-ifs over unrepresentative periods; silent model-version shifts; verified-bot list lag; customers' own clients scoring low; treating rule actions as labels.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Model update moves many requests below {{threshold}}*: annotate immediately, offer a one-click way to pin the previous behaviour or put rules back into log-only, and contact customers whose rule match rate changed most. Prevention: shadow scoring with before/after match rates per rule, cohort rollout gated on those rates, and advance notice.
- *Customer's own app blocked*: help them add an exception for the app's paths or authenticated traffic and show what the exception lets through. Prevention: what-if over a chosen and representative period (show weekday/weekend and release markers), log-only first, and an alert when a new rule blocks a path that has high-volume traffic with consistent client characteristics.
- *Verified-bot list delayed*: allow the known crawler again, quantify which zones were affected and for how long, and tell them. Prevention: monitor list freshness, alert on challenge counts for verified categories, and fail safe (keep the last good list rather than an empty or stale-shrunk one).

**Follow-up guidance.** Keep it at the level of a score you are given; if the candidate starts designing detection internals or discussing how to avoid detection, redirect — not scored. Push on the what-if's method and uncertainty, and on how a model update is made visible.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: dashboards from raw logs, no what-if, no notion of model versions. 3: score histograms by zone and dimensions, a what-if with stated method, log-only rollout, model versions annotated. 4: also uncertainty for sampled what-ifs, verified and own-app impact shown, before/after rule match rates on model updates with gradual rollout, false-positive discovery tools, privacy limits, and precise handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Notices that a customer's tuned threshold is tied to a model version. **Red flags.** Rule actions used as ground truth; per-visitor tracking for reports; any evasion discussion.

## CFX-L4-05

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **At invocation end**: the runtime appends a fixed-size record to a per-process lock-free buffer (script id, version id, route, outcome, CPU ms, wall ms, subrequest count and status classes, storage operation counts, location, timestamp) and, if any, console lines to a separate bounded buffer. Overflow drops telemetry and increments a drop counter; it never blocks or fails the invocation.
- **Local agent per location**: aggregates per (script, version, route, minute): exact counts by outcome, subrequest counts by status class, and mergeable sketches for CPU and wall time (t-digest, DDSketch or HDR histograms). Flushes every few seconds with idempotent keys. Logs: keeps every error line up to a cap per distinct error signature per minute, and samples successes at a rate chosen from the script's volume and its customer allowance; each kept line records its sample rate, and the cap records how many were suppressed.
- **Central**: a stream merges per-location sketches and counts into per-script, per-version metrics (global percentiles from merged sketches); logs go to a search store partitioned by customer and day, retained {{log_days}} days. "No data from location L" is tracked as a state, distinct from zero requests.
- **Live tail**: a subscription service records which scripts are being tailed and with which filters; it pushes the subscription to every location (or to all locations running that script). Each location filters and rate-limits lines for that session and sends them to a regional relay that streams to the developer's WebSocket. When too fast, locations sample and the stream carries "N lines dropped in the last second". Several sessions on one script share one copy per location where filters overlap, or each is capped.
- **Gradual deployments**: metrics are always split by version; a comparison view and an alert compare the new version's error rate and CPU percentiles with the old one's on the same routes over the same minutes (a ratio test with a minimum count), so 10% traffic on a bad version stands out even when the blended rate barely moves.
- **Isolation**: per-customer log allowances enforced at the location (excess lines sampled harder, with counts), per-tenant partitions in collectors and the search store, and per-session caps in live tail.
- **Customer destinations**: the same agent output can be delivered to the customer's own log destination via a log-delivery job, sharing the collection path rather than collecting twice.

**Competing designs to accept.** Tail-based sampling at a central tier instead of at the location (more network cost). Full distributed tracing for subrequests if per-invocation overhead is addressed. Metrics derived from logs only for low-volume scripts.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Telemetry that blocks invocations; noisy-neighbour log volume; averaged percentiles; blended version metrics hiding a bad rollout; telemetry gaps shown as traffic drops; live-tail fan-in overloading a relay.

**Incident guidance.**
- *20 KB line per invocation at {{hot_rps}} per second*: enforce the customer allowance at the location (sample down, count the suppressed volume, tell the customer), isolate their partition in collectors, and restore other customers' tails. Prevention: per-customer limits at the edge rather than central, alerts on per-script log bytes.
- *Bad version on 10% of traffic*: alert on per-version error rate relative to the other version, not on the blended rate; offer automatic or one-click rollback of the gradual deployment when the new version's error ratio exceeds a bound with enough samples.
- *Location telemetry gap*: show "no data from N locations" on the chart and grey the affected period instead of plotting zero; alert on per-location telemetry freshness; backfill from the agent's buffer if it survived. The customer should not be led to roll back a healthy release.

**Follow-up guidance.** Push on how global percentiles are formed, on sampling weights when errors are kept preferentially, and on where live-tail filtering happens. Good candidates quantify the per-invocation overhead budget.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: ship every log line to a central store, compute metrics from logs, averaged percentiles. 3: edge aggregation with exact counts and sketches, sampled logs with recorded rates, live tail as a subscription, per-version metrics. 4: also non-blocking telemetry with drop counts, error-preserving sampling with caps per signature, per-customer limits at the edge, per-version alerting during gradual deployments, "no data" distinct from zero, and precise handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** States that telemetry must never fail an invocation and designs the buffer around it. **Red flags.** Synchronous log writes on the request path; averaging per-location p99s; one shared pipeline with no per-tenant limits.
