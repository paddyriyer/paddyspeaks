# xAI · Loop 4 — evaluator notes (hidden from candidates)

All figures in these scenarios are hypothetical, and every tier, limit, price,
multiplier and retention rule is this exercise's (check current docs). Score
designs on the criteria, not on resemblance to the reference; none of the
references describes how any company's systems are actually built.

## XAI-L4-01

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Limit model**: `tier_threshold(version, tier, min_cumulative_spend, effective_from)` and `tier_limit(version, tier, model, rps, tpm)`, versioned and append-only. A team's tier is computed by the billing side from cumulative spend against the threshold version in force and emitted as an event `(team, tier, threshold_version, effective_at)`. A compact snapshot (team → tier, tier × model → limits) is published to every region with a monotonically increasing version; each region's admission layer reports the snapshot version it enforces, and a monitor alerts when any region lags the newest version by more than a minute.
- **Requests per second**: checked at admission against the region's share with a short sliding or token-bucket window; cheap and exact locally.
- **Tokens per minute**: at admission, reserve `input_tokens + estimate(output)`. The estimate is not the requested maximum by default: use the smaller of the maximum and a per-team, per-model rolling percentile of actual outputs, with a floor. During a stream, settle incrementally (for example every N tokens or every few seconds), so long streams release unused reservation early and count tokens in the minute they were produced. At completion settle to the exact count; an underestimate is charged into the current window (the overshoot this allows is part of the stated bound).
- **Across regions**: each (team, model) limit is split into regional shares. A coordinator rebalances every 1–5 seconds from recent per-region demand, lowering a giver's share before raising a receiver's so the sum never exceeds the limit; a minimum share per active region avoids starvation. When a region cannot reach the coordinator it keeps its last share (overshoot bounded by zero) or decays toward an equal split; the candidate should state the bound and its cost (a team concentrated in a partitioned region is under-served).
- **Upgrades**: the tier event triggers an immediate snapshot delta push (not waiting for a periodic refresh); regions acknowledge the version. Time to enforce = acknowledgement time − spend event time, measured per upgrade and alerted against {{upgrade_minutes}} minutes.
- **429s**: the response names the limit (rps or tpm), the current limit, and a retry hint computed from the window state: for rps the time until the window frees a slot; for tpm the time until enough settled tokens leave the minute window, made conservative because in-flight reservations may settle later, plus jitter so clients do not return together. Overload refusals (the model's serving capacity) use a different status or error code, are not counted against the team's limit, and are recorded separately.
- **Records**: every rejection logs team, model, region, limit type, tier, snapshot version, share at the time and the hint returned; aggregated per minute for teams' headroom view and for analysis of rejections by limit type.
- **Headroom view**: per model, limit, current use in the last minute and peak minute, rejections by type, with the same burst semantics as enforcement.

**Competing designs to accept.** A single strongly consistent counter service per team placed near the team's dominant region, with local fallback (accept if latency and partition behaviour are addressed). Generic cell-based rate limiting with periodic gossip of counts (accept with a stated overshoot bound). Charging tokens per minute only at completion without reservations (accept if the candidate shows the overshoot it allows and bounds concurrency instead).

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** A central counter on the request path; reservations sized by the maximum output locking budget; settlement only at stream end counting a long stream's tokens into one minute; stale tier caches with no version reporting; retry hints that are too short or identical for all clients; overload refusals counted as the team's own rejections, which sends teams to buy a higher tier that would not help.

**Incident guidance.**
- *One region misses the threshold version*: the regional snapshot consumer failed and nothing compared enforced versions. Immediately push the snapshot to that region, confirm the acknowledged version, and tell the 1,200 teams their limits are now in force; check rejections those teams received meanwhile and treat them as the platform's fault in any communication. Prevention: per-region enforced-version metric with an alert, and an end-to-end time-to-enforce SLO measured per upgrade.
- *Lockstep retries after a one-second hint*: the hint ignored the tokens-per-minute window. Fix the hint calculation (time until enough tokens leave the window) with jitter, return the limit type, and coordinate a client library fix; in the meantime, short-circuit repeated rejections cheaply so they do not consume admission capacity. Prevention: a test that compares the hint to the observed time to the next admitted request.
- *Throttled at 35% of TPM by teams' own count*: reservations sized by maximum output and released only at stream end. Switch the estimate to a per-team rolling percentile, settle incrementally during streams, and show reserved versus used tokens in the headroom view. Prevention: track the reserved-to-used ratio per team and model.

**Follow-up guidance.** For the 2,000-input / 8,000-maximum stream, a strong answer reserves 2,000 plus an estimate (not 8,000), releases unused reservation as the stream progresses, and settles exactly at the end. For concentrated traffic, look for a quantified rebalance interval and the effective limit the team sees until it converges. On the hint, credit conservative plus jitter.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: one central counter, tokens counted only at completion, a cached tier with no versioning, a fixed retry hint. 3: reservations with settlement, regional shares with rebalancing, versioned tier snapshots, 429s that name the limit. 4: also incremental settlement for streams, a stated cross-region overshoot bound and partition behaviour, measured time to enforce per upgrade, overload separated from own-limit rejections, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Notices immediately that output tokens are unknown at admission; states the overshoot bound; measures upgrade propagation instead of assuming it. **Red flags.** A synchronous cross-region call per request; reserving the maximum output by default; telling teams to retry after a constant; counting overload refusals against a team.

## XAI-L4-02

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Layout**: shards by hash of post id within time partitions. The newest partition (the last few minutes) is an in-memory index rebuilt or appended continuously from the ingestion stream; older data lives in immutable segments per hour, compacted into per-day segments; partitions older than {{searchable_days}} days are dropped whole. Each partition holds, per post, the latest version only (an edit replaces the entry by post id; the older entry is masked until compaction).
- **Query path**: the tool front end parses the query and time range, selects only the partitions that overlap the range, and fans out to the shards of those partitions. Each shard retrieves candidates (lexical postings, optionally an approximate vector index; the candidate should say which and why), evaluates the given scoring function on its candidates using the match score, recency against the query time and the post's aggregate engagement counts, and returns its top k with `(score, post_id, version)`. The merger takes the global top k with ties broken by post id (a deterministic total order), so identical calls against the same index version return the same list.
- **Deletions**: a deletion event writes a tombstone `(post_id, deleted_at)` into a small, replicated deletion set in every serving region (memory-resident, sized by deletions within the window). Every result from every path (shard, replica, cache) is filtered against the tombstone set at the merger just before returning; physical removal happens at compaction. Replicas expose the deletion-stream offset they have applied; the merger refuses results from a replica whose deletion offset lags beyond the 30-second bound (or filters with the front end's own up-to-date set, which is the simpler design).
- **Caching**: cache candidate lists for popular (query, time-range bucket, filters) keys with short TTLs, keyed also by index version; the serve-time tombstone filter still runs on cache hits, so a cached list can never return a deleted post. Results are shared across teams only when no team-specific filter or setting changes them.
- **Latency**: per-shard deadlines (for example 60% of {{budget_ms}} ms), hedged requests to a second replica for the slowest shards, and results returned with `partial: true` and the missing partitions named when a deadline passes. The model can then say its search was incomplete.
- **Hot partitions**: the newest partitions get more replicas and are split across more shards; recent-window candidate lists for trending queries are cached briefly; routing is load-aware.
- **Logging**: per call, the query hash, time range, partitions searched, per-shard latency, deadline misses, result ids and versions, scoring-function version and outcome; no post text, no author identities in reports.

**Competing designs to accept.** A single global index sharded by term rather than by post (accept with the hot-term trade-off explained). Pure vector retrieval with a lexical re-check (accept if recall is measured). Deleting physically and synchronously instead of tombstones (accept if the candidate shows how immutable segments and caches meet the 30-second bound).

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Caches keyed without any deletion awareness; replicas lagging the deletion stream; the newest partition taking most of the traffic; waiting for every shard; nondeterministic ties producing different citations for identical calls; logs that capture post text.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Cached list cites a deleted post for 20 minutes*: serve-time filtering was skipped for cache hits. Purge cached entries containing recently deleted ids, apply the tombstone filter on every path, and list the calls that returned the post so its citations can be reported. Prevention: a test that deletes a post and asserts no path returns it after the bound, run continuously with synthetic posts.
- *Newest partition hot after an event breaks*: scale out replicas of the newest partitions (pre-provisioned for this pattern), split the newest partition across more shards, briefly cache candidate lists for the top recent queries, and shed with partial results rather than timeouts. Prevention: capacity for the newest partitions sized for event peaks, not the average.
- *Slow shard after a disk fault*: introduce per-shard deadlines and hedging; return partial results marked as such; move the shard's replicas off the faulty disk. Prevention: tail-latency alerts per shard and a deadline on every fan-out.

**Follow-up guidance.** For the walk-through, expect a budget split (parse, partition selection, fan-out, scoring, merge, tombstone filter, serialisation). On determinism, credit a total order on (score, post id) and pinning the index version per call. On scoring-function rollout, look for shadow scoring, comparing rankings, and recording the function version on each call and citation.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: one big index with a delete API and a cache keyed by query; no latency budget. 3: time partitions, pruning, given score applied per shard with top-k merge, tombstones, deadlines. 4: also deletion checks on every path with replica offsets, deterministic ties and version pinning, hot-partition strategy, partial results surfaced to the model, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Treats deletion as an invariant on every read path; budgets the latency step by step; makes partial results explicit. **Red flags.** "Invalidate the cache when a post is deleted" with no way to find the entries; waiting for all shards; logging post text; ranking by anything other than the given function.

## XAI-L4-03

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Usage fact**: one row per request (and per tool call within it) with a deterministic id from the request id and line type, team, key, model, region, endpoint type (global or regional), batch id, input and output tokens, tool calls, served_at, and the pricing applied at write time: price version, batch discount, tool-call charge, regional multiplier version, and the amount in integer minor units (or a fixed-point sub-cent unit with one stated rounding step). Ingestion is idempotent on the row id.
- **Dating rule**: one rule for batch usage (for example completion time of each request), applied in the console, tier progress and the invoice alike.
- **Provisional and closed**: hourly and daily aggregates per team and dimension are recomputed while a period is open; at month close the period is frozen, the invoice is produced from the same rows, and any later correction becomes a dated adjustment line in the next period. Each number on the console carries a status (provisional or invoiced) and an "as of" time.
- **Tier progress**: read from the tier engine's own definition (here paid spend), shown as "toward next tier: X of Y paid" with accrued-but-unpaid spend shown separately and explained; never computed independently by the console.
- **Headroom**: read from the rate-limiting system's per-minute aggregates (limits, use, rejections by type), labelled as near real time and not used for money.
- **Breakdowns**: computed from the same fact rows, grouped by the dimension; every breakdown sums to the total because it is the same rows. Credits and refunds are their own lines.
- **Tie-out**: a nightly and month-close job that compares, per team, the sum of fact rows for the period with the invoice total and alerts on any difference.
- **Isolation**: the team scope comes from the authenticated session and is applied in the data access layer (row-level policies or a scoped query service), not by each view; tests try to read another team's rows. Exports use the same scoped path.
- **Freshness**: stream lag and aggregate age measured and shown; the page says "delayed" when beyond {{fresh_minutes}} minutes.

**Competing designs to accept.** Pricing at aggregation time from versioned prices joined by served time (accept if the version is chosen per row and the invoice uses the identical computation). A separate billing warehouse feeding the console with a published reconciliation (accept if the console never computes money differently). Lambda-style real-time plus batch correction (accept if provisional versus final is visible).

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Two definitions of spend in two systems; applying a price or multiplier version to a whole month; floating point; breakdowns computed from different sources than totals; tenant filters added per query; stale numbers shown as current.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Accrued versus paid spend*: the console used its own definition. Change the console to read tier progress from the tier engine and show accrued spend separately; contact the 600 teams with the explanation and when their tier will change. Prevention: one shared definition, owned by the tier engine, and a reconciliation that compares console tier progress with actual tier events.
- *Multiplier applied to the whole month*: the console joined to the current multiplier rather than the version in force at served time. Recompute provisional numbers with per-row versions; confirm the invoice used the correct rule; tell affected teams which number was wrong. Prevention: pricing recorded on each row at write time and a tie-out test across a version boundary.
- *Three-hour lag shown as current*: scale the consumers, process the backlog idempotently, and show the real as-of time immediately. Prevention: freshness as a visible field with an alert when lag exceeds {{fresh_minutes}} minutes.

**Follow-up guidance.** In the walk-through, expect three lines for the request (tokens, two tool calls), each priced once with the regional multiplier version. For the month boundary, any rule is fine if it is stated once and applied in all three places. For continuous proof, look for a per-team tie-out with an owner and a correction path.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: a dashboard over raw events with prices applied in queries and no reconciliation. 3: one priced fact in minor units, provisional versus closed, breakdowns from the same rows, scoped access. 4: also tier progress and headroom from their enforcing systems with clear labels, a single batch dating rule, version-correct pricing across mid-month changes, continuous tie-out, visible freshness, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Asks which spend definition moves a tier before designing; insists that money is computed once. **Red flags.** Floating-point money; "the dashboard is approximately right"; computing tier progress in the console; per-view tenant filters.

## XAI-L4-04

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Citation record** (content-free outside the opt-in): citation id, response id hash, team hash (for opt-in checks only, never reported), tool, model version, created_at, source type (web or post), canonical URL or post id and version, span offsets, support score, scorer version, threshold version. Response text and span text are kept only for opted-in teams and the probe set, with a retention limit.
- **Link checking**: a URL table keyed by canonical URL with first_cited, last_cited, last_checked, status, status history and checker version. A scheduler checks new URLs within hours and rechecks every {{recheck_hours}} hours while they are still cited. The fetcher sends HEAD (falling back to a ranged GET for status only), follows redirects, honours the site's crawling rules, applies a per-domain rate limit and a per-domain concurrency cap, and keeps no body. Statuses: live, gone (404/410), redirected to a different page, soft-gone (redirect of many paths to the home page, detected by comparing redirect targets across a domain's URLs), transient (5xx, timeout, retried with backoff), blocked (403/429 or crawl rules disallow). Only "gone" and "soft-gone" count as dead.
- **Post citations**: the deletion stream marks citations whose post was deleted as "source removed", a separate status; posts are never re-fetched.
- **Support score**: computed in the request path where the text is available; the number, scorer version and threshold version are recorded. For the probe set, any scorer version can be rerun at any time, which is how a scorer change is separated from a citation change.
- **Probe set**: {{probe_queries}} fixed synthetic queries run daily per tool and model; their citations are fully recorded. Movements in probe metrics with unchanged queries point at tools, models, sources on the web, or the scorer, and can be split by rerunning old scorer versions.
- **Trends and alerts**: daily per tool, model, scorer version and checker version; dead-link rate, source-removed rate, unsupported share; alerts on change points compared with the same weekday, and annotations for every scorer, checker or canonicalisation release.
- **Review sample**: weekly {{sample_size}} citations from the probe set and opt-in traffic, stratified by tool, model and score band, with minimum allocations for rare strata; reviewers see the span and the source passage with written guidance, judge support only, and agreement between reviewers is measured; results are weighted back to the population.

**Competing designs to accept.** A model-based judge in place of human review (accept if it is versioned, calibrated against a human sample, and its changes are separated from citation changes). Checking only a sample of URLs rather than all (accept with a stated error bound). Computing scores asynchronously over a short-lived buffer (accept if the buffer's retention is explicit and opt-in rules hold).

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Mixing scorer or checker changes with real changes; treating blocked or transient fetches as dead; overloading small sites; keeping response text without consent; random samples that never reach rare tools; reporting per team.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Dead-link rate 2% → 15%*: annotate the canonicalisation release; rerun a sample of "dead" URLs with the original cited form; roll back or fix the rule (keep parameters a site needs); recheck the affected URLs and restate the trend. Prevention: canonicalisation changes tested against a fixed URL set that compares statuses before and after, and the checker version on every status.
- *Scorer change doubles unsupported share*: split the trend by scorer version; rerun both scorers on the probe set to measure the shift; correct and reissue the weekly report with a note; decide whether to recalibrate the threshold. Prevention: shadow-score new scorer versions on the probe set before release and keep series per version.
- *Small site blocks the checker*: mark its citations "blocked", not dead; back off sharply and respect its signals; the per-domain cap was too high for bursts from one popular response. Prevention: per-domain budgets that do not scale with citation count, and deduplicated checks per URL.

**Follow-up guidance.** For "unsupported doubles overnight", the fastest answer reruns old and new scorer versions on the probe set. For the model team's request for all text, a strong answer offers the probe set, opt-in samples and content-free aggregates, and declines the rest.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: stores every response and runs a crawler and a judge over it. 3: content-free records, polite status-only checks with distinct statuses, scorer versions, a probe set. 4: also trends split by scorer and checker version with release annotations, stratified and reweighted human review with agreement measured, deletion handled as its own status, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Asks what may be kept before designing; separates instrument changes from real changes. **Red flags.** Keeping customer responses by default; a crawler with no rate limits; treating 403 as dead; reporting individual teams.

## XAI-L4-05

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Catalogue**: `region(id, status)`, `region_model(region, model_version, available_from)`, `multiplier(version, region, value, effective_from)`, all versioned. Team settings: `residency(team, region | none, version)` and `failover(team, mode: fail | allowed_regions[], version)`.
- **Routing**: at the edge, the request's team settings and endpoint type resolve to an ordered list of allowed regions (a regional endpoint or a pinned team: exactly that region plus any regions the team listed for failover; a global endpoint for an unpinned team: any region offering the model). The list travels with the request; every downstream hop (load balancer, serving fleet, log shipper) refuses to act outside it. The serving region writes its id onto the usage record.
- **Model availability**: if the requested model version is not offered in an allowed region, the request is refused with a clear error naming the regions that offer it; it is never silently routed elsewhere.
- **Failover**: health signals per region and model (error rate, latency, capacity) with automatic declaration and human override. On declaration, teams with allowed regions move within {{failover_minutes}} minutes; receiving regions apply admission limits and per-team caps proportional to normal use so the influx cannot exhaust a model's capacity; teams with "fail" get fast, explicit errors and a status page entry.
- **Data placement**: request logs, usage records and traces stay in the serving region's stores. Only aggregates (per team, model and hour: requests, tokens, cost) are exported for global billing and capacity views. Debugging tools run in region; cross-region copies of request-level data for pinned teams are blocked by policy and storage permissions, not convention. Backups stay in region or in an allowed region.
- **Billing**: each usage record carries the serving region, endpoint type and the multiplier version in force at served time ({{multiplier}} times base for regional endpoints in this exercise); the invoice sums records. A request admitted in one region and served in another after a retry is billed by the serving region.
- **Verification**: a continuous job compares each usage record's serving region with the team's allowed list at served time; any violation alerts within an hour, is reported to the team with counts and times, and triggers a billing correction.

**Competing designs to accept.** Separate regional deployments with no global endpoint for pinned teams (simpler, accept with the operational cost). DNS-based routing with region-specific hostnames (accept if enforcement does not rely on DNS alone). Active-passive failover per region pair (accept with the capacity reserve it needs).

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Enforcing residency only at DNS; central logging; backups copied out of region; failover without admission control; billing by admission region rather than serving region; models that are silently served from another region.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Failover overloads the receiving region*: cap admitted failover traffic per team and model, shed the excess with explicit errors, and add a second receiving region where teams allowed it; tell pinned teams with "fail" when the region will return. Prevention: capacity reserves sized for the largest region's failover-eligible traffic, and failover tested regularly.
- *Request logs copied out of region*: delete the copies and confirm deletion in every store and backup; tell the 30 pinned teams what was copied, for how long and who could access it; block cross-region copies of request-level data by policy. Prevention: in-region debugging tools and storage permissions that make the copy impossible.
- *Routing change sends 3% of a pinned team's requests elsewhere*: roll back the configuration, quantify affected requests from serving-region fields, correct billing to the right multiplier, and tell the team. Prevention: the allowed-region list enforced at every hop, a pre-deploy check of routing changes against residency settings, and the hourly verification job.

**Follow-up guidance.** For the midnight multiplier change, any stated rule (for example the version in force when the request started) is acceptable if it is recorded per record and applied by the invoice. For the monthly proof, look for a report built from serving-region fields and the verification job's results.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: geo-DNS routing, central logs, billing by endpoint name. 3: allowed-region lists enforced in serving, in-region logs with aggregates exported, serving region and multiplier version on every usage record. 4: also failover by team policy with admission control in receiving regions, enforcement at every hop including debugging and backups, continuous violation detection with team reporting, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Treats residency as an invariant enforced by construction; records the serving region rather than inferring it. **Red flags.** "Logs are global for convenience"; failover that ignores team policy; billing that cannot say which region served a request.
