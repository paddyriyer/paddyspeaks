# Uber · Loop 4 — evaluator notes (hidden from candidates)

## UBR-L4-01

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Definitions first**: for interval [t, t+30 s) and cell c, *open supply* = distinct drivers whose latest known state at the end of the interval is "open" and whose latest position falls in c; *soon-available supply* = drivers on trip whose drop-off is in or near c and whose estimated trip end is within a few minutes (the trip's destination and estimate come from the trip service, not from the ping); *demand* = requests created in c in the interval, plus app opens with a destination entered (intent), kept as separate measures so pricing and analysts can weigh them. Each definition carries a version.
- **Ingestion**: driver pings go to a durable log partitioned by driver id (so a driver's pings are ordered and processed by one worker), in the region that serves the city. Rider requests and app opens go to a separate stream partitioned by city or cell.
- **Stage 1, driver state**: a keyed state store per driver holds the latest (session, sequence, position, status, event time). Pings with a sequence at or below the stored one are duplicates or stale and are ignored for current state (but kept in the raw log). Each driver's state is mapped to a cell (finest resolution) at the moment of the ping; a position with poor accuracy can be held at its previous cell.
- **Stage 2, cell aggregation**: every 30 seconds, take a snapshot of all driver states and count distinct drivers per (cell, state); join demand counts for the same interval. Counting from current state rather than from pings makes ping rate irrelevant. Exact counts are cheap here: a cell holds at most a few hundred drivers, so no approximate distinct counting is needed.
- **Sparse cells**: compute smoothed values over the cell and its first ring (k = 1) with weights, or roll up to the parent cell when counts fall below a minimum; respect boundaries with a reference layer of barriers and special zones (airports, waiting lots) where smoothing is not applied across the boundary.
- **Lateness**: the interval closes a few seconds after its end (a small allowed lateness, e.g. 5 seconds); later pings update driver state for the next interval but do not reopen a published one. Every published map carries the interval, the snapshot watermark and the definition version.
- **Publication**: per city, the latest map is written to a replicated low-latency key-value store keyed by (city, cell) with a published-at timestamp. Pricing reads it and checks age: older than the staleness limit → use the last good map for a short grace period, then a city-level fallback (a conservative multiplier rule decided with pricing), and emit a metric.
- **History and replay**: every interval's map, plus the raw ping and request logs, goes to the lake partitioned by city and date. Each price quote records the map interval and version it used, so any quote can be explained later.
- **Regional isolation**: each city is served from one region, with a standby. Driver state is checkpointed; on failover the standby rebuilds state from the replicated log (the last few minutes of pings are enough, because state is just "latest ping per driver"), and pricing uses the fallback until the first full map.

**Competing designs to accept.** Counting supply directly in a windowed aggregation keyed by cell with a per-window distinct set of driver ids (fine if the candidate shows how a driver who crosses cells is counted once). Micro-batches every 30 seconds instead of a continuous stream job. Approximate distinct counting at coarse levels if justified. Pricing pulling maps on demand versus the pipeline pushing them.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Counting pings instead of drivers; hot partitions for large events if partitioned by cell or city; GPS error near tall buildings and airports; offline buffers flushing old pings into current state; neighbour smoothing leaking across barriers; failover with empty state; pricing reading a stale map without knowing.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Ping rate doubled on one platform*: supply rising with ping rate means supply is counted from pings, not from distinct drivers in a state. Fix the count (distinct drivers from per-driver state) or, as containment, dedupe per driver per interval; pin pricing to the fallback while supply is wrong. Prevention: a monitor on pings per driver per minute by app version, and a check that supply does not jump when the online-driver count does not.
- *Concert, partition three minutes behind*: pricing must see the map's age and fall back for that city instead of using stale numbers. Recovery: add partitions or a dedicated partition for the hot city, keep driver pings partitioned by driver (spreads load), process the request stream by cell with enough parallelism. Prevention: event calendar inputs and pre-scaling for known events; lag alarms per city.
- *GPS fault at the airport*: a reference layer of special zones (airport waiting lots) where membership is decided by geofence plus status (in queue), not by raw position alone; drop or hold positions with poor accuracy. Prevention: compare airport queue counts from the queue system with the map's supply and alert on divergence.
- *Region down 40 minutes*: cities fail over to a standby region that rebuilds driver state from replicated pings within a few intervals; pricing uses the city fallback until a full map is published with a fresh watermark. Prevention: rehearse failover, keep warm standby state, and test that the first maps after failover are marked as partial until enough drivers have pinged.

**Follow-up guidance.** Push on the exact definition of supply (distinct drivers, state at a moment) and on what pricing does when the map is stale. For the 10-second follow-up: cost is three times the aggregation and publication work for those cities, noise per cell grows (fewer events per interval), so smoothing in time may undo the benefit.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: counts pings per cell in a window, no staleness contract, no dedup. 3: distinct drivers from per-driver state, ordered processing per driver, cell aggregation with sparse-cell handling, a published freshness timestamp with a fallback, history kept. 4: also lateness policy tied to staleness, boundary-aware smoothing and special zones, regional failover with state rebuild, quotes traceable to the map interval, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Asks what counts as supply before drawing boxes; treats the staleness limit as a contract with pricing. **Red flags.** Approximate counting where exact counting is cheap without saying why; a single global aggregation job; pricing that cannot tell how old its input is.

## UBR-L4-02

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Ingestion**: apps and services post events to regional endpoints that acknowledge after a durable write to a log partitioned by trip id. Raw events are also written to the lake unchanged, for replay and audit.
- **Dedup**: on the client event id, and also on (device session, sequence number) for each app, since a retrying client may mint new ids. Duplicates are recorded as such, never applied twice.
- **Ordering**: within a source, by sequence number; across sources, by explicit authority rules per event type and by the state machine itself. Device timestamps are used for display and analytics after validation against receipt time (a timestamp far in the future or before the trip was requested is replaced by receipt time and flagged).
- **State machine**: requested → matched → arrived → started → ended → fare finalised, with cancellation from requested/matched/arrived, and explicit correction events (support reverses a cancellation or a completion). The keyed processor holds each trip's ordered event list and derives state as a pure function of it; a late event is inserted in order and the state recomputed. An event that is illegal in the current history is *held* (it may become legal when a missing earlier event arrives) for a bounded time, then recorded as rejected with a reason, never dropped.
- **Conflicts**: authority per event type: trip start and end from the driver app (with server cross-checks such as location and fare meter), rider cancellation from the rider app, fare and payment events from the server. When two authoritative events conflict (cancelled by rider vs started by driver), a rule chooses (for example, a start with movement and a fare beats a cancellation sent while offline) and the conflict is recorded for support.
- **Outputs**: a change stream of (trip_id, version, previous state, new state, cause event ids) and a current-state table upserted by version. Consumers process by (trip_id, version) idempotently; a version gap signals a missed change. Post-trip corrections are new versions with a "correction" flag. Earnings and receipts consume final states and corrections; safety and analytics consume the full history.
- **Rebuild**: because state is derived, a fixed derivation runs over the raw log for the affected window into a shadow table, is diffed against current state, and the differences are published as corrections with a reason code.
- **Reconciliation**: daily, every event received is either applied, a duplicate, or rejected with a reason; counts per source and per type must balance. Each consumer reports the highest version it processed per trip in a sample, compared with the authoritative state.

**Competing designs to accept.** A stream processor with keyed state versus a database with per-trip rows and transactional updates, if ordering and late events are handled. Event sourcing with consumers deriving their own state, if the candidate shows how consumers avoid disagreeing. Letting the server be the only authority (apps only suggest), if the candidate handles offline periods.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Retrying clients that change ids; device clock skew; offline floods; hot keys for long multi-stop trips or batched deliveries; illegal transitions caused by missing events; consumers that skip versions; rebuilds that flood consumers with corrections.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Offline flood after a network outage*: the log absorbs the burst; processors apply events in per-source sequence order; conflicts with rider-app cancellations are resolved by the authority rule and recorded. Consumers get corrections, not duplicate trips. Watch partition lag and scale consumers; prioritise trips that affect payments. Prevention: load tests with replayed offline bursts.
- *Retries with new ids*: dedup on (session, sequence) catches what the id cannot. Containment: dedupe in earnings by (trip_id, version) and reverse double-counted earnings with correction records; fix the app to keep the id across retries. Prevention: a monitor on events per trip by type and app version.
- *Fare before trip end*: holding illegal events for a bounded time would have applied them when "trip ended" arrived. Replay the 300,000 rejected events after the fix; they are in the rejected store with reasons. Prevention: a contract test between server and state machine on event order, and an alert on rejection rate by type.
- *Consumer skipping versions*: version gaps are detectable; the consumer should alert on gaps and re-read the current-state table for those trips. Platform side: a daily sampled reconciliation of each consumer against authoritative state, with results visible to the consumer team.

**Follow-up guidance.** Make the candidate state who is authoritative for each transition. For the per-driver ordering follow-up: a second stream keyed by driver derived from the trip change stream, or a consumer that orders by the driver's own sequence. For proof of completeness: the applied/duplicate/rejected balance.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: last-event-wins status table, ordering by device time, drops illegal events. 3: event log keyed by trip, dedup by id and sequence, derived state machine with authority rules, change stream with versions, late events and corrections. 4: also held-then-rejected illegal events with replay, rebuild with diffs published as corrections, a reconciliation that accounts for every event and every consumer, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Separates the immutable log from derived state; asks which app is authoritative for each event. **Red flags.** Trusting device timestamps across devices; "exactly once" claimed without saying where; consumers expected to read a table and somehow see every state.

## UBR-L4-03

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Capture**: production writes a marketplace log: every request (time, origin, destination, product, price shown, requested or not), every driver state change and position at ping resolution, every dispatch decision (offers, acceptances, declines, timeouts) and every trip outcome, with versioned schemas, partitioned by city and hour. Periodic full snapshots of marketplace state (all online drivers with state, all open requests) every few minutes let a replay start at any time without reading from the start of the day.
- **Retention and privacy**: precise positions kept for the retention window; after it, positions are coarsened (for example to a parent cell) and identifiers rotated. Deletion requests remove or pseudonymise a person's rows. Replay manifests record which data version a run used, so a rerun after deletions is known to differ.
- **Reconstruction**: a starting snapshot plus the ordered event stream for a city and period. Events are split into *exogenous* (requests, drivers going online/offline, traffic conditions) replayed as they happened, and *endogenous* (driver acceptance, rider cancellation while waiting, driver repositioning when idle) generated by behaviour models once the simulated world diverges from history.
- **Simulator**: discrete-event, single simulated clock, deterministic: a seeded random stream per actor (driver, rider) derived from (run seed, actor id) so a change to one actor does not reshuffle others. The candidate policy runs as a library in-process at each decision point. Travel times come from historical speed tables by road segment and time.
- **Behaviour models**: acceptance probability by pickup distance, fare and time; rider cancellation by wait time and promised time; idle-driver movement. Fitted on history, each with a stated range of validity (for example pickups up to the longest seen). Decisions outside that range are counted and reported; results that depend on them are flagged.
- **Calibration gate**: replay the production policy over the same period; metrics (wait time, match rate, cancellations) must match historical values within a stated tolerance per city, or the city-period is excluded and the reason reported.
- **Runs**: a job per (policy, city, period chunk) on a batch cluster, with a manifest (policy version, simulator version, data version, seeds). Results are compared with the baseline on the same inputs and seeds (paired comparison), with confidence intervals across city-days. A dashboard shows effect sizes, uncertainty, out-of-range counts and calibration status.
- **Handoff**: replay ranks and filters candidates and estimates risk; live switchback experiments decide. The platform tracks replay versus live outcomes to measure its own bias over time.

**Competing designs to accept.** Pure log replay without behaviour models, used only for policies that change little (with the limitation stated). Agent-based simulation with learned behaviour models. Partitioning by sub-city regions with boundary handling for very large cities. Holding prices at historical values versus simulating pricing, if the candidate says what each hides.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Non-determinism (thread scheduling, unordered maps, wall-clock calls, library changes); behaviour models used outside their range; data gaps in the capture; the largest city dominating wall-clock time; retention and deletions changing inputs; over-trusting replay over live tests.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Two runs differ by 1.8%*: non-determinism. Find it by running the same manifest twice with per-decision logging and diffing the first divergence; typical causes are unordered iteration, a shared random source, or parallel event processing within a city. Fix and add a reproducibility test (two runs, identical outputs) as a gate.
- *Calibration off by 9% in three cities*: the capture had a gap (location stream outage), so replay starts from wrong states. Mark capture gaps in the log's completeness table and exclude affected city-periods from evaluations automatically; report exclusions.
- *Live test raises cancellations*: the policy used pickups longer than any in the training data, where the acceptance model extrapolated. Report out-of-range decisions per run; constrain the acceptance model to its observed range or mark such results as unreliable; feed the live result back to refit.
- *Retention and deletions change inputs mid-evaluation*: runs pin a data version (snapshot of the inputs) for the evaluation's duration within the retention window, or the evaluation is restarted on current data; reruns report that inputs changed. Never keep data beyond the retention rule to make reruns match.

**Follow-up guidance.** The key probe is the counterfactual: what the historical driver does after the new policy took their rider. A strong candidate generates their behaviour from models from that point on. For the replay-versus-live gap, look for calibration, out-of-range behaviour and interference between drivers in live tests.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: "re-run history with the new policy" with no behaviour models and no determinism. 3: capture with snapshots, exogenous/endogenous split, deterministic simulator, calibration of the production policy, paired comparisons. 4: also models with stated ranges and out-of-range reporting, confidence intervals, reproducible manifests with data versions under retention rules, a measured replay-versus-live bias, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** States what replay cannot answer before being asked; makes calibration a gate. **Red flags.** Claiming replay replaces live experiments; using wall-clock time or a global random source; keeping location data beyond retention to make reruns convenient.

## UBR-L4-04

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Load store (authoritative)**: a transactional store of loads with state (open, booked, cancelled, expired), version, and attributes. Shippers create and update loads through an API; every change emits an event to a change stream.
- **Search index**: built from the change stream; documents keyed by load id with origin and destination cells (a hex or geohash cell at a few resolutions), pickup window, equipment, weight, eligibility tags. Index lag is monitored; under bulk uploads the indexer prioritises state changes (bookings, cancellations) over new loads.
- **Retrieval**: filter by equipment, eligibility and dates, origin within the carrier's radius of the empty location (widening rings when empty), optional destination preference. Return a few hundred candidates.
- **Ranking**: score candidates with a model on deadhead distance, lane preference, carrier history, timing fit and price, then re-rank the top results. Carriers without location get a default origin from their saved home base or recent drop-offs, and are prompted to set it. Position bias corrected with randomised exploration slots or inverse propensity weighting in training.
- **Pricing and quotes**: lane-level base prices computed in bulk and cached briefly; the per-carrier adjustment computed only for the displayed page (tens of loads). Each displayed price is a quote record (quote_id, load_id, load_version, carrier_id, price, model version, issued_at, expires_at) in a fast store.
- **Booking**: one conditional write on the load row: state open → booked by carrier X at quote Q, only if the load is open and the quote is valid (not expired, load_version matches or the change does not affect price, per a stated rule). The request carries an idempotency key, so a retried tap returns the same result. The losing carrier gets "just booked by someone else" and fresh similar loads. Booking emits a change event that removes the load from the index; search results are also filtered against a fast "recently booked" set to cover index lag.
- **Saved searches**: inverted: when a load is created or changes, match it against stored queries indexed by origin cell and equipment (a percolator-style index), then notify matching carriers with rate limits.
- **Eligibility**: carrier authority, insurance and shipper restrictions in a carrier profile store, applied as filters before ranking.

**Competing designs to accept.** A database with geospatial indexes instead of a search engine at the lower volumes. Pricing every candidate with a cheaper approximate model and repricing only the top ones. Quotes that are re-validated at booking with a small tolerance instead of strict honour, if the carrier is told before confirming. Reservation-style holds for a few seconds during booking.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Pricing cost per search; index lag under bulk uploads; stale bookings visible in search; double booking from check-then-write; quotes outliving a model rollback; carriers without location ranked badly; notification storms for saved searches.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Double booking*: booking was check-then-write (or relied on the index). Make booking a conditional update on the load's state in the authoritative store; for the two trucks, the shipper and carriers are contacted, one carrier is paid a cancellation or reassigned a comparable load. Prevention: a uniqueness constraint (one active booking per load) and a test that races two bookings.
- *Pricing model raised quotes by 30%*: quotes issued are honoured until their expiry (that is the promise); roll back the model; quotes issued during the window expire within the quote window. Bookings at inflated prices: decide with the business whether to adjust shippers' charges; record the model version on every quote so the affected set is exact. Prevention: canary by lane, automated guardrails on lane price change versus the previous model, and a kill switch.
- *Bulk upload, four-minute lag, cancelled loads booked*: prioritise state changes in the indexer; check the authoritative state at booking (the conditional write would reject a cancelled load); throttle or schedule bulk uploads outside the peak; scale indexing ahead of known uploads.
- *Carriers without location lose bookings*: ranking assumed location; give them a default origin from home base or last drop-off and ask them once; monitor bookings by carrier segment so a ranking change that harms a segment is seen within days.

**Follow-up guidance.** Probe the booking race precisely (which write, which condition) and the quote's honour rule. For ranking, look for position-bias awareness and segment monitoring.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: search engine as the source of truth, prices computed on every search, no quotes, check-then-book. 3: authoritative store plus index from a change stream, retrieval then ranking then pricing for the page, quotes with expiry, conditional booking with idempotency. 4: also bounded index lag with prioritised state changes and a recently-booked filter, inverted saved searches, position-bias correction and segment monitoring, model versions on quotes, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Separates matching from pricing cost; treats a quote as a record with a promise. **Red flags.** Booking through the search index; no answer for the losing carrier; pricing every match on every search with no estimate of cost.

## UBR-L4-05

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Definitions as code**: each metric is a file in a repository: owner team, description, source tables, filters, grain, numerator and denominator (for ratios), time attribute and time zone rule, version, and tests (for example on a fixed fixture of trips). Changes go through review by the owner; a definition change creates a new version, never edits the old one in place. The platform compiles definitions into SQL against the warehouse.
- **Grain**: (city, local hour, product) with numerator and denominator stored separately, plus counts for volume. Days, weeks, regions and global values are derived by summing numerators and denominators, never by averaging rates. Local hours come from a versioned time zone reference (cities that observe daylight saving get a 23- or 25-hour day; hours are labelled with their UTC offset so the repeated hour is distinct).
- **Cities**: a versioned city dimension with effective dates; merges and splits map old city ids to new ones, so history can be shown under either the historical or the current boundaries.
- **Incremental computation**: every {{freshness}}, recompute the open window (the last few days, because trips, fares and cancellations change late); outside the window, values are frozen and later changes are recorded as restatements with a reason. The weekly review reads frozen values.
- **Backfills**: run in a separate capacity pool (or a lower-priority queue) with a concurrency cap and progress tracking, chunked by month and city group; the regular runs have reserved capacity. A new definition version is backfilled alongside the old one; dashboards show which version is displayed and can compare both during a transition period.
- **Anomaly detection**: per city and metric, a forecast with daily and weekly seasonality and holidays (per city calendar), alerting when the observed count or ratio is outside a prediction interval that accounts for volume (for ratios, a binomial or Poisson interval from the denominator); minimum volume thresholds; small cities aggregated to longer windows (day instead of hour). Alerts route to the city's team through an ownership table; an alert budget (for example at most a few per city per week) is tracked and the thresholds tuned against it. Definition-version changes reset or re-baseline the detector on the backfilled history of the new version.
- **Trust**: every value is traceable to definition version, run id and input data; a metric catalogue shows owners and changes.

**Competing designs to accept.** A semantic layer product versus an in-house definition format, if versioning, ownership and tests are present. Stream computation for the freshest metrics with batch correction. Showing history only under current boundaries, if the candidate explains what is lost.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Averaged ratios; UTC days; definition changes without re-baselining; backfills starving regular runs; boundary changes breaking series; noise alerts in small cities; restatements that silently change reviewed numbers.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Every city alerts after a definition change*: the detector compared versions. Silence alerts tied to the change, backfill the new version over the detector's history and re-baseline; in future, a definition change is not live for alerting until its backfill completes, and the platform suppresses alerts caused by version boundaries.
- *Merged cities lose history*: the city dimension must be versioned with a mapping; show the merged city's history as the sum of its predecessors (numerators and denominators), and seed the detector from that combined history.
- *Backfill delays the regular updates*: kill or pause the backfill, rerun the regular jobs; isolate backfills in their own capacity with a cap and lower priority; schedule large backfills outside known peaks such as holiday weekends, and alert when regular freshness is at risk.
- *Small-city alert noise hides a real drop*: replace fixed percentage thresholds with volume-aware intervals and longer windows for small cities; track the alert budget per city; route grouped alerts (many small cities in one region moving together, as with a payments outage) to a regional owner.

**Follow-up guidance.** Ask the candidate to write a definition precisely, including re-matched requests (counted once per request) and the denominator. Probe region roll-ups (sum numerators and denominators), daylight saving (a 25-hour day is not an anomaly), and how restatements reach reviewed numbers.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: one SQL view per metric, UTC days, fixed thresholds. 3: versioned definitions with owners and tests, numerator/denominator at a local-hour grain, incremental computation with a lateness window, isolated backfills, volume-aware detection. 4: also restatement policy with frozen reviewed numbers, versioned city boundaries with history mapping, re-baselining on definition changes, alert budgets and ownership routing, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Starts with ownership and versioning; knows that rates are rolled up from numerators and denominators. **Red flags.** Averaging city rates for a region; editing a definition in place; one threshold for every city.
