# DoorDash · Loop 4 — evaluator notes (hidden from candidates)

## DSH-L4-01

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Ingestion**: each signal type lands in a durable log keyed by (store_id, item_id) so evidence for one store-item is ordered: feed rows (`feed_id, merchant_id, store_id, item_id, quantity, as_of`), pick outcomes (`order_id, line_no, store_id, item_id, outcome, picked_at` — no shopper or courier identifier is carried past ingestion), merchant out-of-stock marks (`store_id, item_id, state, set_at, set_by_role`) and consumer missing-item reports. Every event has an id; consumers deduplicate on it. Full feeds are diffed against the previous file per store so that only changed rows become evidence; the file's own `as_of`, not its arrival time, is the evidence time.
- **Evidence store**: per store-item, a compact record of the latest evidence of each kind (last found, last not found, last partial, latest feed quantity and its `as_of`, merchant mark) plus a short history; the full log is kept for replay and audit.
- **Scoring**: on every new evidence event for a store-item, call the given score with that record and the current time. Store the result with `valid_until`: the moment at which, with no new evidence, the state would cross a threshold (found by evaluating the score at a few future times, or a conservative fixed interval per state). A timer wheel or a sorted "re-evaluate at" index wakes items whose `valid_until` has passed, so ageing costs work proportional to items whose state actually changes, not to {{stores}} × {{listings}} per minute.
- **Displayed state**: a stated mapping (for example ≥ 0.8 in stock, 0.4–0.8 likely limited, < 0.4 out of stock) plus `unconfirmed` when the newest evidence is older than a bound; the page can show "availability checked 3 hours ago" for slow-changing stores. Merchant out-of-stock marks bypass the score: they set the state directly and publish an invalidation within seconds; a merchant's in-stock mark is evidence, not an override.
- **Feed reliability**: per merchant (and per store), measure how often feed-in-stock items were found at pick. Feed quantity enters the score's input with its age; a merchant whose feed proves unreliable has its feed down-weighted or ignored by rule, and the rule is visible to the merchant.
- **Serving**: a state table per store (store_id → item states and freshness) replicated to the read tier; store pages and the search index read it. State changes publish to the search index's fast-field update path and invalidate cached store pages. Checkout reads the same state and asks for a substitution preference on likely-limited items.
- **Honesty metrics (aggregate)**: for each order, the state shown at placement per line, joined to the pick outcome. Reported: calibration curves (predicted probability vs found rate), shown-in-stock-but-not-found rate per store, merchant and state, and the share of listings in `unconfirmed`. Never broken down by shopper or courier.

**Competing designs to accept.** Recomputing scores in a streaming job keyed by store-item (with timers) versus a microservice reading a key-value store; showing out-of-stock items greyed rather than hidden; computing state at read time from the evidence record when the score is cheap enough (then ageing is free, but p99 at read and score-call volume must be addressed); a fixed short validity per state instead of computing the exact crossing time.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Feed files that flood the pipeline with unchanged rows; a slow or failing scoring function backing up the evidence queue; hot partitions for very large chains; cached pages that outlive state changes; the "no news is good news" trap where silence keeps items in stock; mixing evidence time and arrival time.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Feed reports everything in stock with quantity 999*: detect it as a distribution shift per merchant (share of listings with identical quantities, share in stock jumping, not-found rate rising against feed-in-stock items). Quarantine the feed (stop using it as evidence for that merchant), fall back to pick and mark evidence, tell the merchant, and backfill once fixed. Prevention: validation rules per feed (row counts, quantity distribution, sudden all-in-stock) that hold a file before it is applied.
- *Score p99 at 2 seconds, states 25 minutes old*: the evidence path must not depend synchronously on a slow scorer for urgent signals. Apply merchant marks and not-found picks with a rule-based fast path (for example a not-found pick caps the state at likely limited until the scorer catches up), shed or coalesce redundant evidence per store-item (only the latest matters), scale or batch score calls, and show the freshness honestly. Alert on evidence lag per partition.
- *Feeds missing for 30 hours over a holiday*: silence must age evidence, not freeze it. Ageing via `valid_until` moves items to likely limited and then unconfirmed; alert on stores whose expected feed has not arrived (expected cadence of {{feed_hours}} hours per merchant), and label those stores' freshness on the page.

**Follow-up guidance.** Follow-up 1 should name the log, the dedup key, the score call and the cache or index invalidation, with a time budget under 2 minutes. Follow-up 2: a strong answer weights recent not-found picks over an hours-old feed quantity and shows likely limited or out of stock, explaining why. Follow-up 4: aggregate per store and merchant, adjust for item mix (rare items are harder), and act through feed fixes and thresholds, never by evaluating a person. Follow-up 5: shadow scoring, compare calibration and not-found rate on held-out stores, staged rollout, rollback.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: a nightly job copying feed quantities into a flag; no ageing, no merchant override path, no honesty metric. 3: an evidence log, the score called on change, thresholds to states, merchant marks as a fast path with invalidation, freshness shown, a not-found-after-in-stock metric. 4: also scheduled re-evaluation as evidence ages, per-merchant feed reliability, calibration-based rollout of a new score version, explicit refusal to attribute pick outcomes to people, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Asks what the consumer is promised by "in stock"; treats silence as ageing evidence; keeps evidence time separate from arrival time. **Red flags.** Trusting a feed because it is the merchant's; any per-shopper or per-courier view or score; showing in stock indefinitely with no evidence.

## DSH-L4-02

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Campaign definitions**: versioned records (`campaign_id, version, funder, audience rules, store/city scope, hours, minimum subtotal, discount type and value, max discount, budget, per_consumer_cap, starts_at, ends_at`). Rules are a constrained, typed language (not free code); a validator rejects contradictions, and a dry run against last week's orders reports the expected redemption count and spend before activation. Running campaigns may only raise the budget or end early; other edits create a new version for future orders.
- **Eligibility index**: active campaign versions compiled into an in-memory index by store, city and audience flags, refreshed on change. A cart (consumer attributes, store, subtotal, items, time) meets only the few dozen candidates for that store and city, evaluated in microseconds.
- **Combination**: under the stacking rule (one marketplace-funded plus one merchant-funded at most; the membership fee benefit applied separately), enumerate the allowed pairs among eligible candidates (small) and choose the one with the largest consumer saving; ties broken by a stated rule (for example earliest ending, then lowest campaign id). The price quote carries the campaign versions and a quote id.
- **Redemption ledger**: entries `(order_id, campaign_id, version, kind ∈ reserve/commit/release/adjust, amount_minor, consumer_id, at)`, unique on (order_id, campaign_id, kind) so retries are idempotent. Budget remaining and per-consumer counts are projections.
- **Budget enforcement**: at order placement, a conditional reserve: decrement the campaign's available budget by this order's discount only if it stays ≥ −1% of the budget, and increment the consumer's redemption count only if it stays ≤ the cap (a conditional write on a (campaign, consumer) row). Commit after payment succeeds; release on payment failure or reserve expiry. For hot campaigns, split the budget into K allotments with a central reserve topped up by conditional moves, or run a single-writer actor per campaign; either keeps total reserved ≤ budget × 1.01. Near exhaustion (for example < 3% left) the platform stops offering the campaign to new carts, while reservations for already-quoted carts are honoured within the tolerance — this is how the shown discount stays the charged discount.
- **Post-order changes**: by this exercise's rule, full cancellation before store acceptance writes a release (budget and cap returned); later cancellations and partial refunds write `adjust` entries for the funder's statement without returning budget or cap. Partial refunds pro-rate an order-level discount across lines by a stated rule, with remainder cents assigned deterministically.
- **Accounting and reconciliation**: a daily job joins committed redemptions to completed orders and refunds; every difference is classified (open reservation, missing release, unmatched order). Merchant statements are a view of their campaigns' ledger entries for their own stores only.

**Competing designs to accept.** Reserving at pricing time with a short hold instead of at placement (better guarantee, more budget held by abandoned carts); an approximate budget counter reconciled every few seconds with a pacing guard, if the candidate bounds overspend within 1%; storing caps in the same transactional store as orders.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** A single budget row for a flagship campaign; caps checked at pricing but not at placement (two devices race); campaigns ending while carts are open; refunds after a campaign closes; unvalidated rules that make everyone eligible; ledger and order system drifting apart.

**Incident guidance.**
- *18% overspend in the first hour*: the budget was checked when carts were priced, not reserved at placement, so carts priced before exhaustion kept spending. Reserve at placement with the conditional decrement; stop offering the campaign to new carts near exhaustion; honour in-flight quotes from a small reserve within the stated tolerance. Recovery: report the overspend to the funder per the funding rule; do not claw back from consumers.
- *Shared code, redemptions up 40-fold from new accounts*: keep the response general: a redemption velocity alert per campaign, campaign-level guards the owner set in advance (eligible audience, daily spend limit, new-account rule for this campaign), pausing the campaign as a lever, and review queues for flagged redemptions with an audit trail. Do not reward evasion detail; credit the candidate for pacing and audience rules decided before launch.
- *0.7% gap from unreleased cancellations*: the cancellation path never wrote release entries. Backfill releases from cancelled-order events (idempotent by order and campaign), add the release to the order state machine as a required step, and keep the daily reconciliation that would have caught it on day one.

**Follow-up guidance.** Follow-up 1: a conditional write on the (campaign, consumer) count and an idempotency key per order; two devices produce two orders, and the second reserve fails cleanly. Follow-up 2: stop offering near the end, honour quoted carts within tolerance, and define the tolerance in money. Follow-up 4: classify differences by type from the ledger, not by sampling. Follow-up 5: per-order attribution to campaign versions and funders, with the rule for splitting when both apply.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: a discount table and a counter; no versioning, no ledger, no answer for cancellations. 3: versioned rules with an eligibility index, combination under the stacking rule, conditional budget and cap enforcement with idempotency, a redemption ledger, cancellations handled. 4: also hot-campaign handling with a stated overspend bound, honouring shown discounts near exhaustion, partial-refund pro-rating with remainder cents, daily reconciliation with classified differences, merchant statements scoped to their stores, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Asks when a redemption counts; treats the ledger as the source of truth; defines the budget tolerance in money. **Red flags.** Charging a consumer more than they were shown; caps checked only on the client; abuse discussion that dwells on evasion techniques.

## DSH-L4-03

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Sources**: order, item, refund, missing-item report, rating and redemption events from the central log; store and merchant reference data (time zone, owner, franchise assignments) from the account service as change events with validity intervals.
- **Facts**: `order_fact` (order_id, store_id, placed_at, local_date, local_hour, subtotal, status), `order_item_fact`, `refund_fact`, `accuracy_report_fact` (one row per order with any missing or incorrect item, reports on one order collapsed), `rating_fact`, `redemption_fact`. Local date and hour are computed at ingestion from the store's time zone in force at the event time and stored.
- **Aggregates**: incremental per (store_id, local_date, local_hour) for sales, orders, accuracy, ratings and promotion results; per (store_id, local_date) and per (merchant_id, local_date) rollups. Each refresh run processes events since a watermark and updates only the affected buckets (a merge on the bucket key), so a late refund touches one bucket, not the history.
- **Restatement policy**: buckets stay open for a stated window (for example 14 days for accuracy and refunds); changes inside the window are applied with a version and visible as "updated" on the dashboard with a changes view; changes after close go into the period they arrived in, labelled. Merchants can see "last updated" per metric.
- **Access control**: one rule: a user may read rows whose store_id is in their current assignment set, enforced in the query layer (row-level security or a mandatory filter added by a service the dashboards and exports call). Assignment changes from the account service invalidate cached permission sets and cached results that include affected stores within minutes. Time-bounded assignments: after a franchise transfer, the previous owner keeps access to hours before the transfer only if the policy says so; the new owner sees hours after it.
- **Privacy**: no consumer identifiers in the analytics tables used by the portal; reviews shown as published; filters and breakdowns hidden below a minimum count; courier-related issues appear only as store-level counts.
- **Serving**: a columnar store or OLAP engine with data partitioned by merchant and date and clustered by store, plus precomputed chain-level rollups for long ranges; results cached per (user's assignment hash, query).
- **Isolation**: backfills run on separate compute with lower priority and write to new versions swapped in when complete, never competing with the hourly path.
- **Audit**: a log of every query and export with user, merchant and store set, so access can be proved after the fact.

**Competing designs to accept.** A streaming aggregation into a serving store instead of hourly micro-batches (meets freshness more easily, more operational work); per-merchant physical separation for the largest chains; recomputing a rolling window of days each hour instead of watermark merges, if cost is addressed.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Year-long queries over thousands of stores; late corrections forcing broad recomputation; stale permission caches; time-zone bugs at day boundaries; exports bypassing the access layer; backfills starving the hourly refresh.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Previous franchise owner sees the store for two days*: a cached permission list outlived the assignment change. Invalidate permission and result caches on assignment events (and bound their lifetime), audit what was viewed in the two days, notify per policy, and add a test that a transfer removes access within minutes.
- *Backfill pushes freshness to 5 hours*: separate compute or quotas for backfills, the hourly path with priority, the backfill writing a new version and swapping it in; alert when data age exceeds 90 minutes and show the data's age on the dashboard.
- *Accuracy changed silently*: adopt the restatement policy: versioned buckets, an "updated" label and a changes view, a stated window after which numbers are closed, and notices for changes above a threshold.

**Follow-up guidance.** Follow-up 2 should mention pre-aggregated daily rollups per store and merchant, and partitioning that limits scans. Follow-up 3: assignments with validity intervals and a stated policy for the transfer day. Follow-up 5: the query audit log plus tests of the access layer; accept periodic access reviews.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: one big table queried directly with a merchant filter in each dashboard; no restatement or time-zone handling. 3: facts with local date and hour, incremental hourly aggregates, a central access rule, a restatement approach, thresholds for small counts. 4: also time-bounded assignments with cache invalidation, versioned restatement with labelled changes, backfill isolation, an access audit, chain rollups that meet the latency target, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Starts from who may see what; keeps exports and caches inside the access rule; labels changed numbers. **Red flags.** Filtering by merchant in the dashboard code only; storing UTC dates for store reports; showing consumer or courier identifiers.

## DSH-L4-04

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Documents**: one document per menu item (item_id, store_id, name, description, dish tags, cuisine, menu_id) and one per store; text analysed per locale (stemming, synonyms by locale and region, spelling tolerance with edit distance on short tokens, transliteration where relevant). Synonym lists are versioned data changed by a reviewed process.
- **Fast fields**: availability, store pause, and a compact schedule (weekly intervals in local minutes, holiday overrides for the next weeks, menu-hour intervals per menu) stored as doc values or in a side table keyed by store and menu, updated in place without reindexing documents.
- **Delivery area**: store polygons are rasterised at index time into grid cells (for example H3 or S2) as fully-inside and partially-inside sets; the consumer's location maps to a cell; candidates from partially-inside cells get an exact point-in-polygon check.
- **Query path**: location → cell → candidate stores (an inverted list per cell) → text match restricted to those stores' items → filters at the query time t (or the scheduled time): the store is open at t in its time zone (weekly interval containing local t, holiday override first, pause flag), the item's menu is in hours, the item is available → candidates with features to the given scoring function → top results. Store time zones resolved with the time-zone database, so midnight crossings and DST are handled by computing local time from the instant.
- **Indexing path**: menu, hours and availability changes arrive as events; slow document changes go through a bulk indexer with per-merchant fairness; fast-field changes (pause, availability, hours) take a priority lane with a 60-second target. Bulk full-menu replaces are diffed against the current menu so only changed items are reindexed.
- **Sharding**: by geography (region, then cells), so a query touches one or a few shards; replicas sized for meal-time peaks of {{qps}}; popular query results cached per cell for seconds, with invalidation on pause events for stores in that cell.
- **Correctness**: checkout re-validates hours, menu hours and availability (the last guard); continuous probes run synthetic searches and compare results with the source of truth, alerting on any closed store, unavailable item or out-of-hours menu shown as orderable.

**Competing designs to accept.** Updating an "orderable now" boolean at each transition (driven by a timer per store) instead of evaluating schedules at query time; a single index with store-item join at query time versus denormalised item documents; a geo-shape query in the search engine instead of precomputed cells, with a latency argument.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Bulk menu replaces at meal times; DST and midnight crossings; synonym mistakes that empty results; hot cells in dense cities; caches that outlive pauses; drift between the index and the source of truth.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Closed for an hour after clocks go back*: hours were converted to UTC once (or evaluated with a fixed offset) rather than evaluated from the instant in the store's zone. Evaluate local time per query with the time-zone database (or recompute transitions daily per zone), define the repeated hour explicitly, and add DST-date tests for every zone served.
- *Bulk menu replace backs up the index to 40 minutes*: diff the replace against the current menu, apply per-merchant rate limits and a separate lane for bulk work so other merchants and fast fields keep their target, and show the merchant progress. Alert on index lag against the {{freshness_minutes}}-minute target.
- *Breakfast items until 11:15*: menu hours were evaluated by a periodic job rather than at query time, or a cache outlived the transition. Evaluate menu hours at query time (or invalidate at each transition), cap cache lifetimes near transitions, and re-validate at checkout so no order is accepted outside the menu's hours.

**Follow-up guidance.** Follow-up 2: the search was correct at 10:59; checkout at 11:02 must re-validate and tell the consumer, unless the rule allows orders placed within a grace period. Follow-up 3: shard by geography; hot cells get more replicas. Follow-up 5: probes against the source of truth and a published metric.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: one text index, open-now computed nightly, no delivery-area design. 3: geo candidate generation, text matching with synonyms, open-now and availability filters at query time, change events into the index. 4: also fast fields separate from documents, correct local-time handling for midnight and DST, bulk-update isolation, checkout re-validation, continuous correctness probes, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Separates what changes every minute from what changes weekly; asks how menus with hours are modelled. **Red flags.** Reindexing a whole store for an availability toggle; hours stored as UTC times of day; designing the ranking model instead of the candidate set.

## DSH-L4-05

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Scheduled order state machine**: `scheduled → release_pending → released → accepted → …` with `cancelled` and `needs_consumer_decision` branches. On placement the order stores the confirmed price breakdown, the substitution preferences, the store's preparation time and `release_at = due_at − preparation_time − margin`. Payment: authorise at placement when due within this exercise's 7-day validity, otherwise verify the payment method at placement and authorise near release.
- **Timer index**: orders indexed by release minute in a durable store; a scheduler with leases claims minute buckets and releases each order with an idempotent transition (conditional on state = scheduled), so a restart or a duplicate worker cannot release twice. The 12:00 peak of {{peak_scheduled}} is known in advance: pre-checks run from, say, 60 minutes before (hours, holiday closures, pauses, availability, price changes, payment), and the final release only confirms what changed since.
- **Early change detection**: when a store edits its hours, adds a holiday closure or pauses, a job finds scheduled orders affected and moves them to `needs_consumer_decision` with a notice (reschedule, another store, or cancel with refund), days in advance rather than at release.
- **Re-validation rules**: price increases above this exercise's 10% threshold ask the consumer; unavailable items follow the substitution preference or are removed and refunded with notice; promotions that ended are honoured as confirmed (or by stated rule); each change is a recorded decision on the order.
- **Group carts**: a group order document with per-participant sub-carts (participant_id, items, version); each participant writes only their sub-cart (optimistic concurrency per sub-cart, so no contention between participants); at the deadline the group is locked and priced once. Payment split by rule: host pays all, or each pays their own items plus an equal share of fees and taxes per this exercise's rule; promotions pro-rated by item value with remainder cents assigned deterministically; members' fee shares waived per the rule. Each participant authorises their share before the host places the order; a decline gives that participant a short window to fix it, then their items are removed and the order re-priced.
- **Audit**: an immutable charge breakdown per person per order (items, fees, taxes, discounts with campaign versions, membership benefit) written at placement and amended only by recorded decisions.
- **Out of scope**: courier assignment; the released order is handed over to that service.

**Competing designs to accept.** A workflow engine with durable timers instead of a hand-built scheduler; the host always pays and participants settle among themselves (simpler, less fair, must be stated); releasing scheduled orders as ordinary orders at release time with full checkout validation, if the candidate handles the peak and the communication of changes.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** The thundering herd at popular due times; scheduler restarts causing missed or double releases; hours changes discovered only at release; authorisations expiring; group carts with last-second edits; charges that cannot be explained later.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Holiday closure, 300 orders cancelled at release*: hours changes did not trigger a re-check. Subscribe to store hours changes, re-check affected scheduled orders immediately, notify consumers the evening before with choices; recovery today: refunds and notices, offering another store where possible.
- *Release job late, availability service throttled*: run pre-checks well ahead of the peak and smooth them across the hour, release by minute bucket with leases, alert on release lag, and protect downstream services with rate limits and caching of store-level checks (one hours check per store, not per order).
- *Group participant charged for an unavailable item*: per-line re-validation at the deadline and at release must flow into each participant's share; re-price the participant's share before charging, and apply refunds immediately with notice. Add a test where an item becomes unavailable between adding and placing.

**Follow-up guidance.** Follow-up 1 should name the state machine, `release_at`, the authorisation decision for {{days_ahead}} days, and the re-checks. Follow-up 3: the declining participant's items are removed after a short window, the order re-priced, and nobody else pays for them. Follow-up 5: the stored charge breakdown with recorded decisions.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: a cron job that places scheduled orders as new orders at the due time; one shared cart list; the host pays an unexplained total. 3: a state machine with release time and re-validation, idempotent release, per-participant sub-carts locked at the deadline, a payment split rule. 4: also early detection of hours changes, a planned peak with pre-checks and smoothing, authorisation timing by validity, remainder-cent and promotion pro-rating rules, an auditable per-person charge breakdown, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Asks what can change between placement and fulfilment; turns every change into a recorded decision; keeps dispatch out of scope as asked. **Red flags.** Charging more than confirmed without asking; double release after a restart; one participant paying for another's items in a per-participant order.
