# CoreWeave · Loop 4 — evaluator notes (hidden from candidates)

All figures in these scenarios are hypothetical. Score designs on the criteria,
not on resemblance to the reference; none of the references describes how any
company's systems are actually built.

## CRW-L4-01

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Inputs and owners**: installed and accepted GPUs per region and type (operations, from acceptance records); halls under construction with a ready-date range, GPU type mix and power allocation (construction and facilities); planned expansions (planning; never promisable); signed contracts with ramps (the contract system); the on-demand floor and spare fraction per region and type (business settings, versioned).
- **Time-phased ledger**: cells keyed by (region, GPU type, month). Supply per cell is computed per scenario: `likely` uses each hall's expected date, `conservative` uses the late end of its range. Every change (hall date moved, acceptance, failure withdrawal, hold, commitment, floor change) is an appended event with its effective month and the time it was recorded; cell values are projections. `promisable = conservative_supply − on_demand_floor − spare − committed − active_holds`.
- **Holds**: a hold covers every month of a ramp. It is one transaction over all affected cells in a fixed order (region, type, month) with a conditional check that each cell's promisable stays ≥ 0, or a single-writer service per region that serialises hold decisions; either way it succeeds for all months or none. Holds carry an id (idempotent retries), an owner, an expiry (14 days here, renewable once with approval) and a reason. Expiry is enforced at read time too, so a stalled expiry job never hides supply.
- **Commitment**: signing converts the hold into committed capacity with a reference to the contract version and to the supply snapshot (event offset) that allowed it. Amendments are new events, never edits.
- **Shortfalls**: when a hall slips, the system recomputes cells and lists every contract whose ramp now exceeds conservative supply, in the order of a documented policy (for example signed date, then contractual delivery terms), proposes options (another region or month, partial delivery with the rest later, an on-demand bridge from the floor) and routes the proposal to people for approval. Customers' names never enter the ranking.
- **As-of history**: because the ledger is bitemporal (effective month, recorded time), "what was promisable in region R, type T on date D" is the projection of events recorded up to D. The contract stores the snapshot offset it was signed against.
- **Interfaces**: account teams get promisable per cell with the reason a hold failed (which month, how short); the contract system gets commitments and amendments; operations gets the gap between likely and conservative supply to prioritise construction.

**Competing designs to accept.** Promising against the likely date with an explicit risk budget and a measured slip history (if the candidate quantifies the exposure and who carries it). Pooling GPU types where contracts allow substitution. A relational database with serialisable transactions instead of a ledger service, if the as-of question is still answered. Allocating shortfalls pro rata rather than by signing order, if documented and approved.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Lost updates when two holds touch overlapping months; holds that never expire and starve sales; promising against optimistic dates; edits in place that destroy the record of what was promisable; a spreadsheet as the system of record; floor changes that silently break existing commitments; shortfall decisions made ad hoc under pressure.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Hall slips by {{slip_weeks}} weeks*: recompute cells under the new range; freeze new holds against that hall's months; list the nine affected contracts in policy order with per-month shortfalls and options; the three waiting deals get a later start or a waitlist, not a hold. Communication goes through account teams with dates and options, not apologies alone. Prevention: promise against the conservative date and track slip history per construction phase to calibrate ranges.
- *Two holds succeed for the same last GPUs*: a race between read and write (check-then-insert without a conditional write or with per-month checks not done atomically). Immediately: identify the later hold by recorded time, contact its owner before anything is signed, release or move it. Fix: atomic all-months conditional write or a single writer per region; a continuous invariant check (`committed + holds ≤ conservative − floor − spare` per cell) that pages on violation.
- *6% of installed GPUs withdrawn, above spare*: post the withdrawal as a supply event for three weeks; cells go negative; first absorb it from the on-demand floor (new on-demand requests in that cell are refused or redirected), then apply the shortfall policy to commitments if still short. Prevention: size spare from measured withdrawal history per type and region, not a fixed fraction.

**Follow-up guidance.** Push on the atomicity of a multi-month hold, on what exactly goes into a signed contract (which date, which scenario), and on the as-of query. For the finance question, the candidate should propose comparing historical slips with the likely-versus-conservative gap and the revenue sold in between.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: a single "available GPUs" number per region, no time dimension, no holds or concurrency story. 3: time-phased cells, conservative supply, atomic holds with expiry, commitments linked to contracts. 4: also bitemporal as-of answers, a documented shortfall policy with options and approvals, on-demand floor and spare treated as policy, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Asks which date is promised against before designing; treats every change as an event; keeps people in the loop for shortfall decisions. **Red flags.** Overbooking without measuring anything; "just use a spreadsheet with a lock"; ranking customers by name or size without a policy.

## CRW-L4-02

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Quota model**: `quota(org, gpu_type, region, limit, effective_from)` plus optional `global_quota(org, gpu_type, limit)`. Changes are versioned events with who, why and the increase request they answer.
- **Admission (per region)**: a regional quota service owns counters per (org, type, region): `in_use` and `claimed`. A request performs one conditional update: `claimed += n` only if `in_use + claimed + n ≤ limit` (a single-row conditional write or a serialisable transaction). It returns a claim id (idempotent on the request id) with an expiry. As nodes become ready the claim moves to `in_use`; failed or abandoned nodes release their share of the claim; released nodes decrement `in_use`. A refusal says "quota" (with limit, in use and pending) or "capacity" (no free GPUs of that type), never both ambiguously.
- **Global caps**: split each global cap into per-region allotments held locally (escrow). Each region admits against its own allotment without cross-region calls. A rebalancer moves unused allotment between regions with conditional transfers that can only lower the giver before raising the receiver, so the sum never exceeds the cap. When a region cannot reach the rebalancer, it keeps its current allotment and no more: overshoot is impossible by construction, and the cost is that a partitioned org may be refused while another region holds unused allotment.
- **Reconciliation**: the regional control plane's node lifecycle events are the source for `in_use`; a periodic job recomputes counters from them and from a direct listing of nodes per org. Differences that lower a counter are applied only after confirming the nodes are gone (two observations or the control plane's own record); differences that raise it are applied immediately (the safe direction).
- **Backpressure**: per-org request rate limits at admission, refusals that carry a retry-after, and coalescing of identical pending requests from the same node pool, so a retry loop cannot degrade admission for others.
- **Increase workflow**: requests with justification; an automatic first check against promisable capacity in that region and type (from the capacity system) and against the org's history; partial grants; decisions recorded with reviewer and reason; target turnaround {{review_hours}} hours.
- **Customer view**: limit, in use, claimed (pending), last refusals with reasons, and open increase requests.

**Competing designs to accept.** A single global strongly consistent store for all quotas (simpler; the candidate must accept cross-region latency and an availability cost during partitions, or show regional fallback). Token-bucket style leases for global caps that expire and must be renewed. Counting only ready nodes and refusing at a separate capacity step (accept if claims still prevent concurrent overshoot).

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Check-then-act races between the read of usage and the provisioning call; claims that never expire after a crashed request; counters drifting from lost events; a global lock that makes every region depend on one; retry storms from autoscalers; confusing quota refusals with capacity refusals, which sends customers to ask for increases that would not help.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Autoscaler retry storm*: rate-limit that org's admission calls and return retry-after; coalesce duplicate pending requests per node pool; isolate per-org work so one org's load cannot occupy the shared admission path; contact the customer with the refusal reason (quota or capacity). Prevention: per-org limits and fair queuing at admission, documented retry guidance.
- *Lost release events, 40 orgs stuck at quota*: reconcile from the control plane's listing of nodes for the affected orgs; lower counters after confirming the nodes are gone; tell affected customers. Prevention: release events written transactionally with the node state change (an outbox), and continuous reconciliation with an alert on drift.
- *Global cap overshoot by 7% during a partition*: the design admitted against a global count it could not see, or let both regions use a shared pool optimistically. Do not evict running work (a quota decrease never evicts); block new requests in that type until the org is back under its cap and tell the customer. Fix: per-region allotments that bound overshoot at zero, or a stated, approved bound if the business prefers availability.

**Follow-up guidance.** Make the candidate execute the two concurrent 64-GPU requests against 100 remaining, step by step. For partial provisioning, a strong answer releases the unfulfilled part of the claim and leaves the 40 ready nodes in use, and tells the customer what happened. For the holiday spike, look for a written, explainable rule (for example grant within promisable capacity in request order, with partial grants), not ad hoc favours.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: reads usage then provisions, no claims, global caps checked by a central call with no partition story. 3: atomic conditional claims with expiry, lifecycle-based counting, quota versus capacity refusals, an increase workflow. 4: also per-region allotments with safe rebalancing, reconciliation that corrects in the safe direction, backpressure for retrying clients, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Separates quota (policy) from capacity (physics) early; states the overshoot bound during a partition. **Red flags.** A distributed lock across regions on every request; counters in a cache with no rebuild story; evicting running nodes to enforce a cap.

## CRW-L4-03

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Line model**: `line_id = hash(customer, resource_id, meter, hour, rate_basis)`; columns: customer, cluster, resource, GPU type, region, meter, quantity in integer base units (seconds, byte-hours, bytes), rate as an exact decimal from the price-book version, `rate_basis` (reservation or on-demand), `price_version`, `cost_microcents` (exact integer), `cost_cents` (after the one rounding step), `version`, `restated_reason`. Credits and adjustments are lines with their own meters.
- **Hour closing**: metering records are aggregated by event time; an hour is closed when its watermark passes (for example hour end plus a grace period chosen to meet the {{file_hours}}-hour target). Records that arrive after closing go into the next version of that hour, never into a later hour.
- **Rounding (one rule, stated)**: cost is computed per line in exact integer micro-cents. Option A: round each line to cents (half-up, stated) and define the invoice as the sum of line cents. Option B: sum micro-cents per invoice, round once, and emit a visible rounding-adjustment line so lines still sum to the invoice. Either is fine if stated and tested at exact halves; mixing them is the classic error.
- **Restatements**: within {{restate_days}} days of month close, affected hours are republished as version n+1 (full replacement files for those hours) with a manifest listing hours, versions, reason and the cost delta; the same change is also published as correction lines (negative of the old line, positive of the new, same line id lineage) for customers who append. A customer can use either path and converge. After the window, corrections become dated adjustment lines on the next invoice.
- **Invoice tie-out**: invoices are computed from the published lines, not from a separate path. A continuous check sums each customer's lines per period and compares them with the invoice; any difference blocks invoice release.
- **API**: queries run against a versioned snapshot (a data version id returned with the first page and carried in the cursor), so a restatement mid-pagination cannot mix versions.
- **Delivery and isolation**: files are written to a staging area under a path derived from the verified customer id, then delivered with per-customer scoped credentials (the customer grants a role limited to one bucket prefix); after writing, the writer verifies the destination's owner matches the customer. Delivery failures retry with backoff for a stated period, notify the customer, and catch up from the manifest.
- **Schema evolution**: additive columns only within a major version; a version field in the manifest; new meters as new rows, not new columns, where possible.

**Competing designs to accept.** Daily rather than hourly restatement granularity if it still meets the window; a table share in the customer's warehouse instead of files; correction lines only (no replacement files) if every loader is append-only and the candidate shows how a customer reconstructs the current state.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Separate pipelines for invoice and export that drift; rounding in several places; overwriting files with no manifest; late data assigned to the hour it arrived; credentials that can write to any customer bucket; path templates built from untrusted fields.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Metering fix four days after close*: within the {{restate_days}}-day window, so restate: recompute affected lines, publish new versions of the affected hours with a manifest and correction lines, re-issue or amend the 300 invoices with the delta explained, and tell each customer what changed and why. Prevention: a metering regression test on the fixed case and an anomaly check on per-customer usage before close.
- *Customer's files sum to $1,284.17 too much*: they loaded both versions. Show them the manifest for the 20 restated hours; confirm the current version sums to the invoice. Fix the product so the manifest is impossible to miss (version in the file name and inside each file, correction lines as an alternative) and publish loader guidance.
- *Files under another customer's prefix*: a data exposure. Stop the writer, delete the misplaced files, determine exactly which files and who could read them, notify both customers through the proper process, and record the incident. Fix: paths derived only from verified identity, per-customer credentials that cannot write elsewhere, and a post-write owner check that halts delivery on mismatch.

**Follow-up guidance.** Make the candidate show the sum-equals-invoice proof concretely. For the month-over-month explanation, a strong answer groups the change by meter, rate basis and resource and points to the lines.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: a nightly CSV per customer, overwritten on corrections, rounding unspecified. 3: line ids, hour closing with late data, versioned restatements, invoices from the same lines, scoped delivery. 4: also the dual restatement path (versions plus correction lines), one stated rounding rule with tie-out checks, snapshot-consistent API pagination, isolation by construction, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Asks how customers load the files before designing restatements; treats isolation as a design property. **Red flags.** Floats for money; "customers should just reload everything"; one shared credential for all buckets.

## CRW-L4-04

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Metering points**: each path has exactly one billing meter. Host-side, the virtual switch or host networking layer keeps per-interface counters split by destination class (the class looked up per packet from the destination address against a compiled prefix table, counting only header-derived bytes; no payload is read or copied). Traffic leaving through gateways with public addresses or translation is metered at the gateway per customer network, and the host excludes that traffic class from billing to avoid double counting (or the reverse; one rule, written down). Dedicated interconnect ports are metered at the port.
- **Reports**: every {{report_seconds}} seconds a host or gateway reports `(interface_id, class, counter_value, agent_boot_id, sequence, report_time)`. Delta rules: same `agent_boot_id` and higher value → delta = difference; new `agent_boot_id` → delta = new value (counter restarted); a lower value with the same boot id → out-of-order or bad data, held for review, never billed as a wrap unless the previous value was near 2^64. Any delta above link capacity × interval is quarantined.
- **Attribution**: interface attachment history maps interface → customer resource over time; deltas are attributed by report interval.
- **Destination table**: bitemporal prefix-to-class table (effective time, recorded time). Classification uses the version effective at the traffic time; reclassifying the past is an audited restatement with a customer-facing explanation.
- **Pipeline**: a stream aggregates deltas into per-customer, region, class, hour totals with watermarks; late reports land in their own hour (bytes since the last report are spread over the gap by time). Hourly totals feed the customer view within an hour; month totals are final three days after month end.
- **Billing**: transfer between regions × rate; internet transfer minus the monthly free {{free_gb}} GB, applied once per customer per month at invoice time (not per hour), × rate; integer minor units with one rounding step.
- **Minimisation**: no payloads anywhere; any per-connection metadata kept for troubleshooting is short-retention (days), access-logged, and never used for billing or shown beyond the owning customer. Customers see totals by region, class and resource.
- **Reconciliation**: compare billed totals with independent aggregate counters at gateways and on physical uplinks per region; differences beyond a tolerance alert.

**Competing designs to accept.** Metering only at gateways and region edges (fewer points; same-region traffic is free so it need not be classified per host) if attribution per customer is still exact. eBPF-based host counters versus counters in a virtual switch. Per-minute rather than per-hour aggregates.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Counter resets read as wraparound; out-of-order reports producing negative deltas; double counting at host and gateway; classification tables changed without effective times; free allowance applied per hour or per region by mistake; sampled flows used as if exact; per-connection logs retained indefinitely.

**Incident guidance.**
- *18-exabyte charges after an agent upgrade*: hold invoices for affected customers; recompute deltas with the boot-id reset rule; add a plausibility bound (link capacity × interval) that quarantines impossible deltas; test agent upgrades against the delta logic before rollout. Correct the usage customers can see, and tell those who saw the wrong figure what happened.
- *Wrong effective time on an address range*: six hours of same-region storage traffic billed as internet transfer. Correct the table with the right effective time, restate the six hours for the ~900 customers through the audited restatement path, and show them the correction. Prevention: changes to the table reviewed, effective times validated against deploy times, and an anomaly check on sudden class shifts per region.
- *Double counting for nine days*: decide the single metering point for gateway traffic, restate the nine days for affected customers, issue corrections or credits, and add a reconciliation that compares billed internet transfer with the region's uplink counters. Thank the customer who found it and say how it was found.

**Follow-up guidance.** For "prove the bill is right without per-connection data", look for hourly totals by class and resource, independent reconciliation at uplinks, and a clear account of which counter is billed. For a pipeline outage, counters keep counting at the source, so the next report carries the bytes; totals stay exact, only freshness suffers.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: sampled flow logs or packet capture as the billing source, no reset handling. 3: counters with reset handling, one metering point per path, destination classes from a prefix table, monthly free allowance, no payload inspection. 4: also bitemporal classification with audited restatements, plausibility bounds, reconciliation with independent counters, clear data minimisation, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** States early that contents are never needed for billing; asks where each byte is counted once. **Red flags.** Deep packet inspection; long-lived per-connection logs "for analytics"; free allowance per hour.

## CRW-L4-05

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Inputs**: work items `(id, kind, nodes or racks or rows, duration, earliest, latest, urgency, dependencies)`; customer preferences `(customer, cluster, weekly windows as local-time rules with an IANA zone, max_share, freezes)`; node-to-customer-cluster mapping over time.
- **Scheduling**: expand windows into UTC intervals for the planning horizon (DST handled by the time-zone library on each date). Time is slotted (for example 30 minutes); for each slot and customer cluster, the nodes under any planned work must stay ≤ {{max_share}}% of the cluster, summed across all work items. A solver or a priority-ordered heuristic places work items; a rack- or row-level item must fit every affected customer's window at once or is split only where the work allows. Items that cannot be placed within 30 days go to a conflict queue for people, with the customers and constraints that block them.
- **Notices**: events are versioned; the first notice goes out ≥ {{notice_days}} days ahead; changes are new versions with reasons; customers can acknowledge or request a move; emergency work is flagged as such and marked as not qualifying for the exclusion.
- **Execution**: per node: cordon, drain within the customer's cluster honouring its disruption limits up to a timeout, then either proceed (after notice) or reschedule per the stated rule; abort criteria (overrun beyond a margin, failure rate of returned nodes, the cluster crossing its share limit) stop the wave and return nodes. Every transition is an event.
- **Status page**: state per region and service from several customer-facing signals (provisioning success rate, API errors, node health, storage and network probes) with thresholds and durations; automation may publish "planned maintenance" and propose "degraded"; "outage" and recovery need a person; a single failing probe opens an internal alert, not a public state.
- **Impact**: per customer, node-minutes unavailable computed from lifecycle events; each minute is labelled planned-in-window (excluded under this exercise's rule if announced with notice), planned-outside-window or unplanned (both count). Quarterly reports per customer from the same events; aggregates guide which work kinds to batch.

**Competing designs to accept.** A greedy scheduler with human review if the per-cluster limit is enforced globally across items; live migration where the workload allows it (rare for GPU training jobs; the candidate should say so); customer self-scheduling within a platform-chosen set of slots.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Per-item scheduling that ignores overlap between items on the same cluster; fixed UTC offsets for windows; drains blocked forever by disruption limits; status derived from one signal; exclusions applied to overruns; impact reported by hand.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Power work overruns by three hours*: notify affected customers immediately with a new estimate; minutes past the announced window count against availability; update the status page; afterwards, measure overrun per work kind and add margins or split work. The {{notice_days}}-day notice covers only the announced window.
- *Fixed UTC offsets and DST*: store windows as local-time rules with zones; recompute schedules for upcoming events; the 60 customers' out-of-window minutes count as unplanned; apologise and explain. Prevention: tests across DST transitions in every zone customers use.
- *Two overlapping work items exceed the share limit*: the scheduler checked each item alone. Stop the wave, return nodes, and enforce the limit as a shared capacity across all items per cluster and slot; add a pre-execution check of the combined share.
- *Status shows operational while provisioning fails*: add provisioning success and latency as status signals; post "degraded" for the region with a human confirmation; review which customer-facing functions are missing from status.

**Follow-up guidance.** For the 40-customer row, look for the intersection of windows, the share limit per cluster (a row may hold more than {{max_share}}% of a small customer's cluster, forcing a conflict for people), and negotiated moves. For the quarter report, the label per node-minute is the key.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: a calendar of maintenance with emails, no limit across items, manual status. 3: local-time windows, a scheduler enforcing per-cluster limits, versioned notices, cordon and drain with timeouts, impact from events. 4: also conflict surfacing to people, status from several signals with human confirmation for major states, exclusions computed per node-minute, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Asks about overlapping work items and DST unprompted; measures impact from events. **Red flags.** "We schedule at night UTC"; status page fully automated or fully manual with no thresholds; counting overruns as planned.
