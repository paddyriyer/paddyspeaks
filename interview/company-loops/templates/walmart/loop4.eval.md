# Walmart Global Tech · Loop 4 — evaluator notes (hidden from candidates)

## WMT-L4-01

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Ingestion**: registers, handhelds and receiving publish inventory events (`event_id, store_id, item_id, delta, reason, device_ts, received_ts`) to a durable log keyed by (store, item), so every change for one store-item is ordered on one partition. Devices buffer while offline and upload with their original ids; the consumer deduplicates on `event_id` (a per-key set of recent ids, or a unique constraint in the ledger).
- **Ledger and projection**: an append-only ledger per store-item; on-hand is a projection maintained in a key-value store keyed by (store, item), holding the balance, the last count (quantity, count time, location) and the event time of the latest applied change. Reasons (sale, return, receipt, transfer in/out, write-off, online pick, count) keep every unit traceable.
- **Counts as balances at a time**: a count at T sets the balance *as of T*. On-hand = counted quantity + Σ deltas with event time > T. A late event with event time ≤ T is recorded in the ledger but does not move on-hand (the count already reflects it); a late event with event time > T is added. Counts covering one location replace only that location's sub-balance (floor, backroom, display) when the model tracks locations; otherwise the candidate states the simplification. The count's own variance (counted minus expected at T) is recorded as the shrink or correction amount.
- **Trust in counts**: a count that moves the balance by more than a threshold (for example over 50% and over N units) is applied but flagged; two items swapped in one count (one to zero, a similar one up by the same amount) trigger a recount task rather than silent acceptance.
- **Online state**: `shown = on_hand − held_for_unpicked_orders − buffer(store, item)`, mapped to in stock / limited / out by thresholds that are this exercise's rule. The buffer grows with the store-item's measured unreliability (recent count variance, failed picks) and shrinks for slow sellers with small facings so an item stocked as one or two units is not always hidden. A regionally replicated read view (key-value or cache) is refreshed from the projection within the freshness target; picking re-checks against the projection.
- **Accuracy metric**: per store, department and category, the share of counted store-items whose system on-hand at count time was within a tolerance of the count (or absolute units variance over units counted), reported only in aggregate. Never per associate.
- **Rebuild**: replay the ledger into a new projection beside the live one, then switch reads atomically; the read path never stops.

**Competing designs to accept.** A streaming state store (keyed operator state) instead of an external key-value store; storing only the latest balance plus a short tail of events instead of a full ledger, if the candidate keeps enough history to apply late events and audit counts; nightly reconciliation against a full count snapshot in addition to streaming. Tracking on-hand per location (floor, backroom) versus only per store, with the trade-off stated.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Partition skew when one large store uploads a backlog (keying by store-item spreads it; keying by store does not); double subtraction when counts overwrite balances; clock skew on devices; pack-size conversion at the wrong version; the read view lagging at peak; stale "in stock" causing failed picks.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Registers offline three hours, 40,000 sales at once*: the projection was right about what it knew; the online state was wrong because it did not know the store was dark. Mark a store "stale" when its event flow stops (heartbeat per register or per store) and widen buffers or show "limited" for fast sellers while stale; process the backlog by event time per store-item; replay is idempotent. Prevention: per-store freshness monitor, and failed-pick rate per store as an alert.
- *Count times recorded in UTC labelled local*: counts applied hours late in western stores, so sales after the real count were ignored and on-hand reads high. Identify affected counts (app version, store offset), recompute each count's effective time, replay ledger from those times into a rebuilt projection; prevention: device timestamps carried with an explicit offset or as UTC instants, and a check that count time ≤ received time and is within the store's open hours.
- *Pack-size change*: units per case are versioned by effective date; receipts are converted using the version in force when the case was shipped (from the manifest) rather than the current one. Repair: re-convert affected receipts with the correct version and post correcting events (never edit history). Prevention: an alert when a category's count variance jumps across many stores at once, which points to a master-data cause rather than shelves.
- *Mis-scanned count*: both large swings flag; a paired-swap pattern (one item to 0, a similar item up by about the same amount) opens a recount task; until resolved, the zeroed item is shown "limited" rather than "out" if its sales history and recent receipts make zero implausible.

**Follow-up guidance.** For follow-up 1 the answer is 12 − 2 = 10 (the 10:02 sale is before the count and already reflected; the 10:07 and 10:09 sales are subtracted). Push on whether the candidate's store can apply a count "in the past". For follow-up 2, a strong candidate notes that accuracy measured only on counted items is biased toward items someone chose to count. For follow-up 5, replay into a parallel projection with an atomic switch.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: a balance updated in place, counts overwrite it, late events double count, online state equals on-hand > 0. 3: ledger with idempotent events, counts applied at count time with later changes replayed, a read view with a stated freshness, a buffer, an accuracy metric in aggregate. 4: also adaptive buffers tied to measured reliability, store-staleness detection, versioned pack sizes, count sanity checks, replay beside live, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Asks what a count means in time before designing; separates exact (picking) from approximate (browsing) paths; refuses to measure individual associates. **Red flags.** "Overwrite on-hand with the count"; keying by store only with no thought for backlogs; no notion of staleness; an accuracy metric per person.

## WMT-L4-02

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Three linked records**: a *return* (return_id, original order line or receipt line, item, quantity, location returned, local business day, reason) captured idempotently by return_id; a *refund* (refund_id, return_id, amount in cents, tender) confirmed by the payments system; a *disposition* (return_id, unit disposition, location, time) that can come days later. Each has its own timestamp and owner.
- **Valuation**: at sale time, order-level discounts are allocated to lines (for example proportionally to line value, with the remainder cents assigned deterministically, such as to the largest line or by line order) so the line's paid amount is stored. A partial return refunds `paid_line_amount × returned_qty / sold_qty` with a stated remainder rule so that returning every unit refunds exactly what was paid. No-receipt returns are valued by this exercise's rule and flagged.
- **Inventory**: units move only on disposition: restock adds to the receiving store's on-hand (a transfer-like event, even if the store never received it from a DC); return-to-supplier, liquidation, donation and destroy move to non-sellable buckets. "Pending disposition" is a visible state with an age.
- **Restatement rule (one acceptable example)**: in finance views a return reduces net sales of the *original* channel and store in the period when the return is accepted (closed periods never change); in the receiving store's operational view, returns of items it did not sell appear as "returns accepted for other channels" and do not reduce its own comparable sales. Both views come from the same return records; the rule is written down.
- **Closed periods**: after the close ({{close_days}} business days after month end), late returns are booked in the open period with `relates_to_period` set; return-rate reports by sale cohort are labelled as maturing until the {{window_days}}-day window has passed.
- **Reconciliation**: daily, refunds in the data are matched to payments-system payouts by refund_id; differences are listed by cause (missing, duplicated, amount mismatch). Marketplace refunds carry the seller's recovery as a separate flow.

**Competing designs to accept.** Reducing the receiving store's sales and adding a transfer adjustment instead (if both views are kept consistent); computing restatement in the warehouse rather than at ingestion; event-sourced returns with projections. Any rule is acceptable if stated, applied consistently and explained to both audiences.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Unlinked returns; double capture after retries; disposition lag hiding sellable stock or inflating on-hand; rounding drift in partial refunds; late returns rewriting closed months; mixing marketplace and first-party refunds.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Online returns counted as in-store negatives*: the restatement rule was applied by return location. Recompute the open period under the stated rule; for closed months, publish a reclassification adjustment in the open period with a note, not a rewrite. Prevention: tests that an online-order return in a store leaves the store's comparable sales unchanged.
- *900 duplicate return events*: capture was not idempotent by return_id. Deduplicate on return_id, post correcting inventory events for the double restocks, reconcile on-hand for the affected items. Prevention: unique key at capture, and a daily check that restocked units ≤ returned units per return.
- *60,000 mail-in returns after the close*: correct behaviour under an immutable close is that they land in the open month with `relates_to_period`; the problem is explaining it. Provide a view of returns by sale period and by booking period, and a backlog metric at the returns centre so finance can accrue for expected returns before close.
- *No-receipt refunds at the current price*: refunds no longer equal the rule's value, and reconciliation flags a daily difference. Roll back or fix the register logic; quantify the over- or under-refund in aggregate; the refunds already paid stand (they are facts) and the difference is booked; prevention: a valuation check comparing register refund to the rule's value per return.

**Follow-up guidance.** In follow-up 1, a 10% order discount over five identical units means each unit's paid amount is 90% of list; two units refund 2/5 of the paid line amount, with remainder cents handled by the stated rule; sales, inventory (on disposition) and refunds each get a row. Push on follow-up 5: cohort return rates mature; a candidate who says they will "update last month" fails the immutability constraint.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: a single "returns" table updated in place, refunds computed from list price, restatement by return location without noticing the store effect. 3: linked return, refund and disposition records; discounts allocated at sale; a stated restatement rule; immutable closes with late returns in the open period; daily refund reconciliation. 4: also a deterministic cent-allocation rule, pending-disposition state and its effect on availability, maturing cohort metrics, marketplace flows kept separate, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Separates money, units and reporting; asks who owns each number. **Red flags.** Floating-point refunds; rewriting closed months; inventory restocked at refund time regardless of disposition; tracking individual customers' return behaviour in analytics.

## WMT-L4-03

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Capacity model**: per (store, slot, resource): picking minutes, totes per temperature zone (ambient, chilled, frozen), pickup bays per slot or delivery route stops. Each order carries an estimate per resource, refined as the order changes. A slot is bookable for an order only if every resource has room (plus the store's overbooking allowance, zero by default).
- **Booking path (exact)**: a booking store keyed by (store, slot) with remaining capacity per resource and booking rows keyed by order id with states `held → confirmed → (changed) → picked`, or `held → released` and `confirmed → cancelled`. A hold is one conditional write that decrements every resource only if all stay ≥ 0 and inserts the booking row; retries carry the order id, so a retried hold returns the existing booking. A hold past its {{hold_minutes}}-minute deadline counts as free at read and is released by a sweeper (idempotent), so a stalled sweeper cannot hide capacity.
- **Hot slots**: per store-slot single-writer actors or sharded counters with rebalancing; at slot opening for the busiest days, a waiting room that admits customers at the rate the booking path can serve, in arrival order.
- **Availability view**: a cached, per-store availability grid derived from the booking store, refreshed every few seconds and on change, with a margin (a slot near full shows "few left" or is shown only if remaining capacity exceeds the typical order); the booking path always re-checks.
- **Capacity changes**: store sets new capacity per resource; if booked exceeds it, the system proposes moves (orders not yet picked, smallest disruption first, adjacent slots or overlapping stores), notifies customers, and records each move with the reason. A person at the store or a central team approves bulk moves.
- **Time**: slots generated per store in local time from a calendar that knows daylight-saving rules; stored as UTC instants with the local label; ambiguous and non-existent local times handled explicitly.
- **Metrics (aggregate)**: failed-on-shown-available rate, time to fill per slot, idle capacity per resource, overfull slots, orders moved.

**Competing designs to accept.** Order-count capacity with per-store size adjustments (simpler; must address large orders); a single relational row per store-slot with conditional updates if the candidate shows the rate per row is manageable with a waiting room; computing availability on read from the booking store for low-traffic stores.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Contention on the few hottest slots; stale availability causing retry storms; holds that do not expire; capacity reductions without a move process; DST; underestimating order size.

**Incident guidance.**
- *30% failures on shown-available slots, traffic triples*: the 60-second refresh is far too stale at peak. Refresh on change (publish remaining capacity on each booking), hide or mark slots near full, return the next best slots in the failure response so retries are useful, add a waiting room, and rate-limit blind retries. Measure failed-on-shown-available as the alert.
- *Parking lot closed, capacity halved with 140 booked*: the system lists orders over capacity, proposes moves to adjacent slots or delivery from an overlapping store, prioritises orders not yet picked and chilled orders that cannot wait, notifies customers with choices, and records each move. Prevention: a playbook and the ability to set a capacity change that stops new bookings immediately while moves are decided.
- *DST slot errors*: generate slots per store in local time with the time-zone database, store UTC instants, and handle the repeated and skipped hour explicitly (for example, no slot starts inside a skipped hour; a repeated hour is labelled). Test with each DST date for every time zone served.
- *Orders twice the usual tote count*: the order-count limit did not reflect the real constraint. Switch to tote-based capacity per temperature zone (or adjust estimates by season), use refined estimates after order edits, and alert when staging use exceeds a threshold before the slot.

**Follow-up guidance.** Follow-up 1 should name the conditional decrement across resources and the idempotent order id. For follow-up 4, accept refusing changes that do not fit, moving the order with consent, or accepting with overbooking within the allowance. For follow-up 5, credit planning from past demand and fill times, never from individual associates' speed.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: one counter per slot in a cache, no holds or idempotency, stale availability ignored. 3: exact conditional booking with holds and expiry, a stated capacity unit, an availability view with re-check, a process for capacity drops. 4: also multi-resource capacity, hot-slot handling with a waiting room, honest-availability metrics, DST-correct slot generation, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Asks what runs out first in a store; measures honesty of what customers are shown. **Red flags.** Planning capacity from per-associate productivity; slots in UTC labels; no answer for already-booked orders when capacity drops.

## WMT-L4-04

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Ingestion**: position reports and trip events into a durable log keyed by trailer (or trip), deduplicated by (trailer, device_ts, type). Trip plans (stops, planned windows) and manifests (per stop: pallets, cases, items) come from the dispatch and shipping systems, keyed by trip id.
- **Trip state**: per trip and stop, a state machine (planned → departed → approaching → arrived → unloading → closed) advanced by event time; a late report can add history and refine an ETA but cannot move a stop backwards. Arrival is defined (geofence entry plus door open, or a check-in event), and dwell before door open is recorded separately.
- **ETA**: raw ETA per remaining stop from current position, typical travel time for the segment and time of day, and unloading time at earlier stops; recomputed on each report. The *published* ETA changes only when the raw ETA moves by more than a threshold (for example 30 minutes) and stays moved for a few reports (hysteresis), or when the change crosses into the store's planning lead time. Each change carries a reason (traffic, earlier stop running long, departure late, feed lost).
- **Feed health**: per trailer and per source, "last report at"; past a threshold, the stop shows "position unknown since …" and the ETA becomes conservative (planned window plus a widening margin), and stores are told so.
- **Units in transit**: from manifests (cases × pack size per item, versioned) per destination store; cleared by store receipt scans, not by arrival. At trip close, manifest versus scans gives shortages and overages per store and item; a pallet found at the wrong store is recorded as an overage there and a shortage at the intended store, linked by pallet id, and corrected by a transfer or re-delivery.
- **Replenishment rule**: units in transit count toward a store's position until received or declared lost (for example, after a trip closes without scanning them, or after N hours late); this rule is written down.
- **Temperature**: readings evaluated by event time against the allowed range and duration; an excursion marks the trip's perishable lines for inspection at receipt, and if the readings arrive after receipt, it opens an inspection task for the units already received.
- **ETA accuracy**: absolute error of the published ETA at a fixed horizon (for example two hours before planned arrival), per DC and route type; arrivals measured to door open or check-in, stated.

**Competing designs to accept.** Keying by trip instead of trailer; a managed streaming state store versus a key-value store; predicting ETAs with a model rather than segment averages, if the candidate keeps the publishing hysteresis and explains fallbacks.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Feed gaps that look like "on time"; ETA flapping; multi-stop mis-deliveries; units in transit never cleared or cleared too early; late temperature data; clock skew on devices.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Feed stops for three hours*: the system treated no news as on time. Add per-source freshness monitoring, switch affected trips to "position unknown since" with conservative ETAs, notify stores, and on recovery process the backlog by event time (idempotently) without firing a burst of stale notifications (suppress notices for changes already superseded).
- *Second-stop pallets unloaded at the first stop*: receipt scans show the overage at store one and, at trip close, the shortage at store two; link them by pallet or case id, post a correcting transfer, keep store two's units in transit as "misdelivered, in recovery" rather than vanishing, and let replenishment see the gap. Prevention: scan pallets against the stop's manifest at unload with a warning on mismatch.
- *ETA flapping*: publish with a threshold and hysteresis, use the store's planning lead time to decide which changes matter, and attach a reason; measure notifications per store per day as a health metric.
- *Late temperature readings after unloading*: evaluate excursions on arrival of the data regardless of trip state; open an inspection task for the received perishable units and hold them from sale where the rule requires; prevention: require the trailer's latest readings at door open (a pull, not only a push).

**Follow-up guidance.** Follow-up 1: the late report becomes history (and can refine travel-time statistics) but cannot reopen the stop. Follow-up 3: push on the replenishment effect: if store two's in-transit units vanish, replenishment over-orders; if they stay as in transit forever, it under-orders. Follow-up 4: a fixed-horizon error is what a store plans on.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: latest-position overwrite, an ETA recomputed and pushed on every report, in-transit cleared on arrival. 3: event-time state machine, published ETA with a threshold, feed freshness, units in transit cleared by receipt with manifest reconciliation. 4: also hysteresis tied to planning lead time with reasons, misdelivery handling across both stores and replenishment, temperature excursions handled when late, a fixed-horizon accuracy metric, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Asks what the store does with an ETA before designing it; distinguishes arrival from receipt. **Red flags.** Naming or assuming a real company's carriers or tracking provider; measuring individual drivers or associates; no plan for missing data.

## WMT-L4-05

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Price history**: two versioned histories per (store, item, channel): *planned* from price files (effective from a store-local time, with the price type: regular, rollback, clearance) and *charged* derived from register line items (shelf price, charged price, override reason). Effective times are stored as UTC instants computed from each store's local time; validity intervals allow "price on a date" lookups.
- **Promotions as data**: promotion id, type, items, store list or zone, planned start and end, and the experiment design (eligible stores, holdout stores, assignment method, assignment date). Price matches are register overrides with their own reason, never promotions.
- **Holdout assignment**: before launch, stratify eligible stores by region, size and recent sales of the item's category (or match pairs), then assign {{holdout_share}} at random within strata; record the assignment; verify after launch from charged prices that holdout stores charged regular price and test stores the rollback (compliance).
- **Lift**: difference in differences on units per store-day: (test during − test pre) − (holdout during − holdout pre), with the pre-period chosen to avoid holiday distortion where possible; store-days with zero on-hand excluded in both groups; reported in units, sales and margin, with a confidence interval from store-level variation (for example by bootstrapping stores). Cannibalisation measured on a defined set of substitutes and halo on complements, reported beside the item's lift; overlapping promotions flagged and excluded from clean estimates or reported jointly.
- **Pipelines**: daily batch computes lift per rollback per region, finalised two days after the rollback ends from snapshotted inputs (price, register, on-hand, assignment) so the result is reproducible; interim reads are labelled provisional.
- **Privacy and aggregation**: results only per item and region with a minimum number of test and holdout stores; never per associate or customer.

**Competing designs to accept.** Synthetic-control or regression adjustment instead of plain difference in differences; matched pairs instead of stratified randomisation; modelling out-of-stock days rather than excluding them. Accept any method that is chosen before launch, handles the stated confounders and reports uncertainty.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Contaminated holdouts; time-zone errors at price boundaries; reason codes reused; out-of-stock censoring demand; holiday seasonality; too few holdout stores for small items; overlapping promotions.

**Incident guidance.**
- *15% of holdouts got the rollback*: detected by the compliance check on charged prices. Analyse by actual exposure (exclude contaminated stores, report intention-to-treat beside it), flag the result's reduced precision, and fix the price-file path so holdout lists override zones; prevention: a pre-launch check that compares the price file's store list with the recorded assignment.
- *Local midnight stored as UTC midnight*: in western stores the planned history starts and ends hours early. Recompute validity intervals from store local time, rebuild affected history and lift, and add a test per time zone; the charged-price history would have shown the mismatch, so add a planned-versus-charged boundary check.
- *Price matches coded as rollbacks*: lift and counts jump with no new promotions. Separate by register reason, restate the affected region's results, and add a monitor on override volume by reason per region.
- *Out of stock in week two*: the lift for the second half reflects missing stock, not demand. Exclude zero-on-hand store-days in both groups, report the out-of-stock rate beside lift, and tell the merchant the rollback likely sold out rather than failed.

**Follow-up guidance.** For follow-up 3, report the item lift and the category net (lift minus cannibalisation) side by side. For follow-up 5, offer daily provisional readouts at region level with clear labelling and a final result after the end; per-store daily lift is mostly noise.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: before-and-after averages in test stores only, planned prices only. 3: planned and charged histories, a recorded pre-launch holdout, difference in differences with out-of-stock exclusion, reason codes separating price types. 4: also stratified or matched assignment with compliance checks, uncertainty and minimum group sizes, cannibalisation and overlap handled openly, reproducible snapshots, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Treats the register's charged price as evidence; asks how holdouts are chosen before discussing the pipeline. **Red flags.** Claiming causal lift from a before-and-after comparison; ignoring out-of-stock days; reporting per store per day as if precise.
