# PayPal · Loop 4 — evaluator notes (hidden from candidates)

## PYP-L4-01

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Two records per payment.** An immutable *payment fact* (`payment_id, participants, note_ref, paid_at`) with no amount in the feed path, and a small mutable *visibility record* (`payment_id, effective_audience, hidden_for, note_removed, visibility_version`). The effective audience is the most restrictive of the participants' choices (each participant's override, or their default in force at payment time). Every change (a participant making it more private, a bulk "all past payments", a hide, a note removal) is an event that bumps `visibility_version`; the record can only move towards more private, so a stale event can never widen it (apply as `min(current, requested)` on an ordered audience scale).
- **Blocks** live in a block store keyed by the unordered pair, with its own version; they are checked on read for every entry whose participants include either party.
- **Fan-out.** Hybrid. For ordinary accounts, fan-out on write puts *references* (`payment_id, paid_at, visibility_version_at_write`) into each eligible viewer's feed list (friends of either participant, depending on the candidate's chosen rule; public entries go to friends' feeds plus a public timeline). For accounts with very large friend lists, no write fan-out: their recent payments are merged in at read time from a per-account recent list. At about {{payments_per_day}} payments a day and a median of {{friends}} friends, write fan-out is roughly payments × 2 participants × friends feed inserts per day; the candidate should do this arithmetic and see why references, not rendered entries, are fanned out.
- **Read path.** Read the viewer's reference list plus merged large-account lists, then batch-fetch visibility records and block state (a fast key-value store, cached with version-tagged entries), drop anything the viewer may not see, then fetch facts and notes. Enforcement happens here, on every surface: home feed, profile, note search, notifications and exports all call the same visibility check library or service. **Fail closed**: if the visibility record cannot be fetched, or a cached copy is older than the latest change the system knows of for that payment (a per-payment "changed at" watermark, or a small recent-changes set held for {{propagation}} plus margin), the entry is not shown.
- **Retroactive bulk change.** "Make all past payments private" is written once as a *per-user floor* (`user_id, audience_floor, effective_from_version`) that the visibility check applies to every payment the user participates in, so it takes effect at read time immediately; a background job then folds it into each payment's visibility record and removes feed references at leisure. No need to rewrite 40,000 × friends entries before the change is honoured.
- **Search, notifications, exports, caches.** The note search index stores the visibility version and is filtered at query time through the same check (and updated from the change stream); rendered page caches are keyed with the viewer and the maximum visibility version they include, or are not used for feed bodies at all. Notifications are re-checked at send time.
- **Rebuilds and replays.** Any rebuild of feed lists or indices reads tombstones and the visibility store, never facts alone; restored snapshots are passed through the read-time check, which uses the current visibility store, so stale references are harmless.
- **Continuous verification.** A sampler re-checks a fraction of served entries against the authoritative visibility store and block store asynchronously and alarms on any violation; synthetic canary accounts with known settings and blocks request feeds and search every minute on every surface.

**Competing designs to accept.** Pure fan-out on read (with good caching of friends' recent lists) if the candidate shows the p99 works at the stated scale. Pure fan-out on write if read-time visibility checks are still applied (writes alone cannot meet {{propagation}} for bulk changes). Showing a new friend old friends-only payments or not, with the choice explained. Enforcing in a central visibility service versus a shared library, if every surface uses it.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Very large friend lists on write; bulk privacy changes generating enormous rewrite jobs; caches and indices with their own copy of visibility; snapshots and replays reintroducing hidden entries; a code path that computes the audience differently from the rule; search refreshed on a slower schedule than the feed.

**Incident guidance.**
- *30,000 payments made private, six hours behind*: the design depended on rewriting every copy before honouring the change. Immediately apply the user's floor at read time (or force-filter that user's payments everywhere) so the target is met; then let the rewrite catch up in its own queue. Prevention: per-user floors, a separate priority queue for privacy changes, and a lag alarm on the oldest unapplied privacy event against {{propagation}}.
- *Failover from a two-hour-old snapshot*: hidden payments came back because rebuilt caches were trusted. Purge or bypass the rebuilt caches, re-filter through the current visibility store, and replay the change stream for the gap. Prevention: read-time checks against the authoritative store, cache entries tagged with visibility versions, and failover runbooks that never serve restored copies unchecked.
- *Audience computed from the payer only*: a privacy violation for three hours. Roll back first; identify every entry served in the window from serving logs and the sampler; follow the privacy incident process (internal notification, assessment, user notification as the process requires — no legal conclusion in the interview). Prevention: one shared audience function with property tests (the result is never less private than any participant's choice), and a canary pair whose recipient is friends-only.
- *Block missing from note search*: search had its own copy of visibility refreshed hourly. Filter search results through the block store at query time now; then move search onto the change stream. Prevention: a list of every surface that serves payments, each with a canary blocked pair.

**Follow-up guidance.** Push on whether the bulk change is honoured before the rewrite finishes; whether any surface (search, notifications, exports) has its own copy of visibility; and what "fail closed" means concretely when the visibility store is slow (serve fewer entries, not unchecked ones). For the new-friend question, accept either answer that is consistent and explained.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: fan-out of rendered entries with privacy applied only at write time; changes require rewriting every copy; no mention of other surfaces. 3: separated facts and visibility, read-time enforcement, hybrid fan-out with numbers, blocks and hides handled, tombstones respected by rebuilds. 4: also per-user floors for bulk changes, version-tagged caches that fail closed, one audience function used everywhere, continuous verification with sampling and canaries, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Says "visibility is checked on read, always" early; computes the fan-out volume; treats search and notifications as feed surfaces. **Red flags.** Amounts in feed entries; a cache with no way to know an entry was hidden; "eventual consistency is fine" for privacy changes with no fail-closed rule; a rebuild that reads only the payments table.

## PYP-L4-02

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Schedule store.** `plan(plan_id, consumer_id, currency, funding_source_id, fallback_opt_in, fallback_order)`, `instalment(plan_id, seq, due_local_date, tz_at_due, amount_minor, state, version)`. Due dates are local calendar dates; the zone used is recorded and changes when the consumer moves (rule chosen and logged). States: `scheduled → attempting → paid | pending_bank | declined → (retry) attempting … → paid | past_due`, plus `paid_manually`, `partially_paid`, `autopay_off`. `pending_bank → returned` reopens the instalment.
- **Scheduler.** A due index partitioned by (due_local_date, market) — the instalments due on a date in a market — and a scheduler that, every few minutes, computes for each time zone whether its local run time (for example 09:00) has passed and enqueues the due instalments for that zone. Run instants are computed at scheduling time from local date + zone (so daylight-saving changes are correct). A checkpoint per (date, zone) lets it catch up after downtime and avoids re-enqueuing; re-enqueuing is harmless anyway because of the next point.
- **Attempt workflow.** A worker does a conditional transition `scheduled → attempting` (compare-and-set on `version`, against the primary, not a replica). Only the winner calls the payment service with attempt id `(plan_id, seq, attempt_no)`; the payment service's idempotency returns the first outcome on repeats. Manual payment, funding change and autopay-off use the same conditional transitions: manual payment moves `scheduled → paid_manually` (or `partially_paid`), so a later autopay CAS fails; if autopay is already `attempting`, the manual path waits for its outcome or the app shows "a payment is in progress". Fallback to the next source only with opt-in, in the consumer's order. Declines schedule a retry on a later day with jitter, capped at {{retries}}.
- **Bank transfers.** Success is `pending_bank` until settled; the consumer is told "pending"; a later return moves the instalment back to due with a clear notice and a new attempt per the rules. Late-fee or hardship decisions come from the given rules service.
- **Notifications.** Every notice is keyed `(plan_id, seq, notice_type, attempt_no)` and recorded before sending (an outbox), so a retry never sends twice; sending is held to 08:00–21:00 in the consumer's zone, and the {{reminder_days}}-day reminder is suppressed if the instalment is already paid.
- **Analytics.** Events for every transition and notice feed aggregates: reminder delivery and open rates, autopay success by attempt number, funding type and market, time from decline to payment, manual versus autopay share. No person-level labels and no predictions about individuals; eligibility and credit decisions are not part of this system.

**Competing designs to accept.** A workflow engine (durable timers per instalment) instead of a due index and scheduler, if the candidate covers catch-up and idempotency. Running autopay early in the local day versus late (trade-off: funds available versus time to retry the same day). Row locks in a relational store instead of version CAS, if lock expiry and fencing are handled.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Due-date spikes (many plans due the same day, often aligned to purchase holidays); one-zone batch jobs; replica lag on the race with manual payments; retry storms after a provider outage; pending bank payments shown as paid or failed; notification duplicates and night-time sends.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Daylight-saving change, autopay a day early*: run instants were computed from a stored UTC offset. Stop the scheduler for affected zones, identify every attempt made before the local due date, refund or reverse charges made early where the consumer asks or the rule requires, and send an apology notice. Prevention: store local date + zone, compute instants at schedule time with a time-zone library, and tests for the transition dates in each of the {{markets}} markets.
- *1,400 double charges after manual payments*: the autopay worker read a replica. Refund the second charges at once and notify; prevention is reading state from the primary and making the attempt conditional on the instalment's version (CAS), with manual payments using the same transition; a reconciliation that alarms on any instalment with two successful charges.
- *Outage, synchronized retries, false failure notices*: retries were scheduled at a fixed hour and pending bank results were reported as failures. Spread retries with jitter and rate limits against the payment service; correct the notices ("your payment is pending; no action needed") to consumers whose transfers were pending; keep failure notices behind a confirmed decline. Prevention: retry budgets, provider-outage detection that pauses attempts instead of burning retries (an outage day should not consume one of the consumer's {{retries}} retries).
- *Reminders at 03:00*: local-hours rule bypassed. Stop the sender, hold remaining notices to the next allowed window, and apologise only if the volume warrants it. Prevention: the quiet-hours check inside the notification path (not in each caller), and a monitor on sends by local hour.

**Follow-up guidance.** For the 08:59/09:00 race, insist on a concrete mechanism (CAS on version or a fenced lock) and on the read being from the primary. For "measure whether reminders help", accept a randomised hold-out of reminder timing (not of reminders altogether, if that is harmful) or a comparison of on-time rates across aggregate cohorts, with no individual labels.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: a nightly cron in UTC, retries in a loop, no idempotency with the payment service, notices sent inline. 3: local due dates with zones, a partitioned scheduler with catch-up, conditional transitions and attempt ids, pending bank state, deduplicated notices in local hours. 4: also fenced handling of the manual-payment race, jittered retry budgets that pause during outages, returns handled with honest notices, aggregate measurement of reminders, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Asks "local date or instant?" first; uses the payment service's idempotency; separates pending from paid. **Red flags.** Due dates stored as UTC timestamps; a charge path that reads a replica; labels such as "risky consumer" in analytics; any credit or eligibility judgement.

## PYP-L4-03

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Events.** `button_rendered` (with `in_viewport` once it becomes visible, measured by an intersection observer in the JS SDK and the platform equivalent in mobile SDKs), `button_clicked` (issues or carries the `session_id`), `signin_started/completed` or `guest_selected`, `funding_category_selected` (balance, bank, card, pay later — category only), `review_shown`, `checkout_cancelled`, `client_error` (error *code* only). Each carries `event_id`, `schema_version`, `sdk_version`, `integration_type`, `merchant_id`, `button_config_id`, platform, country (from the request, coarse), and a client-side sequence number. Server-side outcomes (`completed`, `declined`, `cancelled`) come from the payments platform keyed by `session_id`.
- **Collection.** Fire-and-forget batched sends (beacon-style) that never block checkout; a collector validates against the schema for that `schema_version` and keeps only allowlisted fields; unknown fields and any free text are dropped; values are checked for card-number shapes (digit runs passing a checksum), security-code patterns and email shapes, and events that match are quarantined, not stored. Bot filtering by known automation signals and rate heuristics, kept general.
- **Impressions.** Aggregated in the stream to counts per (minute, merchant, config, sdk_version, integration_type, platform, country, in_viewport) — or sampled with the rate recorded — so the {{renders}} daily renders do not become raw rows forever. Clicks, sessions and outcomes are kept exact.
- **Funnel model.** A session-level table: one row per `session_id` with step reached and timestamps, built by joining client steps with server outcomes. Server outcome is authoritative; a session with a completed outcome but no client events is counted as completed with "client events missing" and reported as a coverage metric per merchant and SDK version. Conversion from impressions uses the aggregated impression counts, with sampling rates applied.
- **Dedupe.** Click dedupe on (`session_id`, step); impression dedupe within a page view on (`page_view_id`, `button_instance_id`) generated by the SDK, so re-renders do not inflate counts.
- **Serving.** Internal dashboards from a warehouse with streaming upserts within {{freshness}}; late events correct the last 48 hours; daily numbers frozen after that. Merchant views read per-merchant aggregates with row-level access by merchant and small-count suppression.
- **Comparing configurations.** Merchants self-select, so compare within merchant (before/after with controls) or by randomised experiments where the SDK can assign configurations, stating the limitation of observational comparisons.

**Competing designs to accept.** Client-side sampling of impressions versus stream aggregation. A lambda-style split (stream for freshness, batch for final). Measuring only "rendered" if the candidate explains why viewport visibility was not worth it, and labels the metric accordingly.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Impression volume; SDK versions that never upgrade; ad-blocked or policy-blocked telemetry; double firing; free text leaking credentials; join gaps between client and server; merchant self-selection in comparisons.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Double "shown" after cart updates*: detect by per-SDK-version ratios (shown per page view, clicks per shown) diverging from other versions. Correct history by deduping on (`page_view_id`, `button_instance_id`) where present, or by applying a per-version correction factor, labelled as such; tell the product team before they roll anything back. Prevention: ratio monitors per SDK version among the {{sdk_versions}} in use and release canaries for the SDK.
- *Merchant blocks telemetry*: completions with no clicks. The funnel must not divide server completions by client clicks blindly; report coverage per merchant and exclude low-coverage merchants from conversion rates, or compute conversion on server-known sessions only. Offer a first-party collection path the merchant can allow.
- *Card-number-shaped strings in raw events*: first hour: stop ingestion of that field (collector rule), quarantine the affected partitions, restrict access, and start the security incident process; following week: purge from raw storage, derived tables, backups per policy and logs, confirm by scanning, ship an SDK fix, and add the field to the denylist forever. Prevention: allowlists per schema version, no free text, scanners on ingestion and at rest.
- *Six hours behind on the busiest day*: dashboards must show freshness, not a fake collapse. Shed load by aggregating impressions harder, scale the stream, and backfill; mark the window provisional. Prevention: capacity tests at peak multiples and a "data as of" banner.

**Follow-up guidance.** Look for the candidate noticing, unprompted, that server outcomes are authoritative and client events are lossy, and that impressions should be aggregated. Push on what exactly prevents credentials from entering (allowlist plus no free text, not only a scanner).

**Rubric anchors.** 2: one events table of everything with a free-form payload; conversion = completions / clicks; "we'll mask card numbers". 3: defined events with schema versions, allowlisted collection, session-level funnel with server outcomes, impression aggregation, merchant-scoped aggregates. 4: also coverage metrics for missing client events, version-level anomaly detection, credential scanners with quarantine and a purge runbook, a fair comparison method, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Distinguishes rendered from visible; asks what joins impressions to sessions (nothing, by design); never wants a shopper identifier. **Red flags.** Card details or emails in events "for debugging"; blocking checkout on telemetry; raw impressions kept forever with no plan; cross-merchant data visible to merchants.

## PYP-L4-04

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Model.** `instrument(instrument_id, user_id, type, institution_id, vault_token, fingerprint, last4, state, state_version)`; `link_attempt(attempt_id, instrument_id, method, provider_id, started_at, outcome, raw_code, normalised_reason, mapping_version)`; `instrument_event` as the append-only history. The fingerprint is a keyed hash produced by the vault (keyed so it cannot be brute-forced back to a number), used to recognise the same card or account across links. States: `started → pending_verification → verified | failed`; `verified → expired → replaced` (new instrument linked by `replaces_instrument_id`) `| removed`; test-deposit links sit in `pending_verification` with `expires_at` = start + {{confirm_days}} days.
- **Providers.** Calls with timeouts; the attempt goes to `awaiting_answer` (not failed) on timeout, and the UI says "still checking" with a bounded wait; callbacks and late answers are applied idempotently by `attempt_id`, and a precedence rule decides conflicts (a verified answer for the same attempt wins over a timeout; if the user started a new attempt, the earlier attempt's verified answer verifies the instrument and the newer pending attempt is closed as superseded). A retry by the user reuses the pending instrument rather than creating another.
- **Card replacements.** The daily network update file is applied as events: old instrument `replaced`, new instrument `verified` with the new token; references (default funding source, pay-later plans, scheduled payments) point at a *funding preference* that resolves to the current instrument in the replacement chain, so they follow automatically. Without an update, an "expired card" decline moves the instrument to `expired` and asks the user, and scheduled payments due soon trigger a prompt before the due date.
- **Failure taxonomy.** Raw provider code + `mapping_version` → stable reason. Unknown codes map to `unmapped` (not "other") and trigger an alert with the raw code, so a new release or provider change is visible within minutes.
- **Analytics.** Funnel per step (start, method chosen, credentials or card entered, provider answer, verified) by method, provider, institution, platform and app version, from attempts and events; aggregates only, no account or card numbers, no user identifiers in shared tables.
- **Linking health and steering.** Per (institution, method, provider) success rate over a short window with a minimum attempt count and hysteresis (trip at a threshold, recover at a better one after a hold time); when tripped, the app offers another method or provider for that institution only. Steering decisions are logged and evaluated against a small hold-out so improvements are real, not displaced failures.
- **Risk.** The given score decides whether an extra step is required; its version is stored on the attempt. No description of how verification could be avoided.

**Competing designs to accept.** A workflow engine per link attempt. Keeping replacement chains in the instrument table versus a separate preference indirection. Per-provider steering decided by an operator with tooling, instead of automatic, if the candidate covers speed.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Retries creating duplicate pending instruments; late answers creating contradictions; mapping drift hiding failures; card replacement waves breaking scheduled payments; stale "pending" screens after expiry; steering that flaps or hides problems.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Provider degraded for a large bank (45% failures, retry pile-up)*: the health signal should trip for (bank, instant check, that provider) and steer to another of the {{providers}} providers or to test deposits; collapse repeated pending instruments into the existing attempt; inform the provider. Prevention: per-institution health with minimum counts, and retries that reuse the pending instrument.
- *Mass card replacement*: apply network updates as they arrive; for scheduled payments and instalments due before an update is likely, prompt users to confirm the new card ahead of the due date; avoid counting replacement-caused declines against consumers in any downstream process. Prevention: a preference indirection so references follow replacements.
- *Unmapped codes counted as "other"*: map unknown to `unmapped`, alert on its share, keep raw codes so history can be re-mapped under a new `mapping_version`; backfill the 40% once mapped. Prevention: a release check that every code the new app version can emit has a mapping.
- *Expired test-deposit links still shown as pending*: the expiry job updated the instrument but not the projection the app reads, or the app caches state. Make the app read state with `expires_at` evaluated on read; send a clear "this link expired — try again" notice; reconcile projections against events daily.

**Follow-up guidance.** For the late "verified" answer, look for idempotency by attempt id and an explicit precedence rule, and for one message to the user. For steering, look for minimum counts, hysteresis and a hold-out.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: one `bank_accounts` table with a status column, provider failures logged as text, analytics on raw logs, numbers stored "encrypted" in the app database. 3: instrument state machine with events, vault tokens, attempts with normalised reasons, test-deposit expiry, card updates applied, per-institution analytics. 4: also late-answer precedence, a preference indirection for replacements, versioned mappings with unmapped alerts, health-based steering with hysteresis and hold-outs, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Treats provider answers as asynchronous facts; keeps raw codes; separates instrument from attempt. **Red flags.** Full card or account numbers anywhere outside the vault; retries that create new instruments; a single global "linking success rate"; describing ways around verification.

## PYP-L4-05

**Reference architecture (one defensible design; score others on the criteria).**
- **Inputs.** Purchase and refund events (purchase id, member, amount minor units, currency, merchant category, time) consumed idempotently by event id; offers and base rates as versioned rule sets with validity intervals; each purchase evaluated under the rule versions in force at purchase time.
- **Reward lots.** One lot per (purchase, rule component) — base and offer, or a combined lot with components — with `amount_minor` (integer, computed once: for example `floor(amount × rate_bp / 10,000)` capped per purchase, or round half up; the rule is stated and versioned), `rule_version`, `state` (`pending`, `available`, `expired`, `clawed_back`), `pending_until` = purchase date + {{return_days}} days, `available_at`, `expires_local_date` = available date + {{expiry_months}} months and `tz`. Remaining amount per lot is a projection of entries.
- **Entries.** Append-only: `earn`, `reduce_pending` (partial refund: reduce proportionally with the same rounding rule; the sum of reductions never exceeds the earn), `make_available`, `redeem` (with a redemption id, against specific lots), `expire`, `claw_back`, `offset_owed` / `offset_applied`, `correction`. Balance, history and statement are projections of entries; nothing else holds a balance of record.
- **Transitions.** A scheduled job moves lots to available after `pending_until`; read paths also treat a pending lot past its date as available and an available lot past its local expiry as expired, so a stalled job delays bookkeeping, not the member's view.
- **Late refunds.** Find the lot by purchase id; if pending, reduce; if available, claw back from that lot's remainder, then from other available lots; under this exercise's rule the balance never goes negative and any remainder becomes an `offset_owed` entry applied to future earnings.
- **Redemption.** A conditional spend: select available lots soonest-expiring first, write `redeem` entries with a redemption id (idempotent) and a per-member version check, so two concurrent redemptions cannot spend the same remainder. Checkout redemption reads the authoritative projection (or a cache validated by member version at commit).
- **Liability and reconciliation.** Daily liability = sum of pending + available remainders by currency (pending often shown separately), computed from entries and checked against the sum of member projections; reconciliations against purchases (every eligible purchase has lots), refunds (every refund has reductions or clawbacks), and redemptions (every redemption has a matching credit in the wallet). Offer funding shares produce merchant-level aggregates for billing.
- **Corrections.** A rule error is fixed by a new rule version and `correction` entries per affected lot, shown to members as dated, explained entries; never a silent recomputation.

**Competing designs to accept.** A double-entry style journal with a liability account (fine, and finance will like it) as long as lots and states remain; a combined lot per purchase; redemption against a member-level balance with lot allocation done at expiry time (if the candidate shows expiry still works soonest-first). Showing pending rewards in a separate balance or only in history.

**Bottlenecks and failure modes.** Late refund feeds; stalled state jobs; expiry in the wrong zone; concurrent redemptions; caches that disagree with entries; rule errors at scale; rounding drift between earn and partial refunds.

**Incident guidance.**
- *Refund feed 12 days late, 8,000 members would go negative*: apply clawbacks by lot state; for members without enough available rewards, record `offset_owed` rather than a negative balance (this exercise's rule); explain the entry in their history. Then reconcile the merchant's refunds against purchases daily and alert on feed delays, so the next late feed is found within a day.
- *Expiry at midnight UTC*: members in the Americas lost rewards the evening before. Reverse those expiries with dated correction entries, re-run expiry by local date and zone, and evaluate expiry on read from `expires_local_date` and `tz`. Prevention: tests for zones on both sides of UTC.
- *Doubled offer rate for nine hours, 400,000 lots still pending*: issue a corrected rule version and `correction` entries for affected lots while they are still pending inside the {{return_days}}-day window, with a clear message; decide (product and finance) whether members keep any of it and record the decision; add a guard on offer configuration (rate bounds, two-person review, a canary on earn totals per hour).
- *Cache disagrees with history for 0.3%*: the cache is not the record. Invalidate on every entry (keyed by member version), make redemption validate against the authoritative version at commit, and run a sampler comparing cached balances with projections.

**Follow-up guidance.** On the 1,999 example, a 3% reward is 59.97, so the stated rule must say 59 (floor) or 60 (half up) and apply the same rule to the partial refund's proportional reduction, with the cap that reductions never exceed the earn. Push on what happens when the pending job stalls and when two redemptions race.

**Rubric anchors.** 2: a `balance` column updated in place, rewards available immediately, expiry by UTC batch, refunds ignored after availability. 3: lots with states and rule versions, entries as the record, return-window pending, clawback, expiry by local date, idempotent redemption. 4: also enforcement on read for stalled jobs, the offset rule, liability reconciled to member balances and to purchases and refunds, corrections as visible entries under new rule versions, and crisp handling of every incident.

**Strong signals.** Lots, not a single counter; integer minor units with one rounding step; "the balance is a projection". **Red flags.** Floating point; silent recomputation of past rewards; negative balances with no rule; any per-member data shared outside the member's own view.
