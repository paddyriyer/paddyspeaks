How to Study

❁

Every verse of the Devī Māhātmyam is laid out the same way, from the words you recite to the grammar that explains them. Read as much or as little as you need: the verse, its words and its meaning are always in view, and the deeper analysis opens when you want it.

The order of each verse

  1. The Sanskrit in large Devanagari, exactly as it is recited, with its Gita Press mantra number (॥२॥). Bījas that belong to the recitation, not to the verse, are shown faintly.
  2. The transliteration (IAST) directly beneath it, line for line. It is generated from the Devanagari, so the two always agree.
  3. Padaccheda — the verse again with every word separated, as in नमः · अस्तु for नमोऽस्तु. Compounds stay whole here; they are opened in the samāsa section.
  4. Word by word — each separated word with its meaning in this verse and its grammar (case, number and gender; root, tense and person).
  5. Translation — a faithful translation that follows the Sanskrit, and a rendering in readable English.
  6. Analysis, in sections you can open: sandhi and sandhi-viccheda with Pāṇini's rules; samāsa and samāsa-vigraha; morphology and the anvaya (prose order); the Śākta commentary; and, where a real connection exists, comparative Śrīvidyā notes.

The commentary and its labels

Every paragraph of commentary says what kind of statement it is, so that what the text says is never confused with what has been read into it:

  • Direct textual meaning what the verse itself says in its story.
  • Traditional interpretation how the tradition reads it; no commentator is named unless the attribution is secure.
  • Comparative Śākta / Śrīvidyā real, specific links with the Lalitā Sahasranāma (Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa) and the Soundarya Lahari, which remain separate works.
  • Modern reflection a present-day reading, marked as such.

Reading controls

On each chapter page the bar at the top shows where you are (“Verse 12 of 104”), moves to the previous or next verse, and jumps to any verse by number. Its Aa menu sets the size of the Sanskrit, shows the verse in Devanagari, IAST or both, and opens or closes all the grammar sections at once. Every verse has a Copy button for its Sanskrit. Your settings are remembered on this device only.

The three caritas

The thirteen chapters fall into three episodes: the Prathama Carita (chapter 1, the slaying of Madhu and Kaiṭabha), the Madhyama Carita (chapters 2–4, Mahiṣāsura) and the Uttama Carita (chapters 5–13, Śumbha and Niśumbha). In the recitation tradition they are presided over by Mahākālī, Mahālakṣmī and Mahāsarasvatī; that assignment comes from the liturgy (the Gita Press viniyoga), not from the Purāṇa itself.

Text and verification

The Sanskrit is the Gita Press Śrīdurgāsaptaśatī, compared letter by letter with two independent witnesses. The separated words are proven by rejoining them by the rules of sandhi, and every chapter's verse counts match its own colophon. The corrections, variant readings and the verification of each chapter are on the Sources page.
