Source, corrections and variants

The text

Edition. Śrīdurgāsaptaśatī, Gita Press, Gorakhpur (Hindi translation by Paṇḍit Rāmanārāyaṇadatta Śāstrī “Rāma”). Digital text: github.com/sanskrit/raw_etexts — purANam/durgA-saptashatI/goraxapura-pAThaH/raw/source.html.

Numbering. Gita Press mantra numbering: every uvāca, half-śloka, śloka and three-part mantra is one mantra; 700 in all, restarting in each chapter.

Witnesses compared. Every chapter was aligned letter by letter against two independent texts:

  • GRETIL Mārkaṇḍeyapurāṇa adhyāyas 81–93 (Sansknet input, not proof-read) — via github.com/sanskrit/raw_etexts mixed/gretil_devanAgarI
  • Peter Freund's Śrī Durgā Saptaśatī transcription — via github.com/sanskrit/raw_etexts kAvyam/padyam/peterFreund/stotram/durga_saptashati.md

Where the Gita Press text differed from both witnesses, the difference was examined. Four were errors of the digital file and were corrected (below); the rest are genuine readings and are kept as printed, with the other reading recorded. Nothing was added from the other recensions and nothing was silently changed.

Recension and metre

  • GRETIL's Mārkaṇḍeyapurāṇa (adhyāyas 81–93) carries several verses the Gita Press text does not (e.g. after 1.101, in 2.20, 2.45, 2.65, 3.21, 3.43). They belong to the Purāṇa recension; the Gita Press 700 is the recited Saptaśatī and is followed here. Nothing was added to it.
  • GRETIL counts ślokas in pairs of half-lines and does not number the uvāca lines, so its verse numbers differ from the Gita Press mantra numbers throughout. This edition numbers by the Gita Press mantra.
  • 1.92: First pāda मधुकैटभौ दुरात्मानौ is nine syllables (hypermetric), as in every witness.
  • 2.2: Second pāda महिषेऽसुराणामधिपे is nine syllables, as in every witness.

Supplementary texts

The Kavaca, Argalā, Kīlaka, the Rātri and Devī Sūktas and the Rahasyas that the Gita Press prints around the Saptaśatī are separate liturgical texts and are not part of this edition. Each chapter shows the viniyoga and dhyāna that the pāṭha recites before it, kept apart from the numbered mantras. The site's Durgā Sūktam is a separate Vedic hymn.

How the analysis is checked

For every mantra the separated words are rejoined by external sandhi (vowel, visarga and consonant rules, with Pāṇini's sūtras) and must spell the recited text; a vowel left unjoined must be declared and explained (a pragṛhya dual, a metrical licence). The word table must list exactly the separated words in order. Every sandhi example must occur in its verse, every compound and analysed form in its word list. Commentary is labelled by its authority, and no commentator or citation is invented. Doubtful readings and interpretations are flagged on the verse and listed in the chapter's report.
