Uttama Carita · Chapter 6 of 13 · 24 mantras (6.1–6.24)

धूम्रलोचनवधः

dhūmralocanavadhaḥ

The Slaying of Dhūmralocana · Complete

Introduction

Summary
Medhas takes up the story where chapter 5 left it (6.1). The messenger, seething with indignation, reports the Devī’s words to Śumbha in full (6.2). Śumbha, enraged, orders Dhūmralocana, a commander of the daityas, to take his own army and bring her by force, dragged helpless by the hair (6.3–4), and to kill anyone — god, yakṣa or gandharva — who comes forward to protect her (6.5). Dhūmralocana sets off at once with sixty thousand asuras (6.6–7), finds the Devī on the snowy mountain, and shouts at her to go to Śumbha and Niśumbha willingly or be dragged there by the hair (6.8–9). The Devī answers with a single ironic verse: if a strong man, sent by the lord of the daityas and surrounded by an army, takes her by force, what can she do to him? (6.10–11). He rushes at her, and Ambikā burns him to ashes with the sound ‘huṃ’ alone (6.12–13). She showers his furious army with arrows, spears and axes (6.14); her lion, mane tossing and roaring, falls upon it and kills with paw, jaws, claws and the flat of the paw, beheading some and drinking the blood of others (6.15–18), until in a moment the whole army is destroyed (6.19). Hearing that the Devī has slain Dhūmralocana and her lion his army, Śumbha, his lip trembling, orders Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa to take a great force and bring her, dragged by the hair or bound; if that is in doubt, to have all the asuras strike her down with every weapon; and, when she and her lion are felled, to bind her and come back (6.20–24).
Forms of the Devī
The Devī (देवी, 6.2, 6.8, 6.20) and Ambikā (6.13, 6.14, 6.24) — the Ambikā who came forth from Pārvatī’s body and is sung as Kauśikī (5.87) — still abiding on Himālaya (6.8); the speaker of one ironic verse (6.11); the one whose ‘huṃ’ burns an asura to ash (6.13); the warrior who rains arrows, spears and axes (6.14); and, through her lion, ‘the Devī’s own mount’ (6.15, 6.19, 6.20), the destroyer of an army. The asuras’ names for her — ‘that insolent one’ (दुष्टा, 6.4, 6.24), the polite ‘lady’ (भवती, 6.9) — are their view of her, not hers. Padmāvatī, of the dhyāna, is the form of the liturgy, not of the text.
Significance of the events
This is the first of the envoys sent against the Devī after her challenge in chapter 5 (5.120–121): Dhūmralocana, then Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa (chapter 7), then Raktabīja (chapter 8), and finally Niśumbha and Śumbha themselves. Each defeat brings a stronger force and a harsher order. The chapter turns on a contrast of scale: sixty thousand asuras and their general (6.7) against one syllable (6.13) and one lion (6.19). Śumbha’s orders also change in the course of it — from ‘bring her’ (6.4) to ‘if it is in doubt, strike her down’ (6.23) — and they end in contradiction (6.24).
Sanskrit patterns to watch
A short narrative chapter: reduplicated perfects (ययौ, जगाद, पपात, जघान, पपौ, चुकोप) beside periphrastic perfects (पाटयामास, 6.17; आज्ञापयामास, 6.21); chains of absolutives leading to one finite verb (6.2, 6.3, 6.8, 6.20); conditional sentences of several shapes (यदि … सः, 6.5; न … चेत् … ततः, 6.9; an implied condition answered by ततः, 6.11; यदि … तदा, 6.23); passive participles with an instrumental agent (6.18, 6.19, 6.20); a double accusative with कृ (तं भस्म चकार, 6.13); commands put in the passive (समानीयताम्, 6.22; विनिहन्यताम्, 6.23) and the impersonal passive (आगम्यताम्, 6.24); and a locative absolute (तस्यां हतायाम्, 6.24). Sentences run across verses at 6.20–21 and 6.22–23.
The teaching (as distinct from the narrative)
Literally, a king sends a general to seize a woman; she burns him with a syllable, her lion destroys his army, and the king sends two more. As teaching, the chapter shows what force cannot do. The asuras speak only of strength — बल is army and violence at once (6.4, 6.9, 6.11) — and imagine that she can be defended only by someone else (6.5); the Goddess needs no one, and her power is so far beyond theirs that a sound is enough (6.13). Read devotionally, ‘huṃ’ is heard as her own sound acting as her weapon, and her lion as her power acting through what belongs to her; that reading is the tradition’s, not a statement of the verse. A modern reflection: the wish to possess, refused, turns to rage and then to the wish to destroy (6.9, 6.23).

Themes

  • Force against power: the army and the syllable
  • The hair-dragging order: the asura will to possess
  • The Devī’s lion: her own protector
  • The escalation of envoys
  • Desire refused turning to rage

    Members: अमर्ष + पूरित · Vigraha: अमर्षेण पूरितः amarṣeṇa pūritaḥ
    Literally “filled with non-endurance”; in the verse: seething with resentment at the Devī’s refusal
  • दैत्यराजाय daityarājāya — ṣaṣṭhī tatpuruṣa

    Literally “to the king of the daityas”; in the verse: to Śumbha. At the end of a tatpuruṣa राजन् becomes the a-stem राज (a samāsānta suffix), hence the dative राजाय.

C. Morphology

  • समाचष्ट samācaṣṭa — Imperfect of सम् + आ + √चक्ष्: the augment a follows the prefixes and merges with आ (सम्-आ-अ-चष्ट); before the ending -त the k of the conjunct kṣ is lost and ṣ + t becomes ṣṭ (ष्टुना ष्टुः, 8.4.41).
  • आकर्ण्य ākarṇya — Absolutive in -य (ल्यप्), the form taken when the verb has a prefix (आ). √कर्ण् ‘to ear, hear’ is a nominal verb from कर्ण ‘ear’.
  • समागम्य samāgamya — Absolutive in -य of सम् + आ + √गम्. Grammar allows the nasal to stay or be lost before ल्यप्: समागम्य or समागत्य; the text has the first.

देव्याः इति वचः आकर्ण्य अमर्षपूरितः सः दूतः समागम्य दैत्यराजाय विस्तरात् समाचष्ट।

Two absolutives (आकर्ण्य, समागम्य) lead to the one finite verb समाचष्ट; the subject is सः दूतः with its predicate adjective अमर्षपूरितः.

6Translation

LiteralHaving heard thus the words of the Devī, that messenger, filled with indignation, having come back, reported (them) in detail to the king of the daityas.

ReadableWhen he had heard these words of the Devī, the messenger, seething with indignation, went back and told the king of the daityas everything at length.

7Commentary

Direct textual meaningThe messenger is Sugrīva (5.102). What he reports is the Devī’s vow — whoever conquers her in battle and matches her strength will be her husband (5.120) — and her invitation to Śumbha or Niśumbha to come and win her (5.121, 5.129). His anger is the anger of a servant whose master has been slighted.

Traditional interpretationThe uttama-carita moves by escalation: after the messenger come Dhūmralocana (this chapter), Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa (chapter 7), Raktabīja (chapter 8), and finally Niśumbha and Śumbha themselves. Each refusal brings a stronger envoy.

C. Morphology

  • असुरराट् asurarāṭ — Nominative of the consonant stem राज् ‘king’ (as in सम्राट्): the final j becomes ṣ, then a stop at the end of the word, ṭ (राट्). The same stem gives असुरराजम्, असुरराजः in other cases.
  • प्राह prāha — प्र + आह: आह belongs to √ब्रू; grammar makes it a present form with perfect-type endings, but the epics and Purāṇas use it as the narrative past ‘(he) said’.

7Commentary

Direct textual meaningŚumbha takes up the threat his messenger had already made (5.126: केशाकर्षणनिर्धूतगौरवा, ‘your dignity shaken off by being dragged by the hair’). 6Translation

LiteralThus addressed, that asura Dhūmralocana rushed at her. Then she, Ambikā, made him ash with the sound ‘huṃ’ alone.

7Commentary

Direct textual meaningThis verse answers her question in 6.11. The man who came to drag her away with sixty thousand soldiers is undone by one syllable; एव ‘alone, merely’ carries the point. The Devī has used her हुंकार before: in 3.12 Ambikā strikes down a hurled spear with it (हुंकाराभिहताम्).

Modern reflectionA modern reflection: the one who meets force with force needs an army; the one in whom power rests needs only a syllable. The asura’s display of strength is answered by stillness.

6Translation

LiteralSome daityas with a blow of his paw, others with his mouth, and others, having pounced on (them), with his lower jaw — he slew the great asuras.

ReadableHe slew the great asuras: some with a blow of his paw, others with his jaws, and others he pinned down and killed with his lower jaw.

7Commentary

Direct textual meaningThe lion fights as a lion fights: paw, jaws, the weight of its body. The next three verses go on with the same catalogue of carnage until the army is gone (6.19).

Textual Verification Required / uncertain: अधरेण: अधर is ‘the lower lip’ (as in प्रस्फुरिताधरः, 6.21), taken here as the lower jaw, so that the lion pins its prey and bites.     visarga sandhi · ससजुषो रुः (8.2.66)
    After ai the visarga is sounded r before a vowel (सर्वैरसुरैः) and before a voiced consonant (असुरैर्विनि-).

B. Samāsa and samāsa-vigraha

  • अशेषायुधैः aśeṣāyudhaiḥ — karmadhāraya; its first member अशेष is a nañ-bahuvrīhi
    Members: अशेष + आयुध · Vigraha: अशेषाणि आयुधानि, तैः (अशेष = न विद्यते शेषः यस्य तत्) aśeṣāṇi āyudhāni, taiḥ (aśeṣa = na vidyate śeṣaḥ yasya tat)
    Literally “with weapons without remainder”; in the verse: with every weapon there is
    Alternative: Also possible as a bahuvrīhi agreeing with असुरैः: अशेषाणि आयुधानि येषां तैः, ‘by (all the asuras) who bear every weapon’. The karmadhāraya, an instrument beside the agent असुरैः, is the more direct reading; the sense hardly differs.

C. Morphology

  • बद्ध्वा baddhvā — Absolutive of √बन्ध्: the nasal is lost and dh + t becomes ddh (बध् + त्वा → बद्ध्वा).
  • विनिहन्यताम् vinihanyatām — Passive imperative of वि + नि + √हन्: stem हन्य- with the ātmanepada ending -ताम्, ‘let (her) be struck down’. The subject (सा) is carried over from 6.22.
  • युधि yudhi — Locative singular of the root noun युध् ‘battle’, a feminine consonant stem.

D. Anvaya — prose order

केशेषु आकृष्य बद्ध्वा वा (सा समानीयताम्)। यदि वः युधि संशयः, तदा सर्वैः असुरैः अशेषायुधैः (सा) विनिहन्यताम्।

keśeṣu ākṛṣya baddhvā vā (sā samānīyatām) | yadi vaḥ yudhi saṃśayaḥ, tadā sarvaiḥ asuraiḥ aśeṣāyudhaiḥ (sā) vinihanyatām |

The two absolutives complete the order of 6.22 (‘bring her, dragging her by the hair or binding her’). Then a conditional clause (यदि … संशयः) and its main clause with the correlative तदा and a passive imperative.

6Translation

LiteralHaving dragged (her) by the hair or having bound (her) — (bring her). If there is doubt for you in the fight, then let (her) be struck down by all the asuras with every weapon.

ReadableDrag her by the hair or bind her, and bring her. If the fight should leave the outcome in doubt, then let all the asuras strike her down with every weapon they have.

The first line depends on समानीयताम् in 6.22; ‘and bring her’ is supplied. यदि वः संशयो युधि, ‘if there is doubt for you in battle’, is taken as ‘if taking her alive proves doubtful’.

7Commentary

Direct textual meaningŚumbha repeats the hair-dragging order (6.4) and adds a fallback: if she cannot be taken, let the whole army kill her. The man who wanted her as the jewel among women (5.100, 5.112) is now willing to see her dead.

C. Morphology

  • हतायाम् hatāyām — Locative absolute (सति सप्तमी): a noun and a participle in the locative make a clause of time or condition — तस्यां हतायाम् ‘when she has been struck down’, सिंहे विनिपातिते ‘when the lion has been felled’.
  • आगम्यताम् āgamyatām — Impersonal passive (bhāve prayoga) of the intransitive आ + √गम्: ‘let it be come (by you)’ — a formal way of commanding ‘come back’.
  • विनिपातिते vinipātite — Past participle of the causative of वि + नि + √पत् ‘to make fall down’: ‘felled, struck down’.
  • गृहीत्वा gṛhītvā — Absolutive of √ग्रह्: samprasāraṇa (ग्रह् → गृह्) and the connecting vowel lengthened to ī, as in all forms of this root.

D. Anvaya — prose order

दुष्टायां तस्यां हतायां, सिंहे च विनिपातिते, अथ ताम् अम्बिकां बद्ध्वा गृहीत्वा (च) शीघ्रम् आगम्यताम्।

duṣṭāyāṃ tasyāṃ hatāyāṃ, siṃhe ca vinipātite, atha tām ambikāṃ baddhvā gṛhītvā (ca) śīghram āgamyatām |

Two locative absolutes set the condition; then the impersonal imperative आगम्यताम्, preceded by two absolutives with their object ताम् अम्बिकाम्.

6Translation

LiteralWhen she, the wicked one, has been struck down, and the lion felled, then, having bound and seized that Ambikā, let (it) be come back quickly.

ReadableWhen that wicked woman has been struck down and her lion felled, then seize Ambikā, bind her, and come back at once.

हत ‘struck’ is taken as ‘struck down, overpowered’, not ‘killed’, since she is then to be bound and brought. The impersonal आगम्यताम् is rendered as a command, and the two absolutives are given in natural order (seize, bind).

7Commentary

Direct textual meaningŚumbha’s orders end in contradiction: strike her down (6.23), yet bind her and bring her back (6.24). He wants both her defeat and her person. The chapter closes on the command, and chapter 7 tells how it was carried out. In the Gita Press pāṭha the closing ॐ attached to this mantra belongs to the recitation.

Textual Verification Required / uncertain: हतायाम्: √हन् means both ‘strike’ and ‘kill’. If she is killed, the order to bind and bring her makes little sense, so हता is read as ‘struck down, overpowered’; some translations render it ‘slain’.

Chapter conclusion

Narrative summary

Śumbha does not answer the Devī’s challenge himself. He sends Dhūmralocana with sixty thousand asuras and the order to drag her back by the hair. She asks, with irony, what a lone woman could do against such strength; then a single ‘huṃ’ turns the general to ash, and her lion, in a moment, destroys his army. The chapter ends not with the battle but with Śumbha’s next and harsher order, to Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa: bring her, or strike her down (6.21–24).

Important grammatical constructions

  • Absolutives leading to one finite verb (इत्याकर्ण्य … समागम्य … समाचष्ट, 6.2; दृष्ट्वा … जगाद, 6.8).
  • Conditionals: यदि … सः हन्तव्यः (6.5), न चेत् … ततः (6.9), an implied condition answered by ततः (6.11), यदि … तदा (6.23).
  • Double accusative with कृ: तं भस्म चकार, ‘made him ash’ (6.13).
  • Passive participle with an instrumental agent: तेन … कृताः (6.18), केसरिणा क्षयं नीतम् (6.19), देव्या निहतम् (6.20).
  • Commands in the passive (समानीयताम्, 6.22; विनिहन्यताम्, 6.23) and the impersonal passive of an intransitive verb (आगम्यताम्, 6.24).
  • The locative absolute: तस्यां हतायां … सिंहे च विनिपातिते, ‘when she has been struck down and the lion felled’ (6.24).
  • The periphrastic perfect of -aya stems (पाटयामास, 6.17; आज्ञापयामास, 6.21) beside the reduplicated perfect (ययौ, पपौ, जघान).
  • सः and एषः drop their visarga before a consonant (स दूतः, 6.2; एष केशा-, 6.9), but before a they follow the ordinary rule (सोऽभ्यधावत्, 6.13).

Śākta insights

  • The Devī needs no instrument against the asura’s general: one syllable, ‘huṃ’, is enough (6.13), and the word एव ‘alone’ carries the point. Devotees hear her sound itself as her power.
  • The protector Śumbha expects to come from outside — a god, a yakṣa, a gandharva (6.5) — is already hers: ‘the Devī’s own mount’ (6.15, 6.19). She depends on no other being.
  • The asuras think in terms of बल; the Devī turns the word back on them three times in one verse (6.11) and answers it with a power of another order (6.13).
  • The Goddess cannot be ‘brought’: three times the asuras demand that she be taken by force (6.4, 6.9, 6.22–24), and she stays where she is, on the mountain (6.8). Her own condition was that only the one who conquers her in battle may claim her (5.120).
  • The wish to possess her turns, when refused, into the wish to destroy her: from ‘my master’ (6.9) to ‘let all the asuras strike her down’ (6.23).

Śrīvidyā connections

Two names of the Devī in this chapter are also names in the Lalitā Sahasranāma (Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa): Ambikā (295, अम्बिका), the name used for her here at 6.13, 6.14 and 6.24, and Daityahantrī (599, दैत्यहन्त्री, ‘slayer of daityas’), which names what the chapter shows. The Lalitā Sahasranāma also tells of the Goddess’s forces destroying an asura army — Bhaṇḍāsura’s (65, 72) — but that is a different myth, and the likeness is one of pattern, not evidence that one text depends on the other. The ‘huṃ’ of 6.13 is a syllable of general mantra practice, not of Śrīvidyā in particular. No specifically Śrīvidyā doctrine is stated in this chapter.
