JobSignal · Methodology

What “verified” actually means here.

Plainly, without marketing language, because a trust claim you cannot check is not a trust claim.

The one sentence

A role is marked verified when the employer’s own applicant tracking system still lists that requisition — the same feed their public careers page is built from. That is all the badge claims. It is not a promise that anyone will read your application, that the headcount is approved, or that the role will still be open tomorrow. Nobody outside the company can promise those things, and a product that implies it can is selling you something.

How a role gets here

  1. We keep a registry of employers and the public, documented JSON endpoint their ATS publishes — Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, SmartRecruiters, Workable, Recruitee.
  2. Every four hours we read each employer’s board: one request per employer, not one per job.
  3. Each posting is normalised — title, location, remote status, seniority, skills — and any salary is read from a structured field or from an explicit range in the employer’s own words. We never estimate one.
  4. Duplicates collapse to a single result. Where the same opening appears in several places, the copy closest to the employer wins.
  5. The role is checked against everything we already knew about it, and published.

Why a job disappears

When a requisition stops appearing in its employer’s feed twice in a row, we mark it closed and move it to the archive. Two runs, not one, because a single network timeout would otherwise close an employer’s entire board. Closed roles are never deleted. They stay in the archive permanently, which is what lets us tell you that the role you are looking at has been posted before.

The rule we care most about

The date a job was first seen is written once and never changed. If an employer deletes a requisition and republishes the same role under a new number, most boards show you “Posted today”. We do not. The new posting really is hours old — so instead of a freshness badge, the card says REPOSTED, and the detail page shows you every run of that role we have on record, with dates. We match a repost on the employer, the normalised title, the location and the department, with a description-similarity check for roles that were lightly reworded. It is not perfect. When we are not confident two postings are the same role, we treat them as different ones, because inventing a history is worse than missing one.

What we will never say

We will never tell you a job is fake, a “ghost job”, or that an employer is acting in bad faith. We have no way to know that, and a job board is not a court. What you get instead is the evidence: where it came from, the requisition number, the employer’s own posting date, the day we first saw it, the minute we last checked, how many times this role has run before, and exactly which host the Apply button opens. Some of that will read as reassuring and some of it as a caution. Both are just facts with a date attached.

What this does not cover

  • We only watch employers in our registry. A role not here is not a judgement about that employer — we simply are not reading their board yet.
  • We read public ATS APIs only. No scraping, no logging in, no working around a rate limit or a robots file.
  • LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor and ZipRecruiter are never a source of truth here, and the Apply button never sends you to one.
  • Seniority, skills and work arrangement are derived from the employer’s own text and are occasionally wrong. The description is always there to check.
  • History starts the day JobSignal first saw a role, which may be well after the employer first posted it. We never shorten it, but we cannot extend it backwards.

Who pays for this

Nobody. No employer can pay to appear here, to appear higher, to look fresher, to hide a repost, or to remove a caution signal. There is no sponsored placement and no advertising. The ranking code takes no such input — not as a matter of policy, but because the parameter does not exist. If that ever changes, sponsored results will be labelled and kept out of organic ones, and this page will say so.

Found something wrong? Tell us — reports are treated as a reason to re-check, never as automatic proof.
The full design, schema and verification algorithm are documented in the repository.
