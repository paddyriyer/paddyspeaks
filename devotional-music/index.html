Read this before you trust a ragam on this page Ramadasu composed sahityam — words. He left no notation, and Telugu devotional song was not systematically notated until much later. Every ragam below therefore comes from later performance tradition, principally 19th and 20th century musicians and Mangalampalli Balamuralikrishna's influential 1950s settings. Where the sources genuinely disagree — and for Rama Daya Judave they disagree three ways — each keertana is tagged sources differ and the disagreement is spelled out rather than resolved by fiat. Attributions consistent across every source consulted are tagged attested. Lyrics are reproduced only as far as they could be corroborated; where they could not, the page says so instead of filling the gap.

The Ragams రాగములు

Every ragam used in this collection — its parent scale, its ascent and descent, the bhava it carries, and why it suits the text it was given. Arohana and avarohana follow common modern practice; several janya ragams have school-specific (bani) variants, so treat these as the usual form rather than the only one.

Ramadasu Is Not Praying Politely ధార్ష్ట్యం

What is unusual in these songs is not what Ramadasu asks for. It is the standing he assumes while asking — and how far he is willing to push it before he stops.
