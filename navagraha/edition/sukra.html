Faithful English translation

Bring forth for the bright, the radiant one, your offering and [your] thought, well purified, to Agni, who goes with knowledge among all births, divine and human.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

A triṣṭubh verse addressed to the priests: they are to bring their offering and their prayer to Agni, the bright and radiant, who knows and moves among all kinds of beings, divine and human. In the Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa (2.8.2) it is the yājyā for the omentum (vapā) of a victim offered to Agni, as Sāyaṇa states; it is also Ṛgveda 7.4.1. For Śukra (Venus) the tradition takes the verse because it begins with the word śukra, ‘bright’: that is all the words themselves show. The use of the verse for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition, not to the Veda: in its source it serves another rite (see the commentary).

In the rite The first mantra of Śukra’s step in the Navagraha sūktam, the graha’s own mantra in this tradition; the Saṃskāraratnamālā has śukraṃ te anyat instead (see the landing page).

Faithful English translation

Indrāṇī, among these women, I have heard [to be] the one with a good husband; for her husband never at all dies of old age.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

A verse praising Indrāṇī, the wife of Indra: among all women she is the one whose husband is best, for Indra never ages and never dies. In the Taittirīya Saṃhitā (1.7.13.1) it is the puronuvākyā of an offering of rice to Indrāṇī, made — Bhāskara says — for one whose army lacks spirit; Bhāskara’s gloss ends by asking that Indrāṇī make that army eager. The verse also stands in the Ṛgveda (10.86.11), in the dialogue hymn of Indra, Indrāṇī and Vṛṣākapi, with a different second word (subhagām for supatnīm) and the refrain viśvasmād indra uttaraḥ. The use of the verse for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition, not to the Veda: in its source it serves another rite (see the commentary).

In the rite The second mantra of Śukra’s step in the Navagraha sūktam, addressed to Indrāṇī. S names the adhidevatā and pratyadhidevatā together in the salutation that follows, without saying which mantra is whose.

What it says and does

A short gāyatrī verse calling on Indra: the priests invoke him on behalf of the sacrificers, ‘above all peoples’, and ask that he be theirs alone — that his help go to them and not to others. The use of the verse for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition, not to the Veda: in its source it serves another rite (see the commentary).

In the rite The third mantra of Śukra’s step in the Navagraha sūktam, addressed to Indra. S names the adhidevatā and pratyadhidevatā together in the salutation that follows, without saying which mantra is whose.

What it says and does

The salutation that closes each graha’s part of the sūktam: homage to Śukra — Venus, ‘the bright one’ — together with the adhidevatā and the pratyadhidevatā, the two deities associated with the graha. The formula names them together and does not say who they are; nor does the witness say which of the graha’s three mantras is addressed to which. The dative शुक्राय names the one honoured; namaḥ, ‘homage’, is the whole of the act.

In the rite Step 3 (Śukra), after Śukra’s three mantras (pra vaḥ śukrāya, indrāṇīm, indraṃ vaḥ). The same formula ends each of the nine steps, with the graha’s name in the dative.

The witness writes the compound in three parts (अधिदेवता प्रत्यधिदेवता सहिताय); grammatically it is one word, agreeing in case with the graha’s name. The svara marks on the last two words are the witness’s.
