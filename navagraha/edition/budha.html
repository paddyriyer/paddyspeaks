Faithful English translation

Wake up, Agni; watch over him; may you two join sacrifice and gift, [you] and this one. Again making you a father, a young one, he has stretched along this thread in you.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

A verse of the fire-altar books of the Taittirīya Saṃhitā (4.7.13). Agni is told to wake and watch over the sacrificer, and the two of them, Agni and he, are to bring about together the rites and the good works — iṣṭāpūrta, what is offered in sacrifice and what is given for the good of others. The second half speaks of the sacrificer, or of Agni, renewing the fire, making him a young father again, and stretching the thread of the rite through him. Sāyaṇa reads iṣṭāpūrta as the rites of the Veda and of smṛti, and the thread as the unbroken course of the sacrifice. The verse addresses Agni; the verb it opens with, budh, ‘wake’, is the root of the graha’s name, Budha, ‘the wakeful’.

In the rite The mantra of Budha (Mercury), first of the three in his step of the Navagraha sūktam (witness S). The verse itself addresses Agni; the word that links it to Budha is its first, ud-budhyasva, from the same root. Its use for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition.

The commentators
Traditional Interpretation

Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa on TS 4.7.13: Agni is to rouse himself and keep the sacrificer alert; then अयं च त्वं च मिलित्वेष्टापूर्ते श्रौतस्मार्तकर्मणी संसृजेथां संपादयतम् — ‘he and you together, bring about iṣṭa and pūrta, the rites of śruti and smṛti’; and Agni, making the sacrificer young again, एतं तन्तुं यज्ञप्रवाहमन्वातांसीदनुक्रमेण तनोतु — ‘may he stretch out this thread, the stream of the sacrifice, in due order’.

What it says and does

The verse of Viṣṇu’s three strides (RV 1.22.17; TS 1.2.13.1). Viṣṇu strode across this world, setting down his foot three times, and the whole world is held in the dust of his footprint. Bhāskara takes the three steps as earth, mid-air and heaven, and the dust as the worlds themselves, gathered in the lotus of his foot; Sāyaṇa, on the Ṛgveda, names Viṣṇu as the one who took the form of Trivikrama and cites Yāska’s discussion of the three places. In the Saṃhitā the verse belongs to the building of the havirdhāna, the shed for the soma carts: an offering is made in the track of the southern cart’s wheel. The verse addresses Viṣṇu.

In the rite The second mantra of Budha’s step in the Navagraha sūktam (witness S). S names the adhidevatā and pratyadhidevatā together, so the edition does not say which this verse is for; the verse itself addresses Viṣṇu. Its use for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition.

What it says and does

A string of short yajus formulas (TS 1.2.13.3) said while the havirdhāna, the shed that houses the soma carts, is put together: the shed is treated as Viṣṇu’s body. Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara gives the act for each formula: the front-board is fastened as Viṣṇu’s brow, the middle mat laid as his back, the side mats as the two corners, a cord drawn through as his seam, the door-knot tied as the fixed one; the last words, ‘you are Viṣṇu’s, to Viṣṇu you’, touch the shed, वैष्णवं हि देवतया हविर्धानम् — the havirdhāna belongs to Viṣṇu as its deity. The verse addresses the parts of the shed as Viṣṇu’s.

In the rite The third mantra of Budha’s step in the Navagraha sūktam (witness S). S names the adhidevatā and pratyadhidevatā together, so the edition does not say which this formula is for; it names Viṣṇu throughout. Its use for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition.

The commentators
Traditional Interpretation

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara

Bhāskara gives the act for each part: रराटीमासञ्जयति — he fastens the front-board, ‘you are Viṣṇu’s brow’, explained as विष्णोर्यज्ञात्मनो ललाटमिव प्रधानमसि; the side mats are श्नप्त्रे शोधके स्थः, ‘you two are the purifiers’; the knot is विष्णुना ध्रुवम् अचलितं करणीयम् असि; and the close, वैष्णवं हि देवतया हविर्धानम्, is cited from the Brāhmaṇa.

Faithful English translation

Homage to Budha, together with the adhidevatā and the pratyadhidevatā.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

The salutation that closes each graha’s part of the sūktam: homage to Budha — Mercury, ‘the wise one’ — together with the adhidevatā and the pratyadhidevatā, the two deities associated with the graha. The formula names them together and does not say who they are; nor does the witness say which of the graha’s three mantras is addressed to which. The dative बुधाय names the one honoured; namaḥ, ‘homage’, is the whole of the act.

In the rite Step 5 (Budha), after Budha’s three mantras (udbudhyasva, idaṃ viṣṇuḥ, viṣṇo rarāṭam). The same formula ends each of the nine steps, with the graha’s name in the dative.

Notes
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

The witness writes the compound in three parts (अधिदेवता प्रत्यधिदेवता सहिताय); grammatically it is one word, agreeing in case with the graha’s name. The svara marks on the last two words are the witness’s.
