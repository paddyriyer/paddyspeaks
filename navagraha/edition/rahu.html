What it says and does

A question in praise: with what help, with what mighty power, will the bright one, our ever-growing friend, come to us? In the Ṛgveda (4.31.1) the friend is Indra, as Sāyaṇa says. In the Navagraha sūktam it is the first mantra for Rāhu, recited before āyaṃ gauḥ and yat te devī. The witness gives no reason for the choice, and none is offered here.

In the rite Step 8 (Rāhu), first mantra. The use of a Vedic verse for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition; the commentary below speaks of the verse in its Vedic setting. In the Saṃhitā (TS 4.2.11) it is a puronuvākyā; Sāyaṇa’s viniyoga names the offering.

The commentators
Traditional Interpretation

Sāyaṇa’s viniyoga

Sāyaṇa’s viniyoga places the verse in the Saṃhitā as the invitatory verse (puronuvākyā) of an offering on one potsherd: अथ कायमेककपालमित्यस्य पुरोनुवाक्यामाह.

Traditional Interpretation

Sāyaṇa on the Ṛgveda

On the Ṛgveda verse Sāyaṇa takes the friend to be Indra: सदावृधः सदा वर्धमानः चित्रः चायनीयः पूजनीयः सखा मित्रभूत इन्द्रः, and the question as कया उती ऊत्या तर्पणेन नः अस्मान् आ भुवत् आभिमुख्येन भवेत्.

What it says and does

A riddle-verse of fire and sun: the dappled bull strides out, comes to rest on its mother the Earth, and goes on to its father, the Sky. Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara reads the bull as Agni praised in the form of the Sun. In the Navagraha sūktam it is the second mantra for Rāhu; the witness names the graha’s adhidevatā and pratyadhidevatā together and does not say which mantra belongs to which.

In the rite Step 8 (Rāhu), second mantra. The use of a Vedic verse for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition; the commentary below speaks of the verse in its Vedic setting. In the Saṃhitā (TS 1.5.3.1) it is among the mantras of the punarādhāna, the setting up again of the sacred fires, as the corpus files it; it is also RV 10.189.1.

Faithful English translation

The cord, not to be loosed, which the goddess Nirṛti bound on your neck — this of yours I untie, as from the middle of life; then, living, eat food, set free.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

A releasing verse: the speaker unties the noose that Nirṛti, the goddess of decay, tied on a person’s neck, and bids him, freed and alive, eat his food. Sāyaṇa reads it as addressed to the sacrificer, whose bonds of evil are loosed. In the Navagraha sūktam it is the third mantra for Rāhu, before the salutation; the witness names the graha’s adhidevatā and pratyadhidevatā together and does not say which mantra is whose.

In the rite Step 8 (Rāhu), third mantra. The use of a Vedic verse for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition; the commentary below speaks of the verse in its Vedic setting. In the Saṃhitā (TS 4.2.5.3) it belongs to the piling of the fire altar: three black bricks for Nirṛti are covered with a net and laid away from the altar (Sāyaṇa’s viniyoga).

The commentators
Traditional Interpretation

Sāyaṇa’s viniyoga

Sāyaṇa cites the Kalpa: यत्ते देवी निर्ऋतिराबबन्धेति शिक्वजालेनैनाः प्रच्छाद्य, the bricks are covered with a net with this verse.

Traditional Interpretation

Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa addresses the verse to the sacrificer: Nirṛti has bound on his neck निर्ऋतिर्देवी यद्दाम स्वकीयं पाशमविचर्त्यं विनाशयितुमशक्यम्; the speaker says तदिदं ते दाम त्वदीयग्रीवास्थं पाशं विष्यामि विमुञ्चामि, and then त्वं जीवीश्चिरंजीवी सन्प्रमुक्तः सर्वैः प्रतिबन्धैर्विरहितः पितुमन्नमद्धि भक्षय. What it says and does

The salutation that closes each graha’s part of the sūktam: homage to Rāhu — the ascending node of the Moon — together with the adhidevatā and the pratyadhidevatā, the two deities associated with the graha. The formula names them together and does not say who they are; nor does the witness say which of the graha’s three mantras is addressed to which. The dative राहवे names the one honoured; namaḥ, ‘homage’, is the whole of the act.

In the rite Step 8 (Rāhu), after Rāhu’s three mantras (kayā naś citraḥ, āyaṃ gauḥ, yat te devī). The same formula ends each of the nine steps, with the graha’s name in the dative.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

The witness writes the compound in three parts (अधिदेवता प्रत्यधिदेवता सहिताय); grammatically it is one word, agreeing in case with the graha’s name. The svara marks on the last two words are the witness’s.
