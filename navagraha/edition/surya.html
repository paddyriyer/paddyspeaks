1 Savitṛ comes on his golden chariot Vedic · TS 3.4.11.2

आ स॒त्येन॒ रज॑सा॒ वर्त॑मानो निवे॒शय॑न्न॒मृत॒म्मर्त्य॑ञ्चहि॒र॒ण्यये॑न सवि॒ता रथे॒ना दे॒वो या॑ति॒ भुव॑ना वि॒पश्य॑न्

ā satyena rajasā vartamāno niveśayannamṛtammartyañcahiraṇyayena savitā rathenā devo yāti bhuvanā vipaśyan

Moving through the abiding sky, setting the immortal and the mortal each in its place, Savitṛ the god comes on his golden chariot, watching over every world.

Faithful English translation

Hither through the true region turning, settling the immortal and the mortal, with the golden chariot Savitṛ the god comes, looking upon the worlds.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

The first verse of the midday upasthāna, recited standing and facing the Sun. It pictures the Sun as Savitṛ, the god who sets everything going: he travels the sky on a golden chariot, and as he goes he puts gods and mortals each in their place and looks over all the worlds. In the Taittirīya Saṃhitā it is the yājyā verse for an offering to Savitṛ in the Rājasūya (TS 3.4.11.2, with Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara’s viniyoga); the same verse is the second of a hymn to Savitṛ in the Ṛgveda (1.35.2). At midday the worshipper stands before the Sun at the top of its course, and the verse describes exactly that: the god overseeing everything below.

In the rite The first mantra of the Navagraha sūktam, recited for Sūrya, the Sun: the verse names Savitṛ, the Sun as the god who impels, and pictures him on his golden chariot. The use for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition; in the Saṃhitā the verse is a yājyā of the Rājasūya (see the commentary). In the Sandhyāvandanam it opens the midday upasthāna.

The commentators
Traditional Interpretation

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara, on TS 3.4.11, reads the verse as a prayer for Savitṛ’s coming: सविता देवः, भुवना भुवनानि भूतजातानि विपश्यन् विविधं पश्यन् हितेन योजयन् आयाति आगच्छतु. ‘Real’ is ‘everlasting’ (सत्येन नित्येन), the region is the world of light, the Sun’s disc (रजसा लोकेन ज्योतिर्मयेन मण्डलात्मना), and ‘turning’ is the daily round of rising and setting (उदयमस्तमयं चानुपूर्व्येण कुर्वाणः).

Traditional Interpretation

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara’s viniyoga

In the Saṃhitā the verse is the yājyā of the offering to Savitṛ: तत्रैव याज्या - आ सत्येनेति त्रिष्टुप्.

Sanskrit analysis
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

Sandhi

  • वर्तमानो निवेशयन् vartamāno niveśayan → वर्तमानः + निवेशयन्

    visarga

    -aḥ before a voiced consonant becomes -o

  • निवेशयन्नमृतम् niveśayannamṛtam → निवेशयन् + अमृतम्

    consonant

    a final n after a short vowel is doubled before a vowel (P. 8.3.32 (ङमो ह्रस्वादचि ङमुण्नित्यम्))

  • रथेना देवो rathenā devo → रथेन + आ + देवः

    vowel

    a + ā becomes ā; then -aḥ before a voiced consonant becomes -o

Compounds

No compound.

Forms

  • भुवना — the old neuter plural in -ā (for later -āni), common in the Ṛgveda.
  • आ — the preverb stands apart from its verb (tmesis) and is repeated before it; both belong to yāti.
Notes
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

The verse is a triṣṭubh (eleven syllables to the pāda). The Taittirīya and the Ṛgveda (1.35.2) have the same words; the difference is only spelling and the accent marks of the two traditions.
