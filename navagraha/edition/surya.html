Faithful English translation

Hither through the true region turning, settling the immortal and the mortal, with the golden chariot Savitṛ the god comes, looking upon the worlds.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

The first verse of the midday upasthāna, recited standing and facing the Sun. It pictures the Sun as Savitṛ, the god who sets everything going: he travels the sky on a golden chariot, and as he goes he puts gods and mortals each in their place and looks over all the worlds. In the Taittirīya Saṃhitā it is the yājyā verse for an offering to Savitṛ in the Rājasūya (TS 3.4.11.2, with Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara’s viniyoga); the same verse is the second of a hymn to Savitṛ in the Ṛgveda (1.35.2). At midday the worshipper stands before the Sun at the top of its course, and the verse describes exactly that: the god overseeing everything below.

In the rite The first mantra of the Navagraha sūktam, recited for Sūrya, the Sun: the verse names Savitṛ, the Sun as the god who impels, and pictures him on his golden chariot. The use for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition; in the Saṃhitā the verse is a yājyā of the Rājasūya (see the commentary). In the Sandhyāvandanam it opens the midday upasthāna.

The commentators
Traditional Interpretation

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara, on TS 3.4.11, reads the verse as a prayer for Savitṛ’s coming: सविता देवः, भुवना भुवनानि भूतजातानि विपश्यन् विविधं पश्यन् हितेन योजयन् आयाति आगच्छतु. ‘Real’ is ‘everlasting’ (सत्येन नित्येन), the region is the world of light, the Sun’s disc (रजसा लोकेन ज्योतिर्मयेन मण्डलात्मना), and ‘turning’ is the daily round of rising and setting (उदयमस्तमयं चानुपूर्व्येण कुर्वाणः).

Traditional Interpretation

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara’s viniyoga

In the Saṃhitā the verse is the yājyā of the offering to Savitṛ: तत्रैव याज्या - आ सत्येनेति त्रिष्टुप्.

Faithful English translation

We choose Agni as messenger, as invoker, all-knowing, skilful in act of this sacrifice.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

A three-line gāyatrī verse to Agni, the sacrificial fire. The sacrificers choose him for three offices: as messenger (dūta) who carries the offering and the prayer to the gods, as hotṛ who calls the gods to the sacrifice, and as the one skilled in the sacrifice’s work. In the Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa (3.5.2) it is the eighth of the sāmidhenī verses, recited while the fire is kindled with sticks at the start of an iṣṭi; it is also the first verse of the Ṛgveda’s twelfth hymn (1.12.1). The use of the verse for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition, not to the Veda: in its source it serves another rite (see the commentary).

In the rite The second mantra of Sūrya’s step in the Navagraha sūktam, addressed to Agni. S recites it with the graha’s own mantra and names the adhidevatā and pratyadhidevatā together in the salutation that follows, without saying which mantra is whose.

The commentators
Traditional Interpretation

Sāyaṇa

Sāyaṇa, on TB 3.5.2, reads it as the eighth sāmidhenī (अष्टम-सामिधेनी-पाठस् तु) and explains: इमम् अग्निं वयं वृणीमहे प्रार्थयामहे, with Agni as दूतं देवान् प्रति प्रेषणार्हं, होतारम् आह्वातारं.

Traditional Interpretation

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara: अग्निं दूतं हितकारिणं सर्वेषां होतारं आह्वातारं विश्ववेदसं विश्वस्य वेदितारं — the messenger is the one who works for the good of all.

What it says and does

A triṣṭubh verse of the animal sacrifice. The victim belongs to Paśupati, the lord of beasts — Rudra — as do all creatures with four feet or two; the verse ‘buys it off’ from him so that it may pass to the gods’ share, and prays that the sacrificer’s wealth in cattle may grow. In the Taittirīya Saṃhitā (3.1.4.1) it belongs to the mantras for leading up the victim (Bhāskara calls it the second of the series). The use of the verse for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition, not to the Veda: in its source it serves another rite (see the commentary).

In the rite The third mantra of Sūrya’s step in the Navagraha sūktam, naming Paśupati (Rudra). S names the adhidevatā and pratyadhidevatā together in the salutation that follows, without saying which mantra is whose.

Faithful English translation

Homage to Āditya, together with the adhidevatā and the pratyadhidevatā.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

The salutation that closes each graha’s part of the sūktam: homage to Āditya — the Sun, ‘son of Aditi’ — together with the adhidevatā and the pratyadhidevatā, the two deities associated with the graha. The formula names them together and does not say who they are; nor does the witness say which of the graha’s three mantras is addressed to which. The dative आदित्याय names the one honoured; namaḥ, ‘homage’, is the whole of the act.

In the rite Step 1 (Sūrya), after Sūrya’s three mantras (ā satyena, agniṃ dūtam, yeṣām īśe). The same formula ends each of the nine steps, with the graha’s name in the dative.

Notes
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

The witness writes the compound in three parts (अधिदेवता प्रत्यधिदेवता सहिताय); grammatically it is one word, agreeing in case with the graha’s name. The svara marks on the last two words are the witness’s.
