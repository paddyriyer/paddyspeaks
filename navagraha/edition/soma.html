What it says and does

A gāyatrī verse to Soma, who is both the sacred drink and the Moon: it asks him to swell and grow full, to gather vigour from every side, and to be present where strength and nourishment are gathered. In the Taittirīya Saṃhitā (4.2.7.4) it belongs to the building of the fire altar: the Kalpa, as Sāyaṇa reports, uses it when the sand is spread out on the altar, and Sāyaṇa explains that a verse in which Soma is the deity is used because Soma bears the seed. It is also Ṛgveda 1.91.16. The verse names Soma, and āpyāyasva ‘swell’ is the word for the waxing of the Moon. The use of the verse for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition, not to the Veda: in its source it serves another rite (see the commentary).

In the rite The first mantra of Soma’s step in the Navagraha sūktam, the graha’s own mantra.

What it says and does

An anuṣṭubh verse in praise of the waters: Soma himself has said that every remedy lies within the waters, together with Agni who is good for all, and that the waters are the remedy of all things. The Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa has it at 2.5.8; it is also Ṛgveda 1.23.20, in a hymn to the waters. The commentary in the sources does not discuss this verse. The verse names Soma, and Soma speaks in it. The use of the verse for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition, not to the Veda: in its source it serves another rite (see the commentary).

In the rite The second mantra of Soma’s step in the Navagraha sūktam, in praise of the Waters. S names the adhidevatā and pratyadhidevatā together in the salutation that follows, without saying which mantra is whose.

What it says and does

A riddle-verse from the long hymn of Dīrghatamas (RV 1.164.41), also in the Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa (2.4.6). A cow, Gaurī, lows and shapes the waters; she grows from one foot to two, four, eight and nine, and becomes a thousand syllables in the highest heaven. Sāyaṇa reads her first as the thunder, the speech of the middle region, whose sound brings the rain and spreads to the quarters of the sky; then, with others, as speech itself, which names and so makes things, and grows from a single undivided sound into countless words. The verse addresses neither Soma nor a graha: it praises Gaurī, speech.

In the rite The third mantra of Soma’s step in the Navagraha sūktam (witness S), after āpyāyasva and apsu me. S names the adhidevatā and pratyadhidevatā together, so the edition does not say which this verse is for; the verse itself praises Gaurī. Its use for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition.

The commentators
Traditional Interpretation

Sāyaṇa on the Ṛgveda

Sāyaṇa, on the Ṛgveda, takes Gaurī first as the thunder: माध्यमिका वाक्, the speech of the middle region; the waters are rain, and the feet are her stations — one in the cloud, two with the sky, four with the quarters, eight with the intermediate quarters, nine with the zenith. ‘A thousand syllables’ means without measure: अपरिमितव्याप्तियुक्ता. He adds a second reading, ‘some say’: Gaurī is speech as the Word-Brahman, शब्दब्रह्मात्मिका वाक्.

What it says and does

The salutation that closes each graha’s part of the sūktam: homage to Soma — the Moon — together with the adhidevatā and the pratyadhidevatā, the two deities associated with the graha. The formula names them together and does not say who they are; nor does the witness say which of the graha’s three mantras is addressed to which. The dative सोमाय names the one honoured; namaḥ, ‘homage’, is the whole of the act.

In the rite Step 4 (Soma), after Soma’s three mantras (āpyāyasva, apsu me, gaurī mimāya). The same formula ends each of the nine steps, with the graha’s name in the dative.
