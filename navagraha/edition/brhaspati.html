Faithful English translation

Bṛhaspati, that which the noble one may deserve above [others], which shines out brilliantly and effectively among men, which may shine with strength, O born of ṛta — that bright wealth place in us.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

A prayer to Bṛhaspati, the lord of sacred speech, the gods’ priest (RV 2.23.15; TS 1.8.22.2). He is asked to place in the worshippers the wealth that a noble man deserves above others, that shines among men and blazes with strength. Sāyaṇa, on the Ṛgveda, takes that wealth to be the radiance of sacred knowledge, brahmavarcasa, and quotes a Brāhmaṇa that uses the verse for one who wants that radiance; Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara, on the Saṃhitā, takes it as wealth a lord rightly deserves. The verse names Bṛhaspati in its first word.

In the rite The mantra of Bṛhaspati (Jupiter), first of the three in his step of the Navagraha sūktam (witness S): the verse addresses Bṛhaspati by name. Its use for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition.

The commentators
Traditional Interpretation

Sāyaṇa on the Ṛgveda

Sāyaṇa, on the Ṛgveda, addresses Bṛhaspati as ऋतप्रजात सत्येनोत्पन्न and reads the wealth as the radiance of sacred knowledge; he cites a Brāhmaṇa that uses the verse for one who is ब्रह्मवर्चसकामो, desirous of that radiance.

Traditional Interpretation

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara, on the Saṃhitā: the noble one is स्वामी विद्वान्राजा वा, a lord, a learned man or a king; the wealth shines शवसा बलेन अन्नेन वा, with strength or with food; and Bṛhaspati is सत्य-प्रभव, sprung from truth.

What it says and does

An invitation to Indra, with his troop of storm-gods the Maruts, to drink the soma here as he once drank it at the sacrifice of Śaryāti’s son (RV 3.51.7; TS 1.4.18.1). The wise who sacrifice well seek him under his guidance and in his shelter. In the Saṃhitā it is the verse with which the second marutvatīya cup of soma is drawn, at the midday pressing. The verse addresses Indra.

In the rite The second mantra of Bṛhaspati’s step in the Navagraha sūktam (witness S). S names the adhidevatā and pratyadhidevatā together, so the edition does not say which this verse is for; the verse itself addresses Indra. Its use for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition.

What it says and does

A verse of creation and revelation (TS 4.2.8.2). Brahman is born first, in the east; Vena, ‘the longing one’, whom Sāyaṇa takes as the lovely sun, opens the bright rays from the horizon and uncovers the deep forms of the world and the womb of both the existent and the non-existent. In the Saṃhitā it accompanies the placing of the rukma, a gold plate, on a lotus leaf at the centre of the fire altar: Sāyaṇa reads the gold plate as Brahman and the rising sun as its likeness. The verse addresses Brahman, the sacred power.

In the rite The third mantra of Bṛhaspati’s step in the Navagraha sūktam (witness S). S names the adhidevatā and pratyadhidevatā together, so the edition does not say which this verse is for; the verse itself speaks of Brahman. Its use for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition.

What it says and does

The salutation that closes each graha’s part of the sūktam: homage to Bṛhaspati — Jupiter, ‘lord of prayer’ — together with the adhidevatā and the pratyadhidevatā, the two deities associated with the graha. The formula names them together and does not say who they are; nor does the witness say which of the graha’s three mantras is addressed to which. The dative बृहस्पतये names the one honoured; namaḥ, ‘homage’, is the whole of the act.

In the rite Step 6 (Bṛhaspati), after Bṛhaspati’s three mantras (bṛhaspate ati, indra marutvaḥ, brahma jajñānam). The same formula ends each of the nine steps, with the graha’s name in the dative.

Notes
Linguistic and Textual Analysis

The witness writes the compound in three parts (अधिदेवता प्रत्यधिदेवता सहिताय); grammatically it is one word, agreeing in case with the graha’s name. The svara marks on the last two words are the witness’s.
