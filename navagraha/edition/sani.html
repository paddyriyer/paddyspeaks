Faithful English translation

For welfare to us, the divine Waters, for help — may they be for drinking; welfare and health may they flow towards us.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

A prayer to the Waters as goddesses (RV 10.9.4, in the hymn whose first three verses are āpo hi ṣṭhā; TB 1.2.1). May they be a blessing to us, for help and for drinking, and may they flow towards us with śaṃ and yoḥ. Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara explains the two old words: śam is the calming of ills that have come, yoḥ the keeping off of those that have not. In the Brāhmaṇa it belongs to the installing of the sacred fires: the ground is sprinkled with water. The verse addresses the Waters; its first words, śaṃ no, sound like the graha’s name, Śani.

In the rite The mantra of Śanaiścara (Saturn), first of the three in his step of the Navagraha sūktam (witness S). The verse itself addresses the Waters; what links it to Śani is the sound of its opening, śaṃ no. Its use for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition.

The commentators
Traditional Interpretation

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara’s viniyoga

Bhāskara gives its use in the Brāhmaṇa’s rite of installing the fires: अद्भिरवोक्षति - शंनो देवीरिति गायत्र्या — he sprinkles with water, with this gāyatrī.

Traditional Interpretation

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara

Bhāskara: the Waters are to be a blessing अभीष्टफलसिद्धये, for the attaining of what is wished, and पीतये पानाय च भवन्तु, for drinking; śam is आगतानां रोगभयादीनां शमनम्, the calming of illness and fear that have come, and yoḥ keeps away those that have not.

Faithful English translation

Prajāpati, no other than you has encompassed all these born things; with whatever desire we offer to you, may that be ours; may we be lords of riches.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

The last verse of the hymn to the unknown god (RV 10.121.10), which names him at the end as Prajāpati, lord of creatures; also in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā (1.8.14.2). No one but Prajāpati encompasses all that is born; may the wish with which we offer to him be fulfilled, and may we be lords of riches. Sāyaṇa takes ‘encompass’ as ‘grasp and create’: only Prajāpati can hold all beings and bring them forth. The verse addresses Prajāpati.

In the rite The second mantra of Śanaiścara’s step in the Navagraha sūktam (witness S). S names the adhidevatā and pratyadhidevatā together, so the edition does not say which this verse is for; the verse itself addresses Prajāpati. Its use for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition.

The commentators
Traditional Interpretation

Sāyaṇa on the Ṛgveda

Sāyaṇa, on the Ṛgveda: no other grasps or pervades these beings — त्वमेवैतानि परिगृह्य स्रष्टुं शक्नोषीति भावः, ‘you alone can hold them and create them’.

Traditional Interpretation

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara

Bhāskara, on the Saṃhitā: Prajāpati pervades all beings निजेन महिम्ना, by his own greatness; and the prayer is for the wish with which we offer, and to be lords of riches always.

What it says and does

A call to Yama, king of the Fathers, to come with the Aṅgirases and sit on the grass spread for the gods, drawn by the poets’ mantras, and to rejoice in the offering. In the Navagraha sūktam it is the third mantra recited for Śanaiścara (Saturn), after śaṃ no devīḥ and prajāpate; the verse itself names Yama, and the witness names the adhidevatā and pratyadhidevatā of the graha together, without saying which mantra is whose.

In the rite Step 7 (Śanaiścara), third mantra, before the salutation. The use of a Vedic verse for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition; the commentary below speaks of the verse in its Vedic setting. In the Taittirīya Saṃhitā (TS 2.6.12.6) the corpus files the verse among the hotṛ’s mantras for the offering to the Fathers; it is also RV 10.14.4.

The commentators
Traditional Interpretation

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara reads Yama as the king of the Fathers and the Aṅgirases as a class of Fathers: अङ्गिरोभिः पितृविशेषैः संविदानः ऐकमत्यं गच्छन्; the mantras are those कविभिर्मेधाविभिः आस्माकीनैः ऋत्विग्भिः शस्ताः.

Traditional Interpretation

Sāyaṇa on the Ṛgveda

What it says and does

The salutation that closes each graha’s part of the sūktam: homage to Śanaiścara — Saturn, ‘the slow-moving one’ — together with the adhidevatā and the pratyadhidevatā, the two deities associated with the graha. The formula names them together and does not say who they are; nor does the witness say which of the graha’s three mantras is addressed to which. The dative शनैश्चराय names the one honoured; namaḥ, ‘homage’, is the whole of the act.

In the rite Step 7 (Śanaiścara), after Śanaiścara’s three mantras (śaṃ no devīḥ, prajāpate, imaṃ yama). The same formula ends each of the nine steps, with the graha’s name in the dative.

Linguistic and Textual Analysis

The witness writes the compound in three parts (अधिदेवता प्रत्यधिदेवता सहिताय); grammatically it is one word, agreeing in case with the graha’s name. The svara marks on the last two words are the witness’s.
