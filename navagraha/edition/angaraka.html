Aṅgāraka (Mars) अङ्गारकः

The mantra of Aṅgāraka, then two more for the deities who attend it (the witness names the adhidevatā and pratyadhidevatā together, not which mantra is whose), then the salutation.

1 Agni, the head of heaven Vedic · TS 4.4.4.1

अ॒ग्निर्मू॒र्धा दि॒वः क॒कुत्पतिः॑ पृथि॒व्या अ॒यम्अ॒पाँ रेताँ॑सि जिन्वति

agnirmūrdhā divaḥ kakutpatiḥ pṛthivyā ayamapāṃ retāṃsi jinvati

Agni is the summit of heaven and the lord of the earth; it is he who quickens the seed in the waters.

Faithful English translation

Agni is the head of heaven, its peak; this one is the lord of the earth; he quickens the seeds of the waters.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

A gāyatrī verse that praises Agni as spanning the whole world: he is the head and summit of heaven, as the sun, and lord of the earth, as fire; and he quickens the ‘seeds of the waters’ — Bhāskara takes this as the rain by which Agni, in his middle form, nourishes all that moves and does not move. In the Taittirīya Saṃhitā (4.4.4.1) it belongs to the fire-altar (agnicayana) mantras; it is also Ṛgveda 8.44.16. Its use for Aṅgāraka, the red planet, belongs to the later gṛhya tradition; the word aṅgāra itself means a glowing coal.

In the rite The first mantra of Aṅgāraka’s step in the Navagraha sūktam, the graha’s own mantra; the two that follow (syonā pṛthivi, Mahānārāyaṇa 10.1.10, and kṣetrasya patinā) are named with it in the salutation.

Traditional Interpretation

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara: अयम् अग्निर् मूर्धा प्रधानभूतः दिवः द्युलोकस्य ककुत् उच्छ्रितः; अयमेव पृथिव्याः पतिः पाता; अग्निर् हि पृथिव्यात्मना वर्तते; he quickens the seeds of the waters माध्यमिकाग्निरूपेण, वृष्ट्या तर्पयति.

Traditional Interpretation

Sāyaṇa on the Ṛgveda

2 Syonā pṛthivi: be kindly, Earth Vedic · Mahānārāyaṇa 10.1.10 (TA 10)

स्योना पृथिवि भवा नृक्षरा निवेशनीयच्छा नः शर्म सप्रथाः

syonā pṛthivi bhavā nṛkṣarā niveśanīyacchā naḥ śarma saprathāḥ

The rite recites part of this unit; the translation is of the whole unit.

Brahman, born first in the east and longed for by all, opened up the shining worlds from their bounds, and laid open the deep quarters near at hand, the dwellings of this world, the source of what is and of what is not. Be kind to us, Earth; you bring forth people and give them a place to live; give us shelter, wide one. [Over the earth taken for the bath:] I call here Śrī, known by her fragrance, unshakeable, ever-nourished, rich in cattle, mistress of all beings. May fortune come to me; may misfortune leave me. The gods, led by Viṣṇu, won these worlds with the metres, never to be won back. May great Indra, thunderbolt in hand, full like the sixteen-digit moon, give us shelter.

This is a mantra of the Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad (Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10), where its text is proven and analysed in full: padaccheda, word by word, translation, the three commentaries. Mahānārāyaṇa 10.1.10 in the Upaniṣad edition.

3 With the lord of the field as a friend Vedic · TS 1.1.14

With the Lord of the Field as our friend we win cattle and horses and food that makes us thrive; may he be kind to us in all of this.

Faithful English translation

With the lord of the field, as with a friend, we win cow, horse, nourishment; may he be gracious to us in such [a matter].

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

A verse to Kṣetrapati, the lord of the field — the divinity of cultivated land. With his help, as with a friend’s, the sacrificers win cattle, horses and nourishing food, and they ask him to be gracious in it. In the Taittirīya Saṃhitā (1.1.14) it stands among the verses for the optional iṣṭis (kāmyeṣṭi), followed by its companion kṣetrasya pate; it is also Ṛgveda 4.57.1. Sāyaṇa on the Ṛgveda notes that some take the lord of the field to be Rudra, others Agni, others a deity of his own. The use of the verse for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition, not to the Veda: in its source it serves another rite (see the commentary).

In the rite The third mantra of Aṅgāraka’s step in the Navagraha sūktam, naming Kṣetrapati. S names the adhidevatā and pratyadhidevatā together in the salutation that follows, without saying which mantra is whose.

What it says and does

The salutation that closes each graha’s part of the sūktam: homage to Aṅgāraka — Mars, ‘the glowing coal’ — together with the adhidevatā and the pratyadhidevatā, the two deities associated with the graha. The formula names them together and does not say who they are; nor does the witness say which of the graha’s three mantras is addressed to which. The dative अङ्गारकाय names the one honoured; namaḥ, ‘homage’, is the whole of the act.

In the rite Step 2 (Aṅgāraka), after Aṅgāraka’s three mantras (agnir mūrdhā, syonā pṛthivi, kṣetrasya patinā). The same formula ends each of the nine steps, with the graha’s name in the dative.
