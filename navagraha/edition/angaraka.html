Aṅgāraka (Mars) अङ्गारकः

The mantra of Aṅgāraka, then two more for the deities who attend it (the witness names the adhidevatā and pratyadhidevatā together, not which mantra is whose), then the salutation.

2 Ambhasy apāre: the one beyond the waters, the gāyatrīs of the gods, and the mantras of the bath Vedic · Mahānārāyaṇa 10.1.10 (TA 10)

स्योना पृथिवि भवा नृक्षरा निवेशनीयच्छा नः शर्म सप्रथाः

syonā pṛthivi bhavā nṛkṣarā niveśanīyacchā naḥ śarma saprathāḥ

The rite recites part of this unit; the translation is of the whole unit.

Brahman, born first in the east and longed for by all, opened up the shining worlds from their bounds, and laid open the deep quarters near at hand, the dwellings of this world, the source of what is and of what is not. Be kind to us, Earth; you bring forth people and give them a place to live; give us shelter, wide one. [Over the earth taken for the bath:] I call here Śrī, known by her fragrance, unshakeable, ever-nourished, rich in cattle, mistress of all beings. May fortune come to me; may misfortune leave me. The gods, led by Viṣṇu, won these worlds with the metres, never to be won back. May great Indra, thunderbolt in hand, full like the sixteen-digit moon, give us shelter.

This is a mantra of the Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad (Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10), where its text is proven and analysed in full: padaccheda, word by word, translation, the three commentaries. Mahānārāyaṇa 10.1.10 in the Upaniṣad edition.
