Faithful English translation

Making a banner for the bannerless, form for the formless, O men, you were born together with the dawns.

Word for word; words supplied for English are in [brackets].

What it says and does

A verse of light at daybreak: one born with the dawns gives a sign to what has none and form to what is formless. The address ‘O men’ (maryāḥ) and the second person make it a riddle in the Ṛgveda (1.6.3). Its first word is ketu, ‘banner’, which is also the name of the graha Ketu, and in the Navagraha sūktam it is Ketu’s own mantra. The choice belongs to the later gṛhya tradition, which does not state its reason; none is added here.

In the rite Step 9 (Ketu), first mantra. The use of a Vedic verse for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition; the commentary below speaks of the verse in its Vedic setting. In the Saṃhitā (TS 7.4.20.1) it belongs to the attendance on the horse of the Aśvamedha; Bhāskara says a banner is fastened on with it.

The commentators
Traditional Interpretation

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara: with this verse a banner is fastened, ध्वजं प्रतिमुञ्चति - केतुं ध्वजं कृण्वन् कुर्वन्तु; the ‘form’ is पेशः रूपं रूपवृन्दं हे मर्याः मनुष्याः, and the last line he takes as ‘be born as if with blazing flames’, उषद्भिः ज्वलद्भिरिव समजायथाः.

What it says and does

A praise of Soma by comparisons: as the brahman priest is first among gods, the seer among the inspired, the falcon among birds of prey, so Soma, singing, passes through the purifying filter and surpasses all that purifies. In the Navagraha sūktam it is the second mantra for Ketu; the witness names the graha’s adhidevatā and pratyadhidevatā together, without saying which mantra is whose.

In the rite Step 9 (Ketu), second mantra. The use of a Vedic verse for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition; the commentary below speaks of the verse in its Vedic setting. In the Saṃhitā (TS 3.4.11.1) it is a yājyā of the Rājasūya offerings, as Bhāskara’s introduction says; it is also RV 9.96.6.

The commentators
Traditional Interpretation

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara

Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara introduces it as a yājyā, तत्रैव याज्या, and reads the comparisons: देवानां मध्ये ब्रह्मा सोमः ब्रह्मात्मना वर्तते; so Soma, among all that purifies, goes beyond: एवं सोमः पवित्राणां मध्ये पवित्रमत्येति.

Faithful English translation

You, O bright one, bright, conspicuous [wealth] for us, O you of bright dominion, most bright, giving vigour, gleaming wealth, rich in men, great, O shining one, with shining [hymns], to the one who praises, bestow.

What it says and does

A prayer for wealth, built on one sound: citra, ‘bright’, comes five times and candra, ‘shining’, three, all around one request — give the singer bright, great wealth. It is a Ṛgveda verse (6.6.7), from a hymn whose first verse praises the ‘son of strength’, the purifying fire. In the Navagraha sūktam it is the third mantra for Ketu, before the salutation; the witness marks it as a ṛk and names the graha’s adhidevatā and pratyadhidevatā together.

In the rite Step 9 (Ketu), third mantra. The use of a Vedic verse for a graha belongs to the later gṛhya tradition. The witness marks this verse (ऋक्), and it is not in the Taittirīya texts the edition draws on; its text is the Ṛgveda’s (GRETIL, without svara), and no commentary on it is in the edition’s sources.

What it says and does

The salutation that closes each graha’s part of the sūktam: homage to Ketu — the descending node of the Moon — together with the adhidevatā and the pratyadhidevatā, the two deities associated with the graha. The formula names them together and does not say who they are; nor does the witness say which of the graha’s three mantras is addressed to which. The dative केतवे names the one honoured; namaḥ, ‘homage’, is the whole of the act.

In the rite Step 9 (Ketu), after Ketu’s three mantras (ketuṃ kṛṇvan, brahmā devānām, sa citra). The same formula ends each of the nine steps, with the graha’s name in the dative.
