Navagraha Sūktam

Sources, Variants and Corrections

The witnesses

  • W1 — Base witness: the Taittirīya Saṃhitā in pañcāśat divisions (vishvAsa/vedAH_yajuH, saMhitA/mUlam/panchAti-kramaH; keyed from TITUS and Parankuśācārya) and the Āraṇyaka of muralīdhara (AraNyakam/mUlam/muralIdharaH.md), svara marked (commit b5d616ee16)
  • W2 — Second witness: the vākya-krama Saṃhitā (Weber’s orthography) and the gaṇeśvara Āraṇyaka (same corpus) (commit b5d616ee16)
  • RV — The Ṛgveda parallels (GRETIL, from Aufrecht 1877; Śākala recension, no svara), github.com/sanskrit/raw_etexts (commit 61cbc00bf5)
  • S — S — NavagrahaSuktam.tex of github.com/stotrasamhita/vedamantra-book (mantras/), the Navagraha sūktam as recited in the Taittirīya tradition (commit 097c82834d)
  • Commentaries — Sāyaṇa and Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara on the Taittirīya Saṃhitā, Brāhmaṇa and Āraṇyaka, as the vishvAsa corpus labels them (witnesses/comm.json).
  • The Mahānārāyaṇa — its units are proven in its own edition.

The vishvAsa corpus records its own sources for the Saṃhitā (TITUS and Parankuśācārya’s edition) and the Brāhmaṇa. GRETIL’s Ṛgveda is from Aufrecht’s edition (1877). The stotrasamhita vedamantra-book is a LaTeX edition for personal study; it is used here as the witness for the order of the sūktam, and the edition’s text of each mantra is its Vedic source.

Corrections to a base witness

  • budha-2.1 → े: In the Saṃhitā the ṛk runs on into the next (पाँसुर इरावती, -e before i becoming -a); the sūktam recites it alone, so its last word stands in its pausal form, पाँसुरे (as in RV 1.22.17).

Verification

Run on every pull request by scripts/prayoga/check.py (Validate Content). It proves that every Vedic unit equals its source in the Taittirīya Saṃhitā, Brāhmaṇa or Āraṇyaka apart from the logged corrections; that every text of the rite equals its base witness; that every difference of the other witness is listed and classified here; that every item of the rite exists, including the Mahānārāyaṇa units it recites; that every padaccheda rejoins by sandhi into the text and every word table lists exactly those words; and that every Sanskrit phrase quoted from a commentator occurs in that commentator’s text on the passage.
