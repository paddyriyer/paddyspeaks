A study edition · the Vedic mantras of the nine grahas

नवग्रहसूक्तम्Navagraha Sūktam

The Vedic mantras recited for the nine grahas, three for each, in the order of the Taittirīya tradition — every mantra cut from the Taittirīya Saṃhitā or Brāhmaṇa (or, once, the Ṛgveda) and verified, divided into words and translated.

The nine grahas — the Sun, Mars, Venus, the Moon, Mercury, Jupiter, Saturn, and Rāhu and Ketu, the two nodes of the Moon — are worshipped together in the rites of the gṛhya tradition, and the mantras recited for them are Vedic. None of these mantras was composed for a graha: each is a verse of the Saṃhitā or the Brāhmaṇa chosen because it names the graha’s deity, or a word that sounds like its name — āpyāyasva for Soma, bṛhaspate for Bṛhaspati, śaṃ no devīḥ for Śanaiścara, ketuṃ kṛṇvan for Ketu. This edition gives the sūktam as the Taittirīya tradition of Tamil Nadu recites it: for each graha its own mantra and two more for the deities who attend it, then a salutation. Each mantra is printed with its svara from its source, its Ṛgveda parallel, and what Sāyaṇa or Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara says of it in its original setting.

Another tradition: the Saṃskāraratnamālā

The Navagraha rite is not fixed in one form. The Saṃskāraratnamālā, a prayoga of the Hiraṇyakeśin sūtra (vishvAsa/vedAH_yajuH, taittirIyam/sUtram/hiraNyakeshI/gRhyam/paddhatiH/saMskAra-ratna-mAlA/01/03), names the same mantras for eight of the nine grahas, but for Śukra it has śukraṃ te anyat, and it sets out the adhidevatās and pratyadhidevatās as two separate lists of its own (for the adhidevatās, among others, tryambakam for Īśvara, gaurīr mimāya for Umā and imaṃ yama for Yama; for the pratyadhidevatās, among others, agniṃ dūtam for Agni, apsu me for the Waters and syonā pṛthivi for the Earth). How the text was established

The order and the choice of mantras are those of witness S, the stotrasamhita Navagraha sūktam. Each mantra was then cut from its own source with svara: the Taittirīya Saṃhitā in its pañcāśat divisions, or the Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa (Drāviḍa text, or muralīdhara’s for agniṃ dūtam), and collated with a second transcription. The last mantra of Ketu, sa citra citram, is marked as a ṛk in S and is not in the Taittirīya texts; its base is the Ṛgveda (6.6.7, GRETIL, without svara). The second mantra of Aṅgāraka, syonā pṛthivi, is Mahānārāyaṇa 10.1.10, shown from that edition.

Places are given in the source’s own numbering, checked against the commentary files’ anuvāka numbers; where the base text’s own markers drift (TS 1.1.14, 1.6.12, 4.2.11) the citation stops at the anuvāka. The commentaries quoted are on the verse in its Vedic setting — a sacrifice, an iṣṭi, the piling of the fire altar — not on its use for a graha: that use belongs to the later gṛhya tradition, and the edition says so on each page.
