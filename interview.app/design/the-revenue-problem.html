The Revenue Problem

Finance reports $4.2 million. Product reports $4.6 million. Both dashboards wear a large green checkmark that says “Data Verified”. Leadership wants one beautiful number, the engineering manager wants to average them, the VP of Product wants a colour-coded chart, Finance knows it is right, and the CEO wants it by Friday. A senior product design interview, worked round by round — starting with questions, not screens — with the SQL that accounts for every one of the four hundred thousand dollars.

◆ At a glance the 20-second version — the full interview below

The senior move

Treat the gap as information. Find out whether it is a definition or an error, give each legitimate number its own name, owner and purpose, and make the bridge between them the centre of the experience.

The two numbers

  • Gross Order Value — Product, $4.6M: what customers tried to buy, by order date.
  • Recognized Revenue — Finance, $4.2M: what was earned, by fulfilment date.

The artefacts

  • A metric card that says what the number is
  • A bridge with an unexplained line
  • A verification sentence instead of a green check
  • Comprehension tests, not preference tests

The trap

Averaging to $4.4M, hiding the disagreement, or assuming Finance is right. On the synthetic data, none of the 32 definitions the rules allow produces $4.4M. It is not a number; it is a truce.

§ 00 — THE PROMPTA $400,000 problem disguised as a dashboard redesign

Interview Prompt

“Finance reports monthly revenue of $4.2 million. Product reports $4.6 million. Both dashboards say ‘Data Verified’. Leadership wants a new dashboard that shows one number. You are the lead product designer. How would you redesign the revenue reporting experience so that different teams can trust, understand and appropriately compare the numbers? Start with questions, not screens.”

LEVEL · SENIOR PRODUCT / UX DESIGNERDURATION · 60 MINFORMAT · SIX ROUNDS, ONE QUESTION AT A TIME

Everyone in the room has already proposed a solution. The engineering manager wants to average the two figures. The VP of Product wants a colour-coded chart. Finance insists its number is correct. The CEO wants the redesign by Friday. Each of these is a confident answer to a question nobody has asked yet: are these two numbers trying to measure the same thing? If they are, one of them is wrong and no design can fix that. If they are not, both may be right — and the real defect is that two different things are wearing the same name and the same green checkmark.

§ 01 — ROUND 1 · PROBLEM DISCOVERYQuestions before pixels

◆ Interviewer

“Before you design anything: what would you want to know?”

Listening for: whether the candidate separates a conflicting calculation — one of the numbers is wrong — from different legitimate definitions — both are right about different things. Everything downstream depends on which one this is, and the honest first answer is "I don't know yet, and here is how I would find out."

A strong candidate asks questions in families, because each family can explain part of a gap on its own.

Definition — what is in each number?

  • Does each figure include sales tax? Shipping? Discounts at list or net? Gift cards when sold or when redeemed?
  • Are refunds, chargebacks and cancellations netted out — and in which month, the order's or the refund's?
  • Are test, demo and internal orders excluded? By what rule, and who maintains it?

Time — which month does a dollar belong to?

  • Which timestamp decides the month: order placed, payment captured, shipped, delivered, or recognised over a subscription term?
  • In which time zone is the month drawn? An order at 9 pm in New York on the 31st is already the 1st in UTC.
  • Is the month closed? Can the number still change, and who decides when it stops?

Population and source

  • Which entities, regions, currencies and channels? Which exchange rate, on which date?
  • Which tables, which pipelines, which transformations? Who wrote each query, and when was it last changed?

Verification and decisions

  • What, exactly, does “Data Verified” verify? That a pipeline ran? That row counts match the source? That the number ties to the general ledger?
  • Who uses each number to decide what — the board deck, the forecast, a pricing change, a checkout experiment?
  • Has the gap always been about $400K? A gap that is stable as a percentage smells like a definition. A gap that wanders smells like a bug.

The distinction the round is really about

Strong answer“I'd pull both queries and spend half a day writing a bridge from one number to the other before anyone draws a screen. If every dollar maps to a rule, we have two legitimate metrics with one name and I'm designing for clarity. If dollars are left over, someone has a bug and the redesign waits for the fix. Either way, I want to know who decides with each number, because the dashboard is for decisions, not for the numbers.”
Weak answer“I'd start with a stakeholder workshop to align on the vision for a unified revenue dashboard, then explore three visual directions.”
◆ Interviewer · follow-up

“Interesting. The workshop sounds lovely. Which number is true?”

Probe: if the candidate says "Finance's", ask what Finance's number would tell a product manager about a checkout experiment. If they say "neither until we check", ask what they would check first.

§ 02 — ROUND 2 · USER RESEARCHFour readers, four decisions, four kinds of distrust

◆ Interviewer

“Your users are Finance analysts, product managers, executives and data engineers. How do you find out what each of them actually needs from revenue reporting?”

Listening for: research organised around decisions and trust, not around feature wish-lists — and the recognition that these groups may legitimately need different metrics.
ReaderDecidesNeedsTrust concernCost of being wrong

Methods that fit the problem

THE LAST SURPRISE
"Tell me about the last time a revenue number surprised you. What did you do next?" The answer reveals the real workflow — usually a spreadsheet, a Slack thread and a meeting — far better than "what do you need?"
ARTEFACT AUDIT
Collect every export, spreadsheet and slide where revenue is re-keyed. Each one is a vote of no confidence in the dashboard, and each one shows which definition the author actually trusted.
CONTEXTUAL INQUIRY
Sit with Finance during close week and with a PM during an experiment readout. Decisions happen under deadlines, in meetings, on phones; the design has to survive that, not a quiet usability lab.
DECISION MAPPING
For each recurring decision, record which number was used, who owned it, and what would change the decision. The output is a decision-to-metric matrix — the true requirements document.
TRUST INTERVIEW
"What would make you believe this number?" Finance will say "it ties to the ledger"; a PM will say "I can see my experiment in it"; an executive will say "Finance and Product agree on why it differs". Three different trust contracts, all designable.
Strong answer“I'd map decisions to metrics before designing a single view. If the board needs earned revenue and the growth team needs demand by order date, that's two metrics, not a conflict — and the research question becomes how each group finds the right one, understands it, and knows when the other one is the better tool.”
Weak answer“I'd send a survey asking each team which chart types they prefer and which KPIs they want on the homepage.”
◆ Interviewer · follow-up

“So should everyone use the same revenue number?”

The strong answer: the same name must always mean the same thing; that does not mean everyone uses the same metric. Semantic consistency, not numeric uniformity.

§ 03 — ROUND 3 · UX ARCHITECTUREMake the definition the interface

◆ Interviewer

“Propose a reporting experience that makes definitions, reporting periods, exclusions, ownership and freshness understandable. And no, you may not assume everyone uses the same revenue metric.”

Listening for: the difference between visual consistency (both tiles look the same) and semantic consistency (the same word means the same thing everywhere). Today the two dashboards are visually consistent and semantically broken: same label, same green check, different meaning.

Principle 1 · A name is a promise

The bare word “Revenue” is retired from every tile. Each metric gets a name that says what it is, and the same name means the same calculation in every view, export and API. Here that gives two metrics with two names: Gross Order Value (Product) and Recognized Revenue (Finance). If a third team needs a third definition, it gets a third name — never a third meaning for an old one.

◆ Interviewer · aside

“A tooltip saying ‘Revenue’ is not a definition. It is a label with confidence issues.”

Principle 2 · Definitions are data, not prose in a wiki

If the definition lives in a wiki, the dashboard will drift from it. Store it as data — purpose, owner, time basis, time zone, inclusions, exclusions, version, approver — and render the card from the record. Changing a definition then means changing a row, with a version number the screen can show.

SQL · POSTGRESQL 16 · EXECUTED — THE METRIC CATALOGUE AND ITS CHECKS

Principle 3 · Replace the green check with a sentence that can be wrong

“Data Verified” verified something — on Product's side, that a pipeline ran and row counts matched. It did not verify that test orders were excluded, and nothing on the screen said so. A check is only meaningful if it names what was checked, for which period, when, and what failed. The catalogue renders exactly that:

SQL · POSTGRESQL 16 · EXECUTED — THE VERIFICATION SENTENCE
-- Replace the green "Data Verified" check with a sentence that can be wrong: -- what was checked, for which period, when, and what failed. SELECT d.display_name, format('%s · %s · month by %s (%s) · owner: %s · v%s', c.period, d.purpose, d.time_basis, d.time_zone, d.owner_role, d.version) AS context, format('%s of %s checks passed', count(*) FILTER (WHERE c.passed), count(*)) AS status, string_agg(CASE WHEN NOT c.passed THEN c.check_name || ': ' || c.detail END, '; ') AS failing, to_char(max(c.checked_at) AT TIME ZONE d.time_zone, 'YYYY-MM-DD HH24:MI') || ' ' || d.time_zone AS as_of FROM metric_definition d JOIN metric_check c USING (metric_key) WHERE c.period = '2026-10' GROUP BY d.metric_key, d.display_name, c.period, d.purpose, d.time_basis, d.time_zone, d.owner_role, d.version ORDER BY d.display_name;

The metric card

FIG. 4 — The card is rendered from the catalogue record. Status, inclusions and checks are words first; colour only repeats them.

Three layers, so the executive never has to read SQL

L1 · SUMMARY
The named numbers for the forum, each with its purpose line and status. When two related metrics differ, the difference appears as one line with a link: "$400,000 explained · $0.00 unexplained".
L2 · METRIC PAGE
The full card, the trend, the bridge to its sibling metrics, the change log of the definition ("v3, 15 Jan 2026: refunds now netted in the month issued").
L3 · LINEAGE
Sources, transformations, the query, every check and its history. Built for Finance analysts and data engineers; the executive never needs it, and is never blocked by it.

Accessible by construction

  • Never colour alone. "Closed", "Preliminary" and "2 of 3 checks passed" are words; the green and amber only repeat them (WCAG 1.4.1).
  • No hover-only definitions. Definitions sit on the card or open on click and keyboard focus, dismissible and persistent — a hover tooltip fails on touch screens and often for keyboards (WCAG 1.4.13).
  • Every chart has a table. The bridge is a waterfall for the eye and a table of signed amounts for screen readers and for copying into a deck.
  • Numbers carry their units. "4.2M" becomes "$4,200,000 (US dollars)" in the accessible name, with the period and status.
Weak answer“A unified dashboard with a hero KPI, a trend sparkline and a green-amber-red status, with definitions in an info tooltip.”

§ 04 — ROUND 4 · THE TWISTWhere the $400,000 went

◆ Interviewer

“New information. The Product dashboard includes sales tax, refunded orders and test orders, and counts an order in the month it was placed. Finance excludes tax and test orders, counts an order in the month it was fulfilled, and nets refunds in the month they were issued. What changes in your design?”

Start with what the two dashboards do today. Both queries are correct implementations of their own rules, which is precisely why the green checks are not lying — they are just answering different questions under the same label.

WITH earned AS ( SELECT sum(subtotal) AS amount FROM orders WHERE NOT is_test AND fulfilled_at >= TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-10-01 00:00 America/New_York' AND fulfilled_at < TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-11-01 00:00 America/New_York' ), refunded AS ( SELECT sum(r.amount) AS amount FROM refunds r JOIN orders o USING (order_id) WHERE NOT o.is_test AND r.refunded_at >= TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-10-01 00:00 America/New_York' AND r.refunded_at < TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-11-01 00:00 America/New_York' ) SELECT to_char(earned.amount - refunded.amount, 'FM$9,999,999.00') AS "Revenue (Finance)" FROM earned, refunded;

The bridge — the most important view in the product

Walk from one named number to the other, one rule at a time, each step computed from the rows. The last line is what no rule explains. It is the design's most important pixel: zero means two honest metrics; anything else means a bug.

SQL · POSTGRESQL 16 · EXECUTED — THE RECONCILIATION BRIDGE
-- The bridge: walk from one named number to the other, one rule at a time. -- Every step is computed from the rows, and the last line is what no rule -- explains. If that line is not zero, you have found a bug, not a definition. WITH p AS ( -- Product's population: placed in October, UTC SELECT * FROM orders WHERE ordered_at >= TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-10-01 00:00 UTC' AND ordered_at < TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-11-01 00:00 UTC' ), f AS ( -- Finance's population: real orders fulfilled in October, New York SELECT * FROM orders WHERE NOT is_test AND fulfilled_at >= TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-10-01 00:00 America/New_York' AND fulfilled_at < TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-11-01 00:00 America/New_York' ), steps AS ( SELECT 1 AS step, 'Gross Order Value (Product)' AS line, (SELECT sum(subtotal + tax) FROM p) AS amount UNION ALL SELECT 2, 'less sales tax collected for the state', -(SELECT sum(tax) FROM p) UNION ALL SELECT 3, 'less test orders', -(SELECT sum(subtotal) FROM p WHERE is_test) UNION ALL SELECT 4, 'less October orders not fulfilled in October', -(SELECT sum(subtotal) FROM p WHERE NOT is_test AND order_id NOT IN (SELECT order_id FROM f)) UNION ALL SELECT 5, 'plus earlier orders fulfilled in October', (SELECT sum(subtotal) FROM f WHERE order_id NOT IN (SELECT order_id FROM p)) UNION ALL SELECT 6, 'less refunds issued in October', -(SELECT sum(r.amount) FROM refunds r JOIN orders o USING (order_id) WHERE NOT o.is_test AND r.refunded_at >= TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-10-01 00:00 America/New_York' AND r.refunded_at < TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-11-01 00:00 America/New_York') ), finance AS ( SELECT (SELECT sum(subtotal) FROM f) - (SELECT sum(r.amount) FROM refunds r JOIN orders o USING (order_id) WHERE NOT o.is_test AND r.refunded_at >= TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-10-01 00:00 America/New_York' AND r.refunded_at < TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-11-01 00:00 America/New_York') AS amount ) SELECT step, line, amount, sum(amount) OVER (ORDER BY step) AS running_total FROM steps UNION ALL SELECT 7, 'Recognized Revenue (Finance)', amount, amount FROM finance UNION ALL SELECT 8, 'unexplained by any rule', (SELECT amount FROM finance) - (SELECT sum(amount) FROM steps), NULL ORDER BY step;

What changes in the design

The twist does not make one team right. It sorts the $400,000 into three kinds of difference, and each kind gets a different design response.

DifferenceAmountKindDesign response
Sales tax−$230,000Definition, but a questionable oneTax is collected for the state. US GAAP lets a company elect to exclude it from revenue and IFRS 15 excludes amounts collected for third parties, so a "revenue" that includes it invites misuse. Rename Product's metric "Gross Order Value" — and agree with Product whether demand should carry tax at all
Test orders−$15,000DefectNo decision wants test orders. Fix the pipeline; until then the card says "2 of 3 checks passed: test orders included"
Order date vs fulfilment date−$180,000 / +$120,000Legitimate definitionDemand belongs to the month a customer ordered; earned revenue to the month it was delivered. Both cards state their time basis; the bridge shows the shift
Refunds−$95,000Legitimate definitionFinance nets refunds in the month issued. Product may want "net of refunds by order cohort" — a third, named metric, not a silent edit to the second

The time zone is part of the definition

Same rule, same rows, month drawn in UTC instead of New York: real money moves between months. A card that does not state its time zone has not stated its definition.

SQL · POSTGRESQL 16 · EXECUTED — ONE RULE, TWO MONTH BOUNDARIES
-- The time zone is part of the definition. The same rule, with the month -- drawn in UTC instead of New York, moves real money between months. WITH bounds AS ( SELECT 'America/New_York' AS tz, TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-10-01 00:00 America/New_York' AS lo, TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-11-01 00:00 America/New_York' AS hi UNION ALL SELECT 'UTC', TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-10-01 00:00 UTC', TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-11-01 00:00 UTC' ) SELECT b.tz AS month_drawn_in, (SELECT sum(subtotal) FROM orders WHERE NOT is_test AND fulfilled_at >= b.lo AND fulfilled_at < b.hi) - (SELECT sum(r.amount) FROM refunds r JOIN orders o USING (order_id) WHERE NOT o.is_test AND r.refunded_at >= b.lo AND r.refunded_at < b.hi) AS recognized_revenue FROM bounds b ORDER BY 1;

And what an error looks like

Suppose someone tries to "fix" Product's number by netting refunds with a join. Orders with two refunds are counted twice, and the bridge's last line stops being zero. That is the moment the design earns its keep: the experience shows the residual instead of absorbing it into a nicer chart.

SQL · POSTGRESQL 16 · EXECUTED — A FAN-OUT BUG, CAUGHT BY THE RESIDUAL
-- A well-meant fix that is a calculation error, not a definition: "net the -- refunds out of Product's number". The join fans out — an order with two -- refunds is counted twice — so the bridge's last line stops being zero. WITH buggy AS ( SELECT sum(o.subtotal + o.tax) - sum(COALESCE(r.amount, 0)) AS amount FROM orders o LEFT JOIN refunds r USING (order_id) WHERE o.ordered_at >= TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-10-01 00:00 UTC' AND o.ordered_at < TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-11-01 00:00 UTC' ), correct AS ( -- aggregate refunds to the order's grain first SELECT sum(o.subtotal + o.tax) - sum(COALESCE(r.refunded, 0)) AS amount FROM orders o LEFT JOIN (SELECT order_id, sum(amount) AS refunded FROM refunds GROUP BY order_id) r USING (order_id) WHERE o.ordered_at >= TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-10-01 00:00 UTC' AND o.ordered_at < TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-11-01 00:00 UTC' ), fanned_out AS ( SELECT count(*) AS orders_counted_twice FROM (SELECT order_id FROM refunds GROUP BY order_id HAVING count(*) > 1) d JOIN orders o USING (order_id) WHERE o.ordered_at >= TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-10-01 00:00 UTC' AND o.ordered_at < TIMESTAMPTZ '2026-11-01 00:00 UTC' ) SELECT buggy.amount AS "as written", correct.amount AS "refunds at order grain", buggy.amount - correct.amount AS "unexplained", orders_counted_twice FROM buggy, correct, fanned_out;

§ 05 — ROUND 5 · VALIDATIONUnderstanding, not applause

◆ Interviewer

“How would you test whether the redesign improves understanding and decisions — rather than simply producing prettier charts?”

Listening for: comprehension and decision tasks with right answers, measured against the current design — and attention to people who are confidently wrong, the most expensive state a dashboard can leave someone in. Preference ("which do you like?") is not evidence.

SQL · POSTGRESQL 16 · EXECUTED — THE STUDY TABLE
-- Did people understand more, or did they just like the colours? Accuracy, -- time, and the dangerous cell: wrong but confident. (Rows are SYNTHETIC here.) CREATE TABLE study_response ( participant TEXT NOT NULL, role TEXT NOT NULL, variant TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (variant IN ('today', 'redesign')), task TEXT NOT NULL, correct BOOLEAN NOT NULL, confidence SMALLINT NOT NULL CHECK (confidence BETWEEN 1 AND 5), seconds INT NOT NULL, PRIMARY KEY (participant, task) );
SQL · POSTGRESQL 16 · EXECUTED — ACCURACY, TIME, AND THE CONFIDENTLY WRONG
SELECT variant, task, count(*) AS n, round(100.0 * avg(correct::int)) AS pct_correct, percentile_cont(0.5) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY seconds)::int AS median_s, round(100.0 * avg((NOT correct AND confidence >= 4)::int)) AS pct_confidently_wrong FROM study_response GROUP BY variant, task ORDER BY task, variant DESC;

The responses were generated from probabilities we chose, so the table shows the shape of the analysis and proves nothing about the redesign. The column to design for is the last one. The hypothesis worth testing is that two identical green checks make people confidently wrong — about whether October is final, for instance. A real study has to show that cell falling, not just accuracy rising.

After launch: measure the organisation, not the pixels

FEWER RECONCILIATIONS
"Which number is right?" threads, tickets and meetings per month — the baseline is countable today.
FEWER SPREADSHEETS
Exports and re-keyed decks for revenue; each one that disappears is a recovered vote of confidence.
RIGHT METRIC, RIGHT FORUM
The share of board and planning documents citing a named, certified metric with its version, audited quarterly.
RESIDUAL WATCH
The bridge's unexplained line, monitored every day. Its first non-zero value is a bug report the dashboard files by itself.

§ 06 — ROUND 6 · EXECUTIVE PRESSURE“Just make both dashboards show $4.4 million”

◆ Interviewer, as the CEO

“Just make both dashboards show $4.4 million. We need alignment, not another meeting.”

Listening for: constructive pushback — respect for the CEO's real goal (alignment, speed), a refusal to publish a number that no definition produces, and an offer that ships by Friday.

First, check the claim behind the request. Is $4.4M the answer to any question? Enumerate every definition the four rules and two time zones allow:

-- "Just average them": is $4.4M the answer to ANY question? Enumerate every -- definition the four rules and two time zones allow, and look for it. WITH rules AS ( SELECT tax, tests, gross_of_refunds, basis, tz FROM unnest(ARRAY[true, false]) AS tax(tax) CROSS JOIN unnest(ARRAY[true, false]) AS tests(tests) CROSS JOIN unnest(ARRAY[true, false]) AS g(gross_of_refunds) CROSS JOIN unnest(ARRAY['placed', 'fulfilled']) AS basis(basis) CROSS JOIN unnest(ARRAY['UTC', 'America/New_York']) AS tz(tz) ), defs AS ( SELECT r.*, (SELECT sum(o.subtotal + CASE WHEN r.tax THEN o.tax ELSE 0 END) FROM orders o WHERE (r.tests OR NOT o.is_test) AND CASE r.basis WHEN 'placed' THEN o.ordered_at ELSE o.fulfilled_at END >= ('2026-10-01'::timestamp AT TIME ZONE r.tz) AND CASE r.basis WHEN 'placed' THEN o.ordered_at ELSE o.fulfilled_at END < ('2026-11-01'::timestamp AT TIME ZONE r.tz)) - CASE WHEN r.gross_of_refunds THEN 0 ELSE (SELECT sum(x.amount) FROM refunds x JOIN orders o USING (order_id) WHERE (r.tests OR NOT o.is_test) AND x.refunded_at >= ('2026-10-01'::timestamp AT TIME ZONE r.tz) AND x.refunded_at < ('2026-11-01'::timestamp AT TIME ZONE r.tz)) END AS amount FROM rules r ) SELECT count(*) AS definitions, count(DISTINCT amount) AS distinct_answers, count(*) FILTER (WHERE amount = 4400000.00) AS definitions_giving_4_4m, min(abs(amount - 4400000.00)) AS nearest_miss FROM defs;

Thirty-two definitions, thirty-two different answers, none of them $4.4M. The average is not a compromise between two metrics; it is a third number that measures nothing, with no owner who can defend it when the board, an auditor or an investor asks how it was computed.

A strong response, in the room “I want alignment too, and you'll have it by Friday — but not on a number nobody can define. $4.4M isn't produced by any rule we have; the first time someone asks how we got it, we lose more trust than we have today. Here's what I can ship by Friday instead: both tiles renamed for what they are, Recognized Revenue at $4.2M for the board and Gross Order Value at $4.6M for growth reviews, a one-click bridge showing every dollar of the $400K with zero unexplained, and the green checks replaced with what was actually checked. I need ten minutes from you, not a meeting: confirm that Recognized Revenue is the company's headline number for external and board use. Finance owns it; Product owns demand. That's the alignment.”

Weak answer B — refusal without an offer“That would be misleading, so I can't do that.” (Correct, and useless by Friday.)
◆ Interviewer · closing

“Should we call it a Single Source of Truth or a Single Source of Trouble?”

The strong answer: a single source of definitions. Truth is plural here — demand and earned revenue are both true — but each name has one meaning, one owner and one bridge to its neighbours.
